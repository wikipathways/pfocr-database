caption: A conceptual framework of pathobiome-host interactions in bleaching disease
  of S. japonica sporelings. A Shift from healthy bacteria community to pathobiome
  under environmental stress. Under deteriorating environmental conditions, the bacterial
  community associated with healthy sporelings undergoes a compositional shift toward
  a pathobiome, which is a critical step in the onset of bleaching disease. B Pathobiome-host
  interactions in bleaching disease. The progression of bleaching disease in S. japonica
  sporelings is driven by dynamic interactions between the pathobiome and the host.
  The core taxa of the pathobiome upregulate VGs involved in chemotaxis, motility,
  and toxin production, facilitating their colonization and infection of the host.
  In response to pathobiome infection, the host reallocates resources from growth
  to defense, activating a series of defense mechanisms, including [LE1] cell wall
  strengthening, ROS burst, and antibiotic production. Simultaneously, the core pathobiome
  taxa modulate the expression of genes associated with antioxidant enzymes and antibiotic
  resistance, thereby counteracting host defense mechanisms and facilitating their
  survival and proliferation within the host environment. When the host’s defenses
  fail to withstand the pathobiome infection, bleaching disease occurs[LE2] in the
  sporelings. Throughout this process, metabolites and proteins encoded by upregulated
  genes are indicated in black, while those encoded by downregulated genes are shown
  in blue. Pathways and functions encoded by upregulated genes in S. japonica sporelings
  are represented in green, with dashed black lines illustrating potential regulatory
