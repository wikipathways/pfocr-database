caption: Endogenous DNA damage often results from mishaps during DNA replication.
  Recent studies have suggested that the context in which recombinogenic DSBs are
  generated influences their processing and repair. (1) Replication-coupled DSBs,
  such as those generated by nick-induced fork collapse, undergo immediate end resection
  with no requirement for BRCA1 and is not inhibited by 53BP1); however, BRCA1 is
  essential for the recruitment of PALB2-BRCA2 leading to canonical RAD51 assembly
  and recombination. (2) Replication-independent DSBs by contrast, such as those generated
  by ionizing radiation or restriction enzymes, do appear to require BRCA1 for optimal
  end resection. BRCA1 may achieve this by directly stimulating the catalytic activities
  of resection nucleases as well as indirectly by counteracting 53BP1 and its effectors.
  Once replication-independent DSBs are resected, BRCA1 is again needed to facilitate
  RAD51 assembly and recombination. Interestingly, loss of 53BP1 affords BRCA1-deficient
  cells an alternative way to assemble RAD51 at both replication-coupled and -independent
  DSBs by enabling direct interaction between PALB2 and ubiquitylated histone H2A.
  However, while this backup pathway can often restore HR to levels sufficient to
  sustain embryonic development in mice, it is not adequate to prevent tumor formation.
  This suggests that BRCA1-mediated RAD51 assembly during repair of spontaneous replication-coupled
  DSBs represents its key function in tumor suppression. Created in BioRender. Zong,
