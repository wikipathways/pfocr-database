caption: 'Key drug targets and corresponding treatment agents and mechanisms. Immune
  checkpoint interactions between T cells and CRC cells are modulated by PD-1 (on
  T cells) binding to PD-L1 (on CRC cells), inhibited by Pembrolizumab and Nivolumab,
  while CTLA-4 (on T cells) binding to B7 (on CRC cells) is blocked by Ipilimumab
  and Tremelimumab. Intracellularly, the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK pathway is activated following
  the binding of the epidermal growth factor (EGF) to EGFR, with targets including
  RAS (modulated by Vemurafenib, Dabrafenib, Regorafenib, Encorafenib), MEK (Binimetinib,
  Trametinib), and ERK. Concurrently, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is also initiated
  by EGFR, where PI3K is targeted by Alpelisib, Taselisib, AKT by Ipatasertib, and
  mTOR by Everolimus, Temsirolimus. The Wnt/β-catenin pathway is modulated by hepatocyte
  growth factor (HGF) binding to c-MET (targeted by Rilotumumab (HGF) and Onartuzumab
  (c-MET)) and directly by PKF115-584 targeting β-catenin. Moreover, EGFR is directly
  inhibited by Cetuximab, Panitumumab, Lapatinib, while the vascular endothelial growth
  factor (VEGF)-VEGFR axis is targeted by Bevacizumab, Ziv-aflibercept (VEGF-directed)
  or Ramucirumab, Fruquintinib (VEGFR-directed). Finally, chemotherapeutic agents
  promote DNA damage via multiple mechanisms. For instance, irinotecan causes double-strand
  breaks, Oxaliplatin induces interstrand crosslinks, and 5-Fluorouracil induces base
  pair mismatches. Abbreviations: CTLA-4, Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein
  4; PD-1, Programmed cell death protein 1; PD-L1, Programmed death-ligand 1; EGFR,
  Epidermal growth factor receptor; VEGFR, Vascular endothelial growth factor receptor;
  mTOR, Mammalian target of rapamycin; CRC, Colorectal cancer'
