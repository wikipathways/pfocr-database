caption: Model for Mtb growth arrest on propionate at acidic pH and mechanisms of
  suppression by mutations in phoPR or pyrazinamide treatment. Propionate-dependent
  acid growth arrest is a PhoPR-regulated response driven by the diversion of carbon
  away from central metabolism and into the synthesis of lipids. At acidic pH, propionyl-CoA
  is used as a substrate to synthesize methyl-branched lipids, either through the
  polyketide synthases Pks2, Pks3, and Pks4 or through the methyl-malonyl pathway.
  Additionally, the enzyme MutAB, a vitamin B12-dependent enzyme, catalyzes the reversible
  interconversion between malonyl-CoA and succinyl-CoA, contributing to the synthesis
  of branched lipids. In either case, low pH triggers remodeling of central metabolism
  toward complex lipid synthesis to alter the mycomembrane structure as a pH-dependent
  adaptation, consuming the CoA pools and slowing or arresting growth in the process.
  This pH-dependent adaptation is lost in the ΔphoPR mutant or under indirect PhoPR
  chemical inhibition using ethoxzolamide. Instead, the propionate is metabolized
  into pyruvate by the methylcitrate cycle, a permissive carbon source, increasing
  CoA pools and permitting fast growth. Additionally, at acidic pH, the ΔphoPR mutant
  upregulates triacylglycerol synthesis using Tgs1. Chemical inhibition of tgs1 expression
  using MSU-39446 prevents diversion of CoA pools into TAG synthesis and away from
  central metabolism, increasing CoA pools, metabolism, and growth in both the WT
  and ΔphoPR mutant. PZA treatment causes enhanced growth in the WT, which may be
  driven by inhibiting lipid synthesis via depletion of CoA pools
