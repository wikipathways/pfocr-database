caption: Regulation of host cellular endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress by various
  ASFV proteins. ASFV modulates all three canonical branches of the unfolded protein
  response (UPR) which include ATF6, PERK, and IRE1 to reshape endoplasmic reticulum
  (ER) function and promote viral replication. ASFV infection triggers ER remodeling,
  a process mediated in part by the viral protein E146L. The viral protein B117L activates
  the ATF6 pathway and disrupts ER calcium homeostasis, leading to elevated cytosolic
  Ca2+ levels that enhance viral replication. Within the PERK branch, the viral protein
  EP152R regulates PERK activation, while MGF110-7L promotes phosphorylation of its
  downstream target eIF2α. Additional viral proteins, including H359L, C315R, and
  E66L, further increase PKR phosphorylation levels, thereby suppressing global protein
  translation while selectively inducing ATF4 expression. In the IRE1 pathway, ASFV
  influences multiple IRE1-dependent signaling outputs, including regulated IRE1-dependent
  decay (RIDD), splicing of XBP1 mRNA to generate the active transcription factor
  XBP1s, and activation of the JNK signaling cascade. Viral proteins highlighted in
  red denote ASFV gene products that interact with or modulate specific host UPR components.
  Collectively, these interactions illustrate how ASFV fine-tunes ER stress signaling
  to balance host cell survival with efficient viral replication. (Created with the
