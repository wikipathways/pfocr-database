caption: The gut microbiota influences neural and tumour activities by regulating
  macrophage polarisation. PS‐A derived from the gut microbiota can activate the TLR2‐STAT3
  signalling pathway, driving macrophages to polarise into the M2 phenotype, producing
  immunosuppressive cytokines, while also acting on Schwann cells via TGF‐β to activate
  ERK/MAPK signalling; Schwann cells secrete Netrin‐1 to influence tumour‐associated
  neurons. M2 macrophages can also secrete GDNF to activate RET receptor‐positive
  neurons, promoting the release of CGRP, which in turn inhibits CD8+ T cell activity
  and facilitates tumour immune evasion. In contrast, M1 macrophages can be activated
  through LPS or inhibition of the HDAC pathway, releasing IL‐12 and TNF‐α to activate
  TRPV1 receptor‐associated neurons, potentially exerting antitumor immune modulation.
