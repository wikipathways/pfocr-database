caption: This model illustrates how SA inhibits hypocotyl elongation in the dark via
  lipid peroxidation (LPO) and 1O2 production. SA-induced LPO triggers a burst of
  1O2, activating EX1-dependent (EX*) retrograde signaling (RS), which inhibits auxin-mediated
  hypocotyl elongation. In the absence of SA, auxin promotes hypocotyl elongation
  by inducing SAURs (e.g., SAUR19 and SAUR63), which inhibit PP2C.D family phosphatases.
  This inhibition prevents the dephosphorylation of the penultimate Thr947 residue
  on PM AHA 2, maintaining their activity. Active H+-ATPases, including AHA2, pump
  protons into the apoplast, lowering apoplastic pH, which loosens the cell wall by
  activating Expansins. In addition, the increased PM potential facilitates K+ uptake
  via inward-rectifying K+ channels. The resulting K+ accumulation promotes water
  uptake, increasing turgor pressure, which is required for cell wall expansion and
  elongation (96). Under SA treatment, NPR1 maintains the expression of auxin-related
  genes, including SAURs and EXPs, either by antagonizing EX1-mediated RS or through
  an unknown mechanism. In npr1 mutants, SA-induced LPO leads to elevated 1O2 and
  altered expression of detoxification genes, amplifying EX1-dependent signaling and
  further repressing auxin-responsive genes, resulting in reduced hypocotyl elongation.
  A similar, although weaker, repression is observed in WT plants. In the absence
  of EX1, this signaling pathway is disrupted, allowing partial restoration of auxin-responsive
  gene expression and rescuing hypocotyl elongation in npr1 mutants grown in the dark.
