caption: Working model. Mitochondrial mitochondrial NAD kinase 2 (NADK2) ‐dependent
  nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) facilitates Tau oligomers’ (TauOs’)
  entry into human neurons. TauOs upregulate the expression of NADK2 and stimulate
  the de novo synthesis of mitochondrial NADPH. Notably, this upregulation of NADPH
  was observed in the PS19 live mouse brain (which naturally produces human TauOs)
  months before cognitive decline onset, and in “inducible AD neurons” obtained from
  direct conversion of human dermal fibroblasts. Activation of this NADK2‐dependent
  biochemical pathway somehow controls the expression of the LRP1, a main TauO receptor
  in the neuronal plasma membrane. This vicious cycle, whereby TauOs orchestrate mitochondrial
  NADPH metabolism and membrane trafficking to stimulate their own uptake, implies
  that TauO‐mediated dysregulation of NADK2 is one of the earliest molecular missteps
  in Alzheimer's disease (AD) pathogenesis
