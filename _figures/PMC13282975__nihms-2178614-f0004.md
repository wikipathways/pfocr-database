caption: ILC2s rely on diet-derived nutrient and micro-nutrient cues to regulate their
  metabolism and phenotype. Various diet-derived nutrients and micro-nutrients have
  been identified as important regulators of ILC2 immunometabolic pathways. Short
  chain fatty acids, such as butyrate, suppress ILC2 effector activity by reducing
  the expression of Gata3, il-5, and il-13. Calcium also suppresses ILC2 activity
  by repressing the NF-kB pathway via increasing KLF2 expression. Contrastingly, Zinc
  has been identified as an essential cofactor for the transcription of pro-inflammatory
  genes in ILC2s, where zinc supplementation precipitates an increase in MAPK and
  NF-kB activity. Changes to intracellular iron concentrations have also been shown
  to alter the metabolism of ILC2s, particularly via the HIF1a pathway, whereby increased
  iron availability has been associated with reduced HIF1a signaling, and by extension,
  a reduction in pro-inflammatory phenotypes. Retinoic acid, a derivative of retinol
  (Vitamin A), has been shown to increase the production of the anti-inflammatory
  cytokine IL-10 by ILC2s
