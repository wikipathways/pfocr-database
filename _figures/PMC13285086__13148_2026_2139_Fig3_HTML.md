caption: 'This figure integrates existing literature and inferential assumptions to
  illustrate three major processes: glucose glycolysis in cells, pyruvate oxidative
  decarboxylation, and protein lactylation. The glycolytic pathway proceeds from glucose
  to Pyr via HK2, GAPDH, PGK, PGAM, ENO, PKM2, among which HK2, PFK-1 and PKM are
  key enzymes. We hypothesize that blocking any enzyme,especially these key ones,can
  effectively interrupt glycolysis, suppress lactate production and thereby inhibit
  lactylation. Secondly, enhancing pyruvate dehydrogenase complex activity to channel
  pyruvate into the TCA cycle may decrease pyruvate conversion to lactate. Likewise,
  inhibiting lactate dehydrogenase activity directly reduces lactate formation, diminishing
  the substrate for lactylation and thus suppressing this modification. Within the
  epigenetic pathway, lactate generated by the Warburg effect is activated to lactyl-CoA,
  which, catalyzed by “writer”, binds to histone lysine residues to establish lactylation;
  “eraser” can reverse this, decomposing the modified histone back to unmodified histone
  plus lactate. Therefore, blocking “writer” or augmenting “eraser” activity may serve
  as direct strategies to inhibit lactylation'
