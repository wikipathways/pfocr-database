caption: Mechanistic model of synthetic lethality between BRCA1/2 and PARP, and major
  resistance mechanisms. A In homologous recombination (HR)-proficient cells, PARP
  inhibitors bind to PARP1 and cause trapping on single-strand breaks (SSBs). Replication-associated
  double-strand breaks (DSBs) can still be accurately repaired by intact HR, allowing
  cell survival with limited cytotoxicity. B In BRCA1/2-deficient tumor cells, HR
  is impaired. PARP inhibition blocks SSB repair and “traps” PARP1 on DNA, leading
  to replication fork collapse and extensive DSB accumulation. In the absence of HR,
  cells rely on error-prone alternative end-joining pathways, resulting in catastrophic
  genomic instability and cell death—this constitutes the classical synthetic lethal
  interaction between BRCA1/2 loss and PARP inhibition. C Restoration of HR represents
  a major acquired resistance mechanism. Secondary BRCA1/2 reversion mutations or
  loss of 53BP1/Shieldin components partially restore DNA end resection and HR activity,
  allowing repair of PARP inhibitor-induced DSBs and conferring drug resistance. D
  PARP1 mutations that disrupt DNA binding or trapping (e.g., mutations in the zinc
  finger or R591C) reduce formation of toxic PARP1-DNA complexes without fully abolishing
  catalytic activity, thereby diminishing PARP inhibitor cytotoxicity and leading
  to clinical resistance
