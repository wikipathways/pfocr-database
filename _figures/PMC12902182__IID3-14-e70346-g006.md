caption: Schematic of GPR108's role in regulating TLR7 signaling in psoriasis. In
  an IMQ‐induced psoriasis‐like skin lesion model, the TLR7/MyD88/NF‐κB signaling
  pathway is activated in skin keratinocytes and macrophages. This activation leads
  to increased pro‐inflammatory cytokines, which in turn cause abnormal keratinocyte
  proliferation and differentiation, thereby exacerbating skin inflammation. GPR108
  acts as a potential inhibitor of TLR7 signaling by competing with MyD88 for binding
  to TLR7. This competition inhibits the nuclear translocation of downstream NF‐κB,
  thus suppressing inflammation. Consequently, GPR108 deficiency enhances the activation
  of the TLR7/MyD88/NF‐κB signaling pathway and promotes psoriasis
