caption: SMYD2-mediated pathways in chronic kidney disease (CKD). SMYD2 drives fibrosis
  through multiple context-specific mechanisms in CKD. In calcium oxalate (CaOx)-induced
  nephropathy, SMYD2 methylates PTEN, resulting in the activation of the AKT/mTOR
  pathway. This promotes glycolytic reprogramming in renal tubular cells, enhancing
  fibrotic progression. In unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) and cisplatin-induced
  CKD models, upregulated SMYD2 contributes to fibrosis by inducing G2/M cell cycle
  arrest, renal fibroblast activation, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT),
  and by activating TGF-β/Smad3, ERK, AKT, NF-κB, and STAT3 pathways. In diabetic
  kidney disease (DKD), SMYD2 is upregulated in response to high glucose (HG) or streptozotocin
  (STZ) exposure. It promotes NF-κB p65 phosphorylation, nuclear translocation, and
  transcriptional activation of fibrotic genes via histone methylation, exacerbating
  renal inflammation and fibrosis
