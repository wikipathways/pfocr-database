caption: In pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages, arginine is metabolized by iNOS into
  nitric oxide (NO) and citrulline, supporting antimicrobial activity, glycolysis,
  a disrupted TCA cycle, citrate/succinate accumulation, ROS production, fatty acid
  synthesis, and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression (i.e., IL-1β, TNFα). In anti-inflammatory
  M2 macrophages, arginine is metabolized by ARG1 into ornithine, fueling polyamine
  and proline synthesis for tissue repair and modulating local T cell responses. M2
  polarization depends on oxidative phosphorylation fueled by fatty acid oxidation,
  with additional regulation via epigenetic mechanisms (acetyl-CoA, lactate, SAM)
  and post-transcriptional pathways that balance inflammation and tissue homeostasis
