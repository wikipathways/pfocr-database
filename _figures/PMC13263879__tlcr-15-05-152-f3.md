caption: Pathways between macrophages and lung cancer cells. The interaction between
  macrophages and lung cancer depends on the signal transmission mediated by cytokines
  and exosomes. IL-6, miR-181b, IL-4/IL-13, and LINC00313 released by tumor cells
  activate JAK2-STAT3 pathway and STAT6 pathway in macrophages, respectively. Moreover,
  tumor-promoting cytokines including miR-3679-5p, IFN-γ, and LINC01001 are secreted
  from macrophages and bind to their corresponding receptors on the surface of lung
  cancer cells to induce PI3K, Akt, and NF-κB responses, promoting lung cancer. miR-501-3P
  promotes the proliferation of lung cancer cells by silencing WD repeat domain 82.
  Integrin αVβ produced by macrophages activates the adhesion enzyme pathway to promote
  tumor metastasis. In addition, JNK and KDM6B are also key links in the interaction
  between macrophages and lung cancer cells, affecting the polarization of macrophages.
  In vivo, IL-17 also promotes lung cancer by acting on the NF-KB pathway in lung
  cancer cells. IL, interleukin; IFN-γ, interferon-γ; JAK2, Janus kinase 2; STAT3,
  signal transducer and activator of transcription 3; STAT6, signal transducer and
  activator of transcription 6; KDM6B, lysine demethylase 6B; JNK, c-Jun N-terminal
  kinase; miR, microRNA; LINC, long intergenic non-protein coding RNA; PI3K, phosphoinositide
  3-kinase; Akt, protein kinase B; NF-κB, nuclear factor kappa-B; PP2AC, protein phosphatase
  2A catalytic subunit; CCL2, C-C motif chemokine ligand 2; CCL7, C-C motif chemokine
  ligand 7; WDR82, WD repeat domain 82; Nedd4, neural precursor cell expressed developmentally
