caption: Integration of C1 photosynthesis, photorespiration, and sulfur‐linked serine
  and methionine metabolism in plants. This diagram illustrates the proposed integration
  of the photosynthetic C1 pathway, photorespiration (C2 cycle), and sulfur and nitrogen
  assimilation in photosynthetic plant cells. It highlights how 5,10‐methylene‐THF
  (5,10‐CH2‐THF) produced in the chloroplast during C1 photosynthesis supports glycine
  recycling and serine regeneration, potentially bypassing mitochondrial glycine decarboxylation
  and thereby minimizing carbon and nitrogen loss. Glycine (C2), generated via both
  C1 photosynthesis and photorespiration, is converted to serine (C3) using 5,10‐CH2‐THF
  in the mitochondrion. In parallel, sulfate (SO4 2−) is assimilated through reduction
  to hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and incorporation into O‐acetylserine, yielding cysteine
  (C3), the first stable organic sulfur compound, which contributes to glutathione
  synthesis and transsulfuration pathways (not shown). Methionine biosynthesis occurs
  in both the chloroplast and cytosol via methionine synthase (MS), which catalyzes
  the condensation of 5‐methyl‐THF (from 5,10‐CH2‐THF) with homocysteine. Methionine
  enters the S‐adenosyl methionine (SAM) cycle, supporting widespread trans‐methylation
  reactions and regeneration of homocysteine. These sulfur‐ and C1‐derived metabolites
  underlie key physiological processes including redox regulation, antioxidant defense,
  phytohormone biosynthesis, and epigenetic control of gene expression. Together,
  this integrated metabolic network enables plants to recycle glycine, conserve nitrogen,
  assimilate sulfate, re‐assimilate photorespiratory CO2 and NH3, and maintain redox
  homeostasis. While C1 photosynthesis and photorespiration are shown here as sources
  of 5,10‐CH2‐THF, a third contributor–the oxidative C1 pathway–is not depicted in
  this figure but appears in Fig. 3. Green denotes C1 photosynthesis processes and
  metabolites in the chloroplast, including the synthesis of 5,10‐methylene‐THF (5,10‐CH2‐THF),
  methionine, and SAM. Magenta includes photorespiratory CO2 and NH3 production in
  the mitochondria via glycine decarboxylation, as well as NH3 re‐assimilation in
  the chloroplast via the glutamine synthetase/glutamate synthase (GS/GOGAT) cycle.
  Blue, marks photorespiratory C2 cycle reactions, including the RuBisCO oxygenation
  pathway and subsequent conversions of glycolate, glycine, and serine across the
  peroxisome and mitochondrion. Orange, indicates sulfur assimilation reactions, from
  sulfate uptake to cysteine and glutathione biosynthesis. Black shows cross‐compartment
  intermediates and SAM‐dependent methylation products exported to the cytosol
