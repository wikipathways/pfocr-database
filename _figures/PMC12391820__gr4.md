caption: Dietary fiber influences bone health by inhibiting osteoclast function and
  promoting bone formation. It acts through multiple signaling pathways (ERK, JNK,
  p38MAPK, PPAR, eNOS, mTOR, etc.), which collectively reduce osteoclast differentiation
  and activity. For example, ERK, and p38MAPK pathways suppress the maturation of
  osteoclast precursors, while PPAR signaling modulates lipid metabolism to inhibit
  osteoclast activation. By these mechanisms, dietary fiber maintains a balance favoring
  bone formation, thereby supporting bone mineral density (BMD) and overall skeletal
  health. ERK = extracellular signal-regulated kinase; JNK = Jun N-terminal kinase;
  PPAR = peroxisome proliferator-actiavted receptors; MAPK = mitogen activated protein
  kinase; eNOS = endothelial nitric oxide synthase; NIK = NF-κB inducing kinase; IDO
  = indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase; TCA = tricarboxylic acid; HSC = hepatic stellate
  cells; HIF = hypoxia inducible factor; mTOR = mammalian target of rapamycine; TRAF6
  = tumor necrosis factor receptor associated factor 6; NF-κB = nuclear factor-kappa
  B; NFATC1 = nuclear factor of activated T cells calcineurin dependent 1; OPG = osteoprotegerin;
  LC3 = microtubule associated protein1 light chain 3; OC = osteoclast; OB = osteoblast
