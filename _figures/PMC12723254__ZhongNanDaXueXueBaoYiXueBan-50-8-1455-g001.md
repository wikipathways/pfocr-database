caption: '脊髓神经胶质细胞参与慢性疼痛发生Figure 1 Spinal cord glial cells involved in the occurrence
  of chronic painAfter nerve injury, microglia and astrocytes are prompted to switch
  from their anti-inflammatory M2 and A2 states to the pro-inflammatory M1 and A1
  states, respectively. The overactivation of microglia and astrocytes leads to the
  release of pro-inflammatory factors through purinergic signaling, MAPK, JAK2-STAT3,
  and NF-κB pathways. Microglia produce and release mature IL-1β, IL-6, BDNF, and
  TNF-α, with TNF-α acting on nearby astrocytes to promote their activation. This
  activation of astrocytes results in the release of mature IL-1β and chemokines such
  as CXCL1 and CCL2. Together, these factors induce the sensitization of primary afferent
  neurons and excitatory nociceptors in the spinal dorsal horn. TLR4: Toll-like receptor
  4; MAPK: Mitogen-activated protein kinase; JAK2: Janus kinase 2; STAT3: Signal transducer
  and activator of transcription 3; TNF-α: Tumor necrosis factor-alpha; NF-κB: Nuclear
  factor-kappa B; IL: Interleukin; CXCL: C-X-C motif chemokine ligand; CCL2: C-C motif
  chemokine ligand 2; Cx43: Connexin 43; JNK: c-Jun N-terminal kinase; ERK: Extracellular
  signal-regulated kinase; CXCR: Chemokine (C-X-C motif) receptor; CCR: C-C motif
  chemokine receptor; BDNF: Brain-derived neurotrophic factor'
