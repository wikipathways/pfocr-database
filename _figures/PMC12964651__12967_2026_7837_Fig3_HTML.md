caption: Mitophagy-mediated PD-L1 driven immune evasion. A. overactivated mitochondria
  promote PD-L1 transcription and expression via the STAT3 and NF-κB pathways. Autophagy-driven
  metabolic reprogramming in these mitochondria suppresses oxidative phosphorylation
  and shifts cellular metabolism toward glycolysis, stabilizing HIF-1α and activating
  STAT3 and NF-κB, thereby directly enhancing PD-L1 transcription and increasing PD-L1
  expression; B. impaired mitophagy promotes PD-L1 transcription and expression via
  the cGAS-STING and interferon signaling pathways. Defective mitophagy leads to the
  cytoplasmic accumulation of mtROS and mtDNA, which activates the cGAS-STING pathway
  and enhances the TBK1–IRF3 phosphorylation cascade, inducing type I interferons
  and pro-inflammatory cytokines. These type I interferons act through autocrine or
  paracrine signaling on tumor cells, activating the JAK-STAT pathway to drive PD-L1
  transcription; C. activation of the PD-1/PD-L1 axis establishes a multi-level tumor
  immune evasion network. PD-L1 engages PD-1 on activated T cells, triggering SHP-2
  mediated inhibition of TCR/PI3K-AKT signaling, which diminishes T cell proliferation
  and effector cytokine secretion, induces co-expression of TIM-3 and LAG-3, and drives
  T cells toward exhaustion. This exhausted phenotype is further reinforced by aberrant
  activation of transcription factors such as TOX and NR4A. PD-1/PD-L1 signaling also
  suppresses antigen presentation and immune cell function by promoting iTreg generation,
  Breg differentiation, TAM M2 polarization, and by inhibiting DC maturation and NK
  cell degranulation, collectively establishing a systemic immunosuppressive network
