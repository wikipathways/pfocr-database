caption: Melatonin biosynthesis and metabolism pathways in plantsTryptophan serves
  as the precursor for multiple melatonin biosynthetic routes in plants. It is converted
  to tryptamine by tryptophan decarboxylase (TDC) or to 5-hydroxytryptophan by tryptophan
  hydroxylase (TPH). Tryptamine is hydroxylated to serotonin by tryptamine 5-hydroxylase
  (T5H), or it can be converted to 5-acetyltryptamine by serotonin N-acetyltransferase
  (SANT). 5-hydroxytryptophan is converted to serotonin by TDC. Serotonin is further
  acetylated by serotonin N-acetyltransferase or arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase
  (SNAT/AANAT) to N-acetylserotonin, which is methylated by acetylserotonin O-methyltransferase
  (ASMT) or caffeic acid O-methyltransferase (COMT) to form melatonin. Additionally,
  serotonin and its derivatives may be conjugated with sugars or amino acids. Stress-related
  transcription factors, i.e., HsfA1a, MeWARKY79, MeRAV1/2, MeHsf20, regulate the
  expression of melatonin biosynthetic genes under environmental stress. Melatonin
  can be further metabolized into cyclic melatonin, 5-methoxyindole-3-acetic acid
  (5-MIAA), and a variety of hydroxylated products such as 2-hydroxymelatoninmelatonin
  (2-OHM), 3-hydroxymelatonin (3-OHM), and 6-hydroxymelatoninmelatonin (6-OHM) through
  enzymatic and non-enzymatic oxidation.Created in BioRender. Kwon, E. (2025) https://BioRender.com/jfk1tqm
