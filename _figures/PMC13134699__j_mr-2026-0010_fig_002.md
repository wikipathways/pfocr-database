caption: 'Major amino acid metabolic pathways involved in aortic diseases. This diagram
  illustrates key interconnected amino acid metabolic networks implicated in aortic
  pathologies. (a) Sulfur-containing amino acids (SCAAs) metabolism: Methionine (Met)
  is metabolized into homocysteine (Hcy) and hydrogen sulfide (H2S) via transsulfuration
  pathways. Elevated Hcy levels are linked to pro-inflammatory effects and upregulated
  matrix metalloproteinase (MMP-2/9). H2S exerts dual roles, with antioxidant and
  anti-inflammatory benefits, but also the potential to exacerbate neutrophil infiltration,
  impair vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) proliferation, and induce senescence,
  blunting compensatory aortic remodeling. (b) Arginine metabolism: Arginine serves
  as the substrate for endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), generating nitric
  oxide (NO), which mediates vasodilation and inhibits VSMC proliferation. Dysregulation
  involves the accumulation of asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA) due to diminished
  dimethylarginine dimethylaminohydrolase (DDAH) activity, arginase upregulation,
  and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) uncoupling. De novo synthesis from
  citrulline via argininosuccinate synthase (ASS) and argininosuccinate lyase (ASL)
  links to glutamine (Gln), which offers protective effects against abdominal aortic
  aneurysm (AAA) by reducing VSMC apoptosis, M1 macrophage activation, oxidative stress,
  and extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation. (c) Gln and glutamate (Glu) metabolism:
  Gln, the most abundant amino acid in circulation, is synthesized mainly in skeletal
  muscle using circulating Glu as a substrate, while the liver produces Glu by deaminating
  Gln via glutaminase-2 (GLS2). A high plasma Gln/Glu ratio (GGR) was traditionally
  considered cardioprotective. However, GLS2 deficiency paradoxically elevates GGR
  yet accelerates atherosclerosis by promoting dysregulated ECM remodeling and arterial
  stiffness. (d) Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) metabolism: Aortic BCAAs catabolic
  defect results from the inhibitory phosphorylation of BCKD E1 subunit alpha (BCKDHA)
  by branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase kinase (BCKDK). This leads to oxidative
  stress damage and inflammatory cell infiltration via hyperactivated mTOR signaling,
  highlighting BCKDK and mTOR as potential therapeutic targets. This figure was created
  with Biorender.com'
