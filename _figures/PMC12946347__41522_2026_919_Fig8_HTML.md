caption: HEG exerted its anti-HCC effects by modulating the composition of the gut
  microbiota, which subsequently repaired the intestinal barrier, enhanced colonic
  immunity, and improved the immune microenvironment of HCC by inhibiting the MAPK
  signaling pathway. By integrating 16S rRNA sequencing with metabolomics data, alongside
  literature mining and in vitro validation, Equol was identified as a key differential
  metabolite through which HEG suppressed HCC via gut microbiota regulation. Equol
  was essential for the anti-HCC effects of HEG. Equol ameliorated the immune microenvironment
  by inhibiting the MAPK signaling pathway, while concurrently inhibited the growth
  of HCC cells by inducing the G0/G1 phase blockade through suppression of Cyclin
  E1-CDK2/Rb signaling pathway
