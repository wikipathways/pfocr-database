caption: Controversial effects of the STING pathway in colitis development. The activation
  of the STING pathway in CD11b+ myeloid cells and macrophages plays a pathogenic
  role in the development of colitis by up-regulating specific immune cells and pro-inflammatory
  factors. The knockout of the STING pathway in Lysm+ cells ameliorates colonic inflammation
  through obstructing the differentiation of Th1 and Th17 cells via suppressing IL-12
  family cytokines. However, STING deficiency in epithelial cells and CD4+ T cells
  aggravates intestinal inflammation, while STING activation in CD4+ T cells exerts
  a remission role in colitis pathogenesis. The absence of nuclear STING inhibits
  AHR activity to exacerbate colitis due to intestinal microbiota dysbiosis. Furthermore,
  congenital absence of STING contributes to the compromised colonic barrier and imbalance
  of immune responses
