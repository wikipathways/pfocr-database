caption: Proposed mechanism of action of NO, CO, H2S. NO exerts its biological effects
  through both direct and indirect mechanisms. Direct effects involve the reaction
  of NO with molecular targets such as metal centers, DNA, and lipid radicals. Indirect
  effects occur when NO first reacts with reactive oxygen species (e.g., O2 or superoxide,
  O2−) to form reactive nitrogen oxide species. A key product, peroxynitrite (ONOO−),
  can modify proteins by nitrating tyrosine residues to form 3-nitrotyrosine (3-NT)
  or by nitrosylating cysteine residues to generate S-nitrosothiols (RSNO). Additional
  pathways for RSNO formation include reactions involving dinitrogen trioxide (N2O3)
  or dinitrosyliron complexes (DNICs), which require chelatable iron. NO also activates
  soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) by binding to its heme moiety, leading to cyclic
  GMP generation and subsequent physiological responses such as vasorelaxation. ONOO−-mediated
  nitration is elevated in the SNc of PD patients, where increased 3-NT levels correlate
  with loss of dopaminergic neurons. In both 6-OHDA-lesioned rats and MPTP-treated
  non-human primates, iNOS inhibition or ONOO− scavenging reduces motor deficits and
  neuronal death [320]. iNOS expression in activated microglia is increased in the
  SNc of PD patients, sustaining a cycle of nitrosative stress and inflammation that
  contributes to neuronal damage [321]. CO shares mechanistic overlap with NO, particularly
  through its ability to bind the heme group of sGC, resulting in modest increases
  in cyclic GMP. Additionally, CO activates antioxidant and cytoprotective pathways
  via upregulation of heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and engagement of transcription factors
  such as Nrf2. CO also modulates ion channels, including KATP and KCa channels, leading
  to membrane hyperpolarization. HO-1 increases in PD brains and in MPTP and A53T
  α-syn transgenic models. Low-dose CO decreases α-syn aggregation, preserves nigral
  dopaminergic neurons, and improves motor behavior [322]. CO-induced activation of
  Nrf2 limits microglial cytokine release, reducing chronic inflammation that worsens
  dopaminergic neuron vulnerability [322]. H2S acts primarily through post-translational
  modification of proteins via persulfidation (also known as S-sulfhydration), where
  a thiol group (-SH) on cysteine residues is converted to a persulfide (-SSH). This
  modification affects protein function and signaling cascades related to antioxidant
  defense, inflammation, and apoptosis. H2S also contributes to cellular redox balance
  by modulating glutathione levels and enhancing mitochondrial bioenergetics at low
  concentrations. Like NO and CO, H2S activates ion channels (e.g., KATP), exerts
  vasodilatory effects. H2S raises cGMP levels through inhibition of phosphodiesterase
  5A (PDE5A) an enzyme that catabolizes it. Persulfidation of the E3 ubiquitin-ligase
  parkin is reduced in sporadic PD, impairing its protective activity. In MPTP-treated
  mice and 6-OHDA-lesioned rats, H2S donors restore parkin persulfidation, improve
  mitochondrial function, and rescue motor deficits [251]. Levels of the H2S-producing
  enzyme CSE are lower in PD tissues, which may weaken persulfidation-mediated protection
  and suggest a role for H2S supplementation [251]
