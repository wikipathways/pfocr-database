caption: Summary of vascular influences in AD. This figure summarizes findings from
  several studies. Vascular risk factors (obesity, hypertension, insulin resistance,
  dyslipidemia, and type II diabetes mellitus) are associated with cerebral small
  vessel disease (CSVD) and the risk of cognitive impairment.50, 51, 52, 70 This may
  occur either directly53, 54 via predominantly frontal WMHs 55 that may lead to MTL
  atrophy and memory impairment55 or other cognitive deficits, or indirectly53, 55,
  58 via predominantly posterior WMHs55, 56, 111, 112 or CAA,58 which may perturb
  the glymphatic system, resulting in Aβ accumulation.67, 70 Neuroinflammation67 may
  mediate the effect of Aβ accumulation on increases in WMH burden. Wider WMH burden
  can lead to reduced regional cerebral blood flow55, 113 and axonal damage, which
  results in further Aβ accumulation. This synergistic interaction between CSVD and
  Aβ creates a feedback loop that exacerbates Aβ accumulation even before the onset
  of clinical symptoms (CU)56 and is worse in APOE ε4 carriers.59 This may lead to
  a unidirectional pathway55 of tau deposition in the MTL, subsequent MTL atrophy,
  and AD‐typical memory deficits.55 Widespread CSVD develops in MCI and AD, including
  frontal WMH.56 The synergistic interaction between CSVD and Aβ appears to be the
  predominant pathway in AD, with the direct effect of CSVD, with frontal WMH playing
  a lesser role
