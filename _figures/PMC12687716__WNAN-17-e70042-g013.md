caption: Depicts various pathways involved in cell death, including apoptosis, necroptosis,
  and pyroptosis, which are regulated forms of cell death, while necrosis occurs without
  regulation. In apoptosis (a), TNF‐α binds to TNFR1, leading to the recruitment of
  TRAF2/5, TRADD, cIAP1/2, and RIP1, which activate caspase 8. This results in RIP1
  cleavage and the formation of Complex IIa, which includes RIP3, FADD, and TRADD,
  ultimately activating caspase 3 and inducing programmed cell death. In necroptosis
  (b), when caspase 8 is inhibited (e.g., by z‐VAD), RIP complex activation leads
  to the formation of Complex IIb, consisting of RIP1, RIP3, TRADD, and FADD. This
  complex phosphorylates MLKL, which disrupts membrane integrity, leading to necrotic
  cell death. In pyroptosis (c), DAMPs/PAMPs activate Toll‐Like Receptors (TLRs),
  which induce NF‐κB activation in the nucleus, upregulating inflammasome components
  such as Nlrp3, Nlrc4, Pycard, and Il1b. This activates caspase 1, which cleaves
  Gasdermin D (GSDMD) into N‐GSDMD, forming membrane pores that release inflammatory
  molecules such as HMGB1 and IL‐1β. This results in pyroptotic cell lysis and the
  release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) as a marker of pyroptosis. These pathways
  regulate different forms of programmed cell death, playing crucial roles in inflammation,
  immune responses, and disease progression
