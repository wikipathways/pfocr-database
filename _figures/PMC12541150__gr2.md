caption: 'Role of tumor and tumor microenvironment (TME) in ENKTL pathogenesis. The
  tumor microenvironment (TME) provides an immunosuppressive environment allowing
  malignant NK cells to proliferate. The malignant cells themselves express LMP1,
  which upregulate the JAK/STAT and NF-kB oncogenic signaling pathways. They also
  exert immunosuppressive interactions with exhausted and regulatory T cells by: a)
  Overexpressing immune checkpoint molecules PD-L1 and CD86, which interact with PD-1
  and CTLA4 respectively found on surrounding T cells, b) Coexpressing IDO1, which
  leads to high expression of immunosuppressive molecules, c) Regulating recruitment
  of immune cells to the TME by (i) upregulation of chemokines e.g. CCL-3, -4 and
  -5, and (ii) secretion of dipeptidylpeptidase IV (DPP4), which degrades other chemokines
  such as CXCLs and inhibits recruitment of normal NK cells. Tumor-associated macrophages
  (TAMs), via various interactions including PD-L1/PD1 and CD86/CTLA4, potentiate
  this effect. Some of the key pathways involved in ENKTL pathogenesis highlighted
  in this figure include: 1) NF-κB, a transcription factor involved in pro-proliferative
  signaling, is upregulated via both canonical,20 and non-canonical pathways 101 2)
  Upregulation of JAK3 and STAT3 lead to the constitutive activation of the Janus
  kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathway which
  promotes proliferation in ENKTL.18 3) Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1) is an
  enzyme that converts tryptophan to kynurenine in the TME. Kynurenine is an immunosuppressive
  metabolite reported to cause T-cell anergy, apoptosis and suppression of T effector
  cell function. High coexpression of PD-L1 and IDO1 in tumor cells to be associated
  with poor overall survival.93 The main signaling pathways involved in tumor pathogenesis
  are also illustrated and their corresponding therapeutic agents under evaluation
  are highlighted'
