caption: The molecular mechanisms of Notch signaling pathway-related PTM processes
  in angiogenesis. (A) Notch receptors undergo O-glycosylation in the Golgi apparatus,
  which modulates tip–stalk cell interactions and enhances tip cell-mediated inhibition
  of stalk cells. As Notch signaling activity increases, EC migration capacity gradually
  decreases. (B) The Notch ligand–receptor complex is internalized into tip cells
  via endocytosis and degraded by E3 ubiquitin ligases (e.g. MIB1), a necessary step
  for Notch signaling pathway activation. In stalk cells, the deubiquitinase USP10
  stabilizes NICD, activating downstream target genes to maintain vascular branch
  stability. (C) In vascular ECs, Cyclin C/CDK8-mediated phosphorylation of NICD promotes
  FBXW7-dependent ubiquitination and degradation, suppressing Notch signaling activation
  and ultimately inhibiting angiogenesis. (D) In bone ECs, ZEB1 binds to the histone
  acetyltransferase CBP/p300, occupying the Dll4 and Notch1 promoter regions to enhance
  their transcription via increased histone acetylation, thereby accelerating angiogenesis.
  Conversely, in lung stalk cells, HDAC6 inhibition induces hyperacetylation of NICD/SNW1,
  blocking their binding and inhibiting VEGF-induced angiogenesis
