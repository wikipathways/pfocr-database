caption: 'Mechanisms of phytochemical intervention in oxidative stress-induced apoptosis.
  Figure illustrates the major molecular pathways modulated by phytochemicals to counteract
  oxidative stress and inhibit programmed cell death (apoptosis) in neurodegenerative
  conditions. Under oxidative stress, excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) damage
  the mitochondria, leading to activation of the pro-apoptotic protein B-cell lymphoma
  2–associated X protein (Bax). Activated Bax permeabilizes the mitochondrial membrane,
  resulting in the release of cytochrome-c (Cyt-c) into the cytosol and the assembly
  of the apoptosome complex. This apoptosome subsequently activates caspase-9 followed
  by caspase-3, culminating in the execution of apoptosis. Phytochemicals exert protective
  effects by upregulating anti-apoptotic proteins such as B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2),
  which antagonize Bax activity and help stabilize mitochondrial membrane integrity.
  They also inhibit pro-apoptotic signaling pathways mediated by the c-Jun N-terminal
  kinase (JNK) and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) pathways, thereby
  blocking downstream signals that promote apoptotic cell death. Additionally, phytochemicals
  enhance neuronal survival mechanisms by upregulating brain-derived neurotrophic
  factor (BDNF), which activates tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) receptors. This
  activation triggers downstream signaling through the phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase/protein
  kinase B (PI3K/Akt) pathway and the MAPK/extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)–ERK
  cascade, hereafter referred to as the MEK–ERK cascade. In this context, MEK denotes
  MAPK/ERK kinase. These pathways converge on transcription factors such as the cyclic
  adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) response element-binding protein (CREB), which promotes
  antioxidant gene expression and supports cell survival. Furthermore, the PI3K/Akt
  signaling axis interacts with the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) and the
  nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) pathways, collectively enhancing
  cellular defenses against oxidative damage and preserving neuronal integrity. PTEN:
  phosphatase and tensin homolog. Reprinted from Ref. [44] with permission'
