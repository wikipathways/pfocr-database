caption: 'Schematic representation of immune mechanisms of HBsAg clearance in HIV/HBV
  coinfection. The immune mechanisms involved in HBsAg clearance in HIV/HBV coinfection
  include multiple key processes. 1) PD-1/PD-L1 pathway inhibition occurs when sPD-1
  blocks the interaction between PD-1 and PD-L1, which reduces immune exhaustion and
  restores T cell function. 2) NK cell function remodeling enhances antibody-dependent
  cellular cytotoxicity through CD3ζ phosphorylation and ZAP-70 translocation, leading
  to the destruction of HBV-infected hepatocytes. 3) Immune cell recruitment is driven
  by upregulated cytokines such as GM-CSF and CXCL10, which attract neutrophils, T
  cells, and macrophages, further enhancing the immune response. 4) The inflammatory
  response, marked by elevated levels of sCD163 and IL-18, promotes inflammation and
  may trigger IRIS, ultimately aiding in HBsAg clearance. Created with BioRender (https://biorender.com/)
  and permission has been obtained. HBsAg: Hepatitis B surface antigen; HIV: Human
  immunodeficiency virus; HBV: Hepatitis B virus; PD-1: Programmed cell death protein
  1; PD-L1: Programmed death-ligand 1; sPD-1: Soluble programmed cell death protein
  1; NK cell: Natural killer cell; CD3ζ: Cluster of differentiation 3 zeta chain;
  ZAP-70: Zeta-chain-associated protein kinase 70; CD16: Cluster of differentiation
  16; Nab: Neutralizing antibody; GM-CSF: Granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating
  factor; CXCL10: C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 10; sCD163: Soluble CD163; IL-18: Interleukin-18;
  IRIS: Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome; IFN-γ: Interferon-gamma'
