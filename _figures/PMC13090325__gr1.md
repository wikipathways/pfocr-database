caption: Innate immune recognition of viral vectorsSchematic illustration of major
  humoral and cellular innate immune pathways engaged by viral vector-based gene delivery.
  Common viral vectors, including adenovirus (AdV), adeno-associated virus (AAV),
  lentivirus (LV), and herpes simplex virus (HSV), and retrovirus (RV), are shown
  interacting with innate immune components at different stages of administration
  and intracellular trafficking. In the extracellular compartment, viral particles
  may be bound by pre-existing antibodies and activate complement pathways, leading
  to opsonization and generation of inflammatory mediators. Following cellular entry,
  viral capsid proteins and nucleic acids are detected by pattern recognition receptors
  (PRRs), including surface and endosomal Toll-like receptors (TLR2, TLR3, TLR7/8,
  and TLR9), cytosolic RNA sensors RIG-I and MDA5, and the DNA sensor cGAS signaling
  through STING. These pathways signal through adaptor proteins and downstream kinases
  to activate NF-κB and interferon regulatory factors (IRFs). In parallel, viral nucleic
  acids and transgene expression can engage the integrated stress response (ISR),
  resulting in phosphorylation of eIF2α and regulation of protein translation
