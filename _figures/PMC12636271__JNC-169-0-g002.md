caption: Shared pathways regulating blood–brain and intestinal barriers in psychiatric
  disorders. In the blood–brain barrier (BBB, top panel), Wnt/β‐catenin and growth
  factors (FGF2, VEGF‐A) regulate angiogenesis, barrier integrity, and inflammation.
  Disruption via GSK3β reduces endothelial stability in major depressive disorder
  (MDD) and bipolar disorder (BD), while selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs),
  serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs), and lithium modulate this
  pathway. Additional cascades, including VEGFR2/PI3K/AKT and FGFR1/MEK/ERK, further
  regulate angiogenesis, proliferation, and neuroinflammation. In the intestinal barrier
  (bottom panel), Wnt/β‐catenin supports epithelial renewal, mucosa healing, and tight
  junction expression (claudins, ZO‐1), whereas dysregulation promotes permeability
  and inflammation in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and MDD. VEGF‐A further drives
  vascular leakage, endothelial fenestration, and leukocyte infiltration. These overlapping
  mechanisms contribute to systemic inflammation and barrier dysfunction, supporting
  a gut–brain axis model where intestinal dysfunction exacerbates BBB breakdown, neuroinflammation,
  and vulnerability to mood and anxiety disorders
