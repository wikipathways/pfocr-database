caption: 'Role and molecular mechanisms of mtDNA methylation in metabolic dysfunction-associated
  steatotic liver disease (MASLD). In the HepG2 liver cell line, overexpression of
  a mitochondrial-targeted GpC-specific methyltransferase (MCviPI) and a CpG methyltransferase
  (M.SssI) induces aberrant 5mC methylation of the mitochondrial ND6 region, a key
  component of respiratory complex I, thereby impairing oxidative phosphorylation.
  This disruption leads to mitochondrial swelling, cristae damage, overactivation
  of the respiratory chain, and concomitant ROS accumulation. Such mitochondrial dysfunction
  activates nuclear receptor signaling pathways (e.g., NR5A2, FXR) and promotes the
  expression of PPARα target genes (e.g., CYP8B1, UGT2B4) via metabolic reprogramming,
  such as an imbalance in the SAM/SAH ratio, thereby triggering bile acid overload
  and cholestatic injury, ultimately promoting the progression of MASLD. Moreover,
  in HepG2 cells, ​​5mC hypermethylation at the HSP1 and LSP promoters of mtDNA​​
  impairs mitochondrial gene transcription and disrupts lipid metabolism homeostasis,
  thereby inducing mitochondrial dysfunction and accelerating the progression of ​​MASLD​​.
