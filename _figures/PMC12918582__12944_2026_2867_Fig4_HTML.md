caption: 'Lipotoxic substances generated by dysregulation of the ASF axis drive MASLD
  through the dysregulation of hepatocytes and immune cells. The accumulation of lipotoxic
  metabolic products in hepatocytes involves a bidirectional AMPK-CD36 regulation.
  AMPK activation upregulates CD36 expression and promotes its membrane translocation,
  thereby enhancing fatty acid uptake. CD36 forms a complex with LKB1 and Fyn, inhibiting
  AMPK through Fyn-mediated LKB1 phosphorylation and nuclear sequestration. Additionally,
  CD36-mediated SREBP1 activation further promotes lipotoxicity. This disrupts mitochondrial
  homeostasis, inhibits β-oxidation, increases ROS, and reduces ATP, ultimately triggering
  inflammation and cell death. Excessive metabolic products also induce endoplasmic
  reticulum stress and activate the UPR (FOXA3/IRE1/PERK pathway), disrupting lipid
  metabolism and inflammatory regulation. Lipotoxicity activates immune cells through
  multi-interrelated mechanisms: (1) Structural damage caused by solid-phase membrane
  domain formation, damaging lipid rafts and creating bilayer microcracks, exposing
  phospholipids; (2) Release of DAMPs due to endoplasmic reticulum protein misfolding,
  activating hepatocyte TLR4/CD36 receptors and downstream NF-κB/NLRP3 pathways; (3)
  Generation of lipotoxic exosomes enhances TLR4 activation and inflammatory phenotypes
  in immune cells. These processes collectively form a vicious cycle, linking hepatocyte
  lipotoxicity with immune activation in the progression of MASLD. AMPK: AMP-activated
  protein kinase; ASC: Apoptosis-Associated Speck-like Protein Containing a CARD;
