caption: The expression levels of co-inhibitory receptors on CD8 + tumor-infiltrating
  T cells are increasing, including PD-L1/PD-L2, TIM-3, TIGIT, LAG3, and CTLA4. The
  production of interferon-γ (IFN-γ) is decreasing, while Tregs are expanding. The
  infiltration of NK cells in EBV-positive NPC decreases, but inhibitory receptors
  such as TIGIT and NKG2A are highly expressed, and the expression of cytotoxic molecules
  such as GZMB and PRF1 is downregulated. Previous studies have identified the ZNF683 + resident
  NK cell exhaustion subpopulation in EBV-infected NPC. The level of PD-L1 in EBV-infected
  NPC cells is significantly increased, which further downregulates the function of
  CD8 + T cells and induces them to enter a lysosomal state. LMP1, through its intracellular
  special domain, further recruits ALIX protein, using the ALIX-dependent pathway,
  enhances the ability of PD-L1 to load into sEV, and the combination of these three
  forms a complex, promoting the transmembrane release of sEV to distant sites, long-distance
  regulation and multi-site inhibition of CD8 + T cell activity and IFN-γ expression.
  Additionally, LMP1 induces the expression of immune checkpoint molecule B7-H3 (CD276)
  through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, promoting the reduction of NK cell numbers. EBV-encoded
  miRNA-BART11 and miRNA-BART17-3p inhibit transcriptional repressor FOXP1 and PBRM1
  expression by suppressing transcription, relieving the transcriptional inhibition
  of PD-L1. EBV-encoded circular RNA circRNA-BART2.2 further enhances the transcriptional
  activity of PD-L1 by promoting the expression of TFs such as NF-κB and IRF3. The
  upregulation of miR-155 is closely related to the loss of these two key immune molecules,
  ICOSL and MHC-I. EBV promotes the release of immunosuppressive factors and the upregulation
  of immune checkpoint B7-H3 (CD276) and induces the upregulation of tissue factor
  F3 through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, thereby promoting platelet aggregation and
  the release of TGF-β, and inhibiting the anti-tumor function of NK cells
