caption: Displayed is a synthetic myocyte with a resting membrane potential between
  −60 to −75 mV. In synthetic myocytes, ClC-2, 3, and 5 are predominantly expressed.
  TMEM16A channels, which are expected to support contractility of quiescent myocytes
  is downregulated in synthetic myocytes. Conflicting data exists regarding TMEM16A,
  and as such its role in synthetic myocytes remains unclear. ClC-3 and ClC-5 were
  proposed to localize to the plasma membrane and endolysosomes, which makes understanding
  their mechanisms of action challenging. ClC channels and transporters were associated
  with fibroproliferative signaling through various signaling pathways. It was proposed
  that ClC-5 directly regulates WNK1. The hyperpolarization-activated ClC-2 channels
  seem to regulate WNT/β-catenin, and ClC-3 regulates AKT, GSK3β, Cyclin D1 and E.
  Whether these functions are mediated by changes in cell volume (e.g. during myocyte
  proliferation) or changes in membrane potential that controls Ca2+ signaling through
  receptor-activated Ca2+ (ROC) channels are not clear. The function of the chloride
  channel CFTR was mainly reported in quiescent myocytes where it contributes to myocyte
  relaxation through hyperpolarization (influx of Cl− ions) and cellular uptake of
  sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), which curtails S1P pro-contractile signaling. Created
