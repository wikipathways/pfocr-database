caption: 'Transcriptional and metabolic regulation of GSLs during vernalization in
  cabbage. A Schematic pathway showing the major metabolic routes leading to GSL biosynthesis.
  The left panel represents the methionine-derived aliphatic GSL pathway, including
  chain elongation, core structure formation, and side chain modification steps. The
  right panel shows the tryptophan-derived indolic GSL pathway. The expression patterns
  of in individual cabbage GSL homologous genes are shown at each biosynthetic step
  in the order NV, V-40D, and AV. The color of cabbage genes indicates their relationship
  with HPLC data as follows: black, no correlation with HPLC data; red, VIG genes
  showing a positive correlation with HPLC data; green, VRG genes showing a positive
  correlation with HPLC data. The color of GSL compound names displayed at the ‘side-chain
  elongation’ step represents their detection status in the HPLC analysis: black indicates
  compounds detected by HPLC system, whereas gray indicates compounds not detected.
  B A schematic model illustrating changes in GSL profiles in cabbage with and without
  vernalization. Young cabbage seedlings (2 weeks old) contained high levels of aliphatic
  (2,384.2 nmol·g⁻1) and indolic GSLs (474.1 nmol·g⁻1). As plants aged, in the absence
  of vernalization, both aliphatic (797.0 nmol·g⁻1) and indolic GSLs (318.4 nmol·g⁻1)
  showed a substantial decline. In contrast, vernalization markedly attenuated the
  reduction of aliphatic GSLs (1,906.6 nmol·g⁻1), and the total amount of indolic
  GSLs even increased following vernalization (696.5 nmol·g⁻.1). The size of each
  pie chart is proportional to the total amount of aliphatic or indolic GSLs. The
  total contents of aliphatic or indolic GSLs are indicated below each pie chart in
  parentheses. Progoitrin (PGT); Glucoraphanin (GRA); Sinigrin (SIN); Gluconapin (GNP);
  4-Hydroxyglucobrassicin (4-HGB); Glucoerucin (GER); Glucobrassicin (GBS); 4-Methoxyglucobrassicin
  (4-MTGB); Neoglucobrassicin (NGB)'
