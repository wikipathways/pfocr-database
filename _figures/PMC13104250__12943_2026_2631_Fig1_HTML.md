caption: 'Cholesterol Metabolism Pathways: Synthesis, Uptake, Esterification, and
  Efflux in Cellular Homeostasis. Cellular cholesterol homeostasis is maintained through
  coordinated de novo synthesis, lipoprotein uptake, intracellular trafficking, esterification,
  and efflux. De novo synthesis proceeds from acetyl-CoA through the mevalonate pathway
  (including HMG-CoA reductase as a key rate-limiting enzyme) to generate sterol intermediates
  and cholesterol, while also producing non-sterol isoprenoids (for example, FPP/GGPP),
  dolichol, and coenzyme Q. Extracellular cholesterol is acquired mainly through LDL–LDLR-mediated
  endocytosis. In endosomes and lysosomes, cholesteryl esters are hydrolyzed to free
  cholesterol, which is exported by the NPC2/NPC1 system and redistributed to cellular
  membranes and organelles; PCSK9 promotes LDLR lysosomal degradation and thereby
  limits LDL uptake. Excess free cholesterol can be esterified by SOAT1/2 (ACAT1/2)
  and stored in lipid droplets. Cholesterol efflux is mediated by ATP-binding cassette
  transporters, including ABCA1/ABCG1, whereas ABCG5/ABCG8 support intestinal and
  hepatobiliary sterol secretion. Together, these pathways preserve membrane integrity,
  signaling competence, and metabolic homeostasis'
