caption: In the tumor microenvironment (TME), Toll‐like receptors (TLRs) play a dual
  role in cancer pathogenesis. (1) DAMPs released from necrotic tumor cells and PAMPs
  from the tumor‐associated microbiota. (2) Activate immune cells. These trigger signaling
  cascades, such as the MyD88‐dependent pathway, leading to the activation of transcription
  factors like NF‐κB. (3) In neoplastic conditions, the sustained activation of NF‐κB
  is a key mechanistic driver of tumorigenesis, as it promotes the transcription of
  genes involved in cell proliferation, survival (anti‐apoptosis), angiogenesis, and
  metastasis. (4) This highlights the therapeutic potential of modulating TLR activity.
  (5) TLR antagonists can block this chronic pro‐tumorigenic inflammation, while TLR
  agonists are used in immunomodulatory therapy to activate dendritic cells (DCs)
  and natural killer (NK) cells, promoting a robust antitumor cytotoxic T‐cell response
