caption: Schematic representation of complement pathway activation in Alzheimer’s
  disease (AD). The three complement activation routes, classical (C1q binding to
  Aβ, tau, or antibody complexes), lectin (Mannose-Binding Lectin [MBL] and its associated
  MBL-associated serine proteases [MASPs]), and alternative (amplification loop),
  converge at C3 activation, generating C3a/C5a inflammatory mediators and forming
  the membrane attack complex (MAC). Excessive activation induces aberrant C1q/C3b-mediated
  synaptic pruning, microglial and astrocytic activation, and neuronal lysis. Age-related
  BBB breakdown permits peripheral complement infiltration, while failure of regulatory
  checkpoints leads to persistent complement-driven neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration
  in AD
