caption: 'Cholesterol-mediated regulation of innate and adaptive immune-cell subsets.
  Cholesterol and its oxysterol derivatives shape lineage-specific phenotypes across
  the tumor immune landscape. T cell. Membrane cholesterol supports TCR nanoclustering
  and immunological synapse organization, thereby strengthening T-cell activation
  and cytotoxic effector function [20, 108]. Macrophage. Intracellular sterol balance
  regulates macrophage inflammatory states: excess free cholesterol can reinforce
  pro-inflammatory programs, whereas tumor-driven cholesterol efflux (including ABCA1/ABCG1-dependent
  pathways) promotes tumor-associated macrophage reprogramming toward immunosuppressive
  phenotypes [81, 109]. Dendritic cell(DC). Oxysterol–LXR signaling modulates dendritic-cell
  maturation, antigen uptake, and inflammatory responsiveness, thereby influencing
  antigen presentation and T-cell priming [110]. NK cell. In hepatocellular carcinoma
  models, cholesterol accumulation in NK cells enhances lipid-raft assembly, activating-receptor
  signaling, and cytotoxic effector programs [83]. B cell. Membrane cholesterol influences
  B-cell receptor compartmentalization and signaling, and cholesterol-enriched lipid
  rafts help organize B–T cell interactions and CD40 signaling platforms, with downstream
  effects on humoral immune responses [111–113]. Treg cell. Cholesterol metabolic
  remodeling (including cholesterol esterification pathways) influences Treg abundance
  and suppressive function in a context-dependent manner, with implications for tumor
  immune suppression [114–116]. Neutrophil. Oxysterols regulate neutrophil recruitment
  and inflammatory programs (for example, through CXCR2-linked chemotactic signaling),
  thereby shaping tumor-associated neutrophil functions within the TME [77, 117].
  Collectively, these mechanisms highlight cholesterol metabolism as a context-dependent,
  cell-type-specific regulator of both innate and adaptive immunity within the tumor
  microenvironment'
