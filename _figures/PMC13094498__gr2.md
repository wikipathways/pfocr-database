caption: Emerging axon navigating roles of 4 synaptic proteins in early development.
  (A) Latrophilin signaling in axon guidance. During early development, latrophilins
  guide navigating axons through context-dependent signaling. Latrophilin1, expressed
  in neuronal axons, interacts with soluble teneurin2 splice variants and induces
  an IP3-mediated Ca²⁺ signaling through Gq protein that enhances vesicle exocytosis
  at growth cones, resulting in directional attraction. Soluble TCAP1, cleaved from
  teneurin, binds to the latrophilin1 to modulate actin and microtubule dynamics via
  the Gs- or Gq-mediated MEK-ERK pathway, controlling growth cone motility. Conversely,
  upon binding to teneurin3, latrophilin2 acts as a repulsive receptor by coupling
  to the G12/13 pathway—a process requiring its tethered agonist—to direct precise
  target selection in hippocampal networks. (B) Non-ionotropic NMDAR signaling in
  axon navigation. In developing somatosensory neurons, NMDARs regulate callosal somatosensory
  innervation independently of ion flux. NMDAR modulates repulsive guidance mediated
  by ephrinB1–EphB2 complex, functioning as a scaffold for EphB2 signaling. This non-ionotropic
  mechanism is crucial for establishing interhemispheric connectivity. (C) Cerebellins
  in long-range axon targeting. In the developing spinal cord, floor plate-derived
  cerebellin1 acts as an attractant for commissural axons, functioning in parallel
  with canonical guidance cues. Similarly, cerebellin1 secretion from medial corticospinal
  neurons (CSNs) actively guides axonal projections toward thoracic targets. (D) Neurexin-mediated
  axon segregation in olfactory system. Combinatorial expression of neurexins in olfactory
  sensory neurons (OSNs) governs precise targeting of aons to specific glomeruli.
  This process relies on cooperative interactions with postsynaptic partners, such
  as neuroligin1 on mitral and tufted (M/T) cells, to mediate attractive guidance
  cues
