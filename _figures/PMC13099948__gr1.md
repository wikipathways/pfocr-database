caption: 'Roles of apoptosis in atherosclerosis (AS). In vascular endothelial cell
  (VEC), high glucose (HG) and oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) activate
  Caspase-3 through nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) pathway, leading to apoptosis of VEC.
  Concurrently, exposure to radiation results in DNA fragmentation, which halts the
  cell cycle. Furthermore, radiation can stimulate the production of ceramide, thereby
  activating the stress-activated protein kinase 1 (SEK1)/c-Jun N-terminal kinase
  (JNK)/c-Jun signaling pathway. In vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC), nitric oxide
  (NO) produced by VEC interacts with reactive oxygen species (ROS) to form ONOO−,
  which subsequently activates Caspase-3 and promotes apoptosis in VSMC. Additionally,
  NO derived from macrophages enhances the interaction between Fas/FasL on VSMC and
  macrophages, facilitating VSMC apoptosis. Moreover, ox-LDL promotes the translocation
  of Fas to the cell membrane, further inducing apoptosis in VSMC. In macrophages,
  both ox-LDL and ferrylhemoglobin induce endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and activate
  caspases, leading to apoptosis. ATM: ataxia telangiectasia-mutated; DNA-PK: DNA-dependent
  protein kinase; PI3K: phosphoinositide 3-kinase; COX2: cyclooxygenase 2; LOX-1 lectin-type
  oxidized LDL receptor 1; VCAM-1: vascular cell adhesion molecule-1; ICAM-1: intercellular
  cell adhesion molecule-1; PRR: pattern recognition receptor; NLRP3: NLR family pyrin
  domain-containing protein 3; CD163: cluster of differentiation 163'
