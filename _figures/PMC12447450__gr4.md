caption: Crosstalk regulation involving tryptophan metabolites under biotic stress.(A)
  The crosstalk between tryptophan metabolites regulates resistance to biotic stress.
  Under biotic stress, the crosstalk among auxin, melatonin, and IGs enhance plant
  defenses against pathogens, repel insects, and reduce susceptibility by regulating
  proteins such as TIR1 and PIN1/2, which are involved in auxin homeostasis and transport.
  Transcription factors MYB34, MYB51, MYB122, and WRKY33 modulate the metabolic flux
  of the IAOx pathway, facilitating the production of defense compounds including
  IGs, camalexin, and 4-OH-ICN. WRKY24 regulates the balance between melatonin and
  auxin during biotic stress. Additionally, melatonin directs sulfur and nitrogen
  metabolism into IG biosynthetic pathways, promoting IG production.(B) The crosstalk
  between tryptophan metabolites and other phytohormones regulates resistance to biotic
  stress. Auxin, regulated by phytohormones such as SA and JA, balances growth and
  defense through mediation of auxin homeostasis and signaling components, including
  GH3.5. Melatonin, induced by NO and ROS, activates plant immunity via the OXI1/MAPKKK3-MKK4/5/7/9-MPK3/6
  cascade, stimulating SA to enhance resistance to biotic stress. Melatonin also induces
  JA and ET pathways to activate pathogen-resistance proteins. Furthermore, IG and
  camalexin production, stimulated by SA, JA, and ET, reinforces plant defenses. Melatonin
  increases MeJA levels, which elevate the signaling molecule H2S and induce defense-related
  enzymes such as TI, CI, PPO, and PAL. Additionally, S-nitrosylation of the melatonin
  synthase COMT2 and sulfenylation of the auxin synthase TSB1 contribute to balancing
  plant growth and defense responses. All figures were created with BioRender (BioRender.com)
