caption: L-lactate-mediated lactylation. KL−la typically initiates when lactate is
  converted into Lactyl-CoA, which subsequently transfers lactoyl groups to target
  proteins via “writer” proteins (AARS1/2, P300/CBP, GCN5, TIP60, KAT5/8, HBO1, P300/GTPSCS
  and YiaC). This dynamic regulation involves not only writers but also “reader” proteins
  that recognize and bind to lactylated residues (Brg1 and DPF2), as well as “eraser”
  enzymes that remove lactyl groups to restore the protein’s original state (HDAC1-3,
  SIRT1/3/6 and CobB). Together, these mechanisms enable KL−la to function as a key
  metabolic signaling pathway, linking cellular lactate levels to diverse physiological
  and pathological processes, such as inflammation, angiogenesis, metabolic disorder,
  fibrosis, cell death and immune dysfunction
