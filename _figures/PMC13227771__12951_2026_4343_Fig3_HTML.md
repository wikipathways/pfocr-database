caption: 'Core Mechanisms of Pyroptosis. Pyroptosis is mediated through two principal
  pathways: the canonical pathway, initiated by caspase-1, and the non-canonical pathway,
  involving caspases-4/5/11. Both pathways lead to the cleavage of gasdermin D (GSDMD),
  generating its N-terminal fragment (GSDMD-NT), which forms pores in the cell membrane
  and facilitates the maturation and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as
  IL-1β and IL-18.The canonical inflammasome complexes—including NLRP3, AIM2, NLRP1,
  NLRC4, and PYRIN—typically consist of a pattern recognition receptor (PRR), the
  adaptor protein ASC, and pro-caspase-1. These inflammasomes are activated by specific
  stimuli: NLRP3 responds to endogenous danger signals such as extracellular ATP,
  crystalline substances, pore-forming toxins, and RNA; NLRP1 is activated by anthrax
  lethal toxin and muramyl dipeptide; NLRC4 recognizes bacterial flagellin and needle
  protein components such as PrgJ; AIM2 binds cytosolic double-stranded DNA from DNA
  viruses; and PYRIN is triggered by exposure to bacterial toxins.The non-canonical
  pyroptosis pathway is directly activated by intracellular lipopolysaccharide (LPS)
  derived from Gram-negative bacteria, leading to caspase-4/5/11 activation and subsequent
  induction of pore formation and inflammatory cytokine release'
