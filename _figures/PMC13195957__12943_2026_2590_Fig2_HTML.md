caption: Molecular mechanisms and driving factors of ferroptosis. This figure illustrates
  the pathway of ferroptosis initiated by the incorporation of polyunsaturated fatty
  acids (PUFAs) into membrane phospholipids (PUFA-PLs), which are highly susceptible
  to reactive oxygen species (ROS)-induced lipid peroxidation. Long-chain fatty acyl-CoA
  synthetase 4 (ACSL4) catalyzes the activation of PUFAs into acyl-CoA intermediates,
  which are then incorporated into phospholipids by lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase
  3 (LPCAT3), forming PUFA-containing phospholipids such as PE-AA and PE-AdA. Iron
  enters cells via transferrin receptor (TfR)-mediated endocytosis of transferrin-bound
  Fe³⁺, which is reduced to Fe²⁺ and released into the labile iron pool (LIP). Excess
  Fe²⁺ is stored in ferritin to prevent toxicity. Free Fe²⁺ catalyzes lipid peroxidation
  through Fenton reactions with H₂O₂, driving ferroptosis. Iron also serves as a cofactor
  for lipid peroxidation enzymes such as ALOX and POR. Mitochondria, as the major
  source of ROS via complexes I and III of the electron transport chain, play a central
  role in ferroptosis regulation. AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) suppresses ferroptosis
  by inactivating ACC and inhibiting fatty acid synthesis. Voltage-dependent anion
  channels (VDAC) and BCL2 family proteins (e.g., BCL2, BAK/BAX) modulate mitochondrial
  membrane permeability, affecting ferroptotic sensitivity. Glutamine metabolism provides
  α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) to fuel the TCA cycle and support ROS production. Dihydroorotate
  dehydrogenase (DHODH), located on the outer surface of the mitochondrial inner membrane,
  protects against ferroptosis by reducing ubiquinone to CoQH₂
