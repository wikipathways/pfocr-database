caption: Schematic depiction of the current model for [FeFe]-hydrogenase (HydA) maturation
  by the maturases HydG, HydE, and HydF. (A). HydG catalyzes a radical decomposition
  of tyrosine to form CO and CN–, which bind to the cysteine-coordinated dangler iron
  attached to its auxiliary cluster. A second tyrosine turnover leads to formation
  and release of the [FeII(cys)(CN)(CO)2]– synthon product. The synthon is a substrate
  for HydE, which is proposed to convert it to the di-iron(I) [2Fe]E complex. HydF
  binds [2Fe]E and interacts with components of the glycine cleavage system (GCS,
  B) specifically the aminomethyl-lipoyl-H-protein (Hmet) generated by aminomethyltransferase
  (T-protein) and serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHMT), to synthesize the DTMA ligand
  and form [2Fe]F. Amino acid ammonia-lyases such as AspA (aspartate ammonia lyase)
  and SdaA (serine ammonia lyase) provide ammonium for DTMA biosynthesis. (C) The
  overall maturation pathway shows how iron and individual atoms of tyrosine, cysteine,
  serine, and ammonia are incorporated into [2Fe]F and ultimately the H-cluster. The
  overall process converts the apo-HydA containing only the [4Fe-4S]H cluster to the
  holo-[FeFe]-hydrogenase containing the H-cluster
