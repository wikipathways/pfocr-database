caption: The tumor microenvironment influences CD47-mediated immune evasion of tumor
  cellsUpon stimulation of the TNF/TNFR1 pathway, the transcription factor NF-κB is
  released and translocates into the nucleus, where it binds to specific superenhancers
  associated with CD47 to activate its gene. IL-1β induces CD47 expression by activating
  NF-κB. IL-6 upregulates CD47 expression via the STAT3 pathway. IL4 induces STAT6-dependent
  upregulation of CD47. IL-7 and IL-13 also regulate CD47 expression, but the specific
  mechanisms involved remain unclear. IFN-γ promotes CD47 expression through the JAK-STAT1-IRF1
  pathway. Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-1 directly binds to the CD47 promoter, activating
  gene transcription and increasing cell surface CD47 expression. MYC directly binds
  to the CD47 promoter, increasing CD47 expression. The IDH1 (R132H) mutation disrupts
  the binding of the PKM2/β-catenin/BRG1 complex with the TCF4 transcription factor,
  inhibiting CD47 expression. The KRASG12C mutation activates the PI3K-STAT3 signaling
  pathway, inhibiting the expression of miR-34a, thereby relieving the posttranscriptional
  inhibition of CD47 by miR-34a. QPCTL modifies the posttranslated CD47 protein, inducing
  the formation of pGlu residues on the CD47 protein. EGF promotes c-Src-mediated
  phosphorylation of CD47, inhibiting the degradation of CD47 by the E3 ubiquitin
  ligase TRIM21. Acetyl-CoA derived from FAO binds to the CD47 gene promoter and upregulates
  CD47 transcription through NF-κB/RelA acetylation. Overexpression of NRF-1 enhances
  the activity of the CD47 promoter and regulates its binding to the CD47 promoter
