caption: Ginsenosides ameliorate liver fibrosis through modulation of the intestinal
  microbiota. (A) Ginsenosides reshape the gut microbiota composition by reducing
  Firmicutes and Proteobacteria abundance while increasing Bacteroidetes and SCFA-producing
  bacteria. These alterations decrease LPS production and elevate SCFA levels, thereby
  inhibiting HSC activation and attenuating liver fibrosis. (B) SCFAs promote phosphorylation
  and activation of the AMPK-SIRT1 axis in hepatocytes. Activated SIRT1 deacetylates
  PGC-1α, preventing its degradation and enhancing its activity, which attenuates
  hepatocyte injury and indirectly suppresses HSC activation. SCFAs also reduce miR-155-5p
  levels, thereby relieving suppression of cytokine signaling 1 (SOCS1) expression,
  consequently decreasing PDGF production and blocking HSC activation. Additionally,
  SCFAs inhibit HSC activation via stimulation of the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. (C) LPS
  induces immunocytes to produce pro-inflammatory factors through TLR4-NF-κB pathway
  activation, thereby stimulating HSC activation. Moreover, LPS activates HSCs by
  promoting autophagy mediated through the AKT-mTOR and AMPK-ULK1 pathways
