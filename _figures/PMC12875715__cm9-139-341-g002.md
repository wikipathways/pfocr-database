caption: 'Glycolytic metabolic intermediates and enzymes regulate cellular signaling
  transduction. This figure focuses on the non-metabolic, signal transduction functions
  of glycolytic products. It highlights glucose-6-phosphate (G6P), which modulates
  cellular redox balance via the pentose phosphate pathway (generating NADPH) and
  regulates glycogen synthesis and enzyme activity. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (FBP)
  is shown to exert anti-inflammatory effects through adenosine signaling and protect
  against hypoxic-ischemic damage. Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP) is depicted as
  a regulator of mTORC1 activity independent of AMPK, linking glucose sensing to lipid
  synthesis. Pyruvate and its derivative ethyl pyruvate are illustrated to modulate
  NF-κB signaling (inhibiting inflammation) and repress histone gene expression (suppressing
  cancer cell proliferation), emphasizing how glycolytic intermediates act as signaling
  molecules to coordinate cellular responses. The glycolysis pathway was created using
  modified templates from BioRender.com. ADP: Adenosine diphosphate; AMPK: AMP-activated
  protein kinase; ATP: Adenosine triphosphate; GSH: Glutathione; GSSG: Glutathione
  disulfide; mTORC: Mechanistic target of rapamycin complex; NAD: Nicotinamide adenine
  dinucleotide; NADH: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (reduced); NADP+: Nicotinamide
  adenine dinucleotide phosphate; NADPH: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate
  (reduced); NF-κB: Nuclear factor kappa- B; ROS: Reactive oxygen species'
