caption: 'Schematic diagram of biogenesis and contents of small extracellular vesiclesThe
  cargos encapsulated into early-sorting endosomes that later mature into late-sorting
  endosomes are carried to MVBs and released to extracellular space through fusion
  with the plasma membrane. Beyond the canonical endosome-MVB pathway, in cases of
  aberrant lysosomal autophagy, particularly when mediated by ROS accumulation, autophagosomes
  fail to undergo normal fusion with lysosomes for degradation. Instead, these autophagosomes
  are compelled to fuse with MVBs and are subsequently released into the extracellular
  space in the form of exosomes. Based on their size variations, exosomes can be further
  classified into three distinct categories of nanovesicles: Exo-L, Exo-S, and Exomere.
  Common membrane molecules are anchored on exosomes, such as integrin, ceramide,
  proteoglycan, and cholesterol, as well as tetraspanin family. Exosomes carry various
  goods from cytoplasm and nucleus, including proteins, noncoding RNAs, and DNAs that
  contribute to tumor progression at different stages. MVBs, multivesicular bodies;
  ROS, reactive oxygen species'
