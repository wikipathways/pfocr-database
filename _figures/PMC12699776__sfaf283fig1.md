caption: Germinal centre–dependent development of alloreactive memory B cell responses.
  After exposure to an allo-antigen (mainly HLA), this antigen is homed into a secondary
  lymphoid organ. There, naïve B cells interact with this antigen, leading to B cell
  maturation and activation. At first, a rapid extra-follicular differentiation pathway
  leads to the generation of extra-follicular memory B cells and extra-follicular
  short-lived plasma cells. These cells classically do not undergo immunoglobulin
  class-switching and somatic hypermutation. Later, the naïve B cell can engage into
  a germinal centre–dependent maturation, with immunoglobulin somatic hypermutation
  and immunoglobulin class-switching, resulting in IgG-producing cells of high affinity
  for its target. This GC-dependant maturation leads to the generation of LLPCs and
  IgG-memory B cells
