caption: Lignin formation in poplar. Phenylalanine produced in the plastids is metabolized
  into lignin monomers (or monolignols) in the cytoplasm via the phenylpropanoid pathway
  (Top). Monolignols are then exported to the apoplast and assembled into the growing
  lignin chain via free-radical coupling (Bottom). Poplar lignins consist of syringyl
  (S) and coniferyl (G) with minor amounts of p-hydroxyphenyl (H) units. The major
  linkage shown in the lignin polymer structure is β–O–4 (∼80%, red); minor linkages
  include β–5, β–β, and 4–O–5 (<5%, blue); and trace linkages include β–1 and 5 to
  5/4–O–β (<3%, not shown). p-Hydroxybenzoate (BA) conjugates represent ~10% on a
  monolignol basis. Key lignin biosynthetic genes include PAL, C4H, C3H, COMT, CCoAOMT,
  4CL, HCT, C3′H, and CSE; polymerization is catalyzed by PRXs and LACs. CHS is the
  first enzyme in the flavonoid biosynthetic pathway
