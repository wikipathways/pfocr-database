caption: Overview of EpCAM biology. EpCAM is a transmembrane protein with an N-terminal
  domain (ND), thyroglobulin domain (TY), extracellular domain (EC), transmembrane
  domain (TM), and intracellular domain (IC). EpCAM also has a 23-amino acid signaling
  peptide, which is not shown. In this study, the three asparagine N-glycosylation
  sites, present at N74, N111, and N198, were mutated to glutamine (Q) to prevent
  N-glycosylation in the mutant mutEpCAM protein. As previously reported in specific
  epithelial cancer contexts6–15, the EpCAM extracellular domain is cleaved near the
  plasma membrane by ADAM17 to form EpEC, which interacts with EGFR to trigger the
  MAPK pathway within the cell. The intracellular domain is cleaved within the transmembrane
  domain by presenilin 2 (PSEN2) to form EpIC. EpIC forms a complex with β-Catenin
  and FHL2, which is translocated to the nucleus and activates the transcription factor
  LEF1. Both the EpEC and EpIC pathways increase the transcription of many genes,
  among them MYC, CCNA2, and CCNE1, to promote cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation.
  The EpCAM intracellular domain also interacts with the actin cytoskeleton and changes
  its organization. This weakens E-Cadherin-mediated cell–cell adhesion by disrupting
  the association between E-Cadherin and the actin cytoskeleton. EpEC–EGFR/MAPK signaling
  and its downstream transcriptional effects have been observed in specific epithelial
  cancer contexts6–15. Created in BioRender. Tressler, C. (2026) https://BioRender.com/iksi26f
