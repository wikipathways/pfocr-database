caption: 'Roles of efferocytosis in atherosclerosis (AS). Apoptotic and necrotic cells
  release ''find-me'' signals that facilitate the recruitment of circulating monocytes
  and macrophages to the vascular wall. Subsequently, the infiltrated macrophages
  then engulf apoptotic cells by binding efferocytosis receptors (e.g., MerTK) to
  phosphatidylserine (PtdSer) on the cells, either directly or through bridging molecules
  (e.g., growth arrest-specific 6 (GAS6), or the interaction between LDL receptor-related
  protein 1 (LRP1) and calreticulin. However, the capacity of macrophages to engulf
  these cells is significantly impaired. In an inflammatory microenvironment, the
  upregulation of proteases leads to the cleavage of MerTK. Furthermore, oxidized
  low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) competes with calreticulin for binding to LRP1.
  Cluster of differentiation 147 (CD147) is known to promote the secretion of pro-inflammatory
  cytokines and facilitate late apoptosis through the TNF receptor-associated factor
  6 (TRAF6) -inhibitor of κB kinase (IKK)-interferon regulatory factor 5 (IRF5) signaling
  pathway, while simultaneously inhibiting efferocytosis by repressing the phosphorylation
  of signal transducer and activator of transcription 6 (STAT6), which in turn suppresses
  GAS6. Concurrently, necrotic cells exhibit an increased expression of ''don''t-eat-me''
  signals, such as cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47), which serve to evade efferocytosis
  by macrophages. LPS: lipopolysaccharide; IFN-γ: interferon-γ'
