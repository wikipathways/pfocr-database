caption: 'Phase-dependent iron dysmetabolism in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury.
  The figure illustrates the dynamic and contrasting shifts in iron metabolism during
  ischemia and subsequent reperfusion.(A) Ischemia phase (left panel, blue background):
  The process begins with the transient depletion of the labile iron pool (Fe2+) within
  cells during early ischemia. Reduced blood flow leads to the suppression of transferrin
  receptor 1 (TfR1) and a corresponding downregulation of iron regulatory proteins
  (IRPs), thereby impairing the cell''s primary iron uptake pathway. In late ischemia,
  the cell undergoes a series of compensatory changes that lead to iron accumulation.
  This reversal is driven by two concurrent events: first, upregulated heme oxygenase-1
  (HO-1) degrades heme, releasing a large amount of iron; second, NCOA4-mediated ferritin
  degradation is inhibited, causing this iron to be simultaneously sequestered within
  the cell. Iron accumulation sets the stage for subsequent injury.(B) Reperfusion
  phase (right panel, orange background): Upon reperfusion, the cellular environment
  dramatically shifts to iron overload and lethal oxidative stress. Reperfusion stimuli,
  including increased USP7-p53 signaling and enhanced ferritinophagy, dramatically
  increase TfR1 expression, leading to massive iron uptake. Simultaneously, systemically
  secreted hepcidin increases, binding to FPN and triggering its internalization and
  degradation, thereby blocking the iron export pathway. This combined massive influx
  and blocked efflux causes a significant expansion of the LIP. Excess iron from the
  LIP floods the mitochondria, where it catalyzes the Fenton reaction, generating
  a burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This leads to widespread lipid peroxidation
  and ultimately, cardiomyocyte injury and death'
