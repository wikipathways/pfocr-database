caption: Schematic overview of circulating neutrophil extracellular vesicle (NEV)-induced
  human renal glomerular endothelial cell activation via a matrix metalloproteinase
  (MMP)-dependent pathway. Circulating NEVs, which contain MMP-8 and MMP-9 proteins,
  are taken up by monocytes, which activate the p38 intracellular mitogen-activated
  protein kinase (MAPK) signalling pathway. Subsequently, this causes monocytes to
  display a proinflammatory phenotype, as demonstrated by the upregulation of cell
  adhesion molecules (intercellular cell adhesion molecule 1 [ICAM-1] and tissue factor
  (TF)), leading to tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNF) release. This secreted TNF
  causes significant activation of glomerular endothelial cells as measured by E-selectin
  and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM)
