caption: 'MSCs (top left) engage in bidirectional organelle exchange with HSCs (mito-donation,
  mitochondria transfer from HSCs to MSCs; mito-transfer, mitochondria transfer from
  MSCs to HSCs), while also secreting growth factors that regulate HSC quiescence
  and differentiation. The FAO–NADPH–cholesterol pathway supports HSC self-renewal
  by driving the production of extracellular vesicles (EVs), which exerts autocrine
  effects and induces SCF release from MSCs. Adipocytes (top right) contribute to
  the niche by releasing growth factors and free fatty acids (FFAs), which promote
  FAOfuelled mitochondrial OXPHOS in cycling HSCs. Additionally, adipocyte-derived
  adiponectin modulates mTORC1 signalling, thereby impacting HSC quiescence. Osteoblasts
  (bottom left) produce a collagen-based matrix in bone, adhere to HSCs via N-cadherin
  and secrete essential niche factors such as osteopontin (OPN), further contributing
  to the balance between HSC maintenance and lineage commitment. Osteoblasts have
  a key role in HSC regulation through the activation of NOTCH1 signalling by expressing
  JAGGED-1, which exerts context-dependent effects on stem cell fate supporting quiescence
  or promoting lineage commitment. Calcium (Ca2+) receptors of quiescent HSCs sense
  Ca2+ in the osteoblastic niche. ECs (bottom right) from arteriolar and sinusoidal
  regions exhibit distinct interactions with HSCs: arteriolar ECs maintain quiescent
  HSCs by sustaining low ROS levels, whereas sinusoidal ECs support more activated,
  migratory HSCs with higher ROS levels. Additionally, EC-derived angiopoietin-like
  protein 2 (ANGPTL2) promotes PPARδ activity, which contributes to quiescence. PI3K,
  phosphoinositide 3-kinase'
