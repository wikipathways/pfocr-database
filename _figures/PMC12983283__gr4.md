caption: Simple model illustrating the roles of NO and H2S in regulating seed dormancy
  and germination.NO promotes germination by antagonizing abscisic acid (ABA) signaling
  and enhancing gibberellin (GA) activity. The binding of ABA to its receptors PYRABACTIN
  RESISTANCE 1/PYR1-LIKE/REGULATORY COMPONENTS (PYR/PYL/RCAR) leads to inhibition
  of type 2C protein phosphatases (PP2Cs) by activating sucrose nonfermenting 1 (SNF1)-related
  protein kinase 2 (SnRK2). This signaling cascade stimulates the basic leucine zipper
  TF ABSCISIC ACID INSENSITIVE 5 (ABI5), which helps maintain seed dormancy. Group
  VII ethylene response factors (ERF-VIIs) have been shown to regulate crosstalk between
  NO and ABA by modulating ABI5 expression (Gibbs et al., 2015; Albertos et al., 2015;
  Wang et al., 2015a). NO can mediate the nitration of PYR receptors, leading to inactivation
  of ABA signaling and thereby promoting seed germination (Castillo et al., 2015).
  Additionally, ABI5 can undergo S-nitrosation, which promotes its degradation and
  consequently relieves dormancy (Albertos et al., 2015; Mata-Pérez et al., 2025).
  Furthermore, the TF ABI4, another positive regulator of dormancy within the ABA
  pathway, can be persulfidated by H2S, which increases its stability and prevents
  degradation, potentially delaying germination (Zhou et al., 2021a). During seed
  dormancy, ABA promotes the interaction between PYL receptors and MYB30 to repress
  the transcriptional activity of MYB30, thereby preventing expression of CYP707A2,
  which encodes a protein with ABA 8′-hydroxylase activity. After germination, increased
  NO levels lead to S-nitrosation of MYB30, enhancing its transcriptional activity
  by disrupting its interaction with PYLs. This shift promotes CYP707A2 expression
  and activity, reducing ABA content and facilitating seed germination (Zhao et al.,
  2024)
