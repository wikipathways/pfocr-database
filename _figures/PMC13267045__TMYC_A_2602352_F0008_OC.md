caption: Comparative overview of host- and fungus-derived metabolites involved in
  agarwood formation. This figure illustrates the metabolic interactions between the
  host tissues of Aquilaria and their associated endophytic fungi. (a) The host mainly
  produces sesquiterpenes, 2-(2-phenylethyl)chromones (PECs), and polysaccharides
  through the enzymatic activities of sesquiterpene synthase, lipoxygenase (LOX),
  and planer, which catalyse the transformation of starch and phenolic precursors
  into bioactive resin components. (b) The fungal metabolites include sterols, phthalate
  esters, aromatic compounds, alkanes, and fatty acids. Fungal enzymes such as keratinases,
  oxidases, and LOX degrade alkanes and fatty acids, generating reactive oxygen species
  (ROS) that activate host defence pathways and promote resin accumulation. Together,
  these interactions elucidate the coordinated biochemical processes underlying the
  induction and maturation of agarwood
