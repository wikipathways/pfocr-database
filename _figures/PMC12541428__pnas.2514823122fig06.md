caption: Summary of effects of 3-NOP on microbe–microbe and host–microbe interactions
  in the rumen of calves. (A) H2 transfer between H2-sensing fermenters (i.e., fermenters
  encoding group C [FeFe] hydrogenases) and H2 oxidizers (methanogens and acetogens)
  in the rumen of untreated calves (low in situ H2 concentrations) and 3-NOP treated
  calves (high in situ H2 concentrations). The displayed fermentation pathway is based
  on the model rumen fermenter Ruminococcus albus (6, 63). However, the genomic potential
  of MAGs encoding group C [FeFe]-hydrogenases varies (Dataset S5), and as such, the
  response to high H2 concentrations can be more diverse. We depict a potential link
  from ethanol to valerate and caproate based on previous literature (38, 55–57, 84),
  as detailed in SI Appendix, Fig. S5. Green arrows indicate pathways that are more
  prominent at higher H2 concentrations (3-NOP treatments). The red crosses indicate
  that 3-NOP inhibits the MCR resulting in reduced CH4 production and H2 consumption
  by methanogens. (B) Metabolic interactions between ruminal microbes and calves treated
  with or without 3-NOP. Bar plots for each group indicate relative changes in abundance
  based on metagenomic (Left) and metatranscriptomic (Right) mapping (Figs. 2D and
  4A and SI Appendix, Fig. S6). Relative changes in measured gas emissions and SCFA
  concentrations in ruminal fluids across treatment groups are shown (*CO2 concentrations
  reflect host respiration and belching), and the smaller size of the bar plots for
  valerate and caproate indicate that the concentration of these two SCFAs in all
  treatments was lower compared to the main SCFAs (acetate, propionate, and butyrate)
  (Fig. 1). Intermediates (depicted in brown) occasionally accumulated to detectable
  concentrations especially in 3-NOP treated groups (Dataset S1)
