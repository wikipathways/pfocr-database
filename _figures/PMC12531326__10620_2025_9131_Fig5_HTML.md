caption: 'MicroRNAs involved in the regulation of NOD2 signaling pathway in intestinal
  epithelium. NOD2: Nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-containing protein 2,
  RIP2: Receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase-2, TAK1: Transforming
  growth factor beta-activated kinase-1, TAB: TAK1 binding protein, MAPK: Mitogen-activated
  protein kinase, IKKβ: IκB kinase beta, JNK: c:Jun N-terminal kinase, P38: p38 mitogen-activated
  protein kinase, AP-1: Activator protein-1, NF-κB: Nuclear factor kappa-light-chain
  enhancer of activated B cells. Several microRNAs (miRNAs) regulate NOD2 expression
  and its associated inflammatory pathways in intestinal epithelial cells, influencing
  the pathogenesis of Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD). MiR-122 suppresses NOD2 expression,
  inhibiting both LPS-induced expression and NF-κB activation, thereby reducing pro-inflammatory
  cytokine production. MiR-495, miR-512, and miR-671 also target NOD2, disrupting
  intestinal defense mechanisms against microbes and promoting excessive inflammation.
  MiR-146a-5p and miR-192 further regulate NOD2 expression, modulating inflammatory
  responses and contributing to IBD development. Additionally, miR-320 has been shown
  to inhibit NOD2 expression, suggesting potential therapeutic applications for Crohn’s
  disease. These miRNAs highlight key regulatory roles in intestinal inflammation
  and immune response'
