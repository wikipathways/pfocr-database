caption: Cancer dormancy and awakening within the invasion–metastasis cascade. During
  tumor progression, primary cancer cells undergo EMT to gain motility and invasive
  capacity, thereby enabling dissemination to distant organs. After seeding in the
  distant organs, DTCs frequently enter a dormant state. Dormancy can be induced or
  sustained by both cell-intrinsic and cell-extrinsic cues. Intrinsic regulators include
  low ERK/p38 activity, a highly mesenchymal phenotypic state, and suppression of
  canonical Wnt signaling. These programs can be triggered or reinforced by microenvironment-produced
  cytokines, such as the TGF-β2 produced by mesenchymal stem cells or alveolar macrophages,
  and the BMP7 secreted by bone marrow stromal cells. Dormant cells evade immune surveillance
  by downregulating MHC class I or upregulating PD-L1 expression. Awakening occurs
  when dormant cancer cells encounter heterotypic awakening signals, such as the NE
  and MMP9 released by NETs, various EGFR ligands from M2-like macrophages, and sFRP1
  derived from aged fibroblasts. Upon awakening, cancer cells increase ERK/p38 activity,
  undergo partial MET to achieve a quasi-mesenchymal state, and reactivate canonical
  Wnt signaling. BMP7, bone morphogenetic protein 7; DKK1, dickkopf-related protein
