caption: 'Activation of the cGAS-STING signaling pathway. The positively charged N-
  and C-termini of cGAS bind to negatively charged DNA, which stimulates the production
  of cGAMP. cGAMP then binds to and activates STING, a protein located in the endoplasmic
  reticulum (ER). Once activated, STING translocates to the Golgi apparatus, where
  it recruits and activates TBK1 and IKK. These kinases then phosphorylate IRF3 and
  release NF-κB dimers, respectively, facilitating the translocation of IRF3 and NF-κB
  into the nucleus. In the nucleus, IRF3 and NF-κB dimers bind to specific DNA sequences,
  regulating the transcription of interferons (IFNs). When IFNs bind to their receptors,
  this activates the receptor, causing JAKs activation (Janus kinases, specifically
  JAK1 and TYK2, come into close proximity and phosphorylate one another). Activated
  JAKs then phosphorylate tyrosine residues on the receptor, forming a receptor complex
  that serves as a docking site for STAT proteins (signal transducer and activator
  of transcription). The receptor complex recruits and activates STAT proteins, which
  then dimerize and bind to IRF9, forming the ISGF3 complex. This complex translocates
  to the nucleus, leading to the expression of interferon-stimulated genes. ER: endoplasmic
  reticulum; IFNs: type I interferons; IKK: IκB kinase; IRF3: interferon regulatory
  factor 3; IRF9: interferon regulatory factor 9; ISGF3: interferon-stimulated gene
  factor 3; JAKs: associated Janus kinases; NF-κB: nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer
  of activated B cells; STAT: signal transducer and activator of transcription; TBK1:
  TANK-binding kinase 1. The figure was created with BioRender.com'
