caption: 'Mechanisms and Drugs of Salivary Gland Cell Senescence in Sjögren’s Syndrome
  Mitochondria-derived reactive ROS impair SG function through four primary pathways:
  oxidizing DNA bases, damaging proteins, inducing mitochondrial self-damage, and
  activating pro-inflammatory and pro-senescence signaling pathways. The activation
  of inflammatory signaling pathways further triggers the DDR, accelerating telomere
  attrition. Telomere shortening, in turn, activates DDR. Although the upregulation
  of ATM attempts to repair telomere damage, under chronic inflammatory stress, it
  instead exacerbates cell cycle arrest, ultimately leading to cellular senescence.
  Cellular senescence and the SASP form a vicious, self-perpetuating cycle. Under
  high lactate conditions, mtDNA damage and leakage occur, activating the cGAS-STING
  pathway and driving inflammation. Additionally, autoreactive immune cells and autoantibodies
  contribute to the initiation and progression of SG cell senescence. Nanozymes, melatonin,
  Cordycepin, and MSCs exert anti-aging effects primarily through combating oxidative
  stress. Rapamycin inhibits senescence by suppressing the SASP. Metformin inhibits
  the SASP via suppression of the IKK/NF-κB pathway. Dasatinib, Quercetin, and Navitoclax
  act directly as senolytics (inducing apoptosis of senescent cells). SS: Sjögren’s
  syndrome; G: Guanine; 8-oxoG:8-oxo guanine; DDR: DNA Damage Response; SASP: Senescence-Associated
  Secretory Phenotype; SG: salivary gland; mtDNA: mitochondrial DNA; MSCs: Mesenchymal
  Stem Cells, (-): suppression'
