caption: 'B-cell differentiation on antigen recognition occurs via 2 main pathways:
  the extrafollicular pathway, where CD11c+ B cells (DN2 and activated naïve B cells)
  and CD11c− B cells (DN3) differentiate into short-lived plasmablasts that secrete
  low-affinity IgM or IgG antibodies with limited SHM mutations, and the germinal
  center pathway, involving repeated cycling between the dark zone (proliferation
  and SHM) and the light zone (antigen re-encounter and selection with Tfh help),
  resulting in high-affinity switched B cells. ActB are cells recently activated via
  the germinal center pathway, displaying traits intermediate between MBCs and PCs.
  These cells are considered precursors to switched MBCs and long-lived PCs that secrete
  high-affinity antibodies. This figure was created using Biorender. ActB, activated
  B cells; BCR, B-cell receptor; CSR, class switch recombination; DC, dendritic cells;

  G; IgM, immunoglobulin M; MBCs, memory B cells; PCs, plasma cells; SHM, somatic
  hypermutation; Tfh, follicular T-helper cells'
