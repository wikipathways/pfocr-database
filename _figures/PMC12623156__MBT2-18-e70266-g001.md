caption: Urea as a core element of the catabolism of guanidine and guanidine‐containing
  metabolites. (A) Creatinine degradation, showing how guanidine metabolites converge
  on urea. The direct pathway (green) converts creatine into sarcosine and urea. A
  second pathway (orange) requires ATP‐dependent hydrolysis of N‐methylhydantoin into
  N‐carbamoylsarcosine before breakdown to sarcosine, ammonium, and carbonic acid.
  A third pathway (light blue) forms acetate and methylguanidine, later hydrolyzed
  to methylamine and urea. Urea degradation occurs either via a nickel‐dependent urease
  or an ATP‐consuming route that produces allophanate before yielding ammonium and
  bicarbonate. (B) Guanidine degradation pathways. A direct route employs a nickel‐dependent
  enzyme (related to arginase or agmatinase, see text) to produce urea, degraded as
  in panel A. A parallel route forms carboxyguanidine, which is unstable and rapidly
  hydrolyzed back to guanidine. This requires a deiminase producing allophanate, subsequently
  broken down to ammonium and bicarbonate (dark blue). These steps must be efficient
  to avoid a futile energy‐dissipating cycle
