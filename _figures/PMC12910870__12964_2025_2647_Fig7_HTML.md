caption: Proposed molecular mechanisms for regulation of VDAC1 overexpression. Cells
  treated with various cell death inducers, leading to different forms of PCD, all
  involving activation of the VDAC1 promoter, resulting in its increased expression,
  followed by its oligomerization and activation of apoptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis.
  A The activation of p38-MAPK is controlled by MKK3 and MKK6 (MAPK Kinases 3 and
  6), which are upstream kinases. The p38-MAPK signaling pathway phosphorylated c-Jun
  and c-Fos that, as a heterodimer, binds to the AP-1 promoter region and leads to
  the activation of VDAC1 transcription. SB203580, an inhibitor of MAPK, inhibits
  c-Fos and c-Jun and, thus, VDAC1 overexpression at the promotor level. B The activation
  of JNK is controlled by the upstream kinases MKK4 and MKK7 (MAPK Kinases 4 and 7).
  JNK activates the c-Jun pathway, leading to increased VDAC1 transcription. SP600125,
  a JNK inhibitor, prevented c-Jun phosphorylation and the increased expression of
  VDAC1 as induced by various stimuli. C Activation of the CaMK-II pathway resulted
  in activation of the TF, ATF-1, which binds to the CRE (cAMP Response Element) sequence,
  5’-TGACGTCA-3’, which is typically found upstream of genes within the promoter or
  enhancer regions. KN-62 is an inhibitor of CaMK-II that inhibits ATF-1 phosphorylation,
  and thereby VDAC1 overexpression at the promoter level. The overexpressed VDAC1
  leads to its oligomerization (D) and to the release of pro-apoptotic proteins, ROS
  overproduction, and increased [Ca2+], and subsequently to PCD
