caption: 'A donor DNA within a plasmid (dashed lines) contains short (~40 bp) homology
  arms on the left (blue box, L) and the right (green box, R) of the new genetic information
  to be incorporated into the chromosome (yellow box, D). Bottom: the chromosomal
  target region is shown. The sites of CRISPR cleavage are denoted with red arrowheads.
  Step 1: two CRISPR cuts liberate a linear dsDNA donor, which has short nonhomology
  at either end (red lines). A third CRISPR-mediated DSB is also introduced on the
  chromosome at the site where the genetic modification is desired. Step 2: the ends
  (four in total) of the donor DNA and the chromosome are resected, exposing the regions
  of homology in ssDNA form. Step 3: in a RAD52-dependent process, the ends are annealed
  together. Step 4: the flaps are trimmed away. Step 5: the residual gaps are filled
  in, and the nicks are ligated. This results in the incorporation of the new genetic
  information (D) onto the chromosome at the desired location in a nonmutagenic, seamless
  fashion. Note that the similarities between SSA (Fig. 3) and this putative HMEJ
  mechanism are compelling'
