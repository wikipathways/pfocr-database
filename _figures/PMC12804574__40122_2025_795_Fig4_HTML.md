caption: Convergent and drug-specific mechanisms of pharmacological agents. The diagram
  illustrates the transition from currently known mechanisms (top) to proposed mechanisms
  for treating comorbid conditions (bottom). Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) and
  selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) exert their effects primarily through
  monoaminergic regulation. Ketamine acts via glutamatergic modulation and noncompetitive
  N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) antagonism, coupled with Tiam1 suppression,
  to restore anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) synaptic function. It additionally demonstrates
  anti-inflammatory properties through MAPK pathway inhibition. Dihydromyricetin (DHM)
  functions as a multi-target agent, alleviating neuroinflammation by suppressing
  the AGE–RAGE axis, P2X7 receptor, and TLR4/Akt/HIF-1α/NLRP3 signaling, while promoting
  microglial polarization toward the M2 phenotype. Minocycline primarily exhibits
  anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting the IL-6/JAK2/STAT3 pathway and facilitating
  microglial transition from M1 to M2. It also modulates NMDAR activity and induces
  protective autophagy via PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway inhibition. Future treatment methods
  can focus on neural regulation targeting specific neural circuits, as well as regulating
  the activation and phenotypic transformation of glial cells towards anti-inflammatory
  effects, and directly antagonizing inflammation-related signaling pathways and cytokines.
  PSP post-stroke pain, PSD post-stroke depression, mPFC medial prefrontal cortex,
  LC–NE locus coeruleus-norepinephrine system
