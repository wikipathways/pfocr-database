caption: Representative ofIFN-independentMITA functions.① MITA, which resides at the
  ER at rest, regulates lipid and glucose metabolism by targeting key metabolic enzymes,
  such as FADS2 and HK2. It inhibits the activity of these enzymes, thereby restricting
  polyunsaturated fatty acid synthesis and aerobic glycolysis, which are critical
  for resolving inflammation and tumor progression. Cytosolic DNA fragments bind to
  cGAS, activating the MITA pathway. Upon binding to cGAMP, MITA interacts with PERK,
  leading to its activation. Activated PERK phosphorylates eIF2α, which promotes a
  selective translational program that is essential for the regulation of cellular
  senescence.② Upon activation by cGAMP, MITA translocates from the ER to the ERGIC.
  A portion of MITA mediates the formation of single-membrane vesicles. The MITA-containing
  ERGIC membrane serves as a potential membrane source. This process, with the assistance
  of ATG16L1-ATG5-ATG12 and LC3, contributes to the formation of these single-membrane
  vesicles, which are critical for the clearance of cytosolic DNA and pathogens.③
  After reaching the Golgi, the proton channel activity of MITA leads to the elevation
  of Golgi and Golgi-derived vesicle pH. Subsequently, the lipidation of GABARAP promotes
  the activation of the transcription factor TFEB and enhances lysosome biogenesis.④
  The MITA-IRF3 signal induces the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines by activating
  NF-κB. Furthermore, the IRF3-BAX complex is transported to the mitochondria, triggering
  apoptosis
