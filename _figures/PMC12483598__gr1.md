caption: The protein export pathway in P. falciparum involves the trafficking of both
  PEXEL proteins and PNEPs are being shownIn case of PEXEL proteins, both integral
  membrane and soluble proteins follow a similar route starting from the synthesis
  of proteins and their entry into the Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) (stage I) followed
  by Plasmepsin V (PM-V) based processing (stage II). However, there are some exceptions
  to this general scheme of PEXEL protein export. Some PEXEL proteins contain recessed
  signal sequences in their N termini while some exported proteins do not contain
  any ER-specific signal sequences, which can be recognized and cleaved by resident
  signal proteases. Following PEXEL processing, these proteins are exported to the
  parasite periphery through ER-based secretory vesicles (stage III). These vesicles
  fuse with the parasite plasma membrane (PPM) to release their cargo. Soluble proteins
  are released into the parasitophorous vacuole (PV) while transmembrane (TM) proteins
  are transferred to the parasite plasma membrane (PPM). Soluble proteins are picked
  up by PTEX translocon (Plasmodium Translocon of Exported Proteins) from the PV,
  while the TM proteins undergo another extraction step at the PPM in order to come
  into contact with the PTEX. Following an unfolding step, all exported proteins are
  translocated by PTEX into the iRBC cytosol. These exported proteins are further
  trafficked into the iRBC cytosol, Maurer’s clefts, and to the iRBC membrane through
  the possible involvement of various secondary factors. As per our current knowledge,
  PNEPs (soluble and transmembrane domain containing) follow a similar export pathway.
  However, PNEPs do not undergo the multistep processing and are directly packaged
  into secretory vesicles from the ER. The PNEPs are similarly recognized and transferred
  by PTEX at the PVM. The export of PEXEL proteins and PNEPs converge into the same
  pathway at the PVM
