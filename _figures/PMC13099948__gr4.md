caption: 'Roles of pyroptosis in atherosclerosis (AS). In vascular endothelial cell
  (VEC), oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) enhances the expression of various
  inflammasome components, including pro-interleukin (IL)-18, pro-IL-1β, NLR family
  pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3), adaptor protein apoptosis-associated
  speck-like protein containing a CARD (ASC), and pro-Caspase-1, through the activation
  of the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling pathway. Caspase-1 plays a dual role
  by cleaving pro-IL-18 and pro-IL-1β into their active forms IL-18 and IL-1β respectively,
  as well as processing gasdermin D (GSDMD) into its active fragment GSDMD-N, which
  subsequently induces pyroptosis. Conversely, forkhead box P1 (Foxp1) and Rho family
  GTPase 3 (Rnd3) suppress pyroptosis. In detail, Rnd3 inhibits the ubiquitination
  of K63-linked TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) while promoting the ubiquitination
  of K48-linked TRAF6 which results in the suppression of NF-κB activation and the
  degradation of TRAF6. In macrophages, upon exposure to ox-LDL, GSDMD-N is activated
  to form pores in the cell membrane and disrupt mitochondrial integrity, thereby
  initiating mitochondria-cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon
  genes (STING)-interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3)/NF-κB signaling axis, thus amplifying
  inflammatory response and contributing to the progression of AS. TLR: toll-like
  receptor; mtDNA: mitochondrial DNA'
