caption: Metazoan cells possess multiple mechanisms to repair DSBs. The choice of
  repair pathway utilization is largely determined by the cell cycle position. Non-homologous
  end rejoining (NHEJ) is the dominant pathway in G1. It is a non-templated process
  that is capable of directly sealing DSBs with little or no end processing, but at
  the cost of potential errors. In S/G2, homologous recombination (HR) becomes available
  as DNA replication generates the sister chromatid, which is used as the repair template.
  To license HR, DSB ends must first undergo extensive end resection, which is promoted
  by BRCA1 and antagonized by 53BP1 (the latter also protects DSB ends in G1). Thereafter,
  BRCA1 recruits PALB2-BRCA2 to assemble RAD51 recombinase on resected DSB ends to
  initiate homology search. The presence of extensive sequence homology within the
  intact sister chromatid allows RAD51 to strand invade into it and prime high fidelity
  DNA synthesis to restore lost genetic information. Finally, HR is terminated when
  the D-loop intermediate is resolved. When HR is not possible due to RAD51 loading
  defects, mutagenic mechanisms such as DNA polymerase θ-mediated end joining (TMEJ)
  or single-strand annealing (SSA) may act on resected DSB ends that contain limited
  sequence homology (perhaps in G2/M), causing genomic instability. Notably, cells
  expressing mutant BRCA1 proteins, such as BRCA-Δ11, are HR defective due to impaired
  end resection and RAD51 loading. As both defects are reversed upon loss of 53BP1
  and since end resection is a prerequisite for RAD51 assembly, it initially suggested
  that BRCA1’s key role in HR is to counteract 53BP1 to promote end resection. Created
