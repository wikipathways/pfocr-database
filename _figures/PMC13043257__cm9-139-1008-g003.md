caption: 'Receptor-mediated and ubiquitin-dependent mitophagy. Mitophagy is mediated
  through two principal mechanisms: The ubiquitin-dependent pathway and the receptor-mediated
  pathway. The ubiquitin-dependent pathway (e.g., PINK1/Parkin) is typically activated
  by severe global mitochondrial damage, often marked by a loss of membrane potential.
  It involves the stabilization of PINK1 on the OMM, which phosphorylates ubiquitin
  to recruit and activate the E3 ubiquitin ligase Parkin. Parkin then catalyzes the
  extensive ubiquitination of numerous OMM proteins. These ubiquitinated substrates
  recruit adaptor proteins such as OPTN and NDP52, which bind LC3 on autophagosomes
  to direct mitochondrial engulfment and degradation. The receptor-mediated pathway
  is often induced by specific stressors such as hypoxia or during development. It
  utilizes OMM proteins, such as BNIP3, NIX, and FUNDC1, which function as mitophagy
  receptors. These receptors contain an LIR motif, enabling them to directly bind
  LC3 on autophagosomal membranes. Together, these pathways constitute a multilayered
  and complementary quality control system. BNIP3: BCL2 interacting protein 3; FUNDC1:
  FUN14 domain-containing protein 1; LC3: Microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain
  3; LIR: LC3-interacting region; MMP: Mitochondrial membrane potential; NDP52: Calcium
  binding and coiled-coil domain 2; NIX: Nip3-like protein X; OMM: Outer mitochondrial
  membrane; OPTN: Optineurin; P: Phosphorylation; PINK1: PTEN induced kinase 1; Ub:
  Ubiquitination'
