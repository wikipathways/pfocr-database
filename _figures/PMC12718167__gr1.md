caption: 'Integrated molecular machinery and regulatory pathways of autophagyThe autophagy
  pathway proceeds through four major stages orchestrated by five core protein complexes
  with their associated regulatory mechanisms.(A) Initiation: the ULK1 initiation
  complex (ULK1/2, ATG13, FIP200, and ATG101) responds to stress signals through mTOR
  inhibition and AMPK activation, initiating autophagosome formation at the phagophore
  assembly site.(B) Nucleation-elongation-maturation: the PI3K nucleation complex
  (beclin 1, VPS34, VPS15, and ATG14L) generates PI3P for vesicle nucleation, while
  the ATG9 trafficking system (ATG9A, WIPI1/2, and ATG2) delivers membranes for phagophore
  expansion. During this stage, cytoplasmic contents and organelles are sequestered
  within the growing isolation membrane.(C) Fusion: the ATG12 conjugation system (ATG12-ATG5-ATG16L
  complex formed through ATG7 and ATG10 activity) acts as an E3-like enzyme facilitating
  autophagosome-lysosome docking and fusion (forming the autophagolysosome, the initial
  fusion product).(D) Degradation: the LC3 conjugation system (LC3/GABARAP proteins
  processed by ATG4B and conjugated to PE via ATG7 and ATG3) enables cargo recognition
  and degradation within the autolysosome (the mature, acidified degradative compartment).
  Each complex panel includes detailed components, primary functions, and key regulatory
  mechanisms, providing a comprehensive reference for understanding autophagy regulation.Abbreviations:
  AMPK, AMP-activated protein kinase; ATG, autophagy-related; Bcl-2, B-cell lymphoma
  2; FIP200, FAK family kinase-interacting protein of 200 kDa; GABARAP, GABA type
  A receptor-associated protein; LC3, microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain
  3; mTOR, mechanistic target of rapamycin; PAS, pre-autophagosomal structure; PE,
  phosphatidylethanolamine; PI3P, phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate; TGN, trans-Golgi
  network; ULK1/2, unc-51-like kinase 1/2; VPS, vacuolar protein sorting; WIPI, WD
  repeat domain phosphoinositide-interacting protein'
