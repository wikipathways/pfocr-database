caption: 'The TME Shapes Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation in Tumor Cells. (Conceptual
  model). Multiple extrinsic factors in the TME dynamically reshape the formation
  and function of biomolecular condensates via LLPS, thereby driving malignant phenotypes
  and therapy resistance.(a) Metabolic products & acidic pH: Lactate accumulation
  and acidification are associated with disrupted LLPS capacity. Lactate-driven modifications
  of LLPS-related regulators (representative examples: cGAS, p53) are shown as potential
  intermediates leading to condensate dysfunction and downstream genomic instability
  and immune evasion. (b) Hypoxia: Low oxygen tension (O₂↓) promotes formation of
  stress granules/condensates (SG/condensates↑) (representative examples: G3BP1, ferritin)
  and supports transcriptional reprogramming. (c) Nutrient deprivation: Amino acid
  and/or glucose shortage (AA↓, glucose↓) induces nutrient-stress–associated LLPS
  responses (representative examples: Sec bodies, SIPAN), facilitating survival and
  metabolic reprogramming. (d) Cytokine alterations: Cytokine signaling (illustrated
  with TGF-β) modulates RNP condensates (representative example: METTL3), leading
  to changes in mRNA stability and rewired downstream signaling (illustrated with
  integrin signaling↑). (e) Changes in physical factors: Physical stresses remodel
  LLPS-related processes, including osmotic stress–linked AMOT/KIBRA condensates activating
  the Hippo pathway, paxillin (PXN)-associated cytoskeletal remodeling, and heat-shock
  protein (Hsp)-linked stress granule assembly to support proteostasis. Symbols denote
  LLPS condensates and disrupted LLPS as indicated in the figure. This diagram is
  a conceptual model and not an experimental schematic.Abbreviations: TME, tumor microenvironment;
  LLPS, liquid–liquid phase separation; SG, stress granule; RNP, ribonucleoprotein;
  cGAS, cyclic GMP–AMP synthase; HIF-1α, hypoxia-inducible factor 1α; METTL3, methyltransferase-like
  3; PXN, paxillin; AMOT, angiomotin; KIBRA, WWC1; Hsp, heat shock protein.Note: This
  image was created by Biorender(https://www.biorender.com/). The authors confirm
  compliance with journal licensing policies. All figures are conceptual models, not
  experimental schematics'
