caption: m6A modification and associated molecules in Th2-driven allergic responses.
  In Th2-driven allergic responses, reduced METTL3 expression, through YTHDF2-dependent
  (e.g. JAK1, Foxp3) or -independent (e.g. SOCS1/3) mechanisms, enhances the stability
  of critical immune factor mRNAs, such as IL-13 and SOCS1/3, thereby decreasing their
  degradation. Conversely, it reduces stability and promotes degradation of various
  molecules, including TLR4 and Foxp3, leading to activation or suppression of key
  pathways in the Th2-driven allergic responses. In contrast, in an anti-inflammatory
  state, increased METTL3 expression reduces stability and promotes degradation of
  Th2 cell transcription factor GATA3 mRNA through various mechanisms. Concurrently,
  WTAP, via a YTHDF1-mediated mechanism, increases Foxp3 mRNA stability, contributing
  to an anti-Th2-driven allergic responses. However, in an arsenic-induced skin injury
  model, elevated METTL3 expression drives increased SOCS3 expression, abnormally
  activating the JAK2/STAT3 pathway. This response differs from that observed under
  Th2-driven allergic responses conditions
