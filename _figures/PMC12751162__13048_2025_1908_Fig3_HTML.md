caption: 'Mechanisms involving glycolysis contribute to the pathogenesis of PCOS. In
  the pathogenesis of PCOS, glycolysis is involved in multiple processes. A: Downregulation
  of key glycolytic enzymes. A reduction in critical glycolytic enzymes, including
  HK1, PFKP, PFKM, and PKM, in the granulosa cells of patients with PCOS leads to
  decreased glycolytic rates and ATP production, resulting in impaired follicular
  development and reduced pregnancy rates. B: Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction.
  Excessive accumulation of ROS in the mitochondria of granulosa cells in patients
  with PCOS causes a decline in ATP levels, thereby damaging oocyte quality and lowering
  the success rate of IVF-ET. C: Regulation by epigenetic and noncoding RNAs. lncRNAs
  and miRNAs in patients with PCOS are associated with pathways such as glycolysis
  and gluconeogenesis. Notably, the differential expression of miR-143-3p and miR-155-5p
  may regulate the expression of key glycolytic enzymes, including HK2, PKM2, and
  LDHA, thereby modulating glycolysis in cultured granulosa cells. D: Crosstalk between
  glycolysis and signalling pathways. The interaction between glycolysis and various
  signalling pathways also influences the development of PCOS. In PCOS, dysregulation
  of the AMPK pathway and hyperactivation of the mTOR signalling pathway increase
  glycolytic activity, affecting follicular growth. TXNIP inhibits the IRS-1/PI3K
  signalling axis, leading to decreased expression of key glycolytic enzymes and contributing
  to PCOS pathogenesis. Additionally, in PCOS, miR-34a-5p suppresses glycolysis by
  targeting LDHA'
