caption: A proposed model involves the overexpression of ZmPEPC, GmAspAT and NtGS
  in Brassica, aiming to utilize more CO2 and NH3 for primary and secondary metabolite
  biosynthesis and increased biomass production as identified by NMR. Transgenic plants
  coexpressing ZmPEPC, GmAspAT and NtGS enhance CO2 and NH3 utilization, channelling
  them towards primary and secondary metabolites. Enhanced PEPC activity produces
  OAA, facilitating Asp synthesis using Glu as an ammonia source. Higher GS activity
  ensures a higher NH3 assimilation rate and Glu synthesis via the GS‐GOGAT pathway.
  The Krebs cycle connects carbon source utilization and NH3 assimilation through
  α‐ketoglutarate, central to C and N metabolism. PAG coexpression increases C utilization
  for carbohydrate (glucose, fructose, sucrose) and amino acid synthesis (alanine,
  aspartate, methionine, cysteine, glutamine, proline, arginine, glycine, serine).
  Increased CO2 utilization enhances glucose production and sucrose synthesis, metabolized
  by invertases and SuSy enzymes, releasing hexose phosphates for the oxidative pentose
  phosphate pathway, providing acetyl‐CoA for fatty acid production. Pyruvate kinase
  directs carbon partitioning towards protein and oil accumulation pathways. Higher
  aspartate levels indicate CO2 utilization towards aspartate family amino acids,
  methionine regulating important metabolites. Photorespiratory C may also be exported
  from the photorespiratory pathway in the form of the amino acid glycine and serine,
  or as CH2‐THF and o‐acetylserine. CH2‐THF supplies one‐carbon (C1) units to the
  C1 metabolism and synthesis of o‐acetylserine by SAT3 draws from the serine pool
  in the mitochondria for cystine synthesis which is the merging point of C, N and
  S metabolism. CO2 and NH3 utilization also channel into the shikimic acid pathway
  for aromatic amino acid and secondary metabolite biosynthesis. The overexpressed
  enzymes PEPC, AspAT and GS were represented by red arrows. The metabolites found
  to be higher in PAG‐coexpressing lines were represented by yellow and grey boxes
  with red arrows. 2PG, 2‐phosphoglycolate; 3PG, 3‐phosphoglycerate; ADPG, ADP‐glucose;
  Asp, aspartic acid; AspAT, aspartate aminotransferase; CH2‐THF, methyltetrahydrofolate;
  DAHP, 3‐deoxy‐D‐arabino‐heptulosonate‐7‐phosphate; FRK, fructokinase; GDC, glycine
  decarboxylase; Gln, glutamine; Glu, glutamate; GOGAT, glutamate‐oxoglutarate aminotransferase;
  GS, glutamine synthetase; HXK, hexokinase; Inv, invertases; OAA, oxaloacetic acid;
  OAS‐TL, o‐acetylserine(thiol)lyase; PEPC, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase; PEP,
  phosphoenolpyruvate; PKP, pyruvate kinase; SAT, serine acetyltransferase; SHMT,
  serine hydroxymethyltransferase; SuSy, sucrose synthases
