caption: 'The functions of the cGAS-STING pathway in PD. Nuclear and mitochondrial
  DNA leakage, induced by α-synuclein aggregates, ROS, and excessive Mn2+ levels,
  activates the cGAS pathway. This activation leads to the production of cGAMP, which
  then triggers STING-dependent signaling. In microglia, the cGAS-STING pathway is
  activated, and α-synuclein aggregates enhance this response by increasing the expression
  of STING. This process promotes the release of pro-inflammatory factors. Furthermore,
  STING can induce the lipidation of GABARAP, which subsequently activates LRRK2.
  The hyperactivity of LRRK2 limits the lysosomal degradative capacity, resulting
  in damage to noncanonical autophagy. In astrocytes, STING binds to the transcription
  factor YY1, effectively trapping it in the cytoplasm. This action derepresses LCN2,
  a potent pro-senescence effector. As a result, this triggers astrocyte senescence,
  characterized by cell cycle arrest marked by the upregulation of p16ink4a and p21,
  disruption of the nuclear envelope evidenced by the loss of lamin B1, and a significant
  increase in SA-β-gal activity. The activated microglia can also trigger astrocyte
  reactivity, which jointly exacerbates the death of dopaminergic neurons. LCN2: lipocalin-2;
  LRRK2: leucine-rich repeat kinase 2; PD: Parkinson’s disease; ROS: reactive oxygen
  species; SA-β-gal: senescence-associated β-galactosidase; SASP: senescence-associated
  secretory phenotype. The figure was created with BioRender.com'
