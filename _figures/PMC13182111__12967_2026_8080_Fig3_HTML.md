caption: Mechanism network of inflammatory regulation myokines in OA progression.
  This figure illustrates the signaling networks and effects of five key inflammation-regulating
  myokines (IL-6, IL-15, IL-10, Metrnl, and Fractalkine) in osteoarthritis (OA). Arrows
  (→) indicate activation or promotion, while perpendicular lines (⊥) indicate inhibition
  or blockade. Solid lines represent direct upstream effects, and dashed lines represent
  indirect downstream effects. IL-6 accelerates OA pathology by activating the JAK2/STAT3
  signaling pathway, enhancing inflammatory cytokine release. IL-15 stimulates monocytes/macrophages
  and neutrophils to trigger pro-inflammatory factor release, creating a pro-inflammatory
  microenvironment. IL-10 exerts anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and chondroprotective
  effects as an anti-apoptotic cytokine. Metrnl alleviates chondrocyte inflammation
  by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/NF-κB pathway, while exerting protective effects by blocking
  the NLRP3/caspase-1/GSDMD signaling pathway. Fractalkine promotes fibroblast migration
  and inflammatory factor release by activating MAPK (p38/JNK/ERK) and Akt signaling
  pathways, exacerbating inflammation and driving OA progression
