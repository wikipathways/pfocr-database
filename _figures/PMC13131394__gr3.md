caption: 'Adrenergic receptor activation by catecholamines in the tumor microenvironment
  and the downstream activation of various signaling pathways: Under chronic stress,
  the catecholamines epinephrine (Epi) and norepinephrine (NE) released by the CNS
  and local nerves activate β2-AR signaling. The ligand binding to the G-protein-coupled
  receptor activates adenylyl cyclase, converting ATP into cAMP (second messenger)
  and triggering PKA to phosphorylate multiple target proteins. This receptor activation
  modulates various cellular processes, including initiation, proliferation, invasion,
  angiogenesis, cell motility, and apoptosis. Stimulation of the JAK/STAT3 pathway
  activated via β2-AR promotes the transition of epithelial mesenchymal phenotype.
  Adenylate cyclase activation via β2-AR increases vascular endothelial growth factor
  receptor (VEGFR) expression and VEGF secretion. MMPs are key in destabilizing the
  extracellular matrix and aiding tumor metastasis via the ERK 1/2 pathway. FAK activation
  modulates cell trafficking and motility through cytoskeletal dynamics and cellular
  resistance to apoptosis (e.g., anoikis). CREB and ATF, transcription factors phosphorylated
  by PKA, bind to the promoter region of target genes regulating apoptosis, proliferation,
  and cell differentiation. The reduced sensitivity of cancer cells to apoptosis is
  due to β2-AR/cAMP-mediated phosphorylation and inactivation of the pro-apoptotic
  protein BAD. ATP: Adenosine triphosphate, PKA: protein kinase A JAK: Janus kinase,
  STAT3: signal transducer and activator of transcription, VEGFR: vascular endothelial
  growth factor receptor, VEGF: vascular endothelial growth factor receptor, ERK 1/2,
  FAK: Focal adhesion kinase, CREB: cAMP response element-binding protein, ATF: Activating
  transcription factor, BAD: Bcl-2 associated agonist of cell death, MMPs: matrix
  metalloproteinases. Created with BioRender.com'
