caption: 'This diagram illustrates the two primary mechanisms by which estrogen (E)
  exerts its diverse physiological effects on the cell: (1) Genomic Signaling (Left):
  Estrogen is a lipophilic hormone that enters the cell and binds to intracellular
  estrogen receptors (ERs, specifically ER‐α and ER‐β). The activated E‐ER complex
  then translocates to the nucleus to regulate gene transcription. This occurs via
  two methods: A Direct Pathway, where the E‐ER complex binds directly to estrogen
  response elements (EREs) in the DNA; and an Indirect Pathway, where the E‐ER complex
  interacts with other transcription factors (TF) to influence gene expression without
  direct ERE involvement. These actions are relatively slow, leading to protein synthesis.
  (2) Non‐Genomic Signaling (Right): This pathway involves more rapid actions, independent
  of gene transcription and protein synthesis. Estrogen interacts with membrane‐bound
  receptors, such as G protein‐coupled estrogen receptor 1 (GPER1) or subpopulations
  of ERs located on the plasma membrane. This binding initiates rapid signaling cascades,
  influencing pathways like phospholipase C/protein C/phosphatidylinositol, leading
  to the rapid production of intracellular secondary messengers (e.g., Ca2+ release)
  and the modulation of membrane‐based ion channels'
