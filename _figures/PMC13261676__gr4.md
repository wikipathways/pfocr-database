caption: Outlook for transcending the JA-mediated GDT.(A) Three proposed pathways
  for transcending the JA-mediated GDT.(B) Enhanced chloroplast functions, including
  increased electron transport/CO2 assimilation rates (DES1–PetC), improved chloroplast
  protein translation (TuA/B), and promoted chloroplast development (IPT), collectively
  address resource limitations, thereby overcoming the JA-mediated GDT.(C) Manipulation
  of DAMP (e.g., GSH and ATP) content/signaling can potentially overcome the JA-mediated
  GDT. Under normal conditions, iGSH and iATP positively regulate nutrient assimilation
  and photosynthesis to promote growth. Under stress conditions, eGSH and eATP activate
  Ca2+ signals and JA biosynthesis to derepress MYCs, thereby alleviating repression
  of MYC-regulated defense responses.(D) Remodeling of key regulators (e.g., MYC2,
  JAR1, and fou2) in JA signaling or biosynthesis pathways remains a largely unexplored
  frontier in crops. Emerging remodeling strategies such as inducible expression,
  genome editing, and protein remodeling enable precise tuning of gene expression
  and protein activity to achieve this goal.DES1, DELAYED SENESCENCE 1; PetC, RIESKE
  FeS PROTEIN; TuA/B, chloroplast elongation factors; IPT, ISOPENTENYL TRANSFERASE;
  CK, cytokinin; DAMPs, damage-associated molecular patterns; iGSH/eGSH, intracellular/extracellular
  glutathione; iATP/eATP, intracellular/extracellular ATP; JAR1, JASMONATE RESISTANT
  1; fou2, FATTY ACID OXYGENATION UPREGULATED 2. Solid lines indicate direct relationships;
  dashed lines indicate indirect or proposed pathways; blunt-ended arrows indicate
  inhibition
