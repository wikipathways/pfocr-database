caption: 'Temporal heterogeneity of AT2 cells during lung injury, repair, and fibrosis.
  (a) Inflammation Phase: AT2 cells play a central role in regulating inflammation
  by interacting with immune cells such as macrophages and CD4+ TRM cells. They release
  CCL2 to recruit macrophages and use CD200–CD200R signaling to control inflammation.
  (b) Repair Phase: Early-stage AT2 cells proliferate via Wnt signaling. As repair
  progresses, later-stage AT2 cells differentiate into AT1 cells through Notch and
  BMP pathways. The transcription factor Tfcp2l1 is key during this stage, initially
  suppressing AT2 proliferation and later promoting both proliferation and differentiation
  at more advanced stages of repair. (c) Fibrosis Phase: In the fibrosis phase, persistent
  IL-1β, TGF-β, and other injury-related signals drive AT2 cells into a damage-associated
  transitional state. In this state, AT2 cells secrete Areg and TGF-, which stimulate
  fibrosis by activating myofibroblasts. Additionally, the downregulation of EGF and
  BMP signaling disrupts AT2 differentiation, leading to transdifferentiation into
  basal-like cells. This figure illustrates the heterogeneity of AT2 cell behavior
  throughout the stages of lung injury, repair, and fibrosis. AT2 alveolar type 2
  cells, TRM tissue-resident memory, CCL2 C-C motif chemokine ligand 2, CD200R CD200
  receptor, BMP bone morphogenetic protein, TGF-β transforming growth factor beta,
  IL-1β interleukin 1 beta, EGF epidermal growth factor, Areg amphiregulin'
