caption: A work model of miR399d in tomato Pi absorption with or without AMS. Under
  high‐Pi conditions, AMS is vigorously suppressed. Plants are dependent primarily
  on the direct Pi uptake pathway to obtain Pi from the environment. To maintain an
  optimal Pi uptake, the expression of miR399 is suppressed, which leads to increased
  accumulation of PHO2 protein and degradation of PT1 Pi transporter involved in the
  direct Pi uptake pathway (yellow cells within the left panel). This is crucial for
  plants to avoid phosphorus toxicity caused by the uptake of excessive phosphorus.
  Under low‐Pi conditions without the presence of the AMF in the environment, Pi deficiency
  triggers the accumulation of miR399, which, in turn, decreases the abundance of
  PHO2 transcripts via cleavage. Weakened translation of PHO2 reduces the ubiquitination
  of PT1 and permits the accumulation of Pi transporters, ultimately increasing Pi
  acquisition (blue cells within the upper right panel). When AMF is present under
  the low‐Pi conditions, the Pi deficiency can induce the establishment of AMS. At
  the early stage of AMS, plants are still dependent primarily on the direct Pi uptake
  pathway by activating the expression of miR399. As the symbiosis progresses, the
  AMS‐mediated indirect phosphate uptake pathway provides most Pi needed by plants,
  resulting in elevated intracellular phosphate concentration, which subsequently
  suppresses miR399d expression and the downregulation of the entire direct Pi uptake
  pathway (blue cells within the lower right panel)
