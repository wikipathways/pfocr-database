caption: The role of ROS in bone resorption and formation. Excessive ROS promotes
  bone resorption (A) and impairs bone formation (B) by modulating multiple osteogenic
  signaling pathways. In osteoclasts, ROS activate the RANKL–RANK signaling cascade,
  promoting differentiation and resorptive function, and induce autophagy to further
  enhance bone degradation. In osteoblasts, ROS causes mitochondrial dysfunction and
  apoptosis, sequesters β-catenin to suppress osteogenic differentiation, and upregulates
  RANKL while downregulating osteoprotegerin (OPG), indirectly augmenting osteoclastogenesis.
  In bone marrow stromal cells (BMSCs), ROS drive adipogenic commitment and trigger
  a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), thereby disrupting normal lineage
  differentiation and regenerative capacity. Collectively, ROS-induced dysregulation
  of the coupling between bone formation and resorption compromises the bone healing
  process. HSC, hematopoietic stem cell; RANKL, receptor activator of nuclear factor
  κB ligand; TRAF6, TNF receptor-associated factor 6; PI3K, phosphoinositol-3 kinase;
  MAPK, mitogen-activated protein kinase; IKK complex, IκB kinase complex; NF-κB,
