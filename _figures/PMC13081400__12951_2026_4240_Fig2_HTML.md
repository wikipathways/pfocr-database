caption: (i) Role of FA in OA involves the excess SFAs and omega-6 PUFAs in the bloodstream,
  which can activate NF-κB signaling pathway, either directly or indirectly, by attaching
  to specific receptors. This activation leads to an increase in the expression of
  Prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase (PTGS) or COX, IL-1, MMP13, ADAMTS5, and COL10,
  worsening the condition of OA. In contrast, MUFAs and omega-3 (n-3) PUFAs can reduce
  the production of proinflammatory molecules, like TNF-α and IL-1, while also inhibiting
  the activation of the MAPK and NF-κB signaling pathways. (ii) Role of leptin and
  adiponectin in OA. The white adipose tissue releases adiponectin and leptin, which
  attach to their respective receptors, triggering the activation of the MAPK and
  NF-κB signaling pathways, leading to increased expression of IL-1β, TNF-α, MMP-2,
  and MMP-9. These proinflammatory cytokines and MMPs affect articular cartilage,
  subchondral bone, synovial membrane, and other regions, worsening the OA condition,
