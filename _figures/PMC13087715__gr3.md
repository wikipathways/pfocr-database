caption: 'Metabolic signatures of the peritoneal microenvironment are associated with
  peritoneal inflammation and with perturbation of glucose metabolism and immunometabolism.
  (a) Principal component analysis of metabolic signatures in patients with high versus
  low peritoneal inflammation. (b) Metabolic pathway enrichment (FDR < 0.01) with
  significantly changed metabolites (n = 154, P < 0.05, linear mixed-model) in patients
  with high peritoneal inflammation. (c) Pathway enrichment network of shared metabolites
  (pathways connected if shared metabolites exceed 25% of the number of their combined
  metabolite sets); P value: circle color; circle size: fold enrichment ratio (hits/expected).
  (d) Activation network analysis of highly changed metabolites in the peritoneal
  microenvironment (n = 136, q < 0.05) with high peritoneal inflammation. (e) Activation
  network analysis of highly changed metabolites (q < 0.05) in patients with high
  cell function (high ex vivo–stimulated IL-6 release, stratified at median). (d and
  e) Oval shape: significantly changed metabolites; green: reduced abundance; red:
  increased abundance; diamonds: affected proteins; blue: inhibited; violet: activated;
  orange lines: findings inconsistent with downstream molecule state (per IPA data
  base). Bile acids: (GCDCA, glycochenodeoxycholic acid), (LCA, lithocholic acid),
  (TCA, taurocholic acid); CE, cholesterol esters; Cer, ceramides; DHA, dehydroascorbic
  acid; LPC, lysophosphatidylcholine; PC, phosphatidylcholines; PE. phosphatidylethanolamines;
  SM, sphingomyelins. (f) Correlation of peritoneal cell function and daily peritoneal
  glucose exposure (g/d), P = 0.0001, linear mixed-model'
