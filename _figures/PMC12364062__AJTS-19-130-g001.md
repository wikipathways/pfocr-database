caption: The activation of the coagulation system starts with the activity of tissue
  factor. At the onset, the TF-FVIIa complex activates Factor IX and Factor X. The
  TF-FVIIa-FXa complex produces a small amount of thrombin but is rapidly inhibited
  by the TFPI. The result of the initial stage is only the production of a small amount
  of thrombin. In the amplification phase, the small amount of thrombin produced at
  the initial stage results in the activation of platelets and conversion of factors
  XI, V, and VIII to activated factors. Activated platelet recalled at the site of
  injury and exposed using membrane phospholipids provide a procoagulant surface to
  continue the coagulation process in the developmental phase. In the developmental
  stage, IXa and FVIIIa factors on the surface of platelets form a complex of tyrosine
  which converts factor X to Xa. Then, FXa, as well as FVa and calcium, forms the
  prothrombinase complex, which rapidly activates the prothrombin to the thrombin
  and generating a large amount of thrombin at this stage leads to the formation of
  a fibrin network. TFPI = Tissue factor pathway inhibitor
