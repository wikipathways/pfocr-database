caption: Colonic pH varies substantially between individuals due to host, microbial,
  and dietary factors4. Diets high in fermentable fiber increase colonic carbohydrate
  availability, enhance microbial SCFA and lactate production, and contribute to a
  lowering of colonic pH compared with low-fiber diets. Lower colonic pH (approximately
  <6.5) represses tryptophanase gene expression, thereby suppressing the conversion
  of tryptophan to indole and increasing its availability for alternative microbial
  pathways, such as Stickland fermentation, which yields indolelactic acid (ILA) and
  indolepropionic acid (IPA). Conversely, higher colonic pH (approximately >6.5) activates
  the indole pathway, increasing indole production while reducing tryptophan availability
  for microbes using alternative metabolic routes. Note that the pH cutoff value of
  6.5 shown in the figure is approximate and not a strict cutoff based on our experiments.
  We expect a gradual change in tryptophanase gene expression with changes in pH.
  In addition, increased carbohydrate availability can directly inhibit the indole
  pathway via carbon catabolite repression, further shifting tryptophan metabolic
  fluxes in favor of alternative pathways46. Figure was created with BioRender.com
