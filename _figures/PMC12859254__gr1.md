caption: 'Mechanisms of malignancy-associated hypercalcemia. (A) Humoral hypercalcemia:
  PTHrP interacts with PTHrP type 1 receptor on bone osteocytes, leading to the activation
  of RANKL on osteoblast, enhancing RANKL-RANK signaling, and activating mature osteoclasts.
  PTHrP also acts on renal tubular cells like PTH, leading to hypercalcemia. (B) Osteolytic
  process: along with the destruction of bone osteoblasts by malignant cells, recent
  research suggests that interaction of RANKL/RANK, release of interleukin-1β, -6,
  -8, and -11, transforming growth factor (TGF)-β and tumor necrosis factor-α by malignant
  cells may lead to excessive osteoclast activation, resulting in enhanced bone resorption.
  Furthermore, TGF-β release can activate Hedgehog pathway, leading to downstream
  activation of Gli family proteins, local expression of PTHrP, and bone resorption.
  Specific malignancies, such as multiple myeloma can release factors like dickkopf-1
  (DKK1), a Wnt signaling pathway inhibitor that suppresses osteoblast activity, potentially
  contributing to hypercalcemia. Ironically, local bone destruction can release factors
  that further stimulate tumor growth, creating a vicious cycle. PTH, parathyroid
  hormone; PTHrP, parathyroid hormone-releasing peptide; RANK, receptor activator
  of nuclear factor kappa-B; RANKL, receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand;
  DKK1, Dickkopf-1; IL, interleukin; TNF, tissue necrosis factor; TGF, transforming
  growth factor'
