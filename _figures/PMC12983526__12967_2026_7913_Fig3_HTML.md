caption: In the digestive system, bitter stimulation of TAS2Rs expressed in intestinal
  enteroendocrine cells triggers intracellular Ca²⁺ release, leading to the secretion
  of ACh or CCK. Acetylcholine activates the sensory neurons of the trigeminal nerve
  that contain nociceptors, inducing neurogenic inflammation, which acts as a protective
  mechanism against pathogens. Bitter receptor agonists (DB) stimulate TAS2Rs expressed
  in Paneth cells of the jejunal crypts in obese individuals, leading to the release
  of antimicrobial peptides (such as α-defensin 5 and REG3A) to combat pathogens.
  Parasites activate TAS2Rs expressed in tuft cells of the small intestine, triggering
  an IL-25-driven positive feedback loop through downstream TAS2R signaling pathways.
  This results in the amplification of ILC2 and tuft and goblet cell proliferation
  driven by IL-13
