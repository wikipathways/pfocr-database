caption: A proposed working model for the co‐domestication of cold tolerance and female
  flower development mediated by CsEIN2. In this conceptual framework, cold stress
  triggers the rapid production of ethylene. In cucumber accessions carrying the CsEIN2
  CTC allele, ethylene quickly accumulates and activates the ethylene signalling pathway.
  Three elite variants in the C‐terminal end of the CsEIN2 (CsEIN2C) protein are proteolytically
  cleaved and translocated to the nucleus, leading to high binding affinity with CsEIN3
  protein to accelerate CsCBF2 and CsERF31 expression, thus enhancing cold tolerance
  and producing more female flowers. In cucumber accessions carrying the CsEIN2 TCG
  allele, the dysfunctional CsEIN2C is unable to interact with CsEIN3 in the nucleus;
  therefore, CsCBF2 and CsERF31 expression is low, resulting in an apparent cold‐sensitive
  and fewer female flower phenotype. The increasing prevalence of accessions with
  CsEIN2 CTC and the decreasing frequency of those with CsEIN2 TCG from low latitudes
  to high latitudes suggest that CsEIN2 plays a significant role in the adaptation
  of cucumber to higher latitudes
