caption: Salmonella infection activates toll like receptors (TLRs) and other pathogen-associated
  molecular pattern receptors causing strong inflammation. A. Lipoprotein components
  of the cell wall activate TLR2. B. lipopolysaccharides activate TLR4. C. CpG oligodeoxynucleotides
  of DNA activate TLR9. D. Bacterial flagellin activates TLR5. E. The peptidoglycan
  components activate the intracellular NOD receptors. Activation of TLRs and NOD
  receptors triggers a cascade of reactions that lead to the activation of NF-κB,
  interferon regulatory factors, and activator protein-1, resulting in the release
  of proinflammatory cytokines [122,123]. The various SPI-1 T3SS effectors, which
  are injected into host cells during Salmonella invasion, augment the inflammation.
  F. The SPI-1 T3SS effectors SopE, SopE2, and SopB activate Cdc42. Activation of
  Cdc42 leads to the formation of a PAK1/TRAF6/TAK1 complex and the phosphorylation
  of PAK1 which in turn activates NF-κB leading to the production of pro-inflammatory
  cytokines [113]. G. SipA activates NOD1/NOD2 signaling pathway causing subsequent
  RIP2-mediated production of NF-κB-dependent inflammatory responses [115]. H. SspH2
  augments NOD1 mediated NF-κB activation [114]. I. SipB activates caspase-1 and induces
  the release of IL-18 from DCs and macrophages. IL-18 induces T cells to produce
  IFN-γ [116]. J. SopA contributes to the host cell invasion and augments the inflammatory
  response [111]. K. SopF activates PDK1 to phosphorylate RSK which downregulates
  caspase 8. Inactivation of caspase 8 inhibits apoptosis and pyroptosis while promoting
  necroptosis [117]
