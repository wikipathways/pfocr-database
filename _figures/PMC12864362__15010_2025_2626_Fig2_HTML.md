caption: Recognition of Human Metapneumovirus (HMPV) by Pattern Recognition Receptors
  and Modulation of Interferon Response. HMPV is detected by various receptors, and
  its ability to modulate the interferon (IFN) response is influenced by viral proteins.
  Pattern recognition receptors in the lower respiratory tract, such as RIG-I and
  MDA-5, detect HMPV replication through two primary pathways. The first pathway relies
  on RIG-I-like receptors (RLRs), including RIG-I and MDA-5. These receptors initiate
  signaling through interactions with MAVS, resulting in the release of type I interferons
  (IFNs). Additionally, pro-inflammatory cytokines and type III IFNs are produced
  via the activation of IRF3 and NF-κB, respectively. RLRs are activated by viral
  replication products, such as cytosolic double-stranded RNAs (dsRNAs) and uncapped
  5′-triphosphates found in genomic RNA [41, 115]. The second pathway involves Toll-like
  receptors (TLRs), specifically TLR3, TLR4, and TLR7, which recognize viral proteins.
  Activation of TLR3 (in most cells) and TLR7 triggers type I IFN secretion via the
  TIR domain-containing adaptor-inducing IFN-β (TRIF)/IRF3-dependent and MyD88/IRF7-dependent
  pathways, respectively [116]. However, the G protein of HMPV impairs TLR4 signaling.
  HMPV proteins play a significant role in the inhibition of viral recognition. The
  G and P proteins (in HMPV strain B1) prevent the detection of viral RNA by blocking
  the RIG-I- and MAVS-dependent signaling pathways. Additionally, the M2-2 and SH
  proteins are critical virulence factors that suppress signaling through both pathways
  [41]. The image was created using BioRender.com
