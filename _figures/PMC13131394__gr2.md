caption: 'Neuro-endocrine axis in chronic stress-induced tumor growth: Chronic stress
  induces the release of catecholamines, which bind to adrenergic receptors on cancer
  cells. Two primary sources of catecholamine release include the sympathetic nervous
  system, the adrenal glands, and the local release of catecholamines by the neurons
  innervating the tumor. The nerve-cancer crosstalk involves the binding of catecholamines
  to β2-AR, which triggers cAMP activation of key oncogenic pathways PI3/AKT, MAPK,
  and EGFR. Additionally, nerve growth factors released by CAFs in the TME promote
  axonogenesis and nerve recruitment, further facilitating tumor progression and metastasis.
  Cancer cells continuously provide positive feedback to neurons to promote neurogenesis
  by secreting neurotrophic growth factors, further increasing the tumor innervation
  by nerves. cAMP: cyclic adenosine monophosphate, NE: norepinephrine, Epi: epinephrine,
  CAFs: cancer-associated fibroblasts, PI3: phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase, AKT: protein
  kinase B, MAPK: mitogen-activated protein kinase, TME: tumor microenvironment and
  EGFR: epidermal growth factor receptor. Created with BioRender.com'
