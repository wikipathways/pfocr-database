caption: Signaling pathways related to the mechanism of action of DAMPs after TBI.
  Following TBI, various nerve cells—including neurons, microglia, and astrocytes—endogenously
  release a range of DAMP proteins into the extracellular space. (a) These DAMPs can
  bind to specific receptors, such as membrane-bound TLRs. TLR2 and TLR4 are recognized
  by intracellular proteins (e.g. HMGB1, HSPs, and histones) as well as extracellular
  matrix components (e.g. LMW-HA). Free mtDNA, outside the cell, is delivered into
  the cell by endosomes through endocytosis, subsequently binding to TLR9 on the endosomal
  membrane. Upon activation by these DAMPs, TLRs initiate downstream signaling cascades
  via the adaptor protein MyD88. MyD88 then activates MAPKs and IKKs, leading to the
  production of inflammatory cytokines by nuclear transcription factors, such as AP-1
  and NFκB; additionally, after HSP binds to CD40, it activates IKK, thereby inducing
  the NFκB inflammation-related pathway. (b) Exogenous HMGB1, S100B, and Aβ can drive
  pro-inflammatory responses by binding to the RAGE, subsequently initiating multiple
  signaling pathways, including the MAPK and JAK–STAT pathways. These pathways further
  influence various transcription factors, including NF-κB, AP-1, and STAT3, promoting
  the expression of pro-inflammatory genes and regulating cell migration, proliferation,
  and apoptosis; within the TREM family, TREM1 and TREM2 are key receptors activated
  by HMGB1 and HSP, which leads to further activation of the AKT–NF-κB pathway through
  PI3K or the NF-κB or AP-1 pathways via MAPK. LMW-HA activates the PI3K–AKT–NF-κB
  signaling pathway by binding to CD44, promoting neuroinflammation. (c) When nuclear
  DNA is damaged or exogenous DNA fragments are transported into cells via EVs, AIM2
  is activated, initiating the assembly of AIM2 inflammasomes and inducing nonclassical
  pyroptosis pathways. Excessive tissue damage leads to the leakage of DNA fragments
  into the cytoplasm, including exogenous mtDNA that enters the cell via endocytosis
  and exogenous DNA that enters via EVs. These fragments can all activate the cGAS
  signaling pathway, promoting the production of the second messenger cGAMP that binds
  to the STING, located in the ER. STING then activates TBK1 and IKK on the Golgi
  apparatus, leading to the production of interferons and inflammatory cytokines through
  transcription factors IRF3 and NF-κB. (d) The NLRP3 inflammasome can be activated
  by various DAMPs, including CL from mitochondria, extracellular Aβ, and S100B, which
  is transported into cells via EVs, resulting in the secretion of IL-1β and IL-18
  through GSDMD and inducing classic pyroptosis; the activation of P2X7R by extracellular
  ATP triggers the formation of nonselective pores, leading to calcium influx and
  potassium efflux, which may promote NLRP3 inflammasome activation; the color of
  the arrows corresponds to that of the molecule from which the arrow originates.
  TLRs toll-like receptors, HMGB1 high-mobility group box protein 1, HSPs heat shock
  proteins, LMW-HA low-molecular-weight hyaluronan, mtDNA mitochondrial DNA, MyD88
  myeloid differentiation primary response 88, MAPKs mitogen-activated protein kinases,
  IKKs IκB kinases, AP-1 activator protein 1, NFκB nuclear factor κB, EVs extracellular
  vesicles, AIM2 absent in melanoma 2, CL cardiolipin, Aβ β-amyloid protein, GSDMD
  gasdermin D, cGAS cyclic guanosine monophosphate–adenylate synthase, STING stimulator
  of interferon genes, ER endoplasmic reticulum, TBK1 TANK-binding kinase 1, RAGE
  receptor of advanced glycation endproducts, NLRP3 NLR Family Pyrin Domain Containing
