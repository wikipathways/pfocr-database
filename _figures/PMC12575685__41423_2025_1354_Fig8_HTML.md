caption: The NLRP12 inflammasome. In mouse macrophages, the NLRP12 inflammasome is
  activated following infection with Yersinia pestis or Plasmodium chabaudi. The combination
  of the heme-containing component of hemoglobin with pathogen-associated molecular
  patterns (PAMPs) or the cytokine TNF activates Toll-like receptors (TLR2 and TLR4),
  leading to depletion of cytoplasmic NAD⁺. NAD⁺ loss upregulates the innate immune
  sensor NLRC5 and induces mitochondrial stress, resulting in reactive oxygen species
  (ROS) production and the assembly of a PANoptosome complex containing NLRP12, NLRC5,
  NLRP3, ASC, caspase-1, caspase-8, and RIPK3. This complex drives PANoptosis, a lytic
  inflammatory cell death pathway mediated by gasdermins, which disrupt the plasma
  membrane and release damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)
