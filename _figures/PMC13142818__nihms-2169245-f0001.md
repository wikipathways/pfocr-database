caption: a, In oral melanoma and head and neck tumours, reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  produced within malignant cells lead to the phosphorylation of JUN, activating the
  transcription of nerve growth factor (NGF). NGF is then secreted and binds to its
  receptor tropomyosin receptor kinase A (TRKA) on tumour-innervating nociceptor neurons,
  promoting peri-tumoural neurogenesis, increased transcription of calcitonin-related
  polypeptide-α (CALCA; the gene encoding calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP))
  and secretion of CGRP. CGRP then binds to the receptor activity-modifying protein
  1 (RAMP1)–calcitonin gene-related peptide type 1 receptor (CALCRL) receptor complex
  on malignant cells, promoting tumour growth and cytoprotective autophagy via the
  mTOR–Raptor pathway. Blocking the CGRP–RAMP1 axis using the US Food and Drug Administration
  (FDA)-approved drug rimegepant reverses these effects77. b, Melanoma cells secrete
  the neuron-activating molecule secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (SLPI), which
  leads to the release of CGRP. CGRP, in turn, activates RAMP1 on CD8+ T cells, driving
  the exhaustion of effector T cells, as evidenced by the upregulation of immune checkpoint
  molecules such as PD1, lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG3) and T cell immunoglobulin
  mucin receptor 3 (TIM3)64,71. c, In melanoma, sensory/sympathetic denervation (SSD)
  recruits both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, promoting the formation of tertiary lymphoid
  structures (TLS) and enhancing antitumour immunity63. Intratumoural high endothelial
  venules are specialized blood vessels resembling those found in lymphoid organs,
  and have a crucial role in facilitating lymphocyte infiltration into the tumour
  microenvironment (TME). SSD promotes the generation of intratumoural high endothelial
  venules, thereby increasing the trafficking of inflammatory effector T cells to
  the tumour. Collectively, these events foster the emergence of structures similar
  to TLS in the TME, resulting in more durable antitumour immune responses. d, In
  breast cancer, substance P interacts with its receptor neurokinin 1 receptor (NK1R)
  to stimulate cancer cell proliferation and lymph node metastasis. Apoptotic NK1Rhigh
  cancer cells release single-stranded RNA, which activates Toll-like receptor 7 (TLR7)
  on other malignant cells and, in turn, induces a pro-metastatic gene profile leading
  to growth and metastasis, leading to decreased survival83. The NK1R inhibitor aprepitant
  can reverse these effects. Neuro-targeted therapies — including RAMP1 antagonists
  (for example, rimegepant or BIBN4096 (also known as olcegepant)), neuron silencing
  agents (for example, the quaternary (lidocaine-derived) local anaesthetic QX-314,
  botulinum neurotoxin type A (BoNT/A) and NK1R inhibitors (aprepitant)) — show promise
  in reducing cancer-induced neuro-immune interactions, curbing tumour growth and
  metastasis while improving survival outcomes (panels b and d). IFNγ, interferon-γ
