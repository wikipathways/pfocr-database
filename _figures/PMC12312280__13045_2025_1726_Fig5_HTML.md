caption: Regulation of necroptosis by histone modification in cancer. In primary breast
  cancer, the promoter regions of RIPK3 and TNF-α are marked by G9a-mediated silencing
  modifications H3K9me2 and H3K27me3, leading to the inhibition of necroptosis. In
  contrast, in recurrent breast cancer, the promoter region of RIPK3 is enriched with
  activating modifications H3K4me3 and H3K9Ac, rendering these cells highly susceptible
  to necroptosis under cysteine deprivation conditions. However, in other contexts,
  elevated RIPK3 expression promotes cell proliferation through the regulation of
  P53 and YAP/TAZ pathways. In AML, inhibition of KDM2B increases the H3K4me3 level
  at the RIPK1 promoter, thereby enhancing RIPK1 expression and promoting necroptosis.
  Similarly, in gastric cancer, MAT2A-mediated accumulation of the methyl donor SAM
  also increases the H3K4me3 level within the RIPK1 promoter region, resulting in
  enhanced RIPK1 expression and necroptosis
