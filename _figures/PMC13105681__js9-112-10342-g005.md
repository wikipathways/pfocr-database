caption: Key roles of the RANKL/RANK/OPG, Wnt, and CXCL12/CXCR4 pathways in prostate
  cancer and bone metabolism. Activation of the Wnt pathway promotes osteoblast differentiation.
  DKK1 is highly expressed in advanced prostate cancer, where it inhibits the canonical
  Wnt pathway, potentially activating the non-canonical pathway and thereby driving
  prostate cancer progression. DKK1 interacts with CKAP4, further promoting prostate
  cancer progression. Anti-DKK1 antibodies represent potential therapeutic agents
  targeting these pathways. The RANKL/RANK/OPG pathway promotes osteoclast differentiation
  and accelerates prostate cancer progression. Corresponding therapeutic agents, such
  as the RANKL inhibitor denosumab, have been developed. CXCL12/CXCR4 activation enhances
  osteoclast activity and promotes prostate cancer progression. CXCR4 antagonists,
  such as AMD3100 and AMD3465, are potential therapeutic agents targeting this pathway.
