caption: Biological substrates underpinning cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic disease
  (CKM) and their associated biomarkers and therapeutic classes. CKM arises from interconnected
  biological processes including inflammation, fibrosis, renal tubular stress, microvascular
  injury, mitochondrial dysfunction, and endocrine crosstalk. Each domain represents
  a distinct yet overlapping pathway contributing to progressive multiorgan dysfunction.
  Representative circulating and urinary biomarkers are shown for each pathway, illustrating
  how molecular signatures may capture upstream disease activity before overt organ
  dysfunction becomes clinically apparent. Major cardiorenal therapeutic classes including
  glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs), sodium–glucose cotransporter-2
  inhibitors (SGLT2 inhibitors), renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) blockade
  and nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (nsMRAs) are mapped to their
  principal biological substrates, although these therapies exert effects across multiple
  CKM pathways. CKM, cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic disease; COL1A1, collagen type
  I alpha 1 chain; FGF21, fibroblast growth factor 21; GDF-15, growth differentiation
  factor-15; hsCRP, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein; IGFBP7, insulin-like growth
  factor binding protein-7; IL-6, interleukin-6; KIM-1, kidney injury molecule-1;
  MMP, matrix metalloproteinase; mtDNA, mitochondrial DNA; NGAL, neutrophil gelatinase-associated
  lipocalin; sST2, soluble suppression of tumorigenicity 2; TIMP, tissue inhibitor
  of metalloproteinases; TNF-α, tumor necrosis factor alpha; TNFR1/2, tumor necrosis
  factor receptors 1 and 2; vWF, von Willebrand factor
