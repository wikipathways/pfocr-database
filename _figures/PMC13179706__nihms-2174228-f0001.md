caption: 'Pathological and protective mechanisms regulating hepatocyte fate during
  H-IRI. H-IRI is initiated by interruption of blood flow and oxygen deprivation,
  which cause ATP depletion, mitochondrial damage, excessive generation of ROS, and
  lipid peroxidation, ultimately driving hepatocyte death. Distinct cell death programs
  are engaged, including apoptosis (relatively non-inflammatory), necrosis/necroptosis,
  and ferroptosis (highly pro-inflammatory). The stressors associated with H-IRI can
  also activate MAPK pathways, particularly JNK and p38, which respectively drive
  hepatocyte death and pyroptosis of inflammatory cells, thereby amplifying inflammatory
  signaling. In parallel, multiple cytoprotective pathways counteract injury. These
  include antioxidant defenses via the KEAP1–NRF2–HO-1 axis and GPX4-mediated suppression
  of lipid peroxidation; gaseous mediators such as carbon monoxide and nitric oxide
  that support microcirculatory and mitochondrial stability; HIF-mediated hypoxic
  adaptation; autophagy-mediated removal of damaged organelles; and pro-survival cascades
  such as cAMP–PKA and JAK–STAT3. The balance between injurious and protective signals
  determines hepatocyte survival versus death, shaping the overall severity of H-IRI.
  Abbreviations: GPX4, glutathione peroxidase 4; H-IRI, hepatic ischemia–reperfusion
  injury; HIF, hypoxia-inducible factor; HO-1, heme oxygenase-1; JAK, janus kinase;
  JNK, c-Jun N-terminal kinase; KEAP1, Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1; MAPK,
  mitogen-activated protein kinase; p38, p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase; NRF2,
  nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2; PKA, protein kinase A; ROS, reactive
  oxygen species; STAT3, signal transducer and activator of transcription 3. Created
