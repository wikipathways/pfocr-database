caption: Pathological mechanisms of mitochondrial dysfunction in NIBI. This figure
  illustrates how systemic infection drives mitochondrial dysfunction and brain injury
  in neonatal sepsis. Peripheral pathogens and endotoxins initially trigger exaggerated
  innate immune activation in neonatal monocytes and dendritic cells, leading to a
  cytokine storm with high levels of TNF‑α, IL‑1β, and IL‑6, as well as systemic inflammation.
  These signals, amplified by abundant TLR expression, promote mitochondrial damage
  and the release of mtROS and mtDNA, thereby establishing a vicious cycle of neuroinflammation
  and BBB breakdown. Within the neurovascular unit, endothelial mitochondrial dysfunction
  together with mtRos/mtdna‑mediated activation of NLRP3 and cGAS–STING signaling
  further disrupt tight junctions, increase BBB permeability, and drive production
  of IL‑1β and type I interferons. In the parenchyma, mitochondrial oxidative stress,
  respiratory chain inhibition, and excessive fission in neurons and oligodendrocytes
  result in loss of membrane potential, cytochrome c release, and apoptosis. Impaired
  mitophagy permits the accumulation of damaged mitochondria, sustaining mtROS and
  mtDNA release and reinforcing inflammatory injury. Together, these interconnected
  pathways link peripheral infection to BBB leakage, neuroinflammation, mitochondrial
  energy failure, and neuronal–oligodendroglial cell death in the developing brain
