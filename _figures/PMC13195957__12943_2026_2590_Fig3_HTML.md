caption: 'Cellular defense mechanisms against ferroptosis. This figure illustrates
  the major antioxidant systems that protect cells from ferroptosis by limiting lipid
  peroxidation: GPX4–GSH System: Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is the only enzyme
  capable of reducing harmful phospholipid hydroperoxides (PLOOH) to non-toxic phospholipid
  alcohols (PLOH). Its activity depends on glutathione (GSH), synthesized from glutamate,
  glycine, and cysteine. Cysteine is imported via the system xc⁻ (composed of SLC7A11/xCT
  and SLC3A2/CD98) or generated through the transsulfuration pathway. GPX4 exists
  in mitochondrial, cytoplasmic, and nuclear isoforms, each contributing to ferroptosis
  resistance in different compartments. CoQH₂ Systems: In GPX4-deficient conditions,
  cells rely on FSP1/ubiquinone (non-mitochondrial) and DHODH/CoQH₂ (mitochondrial)
  systems. FSP1 reduces ubiquinone (CoQ) to ubiquinol (CoQH₂) using N-myristoylation
  and NAD(P)H, acting as a potent lipid antioxidant. It also functions as a vitamin
  K reductase. DHODH, located on the outer surface of the mitochondrial inner membrane,
  catalyzes the oxidation of dihydroorotate (DHO) to orotate (OA), concomitantly reducing
  CoQ to CoQH₂, thereby inhibiting mitochondrial lipid peroxidation. GCH1–BH4 System:
  GTP cyclohydrolase-1 (GCH1) catalyzes the rate-limiting step in tetrahydrobiopterin
  (BH4) synthesis. BH4 acts as a cofactor for neurotransmitter synthesis (e.g., dopamine,
  melatonin) and also scavenges lipid radicals. It inhibits ferroptosis by promoting
  lipid remodeling and nitric oxide synthase uncoupling. Membrane Repair Mechanism:
  Lipid peroxidation leads to plasma membrane rupture. The ESCRT-III complex is recruited
  by Ca²⁺ influx through nanopores and facilitates membrane repair. Studies show that
  CHMP subunits of ESCRT-III accumulate in ferroptosis-induced cells, and their depletion
  increases ferroptotic sensitivity'
