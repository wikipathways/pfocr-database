caption: Transcending the JA-mediated GDT by expanding photosynthetic capacity and
  genetic signal rewiring.(A) Schematic for activation of JA signaling and associated
  growth inhibition. Under low-JA conditions, accumulated JAZ repressors interact
  with TPL–NINJA corepressors to suppress the transcriptional function of MYC TFs.
  Stress-induced JA-Ile facilitates the binding of JAZs to the SCFCOI1 complex, leading
  to their ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Liberated MYCs activate defense
  processes, including biosynthesis of defense compounds/proteins, repression of RCA,
  and induction of leaf senescence by upregulating chlorophyll catabolism genes (PAO,
  NYE1, and NYC1) and SAG expression. In addition, JA signaling interferes with growth-related
  auxin, BR, and GA pathways to inhibit root and rosette growth by suppressing PLT1/2,
  DWF4, and PIFs, respectively.(B) Transcending the JA-mediated GDT by increasing
  photosynthetic capacity. In the jazQ mutant, constitutive activation of JA signaling
  increases herbivore resistance and suppresses growth despite not altering photosynthetic
  capacity per unit leaf area. Introduction of a phyB mutation reshapes leaf architecture,
  enhancing overall canopy light capture and maintaining total photosynthetic output.
  Thus, the combination of jazQ and phyB promotes both robust growth and herbivore
  resistance.(C) Transcending the JA-mediated GDT by genetic rewiring of signaling.
  The hb34 mutation constitutively activates JA defense signaling by upregulating
  JA biosynthesis genes (AOC1, LOX2, and OPR3) and simultaneously repressing auxin-responsive
  genes (YUC8), resulting in JA-mediated growth suppression. Transgenic expression
  of YUC8 in the hb34-1 mutant fully rescues growth inhibition without compromising
  JA-dependent resistance.GDT, growth–defense trade-off; JA-Ile, jasmonoyl-isoleucine;
