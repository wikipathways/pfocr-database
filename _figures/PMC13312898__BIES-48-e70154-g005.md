caption: Pathways regulating MyoII activation and Crb expression/localization in the
  Drosophila salivary gland. Shown is the molecular pathway underlying localization
  of Crb to the plasma membrane for stabilization—resisting apical constriction and
  junction exchange—and of localized MyoII activation—driving apical constriction
  and junction exchange. Fork head (Fkh) and Huckebein (Hkb) are both required for
  high local concentrations of Fog in the region of the salivary gland undergoing
  apical constriction and neighbor exchange. Salivary gland expression of fog requires
  Fkh; whereas apical secretion of Fog is proposed to be regulated by Hkb through
  the downstream microtubule‐associated protein Klar. Fog binds to a proposed salivary
  gland GPCR to activate the downstream signaling pathway shown in the left. This
  pathway culminates in the phosphorylation and activation of MyoII. High levels of
  PM‐associated active MyoII bends the membrane leading to endocytosis of Crb and
  of AJ components—leading to a reduction in apical area. Salivary gland Crb protein
  levels are reduced in fkh mutants and salivary gland expression of arc requires
  Fkh. Arc is required for maximal localization of Crb to the plasma membrane (PM).
  Crb binds to Par6, which through downstream effectors, phosphorylates Rok to reduce
  its association with the PM, leading to reduced activated MyoII at the PM. Crb binds
  to and stabilizes junctions through its extracellular homophilic binding to Crb
  in neighboring salivary gland cells and through its association with the F‐Actin
  cytoskeleton, which is mediated by Crb FBD binding to the FERM domain in Moesin
  (Moe). Moe also binds βH‐Spectrin, linking Crb to the actin cytoskeleton. Pathway
  components and events that lead to apical constriction and cell rearrangement are
  shown in green. Pathway components and events that stabilize the PM—resisting apical
  constriction and cell rearrangement are shown in red
