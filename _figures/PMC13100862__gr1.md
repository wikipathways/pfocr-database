caption: 'Chemical structure and biosynthesis of itaconate. A: Itaconate is an endogenous
  metabolite produced in activated macrophages through the decarboxylation of cis-aconitate,
  a tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle intermediate. This reaction is catalyzed by ACOD1,
  also known as IRG1, localized in the mitochondrial matrix. Once synthesized, itaconate
  can undergo further metabolic transformation into itaconyl-CoA via CoA ligation,
  and subsequently into (S)-citramalyl-CoA, linking it to broader mitochondrial metabolic
  circuits. In vivo, itaconate is eliminated through urinary excretion and further
  metabolized into acetyl-CoA, mesaconate, and citramalate. B: Various pattern recognition
  receptors and cytokine signaling pathways jointly regulate the transcription of
  ACOD1.ACOD1: Aconitate decarboxylase 1; BoNT/A: Botulinum neurotoxin serotype A;
  BRD3: Bromodomain-containing protein 3; CDK2: Cyclin-dependent kinase 2; CpG: Cytosine–phosphate–Guanine
  oligodeoxynucleotide; CREB1: cAMP response element-binding protein 1; CREBBP: CREB-binding
  protein; DRD2: Dopamine receptor D2; IRF3: Interferon regulatory factor 3; IRG1:
  Immune-responsive gene 1; JUN: Jun proto-oncogene, AP-1 transcription factor subunit;
  LTA: Lipoteichoic acid; LPS: Lipopolysaccharide; MAPK8/ JNK1: Mitogen-activated
  protein kinase 8; MYD88: Myeloid differentiation primary response 88; RACK1: Receptor
  for activated C kinase 1; STING1: Stimulator of interferon response cGAMP interactor
  1; TCA: Tricarboxylic acid; TRIM21: Tripartite motif-containing protein 21; TLR:
  Toll-like receptors'
