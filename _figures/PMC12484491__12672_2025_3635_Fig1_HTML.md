caption: 'This figure illustrates the complex molecular mechanisms contributing to
  tamoxifen resistance in breast cancer. The key pathways and alterations include:
  Drug Efflux: Increased expression of efflux transporters leads to reduced intracellular
  concentrations of tamoxifen (A). Angiogenesis: Enhanced blood vessel formation supports
  tumor growth and survival, contributing to resistance (B). Epithelial-Mesenchymal
  Transition (EMT): Transition of epithelial cells to a mesenchymal phenotype promotes
  invasiveness and resistance to therapy (C). Autophagy: Dysregulated autophagy processes
  may allow cancer cells to survive under therapeutic stress (D). Abnormal Epigenetic
  Patterns: Changes in DNA methylation and histone modifications can silence tumor
  suppressor genes and activate oncogenes (E). Estrogen Receptor Defects: Mutations
  or alterations in estrogen receptor (ER) expression diminish tamoxifen efficacy
  (F). Overexpression of Oncogenes: Increased levels of oncogenes drive proliferation
  and survival, undermining the effects of tamoxifen (G). Low Expression of Tumor
  Suppressor Genes: Reduced expression of tumor suppressors weakens the cellular response
  to growth signals (H). Defects in Cell Cycle Regulation: Impaired cell cycle checkpoints
  can lead to uncontrolled cell division despite tamoxifen treatment (I). Defects
  in P450 Genes: Variations in cytochrome P450 enzymes affect tamoxifen metabolism
  and efficacy (J). Defects in Apoptosis Pathways: Alterations in apoptotic signaling
  pathways enable cancer cells to evade programmed cell death (K)'
