caption: 'Model of key changes in Wnt and TGFβ family pathways associated with regulating
  regenerative vs. fibrotic repair Based on the transcriptional analysis there is
  the potential for distinct regulation of canonical and noncanonical Wnt signaling
  and TGFβ family pathways associated with regenerative wound healing vs. fibrosis.
  (A) Key changes associated with canonical and noncanonical Wnt signaling: 1) Engagement
  of distinct Wnt ligands and FZD receptors to drive differential signaling in response
  to lens injury. 2) Discrete extracellular environments. During wound healing Wnt
  signaling may be dampened by an extracellular environment rich with Wnt inhibitors,
  including sFRPs and CER1. In contrast, fewer families of Wnt inhibitors are upregulated
  with fibrosis countered by increased expression of extracellular Wnt activators,
  RSPOs to enhance Wnt signaling, accompanied by increased accumulation of Dishevelled.
  3) Intracellular regulation of β-catenin ubiquitination may be upregulated with
  wound healing vs. fibrosis to also dampen canonical Wnt signaling due to increased
  expression of genes involved in β-catenin ubiquitination (RBX1, BTRC) (highlighted
  within the boxed region). Still, there is the potential to control Wnt signaling
  with fibrosis at the transcriptional level due to upregulation of CBY1 and TLE4
  (see box). 4) Lastly, there is the potential to specifically activate the Wnt–Ca2+
  pathway via NFAT1 and NFAT2 during fibrosis. (B) Key changes associated with the
  TGFβ superfamily: 1) Wound healing is accompanied by a potential increase in BMP
  signaling due to increased SMAD1 expression, as well as by an increase in expression
  of IDs. 2) Fibrosis is associated with the potential to augment TGFβ signaling due
  to increased expression of TGFβ signaling components: elevated TGF-β ligand and
  receptor expression and increased SMAD3 effector and thrombospondin 1 to activate
  TGFβ. Increased LTBP1 may promote increased storage of latent TGF-β binding to the
  extracellular matrix (ECM) for later activation. 3) Elevated INHBA and activin receptors
  may augment activin signaling pathways with fibrosis. 4) The BMP signaling pathway
  and environment for fibrosis is complex, involving increased expression of pathway
  elements to promote BMP signaling, including BMP ligands and receptors countered
  at the same time by an increased expression of extracellular inhibitors FST, CER1,
  and NOG2 to limit BMP signaling. Increased expression of BMP4 and BMP7, well known
  antagonists of TGFβ are also present, which may limit the amplitude of the fibrotic
  response. 5) Lastly, ID function, potentially downstream of TGFβ and/or BMP signaling
  was found to be required for both regenerative and fibrotic repair. Created using
  BioRender.com'
