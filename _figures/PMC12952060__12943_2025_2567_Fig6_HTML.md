caption: 'Ferroptosis-related crosstalk in the tumor microenvironment of PC. Left:
  Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), derived from pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs),
  bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), and endothelial-to-mesenchymal
  transition (EndoMT) related cells, represent a major component of the PC stroma.
  CAFs modulate ferroptosis sensitivity in PC cells through several mechanisms. They
  enhance cysteine availability via the transsulfuration pathway (CBS/CTH), strengthening
  antioxidant capacity. Additionally, CAF-derived exosomes deliver miR-3173-5p to
  suppress ACSL4 expression, thereby limiting iron release and LPO. TGF-β secreted
  by PC cells activates ATF4 signaling in CAFs, upregulating CBS/CTH transcription
  and establishing a positive feedback loop that further inhibits ferroptosis. Right:
  The tumor immune microenvironment in PC is shaped by both tumor-derived and stromal
  signals. Exosomes from KRASG12D-mutant cells, IL-34 secreted under obesity-related
  conditions, and DNA damage-driven activation of the cGAS-STING pathway promote M2
  macrophage infiltration and polarization, facilitating tumor initiation and progression.
  Conversely, ferroptosis PC cells release damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs),
  including HMGB1, CRT, and ATP. These signals promote dendritic cell (DC) maturation
  and antigen presentation, activate cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells and natural killer (NK)
  cells, and induce M1 macrophage polarization. The resulting antitumor inflammatory
  response also enhances CAF phagocytosis. Moreover, CXCL12 secreted by CAFs binds
  to CXCR4 on myeloid cells, promoting their recruitment and contributing to immune
  remodeling within the tumor microenvironment'
