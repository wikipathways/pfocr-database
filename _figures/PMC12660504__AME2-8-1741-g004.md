caption: Schematic overview of miRNA‐mediated regulation in key biological processes.
  This illustration provides a comprehensive view of the diverse functions of microRNAs,
  which govern various biological processes, highlighting their profound significance
  within the complex web of gene expression and cellular function. The central element
  represents miRNAs, depicted as crucial modulators of the process. In the realm of
  development, miRNAs are indispensable. They fine‐tune diverse processes during embryogenesis
  to ensure the proper formation of tissues and organs. In organogenesis, they steer
  organ growth and patterning by targeting essential genes. Postnatally, they continue
  to impact processes such as tissue remodeling, neurodevelopment, and immune maturation.
  Concerning cell proliferation and differentiation, miRNAs play a vital regulatory
  role. They can either enhance or inhibit cell proliferation by targeting genes associated
  with the cell cycle and apoptosis. In differentiation, they trigger or suppress
  the expression of factors that determine cell fate. In apoptosis, miRNAs play multiple
  roles. They can directly target apoptotic genes, regulate signaling pathways involved
  in apoptosis, and epigenetically modulate apoptosis by targeting enzymes related
  to DNA methylation and histone modification. miRNAs also have a significant role
  in metabolism. They regulate glucose metabolism by targeting genes involved in uptake,
  glycolysis, and insulin signaling. They target genes related to lipid metabolism,
  specifically those involved in the synthesis, oxidation, and transport of lipids.
  They balance intake and expenditure in energy homeostasis by targeting relevant
  genes
