caption: 'The modular functional architecture of Tregs: Core suppressive mechanisms,
  lineage stability, and tissue-specific adaptations. (1). Cytokine-mediated control.
  FOXP3+ Tregs secrete inhibitory cytokines, including IL-10, TGF-β, and IL-35, to
  dampen effector T cell proliferation and condition dendritic cells. (2). Metabolic
  conditioning. Tregs enforce metabolic suppression through high-affinity IL-2 consumption
  (via CD25), depriving effector cells of growth signals, and generate immunosuppressive
  adenosine via the CD39/CD73 ectoenzymatic pathway. (3). Checkpoint-driven suppression.
  Contact-dependent mechanisms involve CTLA-4, PD-1, LAG-3, and other checkpoint molecules,
  which physically downregulate co-stimulatory ligands (CD80/86) or modulate signaling
  on antigen-presenting cells (APCs). (4). Tissue adaptation. Tregs acquire context-specific
  phenotypes, such as RORγt+ expression in the gut or amphiregulin production in liver
  repair, to maintain homeostasis in diverse tissue niches. (5). Epigenetic stabilization.
  Lineage identity is preserved by FOXP3-dependent chromatin remodeling, including
  CNS2 demethylation and the coordinated binding of core transcription factors (NFAT,
  Runx1, IRF4, BATF) to resist inflammatory destabilization. (6). Peripheral induction
  (pTregs). Under tolerogenic conditions (TGF-β, retinoic acid), naïve CD4+ T cells
  convert into FOXP3+ pTregs, a pathway critical for mucosal and oral tolerance'
