caption: Evidence Supporting Metformin’s Role in Metabolism, Cancer, and Aging. Schematic
  overview illustrating the pleiotropic actions of metformin in metabolic regulation,
  oncogenic signaling, and aging-related processes. Central metformin activity integrates
  epidemiological evidence from cancer prevention and clinical trials with the systemic
  metabolic effects associated with obesity, hyperinsulinemia, and metabolic syndrome,
  including the modulation of lipolysis, thermogenesis, and gut microbiota homeostasis.
  At the cellular level, metformin exerts direct effects through AMPK-dependent pathways
  (inhibition of mTORC1 via TSC2) and AMPK-independent mechanisms (suppression of
  mTOR, reduction of reactive oxygen species, and induction of autophagy and apoptosis).
  Indirect effects include the inhibition of hepatic gluconeogenesis, improved insulin
  sensitivity, reduced circulating glucose levels, and modulation of immune responses.
  In parallel, metformin influences aging-related hallmarks by reducing oxidative
  stress, DNA damage, inflammation, and promoting autophagy. Collectively, these convergent
  mechanisms regulate cell growth and survival, highlighting metformin as a central
  metabolic modulator at the interface of metabolism, cancer, and aging
