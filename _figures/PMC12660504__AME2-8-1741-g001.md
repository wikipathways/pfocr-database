caption: Multifaceted roles of miRNAs in apoptosis regulation. miRNAs bidirectionally
  regulate apoptosis by targeting genes related to apoptosis, modulating signaling
  pathways, and mediating epigenetic modifications. Directly, miRNAs either suppress
  (e.g., miR‐21 targeting PTEN/PDCD4) or promote (e.g., miR‐15a/16–1 targeting Bcl‐2)
  apoptosis. MiRNAs bidirectionally regulate the cellular survival‐death balance by
  targeting key molecules in the intrinsic (mitochondrial) and extrinsic (death receptor)
  apoptotic pathways. The intrinsic pathway is triggered by mitochondrial electron
  transport chain signals, culminating in the release of cytochrome C, whereas the
  extrinsic pathway is initiated through the binding of death ligands to cell‐surface
  receptors. Both pathways ultimately activate caspases to execute apoptosis. For
  example, the miR‐34 family, acting as downstream effectors of p53, activates pro‐apoptotic
  genes (e.g., Bax and PUMA) by targeting the anti‐apoptotic proteins Bcl‐2 and Survivin.
  Epigenetically, miRNAs induce DNA hypomethylation or histone acetylation by targeting
  PI3K/Akt components (e.g., miR‐375), activating pro‐apoptotic genes while suppressing
  survival signals. These mechanisms underscore the crucial role of miRNAs in regulating
  cell survival and death during development and disease
