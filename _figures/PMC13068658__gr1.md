caption: 'Mechanism of m6A modification. m6A RNA modification is a dynamic process
  collaboratively regulated by "writers," "erasers," and "readers." The "writers"
  function, responsible for adding m6A to RNA, is primarily executed by the methyltransferase
  complex (MTC) with METTL3-METTL14-WTAP as its core. This process is orchestrated
  by the distinct roles of each subunit: METTL3 drives the catalytic activity, METTL14
  enhances substrate binding and stability, and WTAP guides the complex to specific
  nuclear regions (such as nuclear speckles) for modification, enabling recognition
  of the RRACH sequence. Additionally, accessory factors like ZC3H13, RBM15, and KIAA1429
  also participate in regulating MTC function. The "erasers," mainly FTO and ALKBH5,
  are capable of removing m6A modifications; FTO acts through an oxidative pathway,
  while ALKBH5 preferentially targets single-stranded RNA, with ALKBH3 exhibiting
  a similar function. Finally, "readers," such as the YTH family, IGF2BP family, and
  HNRNP family, specifically recognize m6A sites and mediate downstream effects including
  RNA stability, translation, splicing, and transport. Members of the YTH family perform
  different functions depending on their subcellular localization (nuclear or cytoplasmic)
  (By Figdraw)'
