caption: 'The interactions between CTCs and blood cells in the circulation. (A) CTCs
  activate platelets via direct contact or soluble factors. Activated platelets transfer
  inhibitory molecules (e.g., TGF-β, P-selectin, MHC-I) to CTCs, impairing T and NK
  cell surveillance. (B and C) CTCs suppress NK (B) and CD8+ T cell (C) functions
  via immune checkpoints (e.g., PD-L1, HLA-E) and induce Treg differentiation through
  proinflammatory factors, further dampening immune responses. (D) CTC-neutrophil
  clusters, formed via integrins, promote CTC proliferation, dormancy escape, survival,
  and extravasation. Neutrophils release ARG-1, NO, ROS, and PGE2 to aid immune evasion.
  (E) Via CD47-SIRPα, CTCs evade macrophage phagocytosis. Macrophages enhance CTC
  invasiveness by secreting CXCL8, CXCL2, and EGF. (F) CTC-MDSC interaction through
  Nodal-Cripto promotes CTC growth and suppresses CD8+ T cells via ROS and ARG-1.
  (G) CTCs intravasate via MMP1-PAR1 and Ca2+-MLC pathways; extravasation involves
  E-selectin/sLex and CD44, followed by endothelial contraction via ERK and PI3K signaling.
  ARG-1: Arginase-1; CXCL2: C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 2; CXCL8: C-X-C motif chemokine
  ligand 8; CTCs: Circulating tumor cells; EGF: Epidermal growth factor; ERK: Extracellular
  signal-regulated kinase; HLA-E: Human leukocyte antigen-E; MDSC: Myeloid-derived
  suppressor cell; MHC-I: Major histocompatibility complex class I; MLC: Myosin light
  chain; MMP-1: Matrix metalloproteinase-1; NO: Nitric oxide; NK: Natural killer;
  PAR1: Protease-activated receptor 1; PD-L1: Programmed death-ligand 1; PGE2: Prostaglandin
  E2; PI3K: Phosphoinositide 3-kinase; ROS: Reactive oxygen species; SIRP α: Signal-regulatory
  protein α; sLex: Sialyl Lewis X; TGF-β: Transforming growth factor-β; Treg: Regulatory
  T cell'
