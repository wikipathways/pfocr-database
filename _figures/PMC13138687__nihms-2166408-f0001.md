caption: Overview of the mammalian Hippo-YAP pathway. There are multiple recognized
  inputs that regulate the activity of the core signaling components of the Hippo-YAP
  pathway. These include soluble ligand-receptor signals via GPCRs (G-protein coupled
  receptors) and RTKs (receptor tyrosine kinases), interactions between the extracellular
  matrix (ECM) and mechanical forces that engage integrin signaling, and cell-cell
  interactions involving adherens and tight junctions. Intracellular regulation of
  MST1/2 and LATS1/2 is mediated by both positive (e.g. NF2, RASSF1 A, KIBRA, TAOKs,
  MAP4Ks) and inhibitory (e.g. STRIPAK/SLMAP, AJUBA) factors and influence YAP/TAZ
  inhibitory phosphorylation and ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation. YAP/TAZ
  are also restrained in the cytosol through interactions with 14–3-3, AMOT, ZO (zonula
  occludens) proteins, and catenin-containing complexes. Activating tyrosine phosphorylation
  of YAP/TAZ is mediated by ABL, SRC, and YES kinases. Nuclear YAP/TAZ binds multiple
  transcription factors including but not limited to TEAD family members, SMADs, RUNX,
  and TBX5, to control gene expression. The figure was created with BioRender.com
