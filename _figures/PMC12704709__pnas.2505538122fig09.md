caption: A model of eIF3-mediated facilitation of HCV IRES–mediated translation during
  initiation, elongation, and reinitiation through the rearrangement of its subunits.
  (1 and 2) At the initiation step, eIF3 captured by subdomain IIIb of HCV IRES might
  assist in recruiting eIF5B·Met-tRNAi. (2 and 3) At the late stage of initiation,
  eIF3c-NTD in the initiation complex contributes to the 60S joining. During elongation,
  HCV IRES could remain associated with 80S (3) or dissociate and subsequently capture
  another 40S, thereby leading to the formation of polysomes (3’). (3) During elongation,
  subdomain IIIf of HCV IRES (encircled by a black dotted line) is retained at the
  exit site of the mRNA channel. (4 and 5) After translation terminates, subdomain
  IIIf could be the anchoring point for reaccommodating the downstream coding region
  into the mRNA channel via domain II of HCV IRES for the next translation cycle.
  The arrow from (5 to 3) represents a putative pathway to the second round of translation
  without splitting the 80S ribosome. eIF3c-NTD might maintain the 60S subunit and
  conduct efficient reinitiation directly after termination. The scheme enclosed in
  the rectangle at the bottom represents the hijacking of the 80S ribosome translating
  host mRNA by HCV IRES (23). Bound HCV IRES does not impede ongoing cap-dependent
  translation. After the cap-dependent translation terminates, HCV IRES likely initiates
  translation of its own downstream mRNA, by directly proceeding into the elongation
  phase
