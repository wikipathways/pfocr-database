caption: Key pathophysiological mechanisms of PPCs in thoracic surgery. (A) Inflammation
  and immunosuppression in PPCs. Surgical trauma disrupts the pulmonary tissue barrier
  and releases DAMPs, which activate the TLR/NF-κB signaling pathway and trigger a
  proinflammatory cytokine storm (e.g., IL-6, IL-12, TNF-α). This inflammatory response
  acts synergistically with tumor-induced immunosuppression, which is more prominent
  in elderly patients with impaired immune function, increasing capillary permeability
  and resulting in lung tissue injury and pulmonary edema. (B) Oxidative stress pathways
  in PPCs. Surgical trauma, IR injury, and high-concentration oxygen inhalation can
  induce mitochondrial dysfunction, leading to explosive production of ROS such as
  superoxide anions and hydrogen peroxide. Oxidative stress occurs when ROS production
  exceeds the scavenging capacity of endogenous antioxidant defense systems, including
  superoxide dismutase and glutathione. ROS directly disrupts the integrity of pulmonary
  vascular endothelium, increases capillary permeability, and induces pulmonary edema.
  Meanwhile, surgical trauma and oxidative stress activate the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal
  axis, promoting cortisol release and hepatic synthesis of CRP, exacerbating systemic
  inflammation and further aggravating lung injury. (C) Alveolar injury and airway
  obstruction. Surgical procedures and anesthesia impair the function of type II alveolar
  epithelial cells, reducing the synthesis and secretion of PS and weakening alveolar
  structural stability. Concurrently, postoperative mechanical ventilation and inflammatory
  responses increase airway secretions and mucus viscosity, impairing mucus clearance,
  causing airway obstruction and alveolar collapse, disrupting ventilation function,
  and ultimately leading to PPCs. CRP, C-reactive protein; DAMPs, damage-associated
  molecular patterns; IL-12, interleukin-12; IL-6, interleukin-6; IR, ischemia-reperfusion;
  NF-κB, nuclear factor-κB; PPCs, postoperative pulmonary complications; PS, pulmonary
  surfactant; ROS, reactive oxygen species; TLR, Toll-like receptor; TNF-α, tumor
  necrosis factor-α
