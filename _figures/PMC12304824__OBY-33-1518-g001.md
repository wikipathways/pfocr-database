caption: Working model pooling present and previous disrupting events identified after
  a sleeve gastrectomy in LA‐unresponsive patients. 1) The downregulation of TRPM5
  gene expression, by reducing taste bud cell depolarization [39], impairs the taste
  signal. 2) FP also overexpress genes implicated in the inflammatory process (BRD2,
  CCN2, CEACAM6, CXCL9, END1, and LRRFIP1). This proinflammatory status, corroborated
  by high CRP and fibrinogen levels, is strengthened by an oral bacterial signature
  rich in genera Fusobacterium and Pseudomonas, previously found in these patients
  [10]. The functional impact of inflammation should be a reduction of FP number [13].
  3) This phenomenon is amplified by the apoptotic activity of BRD2, KLF6, and SLC39A7
  genes. Taken together, these events result in a reduction of lipid detection efficiency.
  4) By promoting tryptophan (Trp) catabolism along the kynurenine pathway, inflammation
  also produces excitotoxic metabolites known to disrupt receptors involved in the
  taste signal transfer to the brain. Consistent with this event, orosensory detection
  of fat was found to be inversely correlated with kynurenine levels in this cohort
  [44]. 5) The physiological consequence of these metabolic and environmental changes
  is a reduced efficiency of the lipid detection system and, consequently, a diminished
  lipid signal reaching the brain. CRP, C‐reactive protein; FP, fungiform papillae;
  LCFA, long‐chain fatty acid. [Color figure can be viewed at wileyonlinelibrary.com]
