caption: In the syncytiotrophoblast of the female reproductive system, quorum sensing
  molecules (QSM) released by microorganisms may activate signaling pathways similar
  to those in the upper respiratory tract via TAS2R. This mechanism involves calcium
  signaling, which activates nitric oxide synthase (NOS) and promotes the production
  of large amounts of NO, which has antimicrobial effects. Additionally, TAS2R may
  trigger the release of CCK from cells expressing TAS2R, which in turn upregulates
  ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters, promoting the expulsion of harmful compounds
  from the cells. In the vagina, invading pathogens may activate TAS2R in basal epithelial
  cells. Through the downstream signaling pathway of the gustducin G protein, which
  dissociates into its α, GANT3, and βγ subunits, the cells respond by migrating,
  proliferating, and differentiating into superficial protective epithelial cells,
  thus enhancing the defensive barrier. Meanwhile, in the cervix, hormones activate
  TAS2R in epithelial cells, leading to an increase in cytoplasmic Ca²⁺, which stimulates
  the secretion of mucins, helping to protect the mucosal barrier. In the ovaries,
  activation of taste receptors can respectively reduce cAMP and increase cGMP levels
