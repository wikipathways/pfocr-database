caption: 'DNA Damage Repair Defects and CSC-Associated Pathways Driving Drug Resistance
  in Cervical Cancer and Their Targeted Intervention Strategies. Note: The left panel
  highlights the relationship between DNA damage repair and drug resistance in cervical
  cancer cells. BRCA1/2 mutations impair the functions of homologous recombination
  repair (HRR)-related factors RAD51C and RAD51D, rendering cells more dependent on
  poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP). Active PARP promotes DNA repair, while PARP
  inhibitors (PARPi) inactivate PARP, leading to DNA damage accumulation and apoptosis.
  However, in the context of HRR deficiency, some cells may develop PARPi resistance,
  ultimately reducing therapeutic efficacy. The right panel depicts mechanisms by
  which CSCs and cervical cancer cells evade therapy under chemoradiotherapy pressure.
  CSCs survive by enhancing self-renewal, sustaining unlimited proliferation, evading
  immune surveillance, and increasing drug resistance. Key pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin
  and Notch/Hedgehog are highlighted for their central roles in maintaining CSC survival,
  promoting tumor drug resistance through transcriptional and behavioral regulation.
  Targeted strategies are illustrated below, including Wnt/β-catenin inhibitors (e.g.,
  XAV939, ICG-001) to suppress β-catenin activation and Notch/Hedgehog inhibitors
  (e.g., γ-secretase inhibitors, Vismodegib) to reduce resistance-related gene expression'
