caption: 'Biogenesis of microRNAs via the canonical pathway. DGCR8: DiGeorge syndrome
  critical region 8, Ran: Ras-related nuclear protein, GTP: Guanosine triphosphate,
  AGO: Argonaute. RISC: RNA-induced silencing complex, miRISC: miRNA-induced Silencing
  complex. MicroRNA biogenesis begins with the transcription of pri-miRNA by RNA polymerase
  II and/or III within the nucleus. The pri-miRNA is converted into pre-miRNA by the
  Drosha enzyme and the DGCR8 protein. The pre-miRNA is transported from the nucleus
  to the cytoplasm, where it is cleaved by the Dicer enzyme, resulting in a double-stranded
  miRNA molecule. One strand becomes the active miRNA (commonly referred to as "miRNA"),
  and the other strand is the passenger strand. The active miRNA binds to AGO2 and,
  along with other helper proteins, forms the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC).
  RISC facilitates the binding of miRNA to its target mRNA. The binding occurs through
  the complementary sequences between the seed region of the miRNA and the 3'' UTR
  of the mRNA. If the miRNA and mRNA are perfectly matched, AGO2 cleaves the mRNA,
  leading to its degradation. In cases of partial or weak binding, translation is
  inhibited, leading to the initiation of deadenylation. Deadenylation shortens the
  poly(A) tail at the 3'' end of the mRNA, reducing its stability and eventually leading
  to its degradation. Additionally, the cap structure at the 5'' end of the mRNA is
  removed by Dcp1 and Dcp2 enzymes, further promoting rapid mRNA decay. The cap structure
  at the 5'' end of the mRNA is removed by Dcp1 and Dcp2 enzymes, further promoting
  rapid mRNA decay'
