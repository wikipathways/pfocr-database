caption: Scheme of the synthetic procedure and piezocatalytic anticancer of GO/BCT:Mn
  MDM. a, Schematic diagram of GO/BCT:Mn MDM reaching the tumor site for action. GO/BCT:Mn
  MDM is injected into mice via tail vein and enriched at the tumor site via enhanced
  permeability and retention (EPR) effect. GO/BCT:Mn MDM is subsequently endocytosed
  by tumor cells and undergo lysosomal escape into the cytoplasm. Under ultrasound
  stimulation, GO/BCT:Mn MDM polarization generates electron-hole pairs, where the
  electrons reduce H+ to H2, while the holes oxidize NADH to NAD+ via the Seebeck
  effect, preventing the conversion of OAA to malate, and therefore inducing apoptosis
  in tumor cells. b, Schematic of the internal polarization of GO/BCT:Mn MDM. Under
  ultrasound stimulation, the internal electrons of GO/BCT:Mn MDM are shifted due
  to mechanical forces, resulting in the formation of a built-in electric fiel, and
  the formation of electrons and holes on its surface. c, Schematic representation
  of GO/BCT:Mn MDM manipulation of H+ consumption in tumor cells. Electrons generated
  by GO/BCT:Mn MDM in response to ultrasound stimulation reduce H+ to H2 (Reaction
  1), thereby lowering the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), which in turn decreases
  the activity of the mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I-V, and ultimately
  induces apoptosis in tumor cells. d, Schematic illustration of GO/BCT:Mn MDM manipulate
  malate depletion in tumor metabolism. The holes generated by GO/BCT:Mn MDM upon
  ultrasound stimulation oxidize NADH to NAD+ (Reaction 2), thereby inhibiting the
  conversion of OAA to malate. Malate depletion blocks the malate/aspartate shuttle
  pathway, which reduces the MMP and the concentration of malate in the mitochondrial
  matrix, inhibiting the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, thereby affecting the efficiency
  of energy metabolism in tumor cells, and ultimately inducing apoptosis in tumor
  cells
