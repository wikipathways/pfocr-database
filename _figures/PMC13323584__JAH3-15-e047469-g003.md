caption: MI and IS are interconnected through shared metabolic dysregulation and inflammatory
  pathways mediated by distinct ACSL isoforms across immune, cardiac, and neural cells.
  In circulating monocytes, hyperlipidemia and ischemic stress induce ACSL1 expression,
  promoting the LPC/LPA axis, neutrophil activation, and NET formation, thereby amplifying
  thromboinflammation and contributing to both MI and secondary IS. In cardiomyocytes,
  ischemia‐associated lipid accumulation increases LCFA availability. ACSL4 converts
  LCFAs into peroxidizable lipid species, driving lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic
  cell death, which is modulated by mitochondrial quality control pathways and can
  be attenuated by genetic or pharmacological inhibition of ACSL4. Similarly, in neuronal
  cells following IS, elevated ACSL4 activity enhances LCFA incorporation into oxidizable
  phospholipids, promoting lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis, thereby exacerbating
  neuronal injury. Circulating and locally released miR‐22‐3p after MI further modulates
  ACSL4 expression and ferroptosis susceptibility across cardiac and neural compartments.
  Collectively, this schematic highlights ACSL1‐dependent immunometabolic activation
  and ACSL4‐mediated ferroptosis as convergent mechanisms linking MI and IS. Created

  fatty acid; LCFA‐OOH, long‐chain fatty acid hydroperoxide; LPA, lysophosphatidic
  acid; LPC, lysophosphatidylcholine; MI, myocardial infarction; miR‐22‐3p, microRNA‐22‐3p;
  MPO, myeloperoxidase; and NET, neutrophil extracellular trap
