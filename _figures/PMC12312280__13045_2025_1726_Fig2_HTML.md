caption: Overview of necroptosis pathway. The activation of necroptosis signaling
  can be triggered by the receptor-ligand interactions, including TNF-α/TNFR1, LPS/TLR4,
  TRAIL/TRAILR, FasL/Fas and Z-DNA/ZBP1. In the classic TNF-α/TNFR1 signaling, TNFR1
  recruits TRADD, RIPK1, and multiple E3 ubiquitin ligases such as TRAF2, cIAP1/2,
  and LUBAC through its death domain to form Complex I. Within Complex I, RIPK1 undergoes
  ubiquitination, which mediates the recruitment and activation of the TAK1, TBK1,
  and IKK complexes, leading to the activation the NF-kβ pathway and triggering an
  inflammatory response. Under the action of deubiquitinating enzymes such as SPATA2
  and CYLD, RIPK1 dissociates from Complex I and undergoes autophosphorylation at
  the S166 site, resulting in the formation of two distinct complexes, IIa and IIb,
  depending on the availability of caspase-8 within the cell. When caspase-8 is available,
  RIPK1 binds to FADD and caspase-8, forming Complex IIa, which promotes the activation
  of caspase-8 and subsequent cleavage of downstream caspase-3, ultimately resulting
  in apoptosis. However, when caspase-8 is inhibited or absent, RIPK1 will bind with
  RIPK3 to form Complex IIb. RIPK3 then undergoes autophosphorylation and activation,
  recruiting and phosphorylating MLKL, leading to its oligomerization and the disruption
  of membrane integrity, thus mediating the execution of necroptosis
