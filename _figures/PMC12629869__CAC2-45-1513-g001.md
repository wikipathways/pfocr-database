caption: Activation and regulation mechanisms of STING‐induced cellular senescence.
  Chromatin fragments, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction (leading to mtDNA
  release), and retroviruses can trigger cGAS‐STING‐mediated cellular senescence and
  SASPs. Several regulators are involved in the regulation of STING‐induced senescence.
  Nuclear cGAS promotes TRIM41‐mediated ubiquitination and degradation of ORF2p, thus
  inhibiting LINE1 retrotransposition to maintain genome stability. TXNRD1 is a cytoplasmic
  chromatin fragment‐localized protein that promotes cGAS activity. YAP/TAZ directly
  regulates lamin B1 and ACTR2 transcription to prevent the release of nuclear DNA
  fragments. Non‐canonical pathways are involved in the cGAS‐STING‐mediated induction
  of SASPs. IRF3 interacts with RB to block RB hyperphosphorylation and E2F transcription,
  thus promoting senescence. In addition, STING/PERK/eIF2α signaling and STING/PERK/NF‐κB
  signaling also participate in STING‐induced cellular senescence. ACTR2, actin‐related
  protein 2; cGAS, cyclic GMP‐AMP synthase; E2F, early 2 factor; eIF2α, eukaryotic
  initiation factor 2 alpha; IRF3, IFN regulatory factor 3; LINE1, long interspersed
  nuclear element‐1; mtDNA, mitochondrial DNA; NF‐κB, nuclear transcription factor‐kappa
  B; ORF2p, open reading frame 2 protein; PERK, protein kinase RNA‐like endoplasmic
  reticulum kinase; RB, retinoblastoma protein; SASPs, senescence‐associated secretory
  phenotypes; STING, stimulator of interferon gene; TRIM41, tripartite motif‐containing
  protein 41; TXNRD1, thioredoxin reductase 1; Ub, ubiquitination; YAP/TAZ, Yes‐associated
  protein/Transcriptional coactivator with PDZ‐binding motif
