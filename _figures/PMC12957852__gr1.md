caption: 'The catabolism of H2S and the distribution of H2S synthase within the organism.
  A. The enzymatic pathway primarily utilizes L-cysteine as a substrate. Under the
  catalysis of two PLP-dependent enzymes, CBS and CSE, cysteine combines with serine
  to form cystathionine, which is followed by the production of cysteine, α-pyruvate,
  and H2S. 3-MST utilizes cysteine and the metabolite 3MP from CAT as substrates for
  the production of H2S. Non-enzymatic reaction pathways include: i, a small quantity
  of H2S is generated through the catalytic action of 3-MST utilizing DAO as a substrate,
  with the participation of glucose, glutathione, and polysulfide. The metabolism
  of H2S mainly occurs in mitochondria through oxidation reactions. With the catalysis
  of SQR, and PDO, sulfur atoms are transferred to R-SH and oxidized to SO32- and
  SO42-; ii, H2S produces methyl mercaptan and dimethyl sulfide through methylation
  reactions catalyzed by TSMT; iii, H2S decomposes by binding to methemoglobin to
  form sulfur-containing hemoglobin. B. The expression of H2S synthase exhibits a
  distinct pattern of distribution across different tissues. CBS is highly expressed
  in the nervous system. CSE is primarily distributed in the cardiovascular system,
  including the aorta, pulmonary artery, caudal artery, and portal vein. The kidneys
  express both CBS and CSE. Additionally, 3-MST and CAT are mainly expressed in the
  central nervous system, including glial cells. And the expression of these three
  enzymes is negatively regulated with aging. PLP, pyridoxal-5′-phosphate; 3MP, 3-mercaptopyruvate;
  CAT, cysteine aminotransferase; DAO, D-amino acid oxidase; SQR, sulfide-quinone
  oxidoreductase; PDO, persulfide dioxygenase; R-SH, thiol, SO32-, sulfite; SO42-,
  sulfate; TSMT, thiol-S-thiomethyl transferase'
