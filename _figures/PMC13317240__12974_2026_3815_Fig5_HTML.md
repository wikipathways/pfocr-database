caption: Shared cGAS–STING amplification across PD, ALS, and HD. This schematic illustrates
  how disease-specific proteinopathies—α-synuclein aggregation in Parkinson’s disease,
  TDP-43 pathology and C9orf72 repeat expansion in ALS, and mutant huntingtin (mHTT)
  in Huntington’s disease—converge on DNA stress pathways. Common upstream triggers,
  including mitochondrial dysfunction, mtDNA leakage, and DNA damage, engage the cGAS–STING
  axis in the CNS. Activated cGAS–STING signaling promotes IFN/ISG responses and context-dependent
  inflammasome coupling (e.g., NLRP3/IL-1β), contributing to sustained neuroinflammation.
  Solid arrows indicate established mechanistic links, whereas dashed arrows denote
  context- or model-dependent associations. Together, the diagram positions cGAS–STING
  as a shared amplification node linking DNA stress to inflammatory signaling across
  neurodegenerative disorders. Created with BioRender.com
