caption: CCAT2 role in CRC progression and metastasis. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling
  pathway has critical role in tumor proliferation, survival, and metastasis in CRC
  cells. The Wnt ligand binds to the Frizzled receptor and stabilizes β-catenin by
  inhibiting the GSK3β destruction complex, leading to its nuclear translocation.
  This process results in β-catenin interaction to activate genes like cyclin D1 and
  cyclin E1, driving tumor cell cycle progression. CCAT2 amplifies this pathway by
  enhancing ERK phosphorylation, while TAF15 and AURKB contribute to mitotic progression
  and chromosomal instability. The illustration also shows metabolic reprogramming
  via PKM2, LDHA, and GLUT1, increasing glucose uptake and lactate production, with
  MYC transcription further promoting glycolysis. Annotations of colorectal and liver
  metastasis, along with the inhibitory effects of CCAT2 and MYC on Wnt signaling,
  emphasize the pathway’s dynamic role, making it a potential therapeutic target,
  illustrated with color-coded pathways and molecular interactions
