caption: 'Molecular mechanisms of the anti-tumor effects of PDEs. This scheme illustrates
  three primary and interconnected mechanisms through which exosomes from diverse
  botanical sources exert their anticancer activity. (1) Direct tumor cell killing:
  PDEs induce intrinsic apoptosis by modulating Bax/Bcl-2 balance and activating caspases,
  enforce G1/S cell cycle arrest via p53/p21 and GADD45α pathways, and inhibit pro-survival
  signaling such as PI3K/Akt/mTOR. (2) Tumor microenvironment (TME) reprogramming:
  PDEs repolarize tumor-associated macrophages from M2 to M1 phenotype by inhibiting
  STAT3 and activating NF-κB signaling, promoting cytokine release (e.g., TNF-α, IL-12);
  combine with direct effects on T cells (e.g., downregulating PD-1, reducing Tregs),
  enhance CD8+ T cell recruitment and synergizes with immune checkpoint inhibitors.
  (3) Metabolic interference: PDEs disrupt tumor metabolism by inhibiting key enzymes,
  such as glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (pentose phosphate pathway) and acetyl-CoA
  carboxylase 1 (lipid synthesis), and by inducing mitochondrial ROS production, leading
  to oxidative damage and apoptosis. (4) PDEs can be combined with external stimuli
  such as heat, light, or sound to further enhance their therapeutic effects. Their
  ability to act through multiple complementary mechanisms highlights their unique
  potential as versatile therapeutic agents and delivery platforms that can overcome
  drug resistance and achieve strong anticancer activity'
