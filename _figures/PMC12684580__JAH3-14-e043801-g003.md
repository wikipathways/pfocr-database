caption: Glucose enters cells via Glut1 and is metabolized through glycolysis to produce
  pyruvate, which is converted to lactate by LDHB. Lactate can be transported by MCT1
  or react with glutathione to form lactoylglutathione, a precursor for D‐lactate.
  Alternatively, lactate is converted back to pyruvate or acetyl‐CoA, fueling the
  TCA cycle (eg, via oxaloacetate and citrate). Enzymatic lactylation involves “writer”
  enzymes (eg, p300/CBP, GCN5/TIP60, KAT7/8) and “eraser” enzymes (eg, SIRTs), modulating
  transcription through histone and promoter interactions. The diagram highlights
  lactate’s dual role as a metabolic intermediate and an epigenetic regulator. α‐KG
  indicates alpha‐ketoglutarate; α‐MHC, α‐myosin heavy chain; BRG1, Brahma‐related
  gene 1; CBP, CREB‐binding protein; CoA, coenzyme A; DPF2, double PHD fingers 2;
  GCN5, general control non‐depressible 5; Glut1, Glucose transporter 1; GLO1, glyoxalase
  1; GLO2, glyoxalase 2; HDAC, histone deacetylase; KAT7, lysine acetyltransferase;
  KAT8, lysine acetyltransferase 8; LDHA, lactate dehydrogenase A; LDHB, lactate dehydrogenase
  B; MCT, monocarboxylate transporter 1; MCT4, monocarboxylate transporter 4; MDH2,
  malate dehydrogenase 2; Mecp2, methyl‐CpG‐binding protein 2; NLRP3, NLR family pyrin
  domain containing 3; p300, E1A‐binding protein p300; PDH, pyruvate dehydrogenase;
  SIRT, sirtuin (NAD+‐dependent deacetylases); SNAI1, Snail family transcriptional
  repressor 1; TCA, tricarboxylic acid cycle; and TIP60, Tat‐interactive protein 60
