caption: TLR2/1-mediated regulation of the uterine inflammatory environment by endogenous
  and exogenous ligands. The Toll-like receptor 2 (TLR2) acts as a central physiological
  sensor within the bovine uterus, initiating a weak, transient inflammatory response
  essential for post-insemination homeostasis and clearance. This response is triggered
  by a variety of molecules that activate the TLR2/1 pathway, including Pam3CSK4 (PAM3),
  a synthetic TLR2/1 agonist, endogenous factors such as sperm (acting as allogeneic
  cells), triglycerides (TG) from the egg yolk semen extender (SE), and components
  within the seminal plasma (SP). Additionally, pathogenic peptidoglycan (PGN-S) from
  organisms like Staphylococcus acts as a strong TLR2 agonist, inducing inflammation
  that can disrupt the beneficial sperm-immune interaction. Conversely, the system
  is regulated by antagonists that block TLR2/1 signaling; these include the synthetic
  blocker CU-CPT22 and commensal peptidoglycan (PGN-L) derived from Lacticaseibacillus
  and Lactobacillus, which competitively prevent TLR2 dimerization with TLR1 to suppress
  detrimental inflammation. This mechanism highlights the crucial balance between
  beneficial and pathological TLR2/1 activation in the reproductive tract
