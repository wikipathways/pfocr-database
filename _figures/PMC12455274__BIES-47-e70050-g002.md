caption: Molecular mechanisms of guidance cues. (a) Netrin 1 signaling. Netrin 1 bound
  DCC homodimers activate Rac and Cdc42. The activated Rac promotes Arp2/3‐mediated
  lamellipodial protrusion. Conversely, Netrin 1‐bound UNC5 activates RhoA, leading
  to axon repulsion. (b) Slit signaling. Slit binds to Robo and inactivates Cdc42 or
  stimulates Rac activity, which leads to actin depolymerization and repulsion. (c)
  Semaphorin signaling. The C‐terminal PDZ‐binding motif of Plexin interacts with
  Rho GEF, and semaphorin activation induces RhoA‐mediated repulsion. Activated RhoA
  promotes MLC phosphorylation via ROCK and triggers actomyosin bundle formation.
  RhoA via ROCK also activates LIMK and inhibits actin depolymerization. Plexin‐B1
  also inhibits Rac by competing with PAK, which results in a repulsive effect. (d)
  Shh signaling. Shh binds to Boc and Ptch1, activating Smo and Src family kinases
  (SFKs). SFKs phosphorylate ZBP1, which then translocates to the nucleus, where it
  binds to corepressors and coactivators to regulate the transcription of specific
  target genes, ultimately enabling polarized actin polymerization. (e) BMP signaling.
  BMP induces attraction by regulating actin dynamics via ADF/Cofilin through LIMK
  and SSH phosphatase. Phosphorylated Smad recruits Smad4 and translocates to the
  nucleus, where it binds to corepressors and coactivators to regulate the transcription
  of specific target genes. (f) Wnt signaling. In the Wnt/Ca2⁺ pathway, calcium influx
  activates CaMKII, leading to a repulsive effect on the growth cone by regulating
  the reorganization of dynamic microtubules. The Wnt/PCP pathway, mediated by Frizzled
  and Dvl2, utilizes Rho family GTPases to regulate cytoskeletal rearrangements. In
  the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway, Wnt signaling inhibits GSK3β‐mediated β‐catenin degradation,
  allowing its nuclear accumulation and activation of target gene transcription. (g)
  CSPG signaling. Receptors for CSPGs are PTPσ, Nogo receptors, and Contactin 1. When
  CSPGs bind to PTPσ, they promote the dephosphorylation of its substrates, such as
  Cortactin. This leads to the inhibition of actin polymerization. (h) Nogo/MAG signaling.
  Nogo and MAG inhibit axonal outgrowth by activating the NgR signaling pathway. NgR
  functions together with co‐receptors, LINGO1/p75NTR/TROY, and activates RhoA, which
  leads to growth cone collapse. LOTUS binds to NgR1 and antagonizes Nogo‐NgR signaling.
  (i) Ephrin signaling. Eph forward signaling involves Ephexin, which connects Eph
  receptors to Rho GTPases, inducing growth cone collapse via RhoA‐ROCK. Reverse signaling
  by Ephrin‐B occurs when SH2 domain‐containing proteins, such as Grb4, bind to its
  phosphorylated tyrosine residues, activating FAK and promoting attraction. ADF,

  morphogenetic protein; CaMKII, calcium/calmodulin‐dependent protein kinase II; Cdc42,
  cell division control protein 42 homolog; CSPGs, chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans;
  Dvl2, Dishevelled segment polarity protein 2; FAK, focal adhesion kinase; LINGO
  1, Leucine‐rich repeat and Ig domain containing 1; MTs, microtubules; NgR, Nogo

  tyrosine phosphatase sigma; Rac, Ras‐related C3 botulinum toxin substrate; SFKs,
  Src family kinases; Smad, mothers against decapentaplegic homolog; TROY, Tumor necrosis
  factor receptor superfamily, member 19; SSH, slingshot; UNC5, uncoordinated 5; ZBP1,
  zip code‐binding protein 1
