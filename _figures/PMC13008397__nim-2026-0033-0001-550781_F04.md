caption: Altered metabolism in aging myeloid cells. Changes in metabolism toward a
  bioenergetically deficient state are found in aging microglia and macrophages. Increased
  PGE2-EP2 signaling in myeloid cells promotes phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein
  kinase B (PI3K/AKT) signaling pathway activation, resulting in glycogen synthase
  1 (GYS1) activation. This results in the sequestration of glucose into glycogen,
  leading to decreases in glucose flux, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels, and mitochondrial
  dysfunction. Disruption of oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos) due to mitochondrial
  dysfunction leads to energy depletion and accumulation of succinate. Succinate is
  known to stabilize the transcription factor HIF1a, which promotes transcription
  of pro-inflammatory genes. All together these events contribute to release of inflammatory
  cytokines and reactive oxygen species (ROS) and neuronal damage, which have been
  implicated in age-associated neuropsychiatric conditions like Alzheimer’s disease
  (AD) and late-life depression (LLD). EP2, prostaglandin E2 receptor EP2 subtype;
  ROS, reactive oxygen species; PGE2, prostaglandin E2. Created with BioRender.com
