caption: Schematic illustration of the effects of a cafeteria diet–induced obesity
  model on the gut–heart axis and the protective role of semaglutide (SMG). A cafeteria
  diet induced an obesity model that disrupted the gut–heart axis by causing an imbalance
  in the gut microbiota and damaging the epithelial barrier. This was accompanied
  by decreased expression of occludin, claudin and ZO-1, resulting in increased intestinal
  permeability. These alterations triggered systemic inflammation, insulin resistance,
  cardiac injury, and fibrosis through elevated LPS translocation and TMAO levels.
  Semaglutide treatment strengthened the intestinal barrier by activating the GLP-1R-mediated
  HO-1 signalling pathway. It also reduced LPS, TMAO, IL-1β and TGF-β levels, suppressed
  inflammation and improved the MMP/TIMP balance. This attenuated oxidative stress
  and myocardial damage. Thus, semaglutide exerts a protective effect against metabolic,
  intestinal, and cardiac alterations induced by obesity. (Created with BioRender.com)
