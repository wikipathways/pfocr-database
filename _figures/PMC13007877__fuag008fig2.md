caption: 'Schematic representation of the respiratory chains used by S. epidermidis
  during aerobic respiration (A) and anaerobic nitrate respiration (B). Quinone oxidoreductases
  include NADH dehydrogenase (Ndh), glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (G3Pdh), lactate:quinone
  oxidoreductases (Lqo), malate:quinone oxidoreductase (Mqo), and succinate dehydrogenase
  (Sdh). These enzymes transfer electrons to menaquinone. Menaquinone then donates
  electrons to terminal oxidases, namely cytochromes Cyt during aerobic respiration
  (A), or to nitrate during anaerobic respiration (B), initiating nitrate reduction
  pathways. Electron acceptors and intermediates in the anaerobic nitrate branch include
  nitrate (NO3⁻), nitrite (NO2⁻), nitric oxide (NO), nitrous oxide (N2O), and dinitrogen
  (N2), with specific reductases acting at each step, including nitrate reductase
  (NarGHJI), nitrite reductase (NirBD), quinol-dependent nitric oxide reductase (qNor),
  and nitrous oxide reductase (Nos). Metabolic intermediates such as dihydroxyacetone
  phosphate (DHAP), fumarate (Fum), glycerol 3-phosphate (G3P), lactate (Lact), malate
  (Mal), oxaloacetate (OAA), pyruvate (Pyr), and succinate (Suc) are also shown. Transcriptional
  regulators that affect the expression of genes encoding proteins of the respiratory
  chain are shown in dark red boxes. Green arrows mean activation of gene expression;
  red hooked arrows mean repression. Superscript letters refer to source studies:
  lZhu et al. 2017); mWu et al. 2015); jHandke et al. 2008. Due to incomplete characterization
  of the individual aerobic respiratory chain oxidoreductases, and for the sake of
  clarity, this figure presents only the general names of the identified oxidoreductases,
  without indicating their exact number or specific designations'
