caption: 'Enhanced PIEZO1 mechanical responsiveness provides a molecular switch for
  subsequent EV release and prothrombotic pathway initiation in cancer cells. (a)
  In cancer cells, abnormally activated PIEZO1 mediates calcium influx under mechanical
  stimulation and activates the downstream the endosomal sorting complex required
  for transport (ESCRT) pathway to promote exosome release: Ca²⁺ binds to ALG-2/Alix
  proteins, inducing their oligomerization and recruitment to the endosomal membrane,
  which facilitates endosomal budding to form multivesicular bodies (MVBs). After
  MVBs fuse with the cell membrane, exosomes enriched in TF and PS are released [108–112].
  (b) Ca²⁺ activates phospholipase C (PLC), which hydrolyzes PIP₂ into IP₃ and DAG;
  DAG activates PKC, promoting actin cytoskeleton rearrangement to drive the cell
  membrane to protrude outward and shed as PS-rich microvesicles [113–115]. (c) Ca²⁺
  overload in cancer cells induces cytochrome c (Cyt c) release and Caspase-3 activation,
  which further initiates cell apoptosis through the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway
  and leads to apoptotic body formation. The externalized PS on the surface of apoptotic
  bodies can directly activate the coagulation cascade, regulate coagulation cell
  function, and ultimately mediate thrombosis. Meanwhile, the carried DNA fragments
  and active caspase-3 fragments can activate neutrophils to release NETs, which “trap”
  apoptotic bodies, platelets, and coagulation factors to form NETs-apoptotic body-coagulation
  factor complexes, enhancing thrombus stability [116–118]. Created with BioRender.com'
