caption: Ginsenosides alleviate liver fibrosis by suppressing HSC activation through
  multiple pathways. (1) Ginsenosides suppress HSC activation by attenuating TGF-β-SMAD
  signaling. ①Ginsenosides directly inhibit TGF-β binding to TGFR, therefore suppressing
  TGF-β-SMAD pathway activation. ②Ginsenosides upregulate miR-152 expression, which
  targets and suppresses DNMT1. Reduced DNMT1 decreases SMAD7 methylation, and thus
  inhibits SMAD7 ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, ultimately preventing
  TGF-β-SMAD pathway activation. ③Ginsenosides bind to VDR and increase its interaction
  with SMAD3, which inhibits TGFR-mediated SMAD3 phosphorylation, leading to suppression
  of TGF-β-SMAD signaling. (2) Ginsenosides inhibit HSC activation through suppression
  of autophagy. Ginsenosides activate the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway to attenuate TGF-β-
  or LPS-induced HSC autophagy. (3) Ginsenosides inhibit HSC activation by mitigating
  oxidative stress. Ginsenosides suppress the activity of Keap1 which mediates Nrf2
  ubiquitination and degradation, therefore increasing Nrf2 protein levels. Ginsenosides
  also inhibit SMAD2/3 phosphorylation which antagonizes Nrf2 activation. Increased
  Nrf2 levels and activation promote transcription of antioxidant enzymes, suppressing
  oxidative stress. (4) Ginsenosides inhibit HSC activation through modulation of
  cellular metabolism. Ginsenosides directly bind to GLS1 and LDHA and inhibit their
  activity, suppressing glutaminolysis and glycolysis, ultimately inducing HSC inactivation
