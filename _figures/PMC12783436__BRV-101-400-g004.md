caption: Principles underlying the maintenance of cooperation and illustration of
  exploitative replication. (A) Cooperation in egalitarian groups without asymmetric
  replication/reproduction can be enforced by learning or selection. In animals with
  cognitive capacity, the punishment of defection will facilitate cooperation in subsequent
  interactions. Animals also may learn that cooperative behaviours are associated
  with benefits. For unicellular organisms, a cooperative trait can be selected for
  if it confers a survival advantage or selected against if it imposes a fitness reduction.
  (B) If one or a few reproducers give rise to the workers of the colony in an asymmetric
  manner, reproducers could introduce an innate propensity for obedience or heightened
  responsiveness to regulatory signals — the concept of exploitative replication.
  (C) Workers of eusocial Hymenoptera have innate physiological traits that respond
  to queen pheromones. In addition, workers may attack egg‐laying defectors (policing).
  (D) For metazoan multicellular organisms, somatic cells are comparable to the workers
  of a eusocial colony in that they are produced in an asymmetric manner and under
  the regulation of signalling pathways. Comparable to eusocial policing workers,
  immune cells attack cancer cells. Sym. rep., symmetric replication. (E) Certain
  cancer stem cells can produce endothelial‐like progeny that serve to establish blood
  vessels. Generally, bulk tumour cells rather than cancer stem cells perform the
  Warburg effect. Taking into account the likely altruistic nature of the Warburg
  effect, it can be interpreted that bulk tumour cells support cancer stem cells as
  a result of exploitative replication. (F) Major transitions towards metazoan multicellularity
  and eusociality required the unprecedented evolution of exploitative replication.
  By contrast, fraternal cooperation of hierarchical cancers employs mechanisms already
  present in metazoan multicellular organisms. For example, asymmetric replication
  of cancer stem cells and cell–cell communication among cancer cells represent adjustments
  to molecular pathways of multicellular organisms. CSC, cancer stem cell; BTC, bulk
  tumour cell
