caption: HSCs (top) and AML cells (bottom) rely on distinct yet overlapping metabolic
  pathways to sustain their function. HIF-1α enhances glycolysis by promoting pyruvate
  conversion to lactate through LDHA and activates PDK to downregulate oxidative metabolism
  by inhibiting PDH-mediated entry of pyruvate into the TCA cycle. FAO fuels the TCA
  cycle, where long-chain fatty acids are activated to fatty acyl-CoA in the cytosol
  and transported into mitochondria by the carnitine shuttle (CPT1/2), generating
  acetyl-CoA in both HSCs and leukaemic cells. Choline fuels lipid production in HSCs
  by supporting synthesis of the membrane lipids phosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin.
  In AML cells, amino-acid metabolism further supports mitochondrial function. Glutamine
  is converted to glutamate by glutaminase (GLS) and subsequently to α-ketoglutarate
  (αKG) to replenish the TCA cycle, while cysteine contributes to reduced glutathione
  (GSH) synthesis to support succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) activity. BCAAs are catabolized
  by BCAT1 to generate branched-chain keto acids (BCKAs), which enter the TCA cycle
  as acetyl-CoA or succinyl-CoA (SucCoA). BCAT1 upregulation depletes αKG and suppresses
  DNA methylation in AML. Aspartate/glutamate carriers (AGC1/2) mediate amino-acid
  exchange between the cytosol and mitochondria in HSCs. Cytosolic oxaloacetate (OAA)
  is converted to malate, imported by the 2-oxoglutarate carrier (OGC), reconverted
  to OAA, and then transformed into aspartate by GOT2. NADH and FADH2 generated by
  the TCA cycle and FAO fuel OXPHOS, in which ETC complexes I–IV generate the mitochondrial
  membrane potential (Δψmt) used by complex V for ATP synthesis. In AML, mitochondrial
  proteases CLPP and neurolysin (NLN) preserve mitochondrial integrity by degrading
  damaged proteins. Notably, ETC complex IV activity is reduced in AML cells compared
  with HSCs. CoQ, coenzyme Q; Cyt c, cytochrome c; glucose-6-P, glucose-6-phosphate;
  PEP, phosphoenolpyruvate
