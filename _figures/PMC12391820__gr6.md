caption: Dietary fiber regulates bone metabolism through parathyroid hormone (PTH).
  Intermittent administration of PTH activates immune cell signaling pathways, which
  in turn enhances bone formation. In contrast, continuous PTH administration has
  the opposite effect. Short-chain fatty acids bind to G protein-coupled receptor
  43 (GPR43) on the surface of dendritic cells, stimulating the development of regulatory
  T cells (Tregs). Tregs exhibit anti-inflammatory properties and promote the differentiation
  of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) into osteoblasts (OBs) via the Wnt10b
  signaling pathway. Conversely, T helper 17 (Th17) cells and pro-inflammatory cytokines
  stimulate osteoclastogenesis, leading to bone resorption. PTH interacts with osteoblast
  progenitors, initiating the cyclic adenosine monophosphate - protein kinase A (cAMP-PKA)
  signaling cascade. This cascade upregulates transcription factors such as cAMP response
  element-binding protein (CREB) and c-FOS, thereby enhancing the activity of bone
  cells and promoting bone formation. PTH = parathyroid hormone; SFB = sulfate filamented
  bacteria; CREB = cAMP response element binding protein; cFOS = cellular pro-oncogene
  FOS; OB = osteoblast; OC = osteoclast; GPR43 = G-coupled protein receptor 43; BMSC
  = bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells; RANKL = receptor activator of nuclear factor
  kappa-B; TNF-α = tumor necrosis factor- α; OPG = osteoprotegerin; CCL20 = chemokine
  (C–C motif) ligand 20; WNT10b = wingless-related integration site 10b
