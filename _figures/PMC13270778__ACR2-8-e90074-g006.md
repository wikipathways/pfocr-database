caption: 'Contrasting mechanisms of thrombosis in myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs)
  and chronic inflammatory diseases: a cell‐driven versus cytokine‐driven model. The
  primary cause of thrombosis in MPNs is clonal hematopoietic cell expansion and hyperactivation.
  By contrast, the primary cause of thrombosis in chronic inflammatory diseases is
  massive release of inflammatory cytokines from inflamed tissues. The key player
  in MPNs is the JAK2 mutation (JAK2V617F), that causes the JAK/STAT pathway to be
  constitutively active in newly forming blood cells (platelets, leukocytes, and erythrocytes),
  resulting in cell intrinsic abnormalities, in addition to the aberrant expansion.
  These abnormalities comprise increased deformability of erythrocytes, up‐regulation
  of thromboinflammatory genes such as P‐selectin, release of microparticles containing
  TF and excessive cytokine production (IL‐6 and TNF) establishing a proinflammatory
  state. These cytokines together with leukocyte: platelet aggregates activate ECs,
  inducing a proadhesive and procoagulant state. In chronic diseases, the key players
  are inflammatory cytokines (IL‐6, TNF, and IL‐17) leading to a hypercoagulation
  state by directly affecting ECs (the primary site of damage). The combination of
  IL‐17 and TNF affects these cells by inducing a proinflammatory state and by upregulating
  adhesion molecules (VCAM‐1 and ICAM‐1) and TF, whereas suppressing natural anticoagulants
  such as thrombomodulin. In MPNs, JAK2 inhibitors directly target the JAK2V617F mutation
  in blood‐forming cells, which in turn normalizes blood cell counts and reduces their
  prothrombotic properties. In inflammatory diseases such as in rheumatoid arthritis,
  JAKi can inhibit the downstream effects of some cytokines like IL‐6, but they do
  not directly inhibit the prothrombotic effects of TNF and IL‐17 on ECs, therefore
  JAKi might be less effective at preventing cardiovascular disease in this context.
  CAM, cell adhesion molecule; EC, endothelial cell; IL, interleukin; JAKi, JAK inhibitor;
  TF, tissue factor; TNF, tumor necrosis factor; TM, thrombomodulin'
