caption: 'Hypothetical model illustrating the transcriptional and metabolic crosstalk
  between monoterpenoid indole alkaloid (MIA) biosynthesis and root system architecture
  (RSA) regulation in Rauvolfia serpentina. This schematic represents a proposed regulatory
  pathway where environmental stimuli such as abiotic/biotic stress or ABA signaling
  activate the ABA-responsive transcription factor ABI3/VP1, which in turn transcriptionally
  upregulates WRKY25.WRKY25 functions as a central node regulating both secondary
  metabolism and developmental gene expression. On the left, the MIA biosynthetic
  pathway is initiated in leaf tissues with the decarboxylation of tryptophan by Tryptophan
  Decarboxylase (TDC), and the hydroxylation of geraniol by Geraniol 10-Hydroxylase
  (G10H), leading to the synthesis of loganin. Further steps involving Secologanin
  Synthase and Strictosidine Synthase (SLS) yield strictosidine, the universal precursor
  for downstream MIAs such as ajmaline and reserpine. On the right side, WRKY25 might
  also regulates genes involved in root development including LATERAL ROOT DENSITY
  (LRD), AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORS (ARF), and PIN-FORMED (PIN) auxin efflux transporters,
  which modulate root growth, lateral root formation, and architecture in response
  to stress signals. The metabolite strictosidine and its flux toward MIA biosynthesis
  may also indirectly influence RSA by modulating auxin signaling or defense-related
  feedback loops. Together, this model suggests a transcriptionally integrated framework
  linking MIA biosynthesis with RSA plasticity, enabling adaptive responses to environmental
  cues. Note: Dashed arrows indicate proposed or hypothesized regulatory influences
  that require experimental validation'
