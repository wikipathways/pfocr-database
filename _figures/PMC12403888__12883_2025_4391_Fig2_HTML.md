caption: 'Basic neural circuitry between the cerebral cortex and basal ganglia. The
  normal and hemichoreic conditions are presented in the panels a and b, respectively.
  To make a voluntary movement, neural inputs arising from the cerebral cortex initially
  suppress the thalamus extensively via the hyperdirect pathway (constituents, cortex–STN–GPi/SNr;
  a). Subsequently, using the direct pathway (cortex–striatum–GPi/SNr), the inputs
  focally disinhibit the thalamus, which is otherwise strongly restrained by GABAergic
  projections from the GPi/SNr. Finally, the inputs from the cortex suppress the thalamus
  extensively again through the indirect pathway (cortex–striatum–GPe–STN–GPi/SNr).
  Given that glutamatergic neurons in the thalamus project excitatory fibers to the
  cortex, the focal disinhibition of the thalamus evokes excitatory effects on the
  cortex and is associated with the voluntary movement; meanwhile, the extensive suppression
  of the thalamus serves to restrain unnecessary movements. Therefore, disruption
  of the hyperdirect and/or indirect pathways may lead to unnecessary movements (i.e.,
  hemichorea; panel b indicates the hemichoreic condition due to STN damage, which
  is one of the most probable conditions in the presented case). The lesion is expressed
  with an X mark. The bold and broken lines indicate increased and decreased neural
  activity, respectively. Closed circles: gamma-aminobutyric acidergic inhibitory
  neurons; GABA: gamma-aminobutyric acid; glu: glutamic acid; GPe: globus pallidus
  externa; GPi: globus pallidus interna; Open circles: glutamatergic excitatory neurons;
  SNr: substantia nigra pars reticulata; STN: subthalamic nucleus'
