caption: Schematic representation of the molecular mechanisms of lactylation modification
  in rheumatic and autoimmune diseases. In RA, elevated H3K9la directly upregulates
  the expression of IL-6 and MMP genes, aggravating inflammation and cartilage destruction.
  ART enhances the interaction between histone acetyltransferase p300 and pyruvate
  PKM2, promoting PKM2 lactylation at the K433 site, preventing its nuclear translocation,
  and suppressing the proliferation and invasion of FLS. GAS reduces H3K9la levels
  by inducing degradation of the histone acetyltransferase KAT8, decreasing IL-6 expression,
  and mitigating synovial inflammation. In SLE, excessive lactate induces lactylation
  of cyclic GMP–AMP synthase (cGAS), preventing its interaction with the E3 ubiquitin
  ligase MARCHF5 and blocking its degradation. This stabilization of cGAS amplifies
  the type I interferon (IFN-I) response and promotes systemic inflammation. Furthermore,
  lactylation of the transcription factor PBX1 at lysine 40 (K40) enhances its binding
  to the E3 ubiquitin ligase TRIM21, triggering K29/K63-linked ubiquitination and
  proteasomal degradation. Reduced PBX1 levels relieve transcriptional repression
  of the cell cycle inhibitor CDKN1B (p27), leading to abnormal mesangial cell proliferation
  and progression of lupus nephritis. In EAU, lactylation of Ikaros at lysine 164
  (K164) dynamically regulates the expression of IL-2, IL-4, TLR4, and RUNX1, reshaping
  Th17 differentiation and promoting ocular inflammation. Lactylation of the transcription
  factor YY1 enhances microglial proliferation and migration, and activates the transcription
  of pathogenic genes, including STAT3 and CCL5, directly exacerbating uveal tissue
  injury. In OA, lactylation of UGDH at lysine 6 (K6) disrupts its interaction with
  STAT1, relieving repression of MAP3K8 transcription, activating the MAPK signaling
  cascade, and driving ECM degradation. H3K18la promotes glycolytic flux, establishing
  a self-reinforcing cycle of lactate accumulation, while also upregulating the lipid
  metabolism enzyme ACSL4, which induces ferroptotic cell death in chondrocytes. In
  UC, multiple histone lactylation sites, including H3K18la, H3K23la, H4K8la, and
  H4K12la, are significantly upregulated, generating a pro-inflammatory epigenetic
  landscape. H4K12la in CD4⁺ T cells is enriched within the promoter region of PKM2,
  promoting PKM2 expression and subsequent activation of the STAT3 signaling pathway.
  This cascade drives Th17 differentiation, suppresses Treg formation, and intensifies
  mucosal inflammation and barrier injury
