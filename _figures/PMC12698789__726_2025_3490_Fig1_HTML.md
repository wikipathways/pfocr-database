caption: Mechanistic overview of gliadin-induced immune activation and the modulatory
  roles of conditionally essential amino acids, cysteine, and tyrosine in celiac disease.
  In genetically susceptible individuals, gliadin peptides from dietary gluten reach
  the intestinal lamina propria through compromised epithelial barrier integrity.
  These peptides are deamidated by tTG2, which increases their immunogenicity. APCs
  present the resulting deamidated peptides via HLA-DQ2 molecules to CD4⁺ T cells.
  This antigen recognition triggers Th1 and Th2 immune responses, driving inflammation
  and mucosal damage. Within the intestinal epithelium, specific CEAAs, Cys, and Tyr,
  including Cys, Gly, Asn, Gln, Asp, and Glu, support barrier restoration by enhancing
  TJ integrity, while Cys, Gly, Gln, and Asp also inhibit oxidative stress. Arg may
  contribute via the iNOS pathway, producing NO acting as pro-inflammatory mediator.
  Additionally, Gln and Asp exert beneficial effects on gut microbiota composition.
  Tyr serves as a precursor for dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, and serotonin.
  Altered Tyr metabolism may impair neurotransmitter synthesis, potentially contributing
  to CNS symptoms in untreated patients. Glu also contributes to the neurological
  manifestations of CD (tTG2 Tissue transglutaminase 2, Pro Proline, Ser serine, Tyr
  tyrosine, Gln glutamine, Glu glutamate, Gly glycine, Cys cysteine, Asp aspartate,
  Asn asparagine, Arg arginine, iNOS inducible nitric oxide synthase, NO nitric oxide,
  CIT citrulline, APC antigen-presenting cell, HLA-DQ2 human leukocyte antigen DQ2,
  CD4⁺ cluster of differentiation 4 positive T cell, Th1/Th2 T-helper cell subsets,
  IL-15 Interleukin-15, TJ tight junctions, CNS central nervous system)
