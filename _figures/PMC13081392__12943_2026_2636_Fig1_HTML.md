caption: 'Calibrating the RT-cGAS-STING axis to drive cold-to-hot tumor transformation.
  A Initiation via genotoxic stress and negative regulators: Radiotherapy (RT) induces
  nuclear DNA damage (micronuclei formation) and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) leakage.
  cGAS sensing is tightly constrained by intrinsic inhibitory mechanisms, including
  nucleosome-mediated inactivation and apoptotic Caspase-3/7/9–mediated pathway cleavage.
  Pathway activation is governed by a strict dose-fractionation threshold: moderate
  fractionation (6-8 Gy × 3) bypasses TREX1-mediated DNA degradation, whereas ultra-high
  single doses (12-18 Gy) induce TREX1, attenuating the pathway. B Signal propagation:
  2′3′-cGAMP functions as an immunotransmitter, spreading to bystander cells via transporters.
  Binding of cGAMP triggers STING translocation from the ER to ER-Golgi intermediate
  compartments. This recruits the TBK1/IRF3 complex to drive transcription of type
  I IFNs (IFN-β) and chemokines (CXCL9/10). C Dichotomous outcomes and spatial heterogeneity:
  (C1) Positive remodeling: Transient activation promotes dendritic cell (DC) maturation
  and vascular normalization (upregulating VCAM-1/ICAM-1). RT-induced neutrophil NETosis
  forms a barrier restricting circulating CD8⁺ T cell infiltration, rendering tumor
  clearance dependent on reinvigorated tissue-resident CD8⁺ T cells. (C2) Negative
  feedback: Sustained or dysregulated signaling activates STING checkpoints, including
  adaptive PD-L1 upregulation, myeloid skewing (MDSC/Treg enrichment), and tissue
  fibrosis. Translational levers (bottom right) summarize strategies-optimized dosing,
  precise sequencing (RT→STING→ICIs), targeted delivery, and regulator inhibition-to
  maximize synergy while mitigating toxicity. Created with Adobe Illustrator 2024'
