caption: 'GSDM Proteins as Convergent Executors of Pyroptosis to Potentiate Cancer
  Immunotherapy. The Gasdermin (GSDM) protein family serves as a central executioner
  that converts diverse cytotoxic signals into pyroptosis, thereby reshaping the tumor-immune
  microenvironment and potentiating immune checkpoint blockade. This schematic underscores
  the pivotal role of GSDM proteins (GSDMA–E) in transforming distinct tumor cell
  death stimuli into immunogenic pyroptosis, a process essential for initiating robust
  antitumor immunity. Specific upstream signals selectively engage individual GSDM
  members: GSDMC/E: Apoptosis-to-pyroptosis switch: Hypoxia (via HIF-1α/STAT3) or
  chemotherapy/radiotherapy upregulates GSDMC expression. TNF-α/TNFR signaling activates
  caspase-8 to cleave GSDMC, while chemotherapy induces caspase-3–mediated cleavage
  of GSDME-collectively diverting apoptotic signaling toward lytic, immunostimulatory
  pyroptotic death. GSDMA/B: Lymphocyte-mediated killing: Cytotoxic T cells and NK
  cells deliver granzymes (GzmA/B) through perforin to directly cleave GSDMB, triggering
  pyroptosis in target cells. GSDMA can also be activated under conditions of cellular
  stress, contributing to immune-mediated tumor clearance. GSDMD: Canonical inflammasome
  pathway: Pathogen- or damage-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs/DAMPs) activate
  NLRP3 and related inflammasomes, leading to caspase-1 activation and proteolytic
  cleavage of GSDMD. Upon cleavage, GSDM-N-terminal fragments oligomerize and insert
  into the plasma membrane to form functional pores, resulting in cell swelling, osmotic
  lysis, and the release of DAMPs, IL-1β, IL-18, and tumor antigens. This cascade
  establishes a pro-inflammatory and immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment. Released
  antigens are taken up by dendritic cells (DCs), which mature and prime tumor-specific
  cytotoxic T cells. However, this inflammatory response frequently drives adaptive
  immune resistance, including PD-L1 upregulation on tumor cells. Combining pyroptosis-inducing
  therapies—targeting any GSDM member—with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade effectively counteracts
  this feedback inhibition, unleashing potent and sustained T-cell responses and yielding
  synergistic antitumor efficacy. Conclusion: The GSDM family functions as a critical
  signaling nexus that integrates multiple cytotoxic inputs into a unified pathway
  of immunogenic cell death. Harnessing this mechanism represents a powerful therapeutic
  strategy to overcome tumor immune evasion and enhance the potency of cancer immunotherapy'
