caption: Heme synthesis occurs in the mitochondria and the cytoplasm and involves
  an eight-step enzymatic pathway. Porphyria, an inherited genetic disorder that results
  in accumulation of heme precursors, is the result of a defect in one of the enzymes
  responsible for heme production. The acute hepatic porphyrias are characterized
  by overproduction and accumulation of porphyrin precursors in the liver. Porphyrias
  with hepatic manifestation are indicated by callout boxes next to the enzyme that
  contains the disease-causing defect. EPP is included in this review because porphyrin
  that originates in erythropoietic cells accumulates in the liver causing significant
  hepatobiliary involvement in a subset of patients; however, it is clinically classified
  as a cutaneous porphyria
