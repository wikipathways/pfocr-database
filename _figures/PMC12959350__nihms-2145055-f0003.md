caption: This schematic illustrates the anti-inflammatory mechanisms by which neuroactive
  steroids, including allopregnanolone (3α,5α-THP) and pregnenolone (Preg), modulate
  Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling. Activation of TLR4 by lipopolysaccharide
  (LPS) and the MD2 co-receptor initiates a MyD88-dependent cascade that leads to
  the activation of TRAF6, TAK1, and the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, promoting
  the transcription of proinflammatory mediators such as MCP-1 and TNFα. High mobility
  group box 1 (HMGB1), a known endogenous TLR4 ligand, further amplifies the inflammatory
  response. Neuroactive steroids inhibit this proinflammatory signaling at multiple
  points. Allopregnanolone and pregnenolone interfere with the interaction between
  LPS and the TLR4/MD2 complex, thereby preventing MyD88 recruitment and downstream
  activation of the TRAF6-TAK1-NFκB axis. In parallel, allopregnanolone acting via
  GABA-A receptor α2 subunits induces protein kinase A (PKA) signaling, leading to
  increased phosphorylation of CREB (pCREB), which exerts anti-inflammatory effects
  by inhibiting pNFκB activity and nuclear translocation. These actions reduce transcription
  of proinflammatory mediators and promote nuclear sequestration of HMGB1, further
  dampening the inflammatory cascade. Collectively, this figure highlights the dual
  actions of neuroactive steroids in suppressing TLR4-driven inflammation through
  both receptor-level interference and modulation of intracellular signaling pathways,
  underscoring their therapeutic potential in neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative
  disorders characterized by chronic inflammation. Modified from Balan et al (2019).
