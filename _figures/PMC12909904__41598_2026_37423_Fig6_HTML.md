caption: 'Schematic representation of the hypothetical action of Myo-Ins on the phosphatidylinositol
  cycle and Gq/PLC pathway. (1) Receptor activation. Typically, an extracellular signalling
  molecule binds a G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR). Ligand binding promotes GDP/GTP
  exchange on the monomeric Gq α-subunit, which then dissociates from the βγ subunits.
  The active Gq-GTP complex stimulates membrane-bound phospholipase C (PLC). (2) Signal
  transduction. The plasma membrane contains locally synthesised PI(4,5)P₂, which
  can be produced in a stepwise manner from phosphatidylinositol (PI). This process
  begins with the phosphorylation of PI to PI4P, followed by the conversion of PI4P
  into PI(4,5)P₂. PLC-mediated cleavage of PI(4,5)P₂ generates two second messengers:
  diacylglycerol (DAG), which remains in the membrane, and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate
  (IP₃), which diffuses into the cytoplasm and binds IP₃ receptors (IP₃Rs) on the
  smooth ER. IP₃ binding triggers Ca²⁺ release from the ER into the cytoplasm. (3)
  Inactivation and recycling. IP₃ is dephosphorylated to inositol, which can be reused
  to resynthesize PI and then PI(4,5)P₂, completing the cycle. (4) Myo-Ins uptake
  and potentiation of signalling. Cells can import extracellular Myo-Ins through specific
  transporters: SLC5A3/SMIT1: Na⁺/Myo-Ins co-transporter; HMIT: H⁺/Myo-Ins co-transporter.
  Enhanced intracellular Myo-Ins availability supports rapid resynthesis of PI(4,5)P₂,
  thereby potentiating the generation of DAG and IP₃ upon agonist stimulation, leading
  to a stronger and more sustained cellular response. This schematic representation
  illustrates a hypothetical model of Myo-Ins–dependent potentiation of PLC/IP₃ signalling
  and intracellular Ca²⁺ release'
