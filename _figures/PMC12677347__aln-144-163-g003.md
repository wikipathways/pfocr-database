caption: Key rationales for Ang II therapy in vasodilatory shock. (A) Dysfunction
  of the ACE1/Ang II/AT1R axis in septic shock. In septic shock, suppression of the
  classical RAS pathway is characterized by decreased ACE1 activity, increased Ang
  II degradation via DPP3, and reduced AT1R expression, leading to markedly low circulating
  Ang II levels and elevated plasma renin concentrations. (B) Previous exposure to
  RAS inhibitors and Ang II responsiveness. Previous exposure to RAS inhibitors influences
  the response to Ang II infusion. While both ACEi and ARBs increase renin levels,
  only in patients previously exposed to ACEi is Ang II reduced, providing the rationale
  for its use in this context. (C) Renal hemodynamic benefit of Ang II during shock.
  Ang II preferentially acts on AT1R in the efferent arteriole, increasing glomerular
  filtration pressure and rate. (D) Impaired Ang II signaling during extracorporeal
  circulation. Blood passage through extracorporeal circuits limits Ang I exposure
  to pulmonary ACE1, reducing Ang II synthesis and supporting its use in vasoplegia
  after cardiac surgery or mechanical circulatory support implantation. ACE1, angiotensin-converting
  enzyme 1; ACEi, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors; Ang I, angiotensin I;
  Ang II, angiotensin II; ARBs, angiotensin receptor blockers; AT1R, angiotensin II
  type 1 receptor; DPP3, dipeptidyl peptidase 3; RAS, renin–angiotensin system
