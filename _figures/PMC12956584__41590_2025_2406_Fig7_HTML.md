caption: High levels of GDF-15, released in response to pregnancy or in response to
  therapeutic delivery, or secreted from the inflamed tissue during neuroinflammation,
  are sensed by GFRAL-expressing neurons in the brainstem. Activation of a polysynaptic
  pathway downstream of GFRAL+ neurons stimulates β-adrenergic signaling in the spleen.
  As a consequence, TH+ neurons release NE and activate β2-adrenergic receptors (ADRB2)
  on CD4+ T cells, which dampens surface expression of the integrin LFA-1 and reduces
  activation and proliferation. This modulation of autoreactive T cells prevents transmigration
  to the CNS, thereby limiting neuroinflammation
