caption: A working model illustrating the cellular, physiological, and biochemical
  mechanisms underlying polyploid stress resilience.Molecules that show significant
  increases in polyploids are highlighted in red, whereas those that decrease in polyploids
  are highlighted in blue. The bottom left panel depicts reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  scavenging, gibberellin signaling, proline biosynthesis, and the SOS pathway in
  polyploids under stress. Plant stress induces the accumulation of ROS as potential
  signaling molecules; however, ROS can also damage membrane integrity and destabilize
  cellular compartments. Loss of cellular compartmentalization leads to ion leakage
  and physiological perturbations that affect plant growth and development. Redox
  signaling and antioxidant enzyme responses are schematized, as well as membrane
  destabilization resulting from lipid peroxidation. The bottom right panel illustrates
  abscisic acid (ABA) biosynthesis and the major ABA signaling pathway in polyploid
  plants under stress. In the presence of ABA, PYR/PYL/RCAR receptors bind ABA and
  engage with PP2Cs (negative regulators), releasing SnRK2 kinases (positive regulators).
  SnRK2s are activated through phosphorylation of their activation loop by Raf-like
  kinases or casein kinase 2. Once activated, SnRK2s can phosphorylate various downstream
  substrates, including transcription factors, ion channels, and enzymes, ultimately
  triggering ABA-responsive processes. See Table 2 for abbreviations
