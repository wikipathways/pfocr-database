caption: 'Short‐chain acyl‐PTMs play a vital role in cancer progression by regulating
  protein stability, enzyme activity, and protein nuclear localization. (A) Short‐chain
  acyl‐PTMs can affect the stability of essential proteins involved in tumor progression.
  Lactylation inhibits protein degradation processes, while succinylation and 2‐hydroxyisobutyrylation
  exert dual effects on protein stability, either stabilizing or promoting degradation.
  (B) Short‐chain acyl‐PTMs regulate the enzyme activity through addition of acyl
  groups (such as succinyl, crotonyl, and lactyl) to key sites in the enzymatic activity
  center to increase or reduce enzyme activity. (C) Short‐chain acyl‐PTMs, including
  succinylation, crotonylation, malonylation, and lactylation, can facilitate the
  nuclear localization of proteins, thereby promoting tumor initiation and progression.
  The modified proteins, modification sites, and induced biological effects have been
  shown in the same color font. PTMs labeled with dotted lines indicate the removal
  of the modification. The “OFF” in red font refers decrease of protein degradation
  or enzyme activity following acylation modification, while the “ON” in red font
  refers increase of protein degradation or enzyme activity. Abbreviations: AARS1/2,
