caption: Schematic illustration of the potential mechanisms of miR-183C in CSN modulating
  PA keratitis through neuroimmune interactions. In Pathway 1, miR-183C regulates
  CSN density through directly targeting neuronal projection- and axon guidance-related
  genes, including Nrp139, 40, 43. In Pathway 2, miR-183C regulates the production
  of chemoattractant neuropeptides by directly targeting the genes encoding these
  molecules, including Cx3cl140, 73. In Pathway 3, our recent unpublished data73 (reported
  in ARVO 2025. Manuscript in preparation) suggested that miR-183C regulates PA-induced
  activation of TG sensory neurons and neuropeptide secretion, including Cx3cl1, by
  targeting genes encoding receptor molecules involved in PA-induced sensory neuron
  activation, e.g. Tlr4 and Fpr173. The effects of all three pathways work in concert
  to control the level of chemoattractant neuropeptides in the cornea, e.g. Cx3cl139,
  40, which, in turn, modulates the corneal innate immunity, including both number
  of CRMCs at steady-state40, 41 and myeloid-cell infiltration during PA keratitis39,
  43. By modulating corneal innate immunity, miR-183C in CSN imposes significant impact
  on the development of PA keratitis, because infiltrating PMN play a major role in
  bacterial clearance, as well as the destruction of corneal sensory nerves and other
  corneal tissue, contributing to the severity of the disease. Notes in smaller font
  are knowledges referred from previous publications or conference abstract with reference
  numbers specified
