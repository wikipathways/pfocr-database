caption: 'Obesity-derived fatty acids remodel the tumor microenvironment (TME) and
  regulate anti-tumor immunity. Palmitic acid (PA) enters T cells via CD36, suppresses
  Akt/mTOR signaling, disrupts Ca2+ signaling, impairs mitochondrial function, and
  promotes T cell exhaustion, whereas oleic acid (OA) counteracts these effects. In
  selected preclinical settings, linoleic acid (LA) has been reported to enter CD8+
  T cells through SLC27A1, enhance ER-to-mitochondria Ca2+ transfer and oxidative
  phosphorylation (OXPHOS), and be associated with a more memory-like phenotype. However,
  in distinct biological contexts, LA may also impair T-cell fitness. In naïve T cells,
  LA-rich dietary exposure has been associated with E-FABP-mediated mitochondrial
  dysfunction, increased lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation,
  and apoptosis. In CD4+ T cells, LA has also been reported to activate PPAR-α-dependent
  carnitine palmitoyltransferase (CPT) programs, increase mitochondrial fatty acid
  utilization, and promote oxidative stress and apoptosis. Together, these observations
  support context-dependent and potentially bidirectional effects of LA on T cell
  function and anti-tumor immunity. Akt: Protein kinase B; CD36:Cluster of differentiation
  36; CD4+ T cell: Cluster of differentiation 4-positive T cell; CD8+ T cell: Cluster
  of differentiation 8-positive T cell; CPT1/2: Carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1/2;
  E-FABP: Epidermal fatty acid-binding protein; ER: Endoplasmic reticulum; FAO: Fatty
  acid oxidation; Glut1: Glucose transporter 1; MERC/MAM: Mitochondria-endoplasmic
  reticulum contact sites/mitochondria-associated membranes; mTOR: Mechanistic target
  of rapamycin; OXPHOS: Oxidative phosphorylation; PL-: Phospholipid; PLO-: Phospholipase;
  PLOO-: Phospholipid peroxyl radical; PLOOH: Phospholipid hydroperoxide; PPAR-α:Peroxisome
  proliferator-activated receptor alpha; PPP: Pentose phosphate pathway; ROS: Reactive
  oxygen species; SLC27A1: Solute carrier family 27 member 1; TAGs: Triacylglycerols;
  Treg cells: Regulatory T cells'
