caption: 'Hormonal signalling pathways regulating BAHD acyltransferase gene expression
  under biotic and abiotic stresses. The diagram illustrates the hormonal signalling
  pathways regulating BAHD acyltransferase gene expression under environmental and
  biotic stresses. ABA Pathway (blue): Osmotic stress triggers ABA accumulation, which
  binds PYR/PYL/RCAR receptors and inhibits PP2C phosphatases. This releases SnRK2
  kinases, enabling phosphorylation of ABF/AREB transcription factors. Activated ABF/AREBs
  bind ABRE motifs in BAHD promoters, enhancing expression linked to cuticle and suberin
  biosynthesis. An ABA‐independent branch involving SnRK2 (class I) also activates
  DRE motifs, mediating drought‐responsive BAHD expression. JA Pathway (green): Wounding
  or herbivory induces JA biosynthesis, which is converted by JAR1 into the active
  JA‐Ile form. JA‐Ile, via the SCF^COI1 receptor complex, promotes degradation of
  JAZ repressors, releasing MYC2/3/4 transcription factors. These bind JA‐responsive
  elements in BAHD promoters, stimulating phenolic amide synthesis. Negative feedback
  mechanisms include CYP94B3/C1 hydroxylation and IAR3/ILL6 cleavage of JA conjugates,
  attenuating JA signalling. SA Pathway (red): Biotrophic pathogen attack elevates
  SA levels, leading to nuclear translocation of NPR1. NPR1 represses MYC2‐driven
  JA responses by downregulating JA biosynthesis genes such as CAT2 and ACX2/3. Pathogens
  counteract this by producing coronatine (COR), a JA mimic, which activates SCF^COI1,
  leading to JAZ degradation and release of MYC2. MYC2 subsequently induces ANAC transcription
  factors (ANAC019/055/072), which activate SA‐inactivating enzymes BSMT1 and SAGT1,
  converting active SA into inactive derivatives'
