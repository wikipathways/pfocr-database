caption: The regulatory functions of rG4s in cancer cells exhibit spatial specificity.
  In the cytoplasm, rG4s in the 5′UTR form stable structural barriers that impede
  ribosomal scanning and suppress translation, while under certain conditions they
  sustain IRES-dependent translation to activate specific mRNAs. rG4s can also promote
  uORF translation, thereby indirectly inhibiting translation of the main ORF. The
  mTOR signaling pathway regulates the translation of mitochondrial mRNAs to meet
  the metabolic demands of cancer cells. rG4s within ribosomal protein mRNAs inhibit
  ribosomal protein production, globally modulating translational capacity. hnRNP
  A1 binds to rG4s in RON mRNA, relieving translational repression and activating
  RON protein synthesis. Circular RNA circCLASP2 acts as a molecular platform to recruit
  the DHX9 helicase to unwind rG4 structures, promoting PCMT1 translation.In the nucleus,
  rG4s recruit RNA-binding proteins to modulate pre-mRNA splicing, promoting the generation
  of A-Raf isoforms and thereby reprogramming RAF–MEK–ERK/MAPK signaling activity.
  PKM2 binds rG4 structures on pre-mRNAs and inhibits the activity of rG4-unwinding
  proteins, leading to upregulation of the rG4ome and activation of EMT and cell migration
  pathways. Telomere elongation is accompanied by elevated levels of the long noncoding
  RNA TERRA, whose rG4 structures drive gene reprogramming and significantly downregulate
  innate immunity-related genes, including STAT1, ISG15, and OAS3
