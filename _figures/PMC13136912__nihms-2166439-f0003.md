caption: The TNF-BDNF cascade in the trigeminal ganglia in the peripheral nervous
  system and the roles of CXCL1 in the neuroimmune modulation of central sensitization.
  TNF-α binds to receptors in the trigeminal ganglia, leading to the activation of
  PKA and p38 MAPK through cAMP formation by adenylyl cyclase. These two signaling
  pathways cause phosphorylation of CREB in the cell nucleus, leading to BDNF synthesis.
  Subsequently, BDNF binds to the TrkB receptor, promoting the synthesis and translocation
  of NMDA receptors to the neuronal membrane. In the central nervous system, CXCL1
  released by reactive astrocytes attracts neutrophils, which become activated and
  release TNF-α. CXCL1 activates CXCR2 in neurons and triggers the ERK (MAPK) signaling
  pathway, leading to COX-2 mRNA and protein synthesis, which produces PGs. CXCL1
  facilitates excitatory currents induced by NMDA receptors, increasing the depolarization
  threshold and inducing action potentials in the nociceptive pathway. TNF-α, Tumor
