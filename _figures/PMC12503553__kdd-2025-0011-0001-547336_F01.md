caption: Schematic of the role of circulating factor candidates in post-transplant
  recurrence of FSGS. The main lesion site of post-transplant recurrent FSGS is in
  the glomeruli of the kidney, while podocyte injury is the core mechanism (red asterisk).
  suPAR, released from the plasma membrane by uPAR via cleavage of GPI anchor, binds
  to and activates αvβ3 integrins on podocytes, thereby promoting small GTPase activation
  and cytoskeletal rearrangement. Both anti-CD40 antibody and sCD40L could promote
  the pathogenesis of FSGS, among which anti-CD40 antibody could synergistically enhance
  the pathogenic effect with suPAR, and sCD40L could inhibit the expression of nephrin
  and thus lead to cytoskeletal recombination. CLCF-1 promotes FSGS progression through
  JAK/STAT signaling pathway, and heterodimers composed of CLCF-1 and CRLF1 could
  block this effect. ApoA-Ib is increased in the urine of patients with recurrent
  FSGS and has predictive value for recurrence. The nephrin-neph paired combination
  constitutes the molecular sieve portion of the slit diaphragm between podocyte foot
  processes, and anti-nephrin antibody expression is increased in post-transplant
  recurrent FSGS patients. β-arrestin2 and ShcA could promote nephrin endocytosis
  and internalization, while podocin could localize nephrin to the cell membrane
