caption: 'The functions of the cGAS-STING pathway in AD. A. the excessive engulfment
  of Aβ and tau proteins leads to the cytosolic leakage of mtDNA, which activates
  the cGAS-STING pathway in microglia. This aberrant activation of the cGAS-STING
  pathway in microglia drives the exacerbation of neuroinflammation and impairs the
  clearance of Aβ and tau, creating a negative feedback loop that contributes to the
  progression of AD. B. the complement protein C3 marks weak or unnecessary synapses
  for removal. Microglia, which express high levels of the C3R, recognize and bind
  to synapses tagged with C3b, facilitating the phagocytosis of presynaptic terminals.
  Excessive pruning of synapses can lead to the loss of functional synapses and cognitive
  deficits. C. HSV-1 DNA activates cGAS, resulting in the production of cGAMP, which
  stimulates STING. In turn, STING recruits and activates TBK1, leading to the phosphorylation
  of tau via CDKs and SCR/Fyn. This phosphorylated tau suppresses the expression of
  viral proteins (e.g., ICP27) and promotes neuronal survival. The acute phosphorylation
  of tau induced by viral infection initially serves as a protective response to limit
  viral spread and preserve neurons. However, chronic tau phosphorylation may promote
  tau toxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, and the generation of ROS, exacerbating
  the progression of AD. AD: Alzheimer’s Disease; Aβ: amyloid-beta; C3R: C3 receptor;
  CDKs: cyclin-dependent kinases; HSV-1: cytosolic herpes simplex virus type 1; ROS:
  reactive oxygen species; SCR/Fyn: SRC family kinases. The figure was created with
  BioRender.com'
