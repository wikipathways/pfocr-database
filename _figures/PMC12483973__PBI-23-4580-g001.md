caption: 'DNA methylation and siRNA contribute to soybean adaptation under seasonal
  environmental changes. Soybean plants experience significant environmental variation
  between South Winter (SW) and South Summer (SS), with SW characterized by shorter
  day length and lower temperature, and SS by longer days and higher temperature.
  The model illustrates the involvement of various epigenetic mechanisms in regulating
  soybean growth and flowering time across seasonal conditions. Circadian Clock and
  Gene Expression: The circadian clock plays a key role in regulating epigenetic changes
  through rhythmic expression of genes involved in growth and development. E2 (a key
  repressor of flowering) responds differently under SW and SS conditions, influencing
  gene expression by modulating CHG DNA methylation levels in the gene body regions.
  CHH Methylation and TE Regulation: Compared to SS conditions, the histone deacetylase
  HDA6 and RdDM pathway are activated, leading to chromatin condensation and increased
  CHH methylation under SW conditions. This methylation pattern represses TEs and
  maintains the stability of TE elements. siRNA Production and Regulation: The production
  of small RNAs, particularly 22‐nt siRNAs, is lower under SW conditions, which plays
  a central role in regulating the expression of TEs and other genes. In SS, the abundance
  of siRNAs is increased, indicating a shift in the regulation of transposable elements
  and related genes. The model suggests that CHH methylation and small RNA production
  are key drivers of phenotypic variation in soybean under different seasonal conditions,
  with SW promoting dwarfism and late flowering, and SS favouring taller growth and
  early flowering'
