caption: 'Bidirectional communication between the gut and the brain: the roles of
  the vagal and spinal afferent pathways, the circulatory system, and the HPA axis.
  In pathological conditions, both intestinal and BBB permeability are increased,
  allowing gut-derived molecules such as Trp derivatives, LPS, cytokines, and immune
  cells to translocate into systemic circulation through the compromised intestinal
  barrier. These molecules subsequently reach cerebral regions via blood flow. Simultaneously,
  certain molecules traverse the BBB to access the CNS, where they modulate neuronal
  function and synaptic plasticity. In the HPA axis pathway, gut microbiota dysbiosis
  and disrupted Trp metabolism trigger HPA axis hyperactivity, accompanied by aberrant
  elevation of corticosterone/cortisol levels that interfere with neuronal homeostasis.
  Through the vagal and spinal afferent pathways, Trp metabolites binds to receptors
  on vagal and spinal afferent neurons, transmitting signals to central neural circuits
  and exerting regulatory effects on brain function'
