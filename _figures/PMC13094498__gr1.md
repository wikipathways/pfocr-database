caption: 'Canonical synaptic functions of 4 synaptic proteins in mature neurons. (A)
  Latrophilin-mediated postsynaptic function. At mature synapses, trans-synaptic interactions
  of latrophilins with their presynaptic partners—including teneurins, FLRTs, and
  α-neurexins—trigger G12/13-, Gq-, or Gs-mediated signaling. This adhesion GPCR signaling
  cascade facilitates synaptic formation or modulates neurotransmitter release. (B)
  Canonical postsynaptic N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) function. NMDARs at
  the postsynaptic density serve as glutamate-gated ion channels critical for synaptic
  plasticity. Ca²⁺ influx through NDARs activates CaMKII to promote α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic
  acid receptor (AMPAR) insertion (long-term potentiation; LTP) or phosphatase pathways
  to drive AMPAR removal (long-term depression; LTD). Additionally, NMDAR-dependent
  Ca²⁺ signaling regulates the actin cytoskeleton, supporting structural spine remodeling.
  (C) Context-dependent synaptic organization by cerebellins. Cerebellin orchestrate
  distinct signaling pathways across brain regions. In the cerebellum, the neurexin–cerebellin–GluD
  complex scaffolds mGluR1-PLC signaling, driving protein kinase C (PKC)-mediated
  AMPAR endocytosis (LTD). In the subiculum and prefrontal cortex, cerebelins signaling
  upregulates NMDAR response and downregulates AMPAR transmission via kinase/phosphatase
  modulation. In the dentate gyrus, cerebellin interactions with Neogenin1 (Neo1)
  and NMDAR activate CaMKII pathways to support LTP. (D) Presynaptic neurexins. Neurexin
  isoforms (α, β, γ) couple to voltage-gated calcium channels (VGCCs) to regulate
  Ca²⁺ influx and neurotransmitter release. Their trans-synaptic binding partners
  dictate postsynaptic outcomes: Neuroligins promote PSD-95-mediated synapse maturation;
  LRRTMs enhance AMPAR recruitment and synapse differentiation; and cerebellin–GluD
  complexes specify synaptic properties'
