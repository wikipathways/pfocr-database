caption: Crosstalk between the cGAS-STING and Hippo-YAP signaling pathways. Activation
  of the cytosolic DNA sensor cGAS by dsDNA leads to the synthesis of cGAMP, which
  subsequently activates the adaptor protein STING located on the endoplasmic reticulum
  and Golgi apparatus. STING activation promotes IRF3 phosphorylation and nuclear
  translocation, thereby inducing the expression of genes involved in the immune response.
  Meanwhile, cGAS-STING signaling upregulates MST1, a core kinase in the Hippo pathway,
  which phosphorylates and activates the LATS1/2-MOB1 complex. This activation results
  in the phosphorylation of YAP/TAZ, promoting their sequestration by 14–3-3 proteins
  and subsequent degradation through the proteasomal pathway. The inhibition of nuclear
  YAP/TAZ-TEAD transcriptional activity reduces vascular remodeling and inflammation.
  Collectively, this figure illustrates how cGAS-STING activation restrains Hippo-YAP
  signaling to modulate vascular homeostasis and limit pathological vascular remodeling
