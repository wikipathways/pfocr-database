caption: Methionine restriction (MetR) initiates a variety of adaptive responses that
  collectively promote stress resistance and delay aging. Limitation of methionine
  (Met) availability is sensed as amino acid deprivation which activates integrated
  stress response (ISR) through general control nonderepressible 2 (GCN2) and induction
  of activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4). Reduced intracellular Met decreases
  synthesis of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the universal methyl donor, thereby modulating
  the activity of multiple classes of methyltransferases. This leads to widespread
  epigenetic reprogramming, such as hypomethylation of DNA and RNA, as well as altered
  histone methylation patterns (e.g., reduced H3K4me3, H3K27me3, and H3K9me2). MetR
  also promotes NRF2-dependent antioxidant responses, enhancing the expression of
  cytoprotective genes regulated by antioxidant response elements (AREs). Systemically,
  MetR reduces circulating insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) while increasing fibroblast
  growth factor 21 (FGF-21) and adiponectin, shifting the system towards improved
  insulin sensitivity, lipid metabolism, and metabolic flexibility. At the cellular
  level, MetR stimulates autophagy, facilitating the clearance of damaged proteins
  and organelles, and preserving proteostasis. Furthermore, MetR reduces the accumulation
  of oxidized methionine (methionine sulfoxide, MetSO) by upregulating methionine
  sulfoxide (MetSO) reductase A (MsrA) and limiting oxidative damage. Finally, decreased
  SAM availability attenuates SAMTOR–GATOR1–mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) signaling, and
  promoting catabolic, pro-longevity pathways. Together, these interconnected processes
  highlight the role of MetR as a potent metabolic intervention for enhancing cellular
  resilience and organismal healthspan
