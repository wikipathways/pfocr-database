caption: 'Histone modification reprogramming drives core pathological processes in
  diabetic nephropathy. (A) Inflammatory axis. Persistent hyperglycemia (high glucose)
  and related metabolic stress reshape chromatin states in renal resident cells (tubular
  epithelial cells, mesangial cells) and infiltrating immune cells by dysregulating
  histone-modifying enzymes. Increased HAT activity elevates activating histone acetylation
  at inflammatory loci; for example, PCAF promotes transcription of adhesion molecules
  and chemokines by increasing local activating acetylation and promoter occupancy.
  In parallel, HDAC6 upregulation promotes inflammatory injury and pyroptosis, in
  part via NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Histone methylation reprogramming further
  amplifies inflammation: SET7/9 deposits activating H3K4me1/me2 to facilitate NF-κB
  pathway activation and inflammatory gene expression, while KDM6A removes repressive
  H3K27me3 at pro-inflammatory gene promoters, derepressing transcription and sustaining
  inflammation. (B) Fibrotic axis. High glucose cooperates with the TGF-β1/Smad signaling
  pathway to recruit chromatin modifiers and drive a pro-fibrotic transcriptional
  program. p300/CBP-mediated histone acetylation increases chromatin accessibility
  at profibrotic genes (e.g., PAI-1, p21), promoting assembly of transcriptional complexes
  and extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation. Beyond canonical PTMs, metabolic reprogramming–driven
  lactate accumulation induces histone lactylation, which activates downstream transcriptional
  regulators (e.g., KLF5) and represses epithelial markers, thereby accelerating epithelial–mesenchymal
  transition and fibrosis progression. C Podocyte injury and multicellular epigenetic
  crosstalk. In podocytes, diabetic cues establish persistent epigenetic memory that
  alters expression of key functional genes and injury pathways. Dysregulated HDACs
  and methylation programs (e.g., EZH2-H3K27me3; H3K4me3-associated regulation involving
  DACH1/PTIP) shift the balance from protective to injurious transcriptional states,
  contributing to cytoskeletal dysfunction, podocyte loss, and proteinuria. Histone
  modifications also mediate pathogenic podocyte-endothelial communication: high glucose–stimulated
  podocytes release CCL2 to activate CCR2 on glomerular endothelial cells, triggering
  p38 MAPK/MSK1/2-dependent H3S10 phosphorylation (H3S10ph) and VCAM-1 upregulation,
  thereby amplifying endothelial dysfunction and disrupting the glomerular filtration
  barrier. PCAF p300/CBP-associated factor, ICAM-1 intercellular adhesion molecule
  1, VCAM-1 vascular cell adhesion molecule 1, MCP-1 monocyte chemoattractant protein-1,
  HDAC histone deacetylase, NLRP3 NOD-, LRR- and pyrin domain-containing protein 3,
  KDM6A lysine demethylase 6 A, CitH3 citrullinated histone H3, NETs neutrophil extracellular
  traps, TGF-β transforming growth factor-beta, CBP CREB-binding protein, Smad2/3
  Mothers against decapentaplegic homolog 2/3, PAI-1 plasminogen activator inhibitor-1,
  EZH2 enhancer of zeste homolog 2, Deptor DEP domain-containing mTOR-interacting
  protein, mTOR mechanistic target of rapamycin, ASH2L ASH2 like histone lysine methyltransferase
  complex subunit, HIPK2 homeodomain-interacting protein kinase 2, ADAM12 A disintegrin

  suppressor of variegation 3–9 homolog 1, KLF5 Krüppel-like factor 5, ECM extracellular
