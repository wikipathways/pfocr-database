caption: Integrated pathways for the metabolism of lignin-derived aromatics and single-carbon
  compounds in Methylobacterium species. Lignin is depolymerized, releasing a complex
  mixture of methoxylated aromatic compounds, including vanillate (VA). VA undergoes
  vanillate demethylation, catalyzed by vanillate monooxygenase (VanAB), converting
  it into protocatechuate (PCA) and generating formaldehyde—a toxic byproduct. The
  aromatic rings are cleaved and dissimilated via β-ketoadipate which enters the β-ketoadipate
  pathway. In Methylobacterium species, the usual two-step conversion of β-ketoadipate
  to TCA cycle intermediates is bypassed by a single-step reaction catalyzed by the
  homologous enzyme, labeled here as KCE, directly linking β-ketoadipate to the TCA
  cycle. Meanwhile, formaldehyde is further dissimilated to formate and assimilated
  via serine cycle into biomass. Key enzymes noted in purple text
