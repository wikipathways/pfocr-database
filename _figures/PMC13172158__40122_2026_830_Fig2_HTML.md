caption: Schematic diagram of plasma lipoprotein transport within the body and regulatory
  pathways of neuropathic pain. In humans, lipids are transported from the intestine
  as chylomicron particles and from the liver as VLDL/LDL, and are used by various
  organs, while reverse cholesterol transport occurs via HDL. HDL suppresses the release
  of inflammatory factors by immune cells, thus exerting anti-inflammatory effects.
  It also activates signaling pathways through structures such as ApoA1 and ApoM,
  exerting anti-inflammatory and oxidative stress-suppressing effects. LDL primarily
  binds to the LOX-1 receptor via its oxidized form (oxLDL), activating the NF-κB
  pathway to promote inflammation and directly inducing ROS within neurons. The mechanism
  of VLDL is more complex. On one hand, VLDL can generate large amounts of LPC through
  molecular modifications such as Lp-PLA2, driving inflammation or sensitizing pain
  receptors. On the other hand, VLDL can bind to LPS to promote its clearance or directly
  neutralize specific viruses. (Created in https://BioRender.com)
