caption: 'cAMP signaling pathways and downstream effectors. cAMP is generated through
  two mechanistically distinct pathways: (i) G-protein-dependent manner, the dissociation
  of the Gαs from the dimer Gβγ subunits, leading to the activation of the adenylyl
  cyclases (ACs), which form the cAMP by catalyzing the conversion of ATP into cAMP
  (tmAC-derived cAMP; global signaling at the plasma membrane); and (ii) GPCR-independent
  manner, bicarbonate (HCO3-) and calcium ions activate the soluble adenylyl cyclase
  (sAC) to synthesize cAMP(sAC-derived cAMP; localized microdomain signaling within
  intracellular compartments including the cytoplasm, nucleus, and mitochondria).
  These sAC-generated cAMP microdomains enable compartmentalized signaling that is
  functionally independent from plasma membrane–associated tmACs. However, cAMP is
  degraded to adenosine 5-monophosphate (5’AMP) via phosphodiesterases (PDEs). The
  cAMP regulates three different downstream protein effectors: the protein ion channel,
  associating in the regulation of the heart rate, olfactory transduction, and calcium
  signaling pathways; holoenzyme PKA (two C subunits, two R subunits), C subunits
  phosphorylate transcription factor (CREB) responsible in regulating the CRE genes
  with the association of CBP and CRTCs coactivators, and, the phosphorylation of
  specific targets by PKA particular, due to the A kinase anchor proteins (AKAPs),
  anchoring the PKA in localized sites; EPAC/Rap pathway activate different signaling,
  implicating in many biological and pathophysiological processes via Rap1/2 activation,
  through releasing the Rap1 and Rap2 (Ras family proteins) the GDP and binding to
  GTP to activate the Rap1/2. Furthermore, sAC-derived cAMP microdomains in the nucleus
  and mitochondria contribute to the regulation of metabolic rewiring and apoptosis'
