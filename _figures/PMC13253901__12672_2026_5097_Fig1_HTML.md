caption: 'Mechanistic framework of metformin in prostate cancer progression: linking
  molecular actions to disease stages. Illustrates the dual mechanisms by which metformin
  exerts antitumour effects in prostate cancer through both direct and indirect actions.
  Direct effects include inhibition of oncogenic pathways, such as the SE–TF–PTGR1
  axis, thereby suppressing tumour proliferation and cell-cycle progression. Indirect
  effects involve modulation of tumour-suppressive processes, including the restoration
  of mitochondrial function and redox balance, particularly in the context of tumour
  suppressor deficiency (e.g., NKX3.1 loss). Importantly, these mechanisms may exhibit
  stage-specific relevance during prostate cancer progression. In early-stage or androgen
  receptor (AR)-dependent disease, metformin is more likely to exert antiproliferative
  effects through gene regulation and metabolic modulation. In contrast, in advanced
  or metabolically reprogrammed tumours, its effects are more closely associated with
  mitochondrial stress and redox imbalance, which may contribute to reduced tumour
  survival and enhanced therapeutic sensitivity. Overall, this framework highlights
  that metformin’s antitumour effects are context-dependent and dynamically interact
  with both tumour-intrinsic pathways and disease progression stages'
