caption: 'Chemogenetic and optogenetic control of representative physiological processes.
  (a) Subcellular organelle repositioning. CRISPR-GO repositions genomic loci relative
  to key subnuclear compartments, such as the nuclear periphery, Cajal bodies, and
  promyelocytic leukemia bodies in a chemically inducible manner. LiMETER enables
  light-induced ER–plasma membrane contact site formation and manipulation of intermembrane
  gap distances to regulate the diffusion of membrane proteins at the nanoscale. (b)
  Remote control over ion channels. COSMO-STIM1ct induces calcium influx in mammalian
  cells via caffeine-induced dimerization of the cytosolic STIM1 fragment, leading
  to ORAI1 channel activation. LOCa directly incorporates LOV2 into an engineered
  ORAI1 mutant (H171D/ P245T) for light-induced channel gating. (c) Inducible programmed
  cell death. iCasp9 utilizes AP1903-induced dimerization of FKBP12 (F36V) fused with
  the caspase-9 catalytic domain to activate the apoptosis pathway. The LiPOP1 system
  employs CRY2-induced MLKL oligomerization and plasma membrane translocation to trigger
  necroptosis, whereas light-activatable RIPK3 or La-RIPK3 induces lytic cell death
  by CRY2olig-mediated RIPK3 oligomerization. (d) Conditional genome editing tools.
  The split-Cas13b/dCas13b system utilizes ABA-induced dimerization for RNA manipulation
  in an ABA-dependent manner. The CASANOVA system incorporates LOV2 into a specific
  loop of AcrIIA4, an anti-CRISPR protein, where light irradiation disrupts AcrIIA4
  function to initiate gene editing. (e) Tunable CAR-T cell activation for precise
  immunotherapy. SNIP CAR incorporates the HCV NS3 protease into CAR constructs, ensuring
  CAR activity only in the presence of US Food and Drug Administration–approved HCV
  NS3 protease inhibitors. LiCAR utilizes the iLID optical dimerizer in a split-CAR
  construct to restore immune responses upon light stimulation. In vivo applications
  are enabled by UCNPs, which convert NIR light into blue emission for activation
  in deeply buried tissues. (f) Neuromodulation tools. DREADDs rely on designer drugs
  for selective inhibition or activation of engineered G protein–coupled receptors
  in neurons. OptoRAF and optoAKT enable precise light-controlled modulation of the
  Raf-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathways, respectively, to facilitate axon growth
  and regeneration. Abbreviations: ABA, abscisic acid; AKT, protein kinase B; CAR,
  chimeric antigen receptor; CASANOVA, CRISPR-Cas9 activity switching via a novel
  optogenetic variant of AcrIIA4; COSMO-STIM1ct, caffeine-operated synthetic module
  fused with cytosolic fragment of STIM1; CRISPR, clustered regularly interspaced
  short palindromic repeats; CRISPR-GO, CRISPR-genome organization; CRY2, cryptochrome
  2; DREADD, designer receptor exclusively activated by designer drugs; ER, endoplasmic
  reticulum; ERK, extracellular signal–regulated kinase; FKBP12, FK506-binding protein
  12; iCasp9, inducible caspase 9; iLID, improved light-induced dimer; LiCAR, light-switchable
  CAR; LiMETER, light-inducible membrane-tethered cortical ER; LiPOP, light-inducible
  necroptosis or pyroptosis; LOCa, light-operated calcium channel; LOV2, light-oxygen-voltage
  domain 2; MEK, MAPK kinase/ERK kinase; MLKL, mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein;
  mTOR, mammalian target of rapamycin; NIR, near-infrared; ORAI1, ORAI calcium release–activated
  calcium modulator 1; PI3K, phosphoinositide-3-kinase; RAF, rapidly accelerated fibrosarcoma;
  SNIP, signal neutralization by an inhibitable protease; UCNP, upconversion nanoplate'
