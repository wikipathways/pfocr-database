caption: 'Estrogen-Regulated H₂S Pathways in Uterine Vasodilation. H₂S mediates estrogen
  (E₂β)-induced uterine vasodilation through genomic and non-genomic mechanisms. Genomic
  Pathway: E₂β crosses the cell membrane and binds to ERα and ERβ, which function
  as nuclear TFs. Upon binding to EREs, these receptors promote CBS gene transcription.
  The human CBS promoter contains multiple ERE sites, including αERE (ERα binding
  site), three βEREs (ERβ binding sites), a full α/βERE, and a half α/βERE (½α/βERE).
  Among these, α/βERE2 and ½α/βERE1 are crucial for basal CBS expression and E₂-induced
  CBS promoter activation. ERα and ERβ can form homodimers (ERα/ERα or ERβ/ERβ) or
  heterodimers (ERα/ERβ), regulating H₂S production.Non-Genomic Pathway: E₂β binding
  to membrane-associated ERα and ERβ directly activates Gαi-2/3, which subsequently
  stimulates pGC-A, catalyzing GTP conversion to cGMP. cGMP then activates cGMP-dependent
  PKG-I, which phosphorylates CSE, enhancing H₂S production. The generated H₂S diffuses
  into SMCs, promoting vasodilation via K-ATP or BK-Ca channels, thereby increasing
  uterine arterial blood flow. Notes: EC: endothelial cell; SMC: smooth muscle cell;
  TFs: transcription factors; EREs: estrogen response elements; pGC-A: particulate
  guanylate cyclase A; PKG-I: protein kinase I'
