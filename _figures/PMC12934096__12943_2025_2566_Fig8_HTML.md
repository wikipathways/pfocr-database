caption: 'Schematic diagram of the multiple mechanism models by which ITM regulates
  the TIME and immune responses. Metabolic regulation: SCFAs and inosine enhance CD8+
  T-cell activity; hypoxanthine promotes CD8+ T-cell function and inhibits Tregs via
  the adenosine A2A receptor; tumor-colonizing bacteria degrade glycerophospholipids
  to suppress CD8+ T-cell exhaustion. ②Immune escape: Abundant polymorphic Sneathia
  in breast cancer secretes chemokines CCL20 and CXCL8, recruiting Tregs and inhibiting
  CD8+ T-cell infiltration. Its metabolite propionylcarnitine may serve as a potential
  biomarker for immunotherapy response; lactate activates the solute carrier MCT11,
  promoting lactate uptake by Tex and suppressing their effector functions [223].
  ③Antigen mimicry and immune activation: ITMs activate DCs and macrophages by expressing
  tumor antigen-mimicking molecules or releasing OMVs and PGs, enhancing CD8+ T-cell
  anti-tumor function; synthetic biology-engineered bacteria expressing a human chemokine
  CXCL16 variant (hCXCL16K42A) release this molecule at tumor sites to attract and
  activate adaptive immune cells. ④Immune checkpoint regulation: Certain ITMs inhibit
  neutrophil activity and activate CD8+ T cells via the IL-10 signaling pathway; pro-inflammatory
  cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) induce soluble PD-1, interfering with the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling
  axis [224]; Enterococcus improves ICI response in mouse models [225]; interferons
  upregulate PD-L1 expression by activating the STING pathway, promoting IFN-γ+ CD8+
  TIL aggregation and enhancing PD-L1 blockade efficacy'
