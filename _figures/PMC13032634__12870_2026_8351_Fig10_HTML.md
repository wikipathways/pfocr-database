caption: Predicted disease resistance mechanisms involving CsCOMT23, CsCOMT25, and
  CsCOMT26 in sweet orange. During pathogen infection, cell wall-derived elicitors
  (CWDEs) activate the phenylpropanoid pathway in chloroplasts, where phenylalanine
  is converted to cinnamic acid by phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL). Subsequently,
  cinnamic acid is converted to caffeic acid via catalysis by cinnamic acid 4-hydroxylase
  (C4H) and coumaric acid 3-hydroxylase (C3H). Key CsCOMT genes (CsCOMT26, CsCOMT23,
  CsCOMT25) catalyze the methylation of caffeic acid and 5-hydroxyferulic acid, respectively,
  yielding ferulic acid and sinapic acid. These compounds serve as precursors for
  lignin synthesis, enhancing sweet orange resistance to P. digitatum by thickening
  cell walls
