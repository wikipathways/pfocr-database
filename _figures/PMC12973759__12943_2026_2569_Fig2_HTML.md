caption: 'Remodeling the Tumor Immune Microenvironment: Synergistic Induction of Pyroptosis
  to Convert Immunologically “Cold” Tumors into “Hot” and Sensitize to Immunotherapy.
  Schematic representation of therapeutic strategies that promote the transition from
  an immunologically “cold” to a “hot” tumor microenvironment by inducing immunogenic
  cell death, with a focus on pyroptosis. Top Panel: “Cold” vs. “Hot” Tumor Microenvironment
  (TME). Left (“Cold” TME): Minimal T-cell infiltration, dominance of immunosuppressive
  cells (TAMs, MDSCs, Tregs), and inhibitory cytokines (TGF-β, IL-10). Low PD-L1,
  impaired antigen presentation, and stromal fibrosis lead to resistance to immune
  checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Right (“Hot” TME): Dense CD8⁺ and CD4⁺ T-cell infiltration,
  fewer immunosuppressive cells, and high pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-18,
  IFN-γ). Upregulated PD-L1, restored antigen presentation, and matrix remodeling
  enhance immune cell entry and sensitivity to ICIs. Bottom Panel: Therapeutic Drivers
  of the “Cold” to “Hot” Transition. Multiple strategies synergize to reprogram the
  TME: ·Chemotherapy and epigenetic therapies induce immunogenic cell death, release
  tumor antigens, and reverse silencing of immune genes. ·NP-delivered GSDMs (e.g.,
  GSDM vectors or activators) trigger tumor pyroptosis, releasing DAMPs and IL-1β/IL-18
  to activate dendritic and T cells. ·Small-molecule inhibitors block immunosuppressive
  pathways (VEGF, IDO, TGF-β), reducing inhibitory cell activity. ·CAR-T/CAR-NK cells
  lyse tumors via perforin/granzymes, cleaving GSDMB/E to induce pyroptosis and boost
  immunity. Conclusion: These approaches—especially pyroptosis induction—convert immunosuppressive
  “cold” tumors into inflamed “hot” TMEs, overcoming resistance and enhancing ICI
  efficacy'
