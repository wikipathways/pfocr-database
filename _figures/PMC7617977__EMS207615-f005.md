caption: The figure depicts various metabolic pathways and highlights several metabolites
  that are dysregulated in cervical cancer patients. The pathways shown include purine
  metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, amino acid metabolism (such as tyrosine, histidine),
  and lipid metabolism. The schematic also highlights various metabolic intermediates
  involved in these pathways, providing a comprehensive overview of the metabolic
  alterations observed in cervical cancer patients. Several metabolites seen dysregulated
  such as nicotinamide, a precursor of NAD+, which plays a crucial role in energy
  metabolism and is often dysregulated in cancer cells. Several metabolites outside
  core glycolysis/TCA cycle also show significant alterations, highlighting broader
  metabolic rewiring in cancer. For instance, nucleosides like hypoxanthine, and 3-CMP
  indicate heightened nucleotide turnover and purine/pyrimidine metabolism, consistent
  with proliferative demands. 1-methyl nicotinamide point to changes in NAD/NADP and
  vitamin B2 metabolism, supporting redox and biosynthetic reactions. Proline betaine
  and cinnamate reflect shifts in amino acid metabolism, while uric acid further supports
  deregulated purine pathways in cancer contexts. The color coding shows the category
  in which the metabolite was differentially present
