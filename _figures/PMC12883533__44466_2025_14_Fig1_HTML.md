caption: The Canonical and Non-Canonical NF-κB Pathways. The left diagram depicts
  the key molecular mechanisms of the canonical pathway that are engaged by TLRs,
  TNFR, and antigen receptors on B and T cells. They converge on the NEMO-containing
  IKK complex which phosphorylates canonical IκB proteins (e.g. IκBα) for degradation.
  Pre-existing NF-κB dimers (e.g. p50:RelA, also known as p50:p65) are released to
  translocate to the nucleus and bind target genes. The right diagram depicts the
  key molecular mechanisms initiated by BAFF-R, CD40 or LTβR. They converge on NIK
  and NEMO-less IKK, which phosphorylates the C-terminal domain of p100. C-terminal
  degradation of pre-existing p100 releases bound dimers (same as canonical pathway)
  and of newly-synthesized p100 allows for the generation of new p52 containing dimers
  (e.g. p52:RelB). BAFF-R, B-cell activating factor receptor; BCR, B-cell receptor;
  cIAP, cellular inhibitor of apoptosis protein; IKK, IκB kinase; IL1R, interleukin-1
  receptor; IRAK, interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase; LTβR, lymphotoxin-β receptor;
  MALT1, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma translocation protein 1; NEMO,
  NF-κB essential modulator; NF-κB, nuclear factor kappa-light-chain enhancer of activated
  B cells; NIK, NF-κB-inducing kinase; RIPK, receptor-interacting protein kinase;
  TAK1, transforming growth factor-β-activated kinase 1; TCR, T-cell receptor; TLR,
  Toll-like receptor; TNFR, tumor necrosis factor receptor; TRAF, TNFR-associated
  factor
