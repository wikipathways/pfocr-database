caption: Germline mutations, key predictors of hereditary cancer risk, are inherited
  genetic changes present in the egg or sperm that become part of the DNA in every
  cell of the offspring. Both sperm and egg can equally contribute to germline mutations
  that lead to hereditary prostate cancer. For example, a mother or father carrying
  a mutation in BRCA1, BRCA2, HOXB13, ATM, or CHEK2 can pass it on to their son, who
  will have a higher risk of developing prostate cancer. Many of these mutations are
  involved in DNA repair pathways, making them attractive targets for therapeutic
  intervention. Most hereditary prostate cancer mutations are inherited in an autosomal
  dominant manner, meaning that just one copy of the mutated gene (from either parent)
  is sufficient to increase risk. Created in BioRender. Anand, S. (2026) https://BioRender.com/k3kecd7
