caption: Phase‑dependent evolution of mitochondrial dysfunction in neonatal HIE. The
  upper panel depicts the temporal progression of hypoxic–ischemic brain injury from
  the primary phase (0–6 h) through the latent and secondary phases (hours to days)
  to the tertiary or remodeling phase (days to weeks/years). The four lower panels
  summarize the dominant mitochondrial abnormalities at each stage. During the primary
  phase, acute hypoxia–ischemia causes excitotoxicity, intracellular acidosis, loss
  of mitochondrial membrane potential, and an initial energy crisis characterized
  by oxidative phosphorylation collapse, ATP depletion, ionic imbalance, and early
  low‑level ROS generation. In the latent phase, cerebral high‑energy phosphates transiently
  recover, but mitochondria remain functionally unstable, with exacerbated oxidative
  stress, electron transport chain leak, sensitization of the mitochondrial permeability
  transition pore (mPTP), and emerging Drp1‑mediated fission. The secondary phase
  is marked by global mitochondrial collapse with sustained mPTP opening, a pronounced
  oxidative/nitrosative storm, severe Ca2 + overload, excessive fission with impaired
  fusion, and activation of multiple regulated cell death pathways, leading to delayed
  neuronal death. In the tertiary phase, surviving cells exhibit chronic mitochondrial
  quality‑control failure, including persistent fusion–fission imbalance, insufficient
  mitophagy and biogenesis, and mtDNA‑driven chronic inflammation (cGAS–STING activation),
  which are linked to long‑term cognitive and behavioral sequelae (created in https://biorender.com)
