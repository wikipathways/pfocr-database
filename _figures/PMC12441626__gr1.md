caption: ICI-Based Therapies and Mechanisms Underlying Cardiac irAEsImmune checkpoint
  inhibitors (ICIs) such as anti-PD-L1, anti-cytotoxic T lymphocyte–associated antigen
  4 (anti-CTLA-4), and anti-TIGIT block checkpoint proteins from binding their partners,
  preventing inhibitory signals and enhancing T cell priming, cytotoxic T lymphocyte
  (CTL) proliferation, cytokine release (eg, interferon γ [IFN-γ], tumor necrosis
  factor α [TNF-α]), and regulatory T cell (Treg) suppression—promoting cancer cell
  apoptosis. Cardiac immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are driven by clonal autoreactive
  CTLs targeting antigens shared by tumors and cardiac or vascular tissue, resulting
  in myocyte necrosis. Myocarditis involves tissue-resident memory T cells, reactivated
  PD-1+ T cells at prior injury sites, macrophages, terminally differentiated effector
  memory (TEMRA) CD8+ T cells, and CXCL9hiHLA-DRAhi fibroblasts—all recruited and
  retained via CXCR3 ligands (CXCL9/10/11). Dendritic cells (DCs) contribute by presenting
  antigens to CTLs. The gasdermin E/STING pathway induces pyroptosis in cardiomyocytes,
  releasing mitochondrial DNA and activating IFN-mediated inflammation, leading to
  myocarditis and fibrosis. Activation of cardiac myosin–specific CD69+PD-1+ TRMs
  in pericardial fluid by anti–PD-1 ICIs exacerbates ischemic and dilated cardiomyopathy.
  Immunosuppressants—abatacept (CTLA-4 agonist), ruxolitinib, and corticosteroids—suppress
  T cell activation and improve myocarditis. In irAE-associated atherosclerosis, CD8+
  T cells and CCR7+FSCN1+DCs drive inflammation. DCs enhance T cell cytotoxicity and
  destabilize plaques; macrophages expand necrotic cores. ICIs reduce plaque calcification
  and increase IFN-γ signaling, promoting plaque rupture. Figure created with BioRender.
  APC = antigen-presenting cell; Teff = T effector; Treg = regulatory T cell; TRMs
  = Tissue Resident Memory T cells
