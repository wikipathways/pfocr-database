caption: Schematic demonstrating a “seesaw” working model of the VTA-mOT dopaminergic
  pathway in orchestrating preference for fed over unfed larvae. Mice prefer fed over
  unfed larvae, due to a relatively higher level of LA and lower level of (Z)-9-TE
  from fed larvae. Under physiological conditions, the VTA-mOT dopaminergic pathway
  interacts with both D1 and D2 SPNs in the mOT, responding to dopamine released from
  the VTA. This creates an unbalanced “seesaw”, resulting in a preference for LA and
  avoidance of (Z)-9-TE. Inactivation of the pathway or blockade of dopamine D1 receptors
  (inactivating D1 SPNs) in the mOT disrupts the originally unbalanced pattern, abolishing
  the preference for LA and leading to a balanced “seesaw” with no preference for
  either LA or (Z)-9-TE. Conversely, activation of the pathway or blockade of dopamine
  D2 receptors (activating D2 SPNs) in the mOT reverses the preference for LA and
  avoidance of (Z)-9-TE, resulting in a mirrored “seesaw” opposite to that observed
  under physiological conditions. Diagrams were created using BioRender.com
