caption: 'Role of the intratumoural microbiota in the development of cancer. Delineation
  of seven major mechanisms by which the initiation and progression of cancer are
  influenced by the intratumoural microbiota. (a) Chronic inflammation: Porphyromonas
  gingivalis induces PD-L1/PD-L2 receptor overexpression in oral epithelial cells,
  driving proinflammatory cytokine production and promoting chronic inflammation.
  (b) DNA damage: Escherichia coli and Bacteroides fragilis co-colonize epithelial
  surfaces, stimulating IL-17 production, resulting in extensive DNA damage in epithelial
  cells. (c) Epigenetic modification: Helicobacter pylori infection promotes aberrant
  DNA methylation in gastric cancer cells. (d) Immune evasion: Intratumoral fungi
  promote pancreatic tumor growth by upregulating IL-33 in PDAC cells, leading to
  recruitment and activation of TH2/ILC2 cells. (e) Metabolic regulation: F. nucleatum
  induces GLUT1 clustering in the plasma membrane of OSCC cells, subsequently mediating
  extracellular lactate accumulation. (f) Bacterial–fungal interactions: Fungi engage
  in dynamic interactions with their bacterial counterparts, which can upregulate
  D-arginine and D-ornithine levels and stimulate butyrate metabolic pathways, thereby
  accelerating colorectal cancer progression. (g) Activation of invasion and metastasis:
  F. nucleatum triggers chemokine CCL2/CCL7 secretion from macrophages, which in turn
  suppresses E-cadherin expression while upregulating N-cadherin levels in CRC cells.
