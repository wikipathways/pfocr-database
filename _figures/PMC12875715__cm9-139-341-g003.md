caption: 'Sphingolipids, oxLDL and ammonia serve as signaling molecules. This figure
  describes the signal transduction functions of lipid and amino acid metabolites.
  Sphingolipids, synthesized via Sptlc2-mediated pathways, are shown to regulate CD8+
  T cell effector function (via mTORC1 modulation), Treg cell differentiation (through
  sphinganine-Fos-PD-1 signaling), and TLR4-mediated macrophage activation (by promoting
  MyD88 membrane recruitment). Oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL) is depicted
  to induce CD8+ T cell dysfunction in the tumor microenvironment via CD36-mediated
  uptake, leading to lipid peroxidation and p38 activation. Ammonia, a byproduct of
  amino acid metabolism, is illustrated to impair T cell function through lysosomal/mitochondrial
  damage, with memory T cells using the urea cycle and exhausted T cells utilizing
  the malate shuttle (via GOT1 and 2-ketoglutarate) for detoxification, highlighting
  metabolite-driven regulation of immune cell fate and function. Some of the elements
  were created using BioRender. MyD88: Myeloid differentiation primary response 88;
  mTORC1: Mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1; PD-1: Programmed cell death protein
  1; Treg: Regulatory T cell; TLR4: Toll-like receptor 4'
