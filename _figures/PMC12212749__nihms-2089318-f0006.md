caption: Macrophages (and monocytes) express C3aR, C5aR1 and C5aR2 (A). In response
  to E. coli infection, bacteria bind pre-existing antibodies thereby causing activation
  of the classical complement pathway leading to production of C3a, C5a and membrane
  attack complex (B). C3a binds to C3aR on macrophages generating resolving macrophages
  and C5a binds to C5aR1 and C5aR2 on macrophages and neutrophils leading to their
  activation and degranulation (C). In Cpn−/− mice the lack of CPN will cause increased
  levels of C5a leading to increased numbers of proinflammatory macrophages and neutrophils
  while CPB2 will inactivate C3a reducing the number of resolving macrophages and
  decreased survival (D). In Cpb2−/− mice lacking CPB2, there will be increased levels
  of C3a resulting in more resolving macrophages while CPN inactivates C5a reducing
  numbers of proinflammatory macrophages and neutrophils and increasing survival (E).
