caption: 'Schematic diagram of representative CRISPR/Cas systems. (A). The CRISPR/Cas9
  system consists of a Cas9 protein and a gRNA. Upon entering the host nucleus, the
  gRNA directs Cas9 to the target DNA via a PAM. Cas9 functions as a molecular scissor
  to introduce DSBs through its two nuclease domains (HNH and RuvC). These DSBs are
  repaired either by error-prone NHEJ pathway, leading to deletion/insertion, or by
  HDR pathway with a donor DNA template for precise modification. (B). Cas9 nickase
  (Cas9n), generated by mutating one endonuclease domain, induces single-strand breaks
  in DNA. (C). Catalytically dead Cas9 (dCas9), generated by mutating both endonuclease
  domains, lacks cleavage activity but retains DNA-binding ability. When fused to
  transcriptional repressors or activators, it can modulate gene expression by silencing
  or activating specific genes. (D). Base editors (BEs) combine dCas9 or Cas9n with
  a deaminase to introduce precise point mutations without generating DSBs. Cytosine
  deamination converts C to U, leading to C→T (or G→A) substitutions. Adenine deamination
  converts A to I, leading to A→G (or T→C) substitutions. Abbreviations: gRNA, guide
  RNA; PAM, protospacer adjacent motif; G, Guanine; C, Cytosine; U, Uracil; A, Adenine;
  T, Thymine; I, Inosine'
