caption: Illustration of lysophagy pathway. When lysosome membranes are damaged under
  various stress conditions, cytosolic sensors known as galectins (including Gal1,
  Gal3, Gal8, and Gal9) bind to exposed glycans on damaged lysosomes. E3 ubiquitin
  ligases can directly or indirectly tag damaged lysosomes with polyubiquitin chains.
  The binding of Gal3 to glycans further recruits the E3 ligase TRIM16 to ubiquitinate
  ULK1 and lysosomal membrane proteins. Myristoylated FBXO27 binds to exposed glycans
  directly and promotes the ubiquitination of lysosomal membrane proteins. Gal8 binds
  to the exposed glycans of damaged lysosomes and directly recruits the autophagy
  receptor NDP52. Furthermore, the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme UBE2QL1 is recruited
  to the damaged lysosomes, facilitates the K48-linked polyubiquitination of lysosomal
  membrane proteins, and recruits VCP/p97 to the damaged lysosomes. VCP recognizes
  ubiquitinated substrates and delivers them to autophagosomes. Finally, ubiquitinated
  lysosomes are recognized by autophagy receptors, such as NDP52, p62, and TAX1BP1,
  which bind to LC3, and then damaged lysosomes are engulfed into autophagosomes,
  which fuse with intact lysosomes for degradation. Created with BioRender.com
