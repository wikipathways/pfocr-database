caption: Thiamine biosynthesis and uptake pathways in S. cerevisiaeThiamine (vitamin
  B1) is an essential cofactor for all living organisms and can be taken up from the
  environment or synthesized de novo. The acid phosphatase Pho3p dephosphorylates
  thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) in the extracellular space. Thiamine (T) is absorbed
  from the extracellular environment by the main thiamine transporter Thi7p (Thi10p)
  and phosphorylated by Thi80p to its bioactive form thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP).
  Thi71p and Thi72p can also transport thiamine but play only a minor role. TPP is
  used as a cofactor by enzymes that have a role in primary metabolism, such as transketolase.
  Most of these enzymes are located in mitochondria. Tpc1p and Thi74p transport TPP
  into the mitochondria. The biosynthetic pathways can take different routes to produce
  different precursors. Thi6p can synthesize the precursor 4-methyl-5-(2-hydroxyethyl)
  thiazole phosphate (HET-P) via 4-methyl-5-(2-hydroxyethyl) thiazole (HET) or Thi4p
  by means of NAD+, glycine (Gly), and cysteine (Cys). 4-amino-2-methyl-5-hydroxymethylpyrimidine
  phosphate (HMP-P) can be synthesized by Thi20p or Thi21p via 4-amino-2-methyl-5-hydroxymethylpyrimidine
  (HMP) or by the enzymes Thi5p, Thi11p, Thi12p, and Thi13p using histidine (His)
  and pyridoxal-5-phosphate (PLP). HMP-P is further converted to 4-amino-2-methyl-5-hydroxymethylpyrimidine
  pyrophosphate (HMP-PP). Thi6p uses HMP-PP and HET-P to produce thiamine monophosphate
  (TMP), which gets converted to thiamine by dephosphorylation. At lower TPP levels,
  Thi3p associates with transcriptional activators Thi2p and Pdc2, respectively. In
  the nucleus, these protein dimers bind to specific promoter regions to activate
  the expression of genes encoding thiamine biosynthesis enzymes. At higher TPP levels,
  Thi3p binds TPP and dissociates from Thi2p and Pdc2, leading to transcriptional
  arrest of these genes
