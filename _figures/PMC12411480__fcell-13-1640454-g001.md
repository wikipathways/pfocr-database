caption: Mechanisms of HIF-α subunit regulation under normoxic and hypoxic conditions.
  (A) Under normoxic conditions, prolyl hydroxylases (PHDs) hydroxylate specific proline
  residues of the HIF-α subunit using oxygen, Fe2+, and α-ketoglutarate (α-KG). Hydroxylated
  HIF-α is recognized and ubiquitinated by VHL proteins and subsequently degraded
  via the proteasomal pathway. (B) Under hypoxic conditions, where oxygen deprivation
  results in suppressed activity of PHDs, the HIF-α subunit is not hydroxylated, thus
  evading recognition and degradation by VHL and stabilizing and accumulating within
  the cell. Subsequently, HIF-α translocates to the nucleus, forms a heterodimer with
  HIF-1β, binds to coactivators such as p300/CBP, recognizes and binds to hypoxia
  response elements (HREs), activates the transcription of downstream genes, and facilitates
  cellular adaptation to a hypoxic environment
