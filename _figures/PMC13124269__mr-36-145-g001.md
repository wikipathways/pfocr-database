caption: 'GNAQ/GNA11 signaling pathway. ① Ligand binding and ARF6 activation: when
  the ligand binds to CysLTR2, GNAQ/GNA11, and the ARF-guanine nucleotide exchange
  factor GEP100 coactivate ARF6. This interaction redistributes GNAQ/GNA11 from the
  cell surface to active signaling vesicles in the cytoplasm, initiating subsequent
  signaling pathways [61]. GNAQ/GNA11 activates PLCB4, which hydrolyzes PIP2 to generate
  the second messengers IP3 and DAG. DAG binds to PKC and Ras, leading to the sequential
  phosphorylation of Ras, Raf, MAPK, and other downstream signaling molecules. These
  signals are then transmitted to the nucleus to regulate gene expression and induce
  UM. ② Rho/Rac GTPase pathway: GNAQ/GNA11 directly binds to Trio-mediated Rho/Rac
  GTPases [69]. Activated ARF6 can also induce the Rho/Rac signaling pathway directly
  [61]. This activation leads to the downstream activation of FAK, which subsequently
  activates YAP via MOB1. Additionally, activation of Rho/Rac GTPases stimulates F-actin,
  further activating YAP. Ultimately, this process triggers YAP/TEAD-mediated transcription
  [70]. ③ β-catenin pathway: oncogenic GNAQ/GNA11 activation of ARF6 leads to the
  release of β-catenin from the plasma membrane and its translocation to the nucleus.
  In the nucleus, β-catenin induces gene transcription and promotes UM cell proliferation
  [61,64]. ④ PI3K/AKT pathway: although not directly controlled by GNAQ/GNA11, activation
  of the PI3K/AKT pathway can also induce UM [71,72]. ARF6, ADP-ribosylation factor;
  GEP, gene expression profiling; UM, uveal melanoma'
