caption: The core mechanism of sepsis-induced organ damage. Sepsis can lead to excessive
  activation of immune cells, such as macrophages and neutrophils, resulting in the
  release of high levels of pro-inflammatory factors like TNF-α and IL-1β. This cytokine
  storm damages endothelial cells, increases adhesion molecules, promotes microcirculation
  thrombosis, and causes ischemia and hypoxia, which can trigger multiple organ dysfunction.
  The inflammatory response also inhibits monocyte, T cell, and B cell functions,
  leading to immunosuppression that exacerbates sepsis. Additionally, sepsis activates
  cell death pathways that further increase inflammatory cytokine production. It also
  stimulates the glycolytic pathway to enhance lactate production, promoting macrophage
  activation via MCT1 transport
