caption: 'Stress-induced catecholamine-activated β2-adrenergic receptor and its action
  on immunomodulation and tumor immune response: Chronic stress can have a differential
  effect on various immune cells. Neural signaling via β2-AR regulates cellular activities
  in both innate and adaptive immune cells. β2-AR has extensive expression in most
  of the immune cells. β2-AR signaling on the neoplastic cells via cAMP/PKA pathway
  enhances the proliferation, and in parallel, it remodels the TME, primarily affecting
  the immune cells. Catecholamines suppress T-cell activity, polarize macrophages
  toward an anti-inflammatory (M2) state, impair B-cell antibody production, impair
  dendritic cell antigen presentation, and decrease NK cell cytotoxicity, resulting
  in immune suppression, tumor growth, and metastasis. CAF cells in the TME can remodel
  the TME structurally and functionally, with its predominant action on immune modulation
  and angiogenesis. cAMP: cyclic adenosine monophosphate, PKA: protein kinase A, M2:
  macrophage 2, NK: natural killer, CAF: cancer associated fibroblast. Created with
  BioRender.com'
