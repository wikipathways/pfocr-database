caption: 'Role of TRPM2 in neurological disorders: A. Alzheimer’s disease (AD), B.
  Parkinson’s disease, C. Epilepsy. A. In Alzheimer’s disease (AD), amyloid-beta (Aβ)
  plaque induces reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation that stimulates TRPM2 activation
  in brain tissues. TRPM2-mediated high Ca2+ influx regulates the downstream intracellular
  signaling pathways and molecules that trigger neuronal apoptosis, neuroinflammation,
  and neurovascular dysfunction, contributing to the AD pathogenesis. In neurons,
  TRPM2-regulated Ca2+ overload disrupts lysosomal and mitochondrial activity that
  induces overproduction of mitochondrial ROS and activation of the mitochondrial
  apoptotic pathway, resulting in neuronal cell death. Simultaneously, TRPM2 mediates
  neuroinflammation by inducing the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including
  TNF-α, IL-6, IL-β, leading to the activation of the glial cells (microglia and astrocytes).
  Additionally, TRPM2-regulated elevated Ca2+ level disrupts the endothelial cell
  permeability, causing neurovascular dysfunction and aiding Aβ accumulation. B. In
  Parkinson’s disease, TRPM2 mediates dopaminergic neuronal damage by regulating the
  AKT/Gsk-3β/Cas-3 apoptotic signaling axis and triggering the mitochondrial apoptotic
  pathway. Concurrently, TRPM2 triggers neuroinflammation through Ca2+ signaling that
  stimulates pro-inflammatory cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-β) in glial cells
  (likewise AD pathogenesis). C. In epileptic seizure, TRPM2 mediates activation of
  the BNIP3/apoptosis-inducing factor (AIF)/endonuclease G (Endo G) signaling cascade
  that causes DNA fragmentation and nuclear damage, activating the caspase-independent
  apoptosis of neuronal cells. Additionally, it induces the mitochondrial apoptotic
  pathway and excessive autophagy, exacerbating the cell damage. Moreover, TRPM2 triggers
  neuroinflammation by activating the glial cells and modulating the AMPK/mTOR pathway
  to inhibit autophagy, thus preventing glial cells from transitioning towards a non-inflammatory
  state'
