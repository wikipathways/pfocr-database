caption: Molecular mechanisms of HPV infection leading to cervical cancer. (A) HPV
  infects cervical epithelial basal cells, with its DNA entering the nucleus via microinjuries.
  HPV DNA integrates into fragile host genome sites, disrupting the E2 gene and causing
  persistent overexpression of the E6/E7 oncogenes. (B) E6 binds to the tumor suppressor
  p53, recruiting the E6AP ubiquitin ligase to degrade p53 via the ubiquitin–proteasome
  pathway. E7 degrades retinoblastoma protein (pRb), releasing the transcription factor
  E2F to relieve G1/S arrest, leading to cell cycle dysregulation and excessive proliferation.
  (C) HPV immune evasion mechanisms. HPV-infected cells overexpress PD-L1, PD-1, and
  CTLA-4, which inhibits T-cell activity upon binding. Proinflammatory factors (TGF-β
  and IL-10) increase while anti-inflammatory factors decrease, suppressing antiviral
  immunity. (D) Hypoxia activates HIF-1α to promote VEGF secretion, stimulating angiogenesis
  and tumor metastasis. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) secrete MMPs and LOX to
  degrade the extracellular matrix (ECM), facilitating cancer cell spread
