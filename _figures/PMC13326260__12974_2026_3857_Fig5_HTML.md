caption: Effects of mechanical stimuli on immune cells in the brain tumor microenvironment.
  Mechanical cues including ECM stiffness, interstitial flow, hydrostatic pressure
  and shear stress influence the function of diverse immune cell types. a ECM stiffness
  activates astrocyte Piezo1–Ca2+ signaling, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine
  secretion. ECM HA enhances astrocyte oxygen consumption and HIF-1α expression. The
  combined effects shown in this figure represent a hypothesized integration of separately
  validated mechanisms. b ECM stiffness promotes microglial YAP nuclear localization,
  increasing IL-10 and reducing IL-6 production. Interstitial fluid flow enhances
  microglial CCL2 secretion, promoting glioma proliferation and stemness. The combined
  effects shown in this figure represent a hypothesized integration of separately
  validated mechanisms. c ECM stiffness activates macrophage TGF-β-Smad signaling,
  driving ARG1-dependent arginine metabolism and suppressing CD8+ T cell function.
  ECM stiffness can also bias macrophage towards M2 polarization and further inhibiting
  CD8+ T cell activity. The combined effects shown in this figure represent a hypothesized
  integration of separately validated mechanisms. d ECM stiffness suppresses CD8+
  T cell activation and reduce cytokine production, while enhancing Treg induction
  and immunosuppressive function. The combined effects shown in this figure represent
  a hypothesized integration of separately validated mechanisms. e Shear force induces
  neutrophil Piezo1-mediated Ca2+ influx to promote NETosis, which has been reported
  to enhance glioma progression in other studies [89, 90]. This should be considered
  a hypothesized sequential mechanism supported by separate lines of evidence, as
  the integrated causal pathway has not been directly tested in a single study. f
  Hydrostatic pressure activates TRPV2-mediated Ca2+ influx in mast cells, inducing
  degranulation and histamine release. COL6A1 stimulates integrinβ1-FAK-YAP signaling
  in ECs to promote claudin-5 degradation (not shown in this figure) [57], increasing
  barrier permeability, MDM infiltration and glioma chemoresistance. NETosis, neutrophil
  extracellular trap formation; ECs, endothelial cells
