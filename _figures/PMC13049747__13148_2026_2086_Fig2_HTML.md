caption: 'Choline metabolism and its integration into methylation and phospholipid
  synthesis pathways. Choline is oxidized to betaine via choline dehydrogenase (CHDH),
  supporting remethylation of homocysteine to methionine through betaine-homocysteine
  methyltransferase (BHMT). Methionine is converted to S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)
  via methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT). SAM serves as a universal methyl donor
  in methylation reactions catalyzed by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs). SAM demethylation
  yields S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH), which is hydrolyzed to homocysteine by adenosylhomocysteinase
  (AHCY). Homocysteine can also be remethylated via methionine synthase (MTR), using
  5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) and tetrahydrofolate (THF). Independently, choline
  contributes to phosphatidylcholine (PC) synthesis via the cytidine diphosphate (CDP)-choline
  pathway, involving choline kinase (CK), CTP: phosphocholine cytidylyltransferase
  (CCT), choline phosphotransferase (CPT), and phospholipase D (PLD). Additionally,
  phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) can be methylated to PC, linking methylation demand
  to membrane biosynthesis'
