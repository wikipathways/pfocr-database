caption: Proposed mechanism of MPro autoprocessing.A, the ∼30 kb SARS-CoV-2 genome
  codes for the various proteins in 14 ORFs (33). Two major polyproteins (pps) are
  encoded in ORFs, 1a (nsp1–nsp10) and 1 ab (nsp1–nsp16), the processed proteins of
  which make up the replication–transcription complex. pp1ab is synthesized via a
  translation frameshifting (denoted FS) mechanism. The two virally encoded proteases,
  PLPro (papain-like), within nsp3, and 3C-like main protease (MPro, nsp5), are responsible
  for the processing of pp1a and pp1ab. In the precursor form, MPro is anchored on
  either side through transmembrane helices (black bars) within nsp4 and nsp6 (1,
  32, 33). MPro is responsible for its own release (termed autoprocessing) and cleavage
  of the rest of the sites between nsp7 and nsp16. B, catalytic (domains I and II)
  and helical (domain III) regions are shown as orange and blue ovals, respectively.
  Solid black lines denote the N-terminal residues (N-finger) of MPro. Dashed black
  and solid blue lines represent the truncated nsp4 and nsp6 regions flanking the
  N and C termini of MPro. Catalytic dyad H41 and C145 residues are indicated as red
  circles in the top-left monomer cartoon. Details of the N-terminal intramolecular
  autoprocessing mechanism are described in the study by Aniana et al. (3). The low
  catalytic activity, because of the active site loop equilibrium being mainly in
  the inactive conformation of the monomeric precursor, permits slow N-terminal intramolecular
  cleavage as displayed by first-order kinetics, that is, unimolecular reaction. Since
  inhibitor (or substrate) binding promotes dimer formation, a population of the dimeric
  precursor with increased catalytic activity can undergo fast and faster N-terminal
  intramolecular processing of the homodimer (3) and heterodimer (21), respectively,
  relative to the monomeric precursor’s processing in the reaction pathway. The fact
  that the monomeric intermediate precursor does not undergo C-terminal cleavage (3)
  and is cleaved only upon adding mature MProWT indicated that the C-terminal reaction
  is intermolecular. The current study demonstrates that N-terminal cleavage is concomitant
  with stable dimer formation of the intermediate precursor and describes the mechanism
  of the C-terminal autoprocessing reaction by comparing the efficacy of cleavage
  of the intermediate precursor being in equilibrium between a dimer and monomer.
  C, pathway to C-terminal processing of MPro from a model precursor. S, D, M, and
  (IP)2 denote substrate, dimer, monomer, and two molecules of IP, respectively. The
  presence of SD-IP species in the dimer pathway is confirmed by the presence of MProC145A
  in the bound fraction (Fig. 3D, lane [B]), where MProC145A will be present only
  when it purifies in the bound fraction as a heterodimer. MPro, main protease; nsp,
  nonstructural protein; SARS-CoV-2, severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus
  2
