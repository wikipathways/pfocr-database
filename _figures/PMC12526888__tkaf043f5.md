caption: 'TBI treatment strategy and treatment effect based on DAMPs. Given the connection
  between DAMPs and TBI, several pharmacological agents have been explored in clinical
  or experimental settings, resulting in DAMP-related treatment strategies. The primary
  mechanisms of these strategies include: reduction of DAMP production: This can be
  achieved by decreasing the expression of related DAMP precursors or of DAMPs themselves,
  utilizing agents such as apocynin and minocycline; inhibition of DAMP binding to
  receptors: Competitive or noncompetitive inhibitors can block downstream signaling
  pathways, as demonstrated by compounds such as arundic acid, papaverine, and curcumin;
  decreased release of DAMPs from cells: This can be accomplished by targeting the
  primary DAMP release pathways, such as those related to exosomes, lysosomes, necroptosis,
  apoptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis, and DAMP translocation, by using agents such
  as glycyrrhizin and ethyl pyruvate. The reported effects of these treatment strategies
  include: anti-neuroinflammation: This involves reducing the release of inflammatory
  factors, minimizing axonal damage, decreasing ROS accumulation, limiting DAMP release,
  and preserving synapses. Furthermore, these strategies inhibit the decline of neurotrophic
  factors in microglia and astrocytes, preventing their polarization toward the pro-inflammatory
  M1 microglial and A1 astrocyte phenotypes, respectively, while promoting an anti-inflammatory/neuroprotective
  M2 microglial and A2 astrocyte phenotype, respectively. For instance, glycyrrhizin
  administered via intravenous injection in the fluid percussion injury rat model
  achieved anti-neuroinflammatory effects and improved neurocognition. In a lipopolysaccharide-induced
  TBI mouse model, curcumin reduced the release of inflammatory mediators and inhibited
  neuronal apoptosis. Anti-brain edema: This entails reducing the severity of brain
  edema through the mitigation of inflammation, oxidative stress, and regulation of
  ATP and Ca2+ levels. For example, in a weight-drop mouse model, papaverine competitively
  binds to RAGE, resulting in reduced brain edema, neuroinflammation, apoptosis, and
  neuroprotection while also inhibiting upstream S100B expression. Similarly, apocynin
  was used to lower NOX2 expression, an enzyme responsible for ROS generation, indirectly
  reducing ROS levels and achieving multiple therapeutic effects, including diminished
  brain edema, cell apoptosis, neuronal death, and inflammatory factor release. Promoting
  neuronal and myelin repair and maintaining blood–brain barrier integrity: For instance,
  EPO has been shown to reduce neuroinflammation by activating pathways that lower
  ROS levels, thereby promoting neuronal regeneration and repair and mitigating microglial
  activation. Anti-neuronal programmed cell death: This includes inhibition of cell
  death mechanisms, such as apoptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis. For
  example, in the weight-drop TBI mouse model, apocynin effectively reduced NOX2 expression,
  indirectly lowering ROS generation and achieving therapeutic effects, such as a
  reduction in brain edema, cell apoptosis, neuronal death, and inflammatory factor
  release. DAMPs damage-associated molecular patterns, TBI traumatic brain injury,
  ROS reactive oxygen species, RAGE receptor of advanced glycation endproducts, S100B
  S100 Calcium Binding Protein B, NOX2 NADPH oxidase 2, EPO erythropoietin'
