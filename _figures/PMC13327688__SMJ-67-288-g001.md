caption: 'Mechanism of action of advanced therapies for Crohn’s disease. (1) Antigen
  encounter and activation of antigen-presenting cells (APCs) (dendritic cells, macrophages).
  JAKi (Janus kinase inhibitor) reduces APC cytokine output by blocking the JAK– STAT
  (signal transducer and activator of transcription) signalling pathway, which is
  essential for APC maturation, activation and survival. (2) T cell differentiation
  and activation: APC migrates into mesenteric lymph nodes and/or mucosal lymphoid
  follicles and induces the differentiation of naive T cells into effector T cells,
  which leave the lymph node via efferent lymphatic vessels into blood stream. Anti-IL-23p19
  blocks IL-23, anti-IL-12/23p40 blocks IL-12 and IL-23, and JAKi blocks signalling
  of IL-6. (3) Gut homing: Mature effector T cells migrate into intestinal tissue
  via gut-specific integrin/cell adhesion molecule interactions. Lymphocytes express
  integrin α4β7 and bind to MAdCAM-1 (mucosal vascular addressin cell adhesion molecule
  1) on endothelium of intestinal venules, migrating into intestinal mucosa. (4) Effector
  phase: Activated T cells + macrophages produce TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-17, interferon-γ.
  Anti-TNF neutralises TNF-α and JAKi blocks intracellular JAK–STAT signalling. IL:
  interleukin, TNF: tumour necrosis factor [Created in BioRender.com]'
