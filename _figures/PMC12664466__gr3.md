caption: 'S-Nitrosylated components of the complement cascade. The complement system
  can be activated through three distinct pathways, designated classical, lectin,
  and alternative. The classical pathway is activated upon binding of C1q to antigen–antibody
  immune complexes, which triggers activation of two proteases, C1r and C1s, to form
  a complex with C1q known as the C1 complex. The C1 complex then cleaves C4 and C2,
  whose fragments combine to form C3 convertase (C4bC2b). The lectin pathway is activated
  by binding carbohydrates such as mannose-binding protein (MBP) or ficolins on the
  surface of pathogens, which activate two proteases, MASP1 and MASP2 (mannose-binding
  serine proteases 1 and 2). These proteases subsequently cleave C4 and C2, whose
  fragments combine to form the same C3 convertase as in the classical pathway. The
  alternative pathway is activated by spontaneous hydrolysis of C3 to C3–H2O, which
  binds complement Factor B (CFB) and complement Factor D (CFD) to generate C3 convertase
  (C3(H2O)Bb), which then cleaves other C3 molecules to C3b. The alternative pathway
  also forms an amplification loop by generating C3 convertase (C3bBb), which continues
  to cleave C3, thereby enhancing the effect of activation. Both C3 convertases (C4bC2b
  and C3bBb) cleave C3 into C3a and C3b. C3b can combine with two different C3 convertases
  to form C5 convertase: C4bC2aC3b from the classical and lectin pathways, and C3bBbC3b
  from the alternative pathway. This process cleaves C5 into C5a and C5b. C3a and
  C5a are anaphylatoxins that bind to C3aR and C5aR, respectively, to recruit immune
  cells to sites of injury or infection. These immune cells then secrete pro-inflammatory
  cytokines. Complement component C3b and its inactivated product, iC3b, which is
  cleaved by complement Factor I (CFI) along with complement Factor H (CFH), opsonize
  or tag pathogens, immune complexes, or synapses for phagocytic removal by microglia
  bearing their receptor, CR3. C5b binds C6, C7, C8, and multiple copies of C9 to
  form a channel in the cell membrane (designated C5b-9 or MAC [membrane attack complex]),
  leading to cell lysis. Multiple components of the complement system can be S-nitrosylated,
  as illustrated at right'
