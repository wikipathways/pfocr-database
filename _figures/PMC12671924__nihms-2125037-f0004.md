caption: In the pro-inflammatory setting (left), APCs such as DCs present myelin-derived
  autoantigens via MHC II to naive CD4+ T cells (Signal 1). Co-stimulatory molecules
  CD80/86 on APCs bind to CD28 on T cells (Signal 2), and pro-inflammatory cytokines
  such as IL-12, IL-6, IFN-γ, and TNF-β (Signal 3) further drive the differentiation
  of pathogenic effector T cells. These T cells, along with autoantibodies produced
  by activated B cells, contribute to CNS demyelination and progression of autoimmune
  pathology in MS. In contrast, as shown through the tolerogenic pathway (right),
  MPs can be used for inverse vaccination by targeting DCs and modulating their phenotype.
  Upon uptake, these particles suppress the expression of co-stimulatory molecules
  (blocking Signal 2), while still presenting antigen (Signal 1) in the context of
  anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10, IL-27, and TGF-β (Signal 3). This altered
  signaling environment promotes the differentiation of naive CD4+ T cells into regulatory
  T cells (e.g., Foxp3+ Tregs) instead of pro-inflammatory effectors, preserving immune
  tolerance and preventing autoimmunity
