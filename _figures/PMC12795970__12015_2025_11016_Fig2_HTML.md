caption: 'Low ROS levels activate cell cycle inhibitory regulatory factors such as
  FoxO and SIRT1, keeping HSCs in a quiescent state, maintaining stemness, and preventing
  DNA damage.Moderate ROS (beneficial effects) activates cell survival and proliferation
  signals. The NF-κB pathway promotes anti-apoptosis (e.g., BCL − 2, IL − 6, etc.),
  and the MAPK pathway promotes cell proliferation (Ras - Raf - MEK - ERK). This enables
  LSCs to adapt to oxidative stress and enhance their proliferative capacity.Excessively
  high ROS levels cause cellular DNA damage, leading to cell senescence and apoptosis
  (programmed cell death). Balancing is required through the antioxidant system.Antioxidant
  responses of LSCs (core defense mechanisms).LSCs reduce ROS and avoid damage by
  activating the antioxidant system: The Nrf2 signaling pathway promotes the expression
  of antioxidant genes (HO − 1, NQO1). The GSH/Trx (glutathione/thioredoxin) system
  scavenges free radicals to maintain redox balance. Catalase decomposes hydrogen
  peroxide to reduce the ROS level.Feedback regulation of moderate ROS: After a moderate
  increase in ROS, a positive feedback loop can be formed: promoting drug resistance
  and reducing chemotherapy - induced ROS damage. This further enhances the adaptability
  of LSCs, enabling them to continuously expand and evade immune surveillance. Abbreviation:
  ROS, Reactive oxygen species; HSC, Hematopoietic stem cell; LSC, Leukemia stem cell;
  FoxO, Forkhead box protein O; SIRT1, Silent Information Regulator 2 Related Enzyme
  1;HO – 1, Heme Oxygenase-1; NQO1, NAD (P) H: quinone oxidoreductase 1; MAPK, Mitogen-activated
  protein kinase; BCL − 2, B-cell lymphoma 2;IL – 6,; Nrf2, Nuclear factor erythroid
  2-related factor 2; GSH/Trx, glutathione/thioredoxin; NF-κB, nuclear factor-Κb'
