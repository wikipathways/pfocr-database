caption: Summary of targets proteins of S-sulfhydration and their biological effects
  in bone diseases. H2S exerts its influence on osteoblasts, osteoclasts, and chondrocytes
  by S-sulfhydration. BMP2 is S-sulfhydrated and then promotes the phosphorylation
  of SMAD1/4/5/8 complex, thereby promoting RUNX2 phosphorylation and accelerating
  osteogenesis. This process can be suppressed by several miRNAs, including miR-17,
  miR-26, miR-23a, and miR-31. H2S also facilitates S-sulfhydration of cysteine residues
  located on the Ca2+ transient receptor potential channel protein TRPV3/6, and activates
  the PKC/ERK/β-Catenin signaling pathway, ultimately leading to the activation of
  RUNX2 and subsequent promotion of BMSCs osteogenic differentiation. The S-sulfhydration
  of SIRT3 enhances new bone formation by promoting OPA1-mediated mitochondrial fusion
  and H3K9me3-mediated heterochromatin stabilization. In contrast, SAM transfers a
  methyl group to a substrate to generate SAH, which is subsequently hydrolyzed into
  adenosine and homocysteine. H2S regulates osteoclastogenesis mediated by OPG/RANKL
  by inhibiting C-Jun/DNMT1, thereby reducing bone loss caused by HCY. SP1 S-sulfhydration
  inhibits the expression of HDAC6, which in turn delays arthritis through the HDAC6/MyD88/NF-κB
  signalling axis, ultimately leading to a reduction in inflammatory response. In
  addition, H2S can also stimulate the PI3K/AKT pathway to suppress NFκB activation
  mediated by ROS generation, thereby attenuating inflammatory response and promoting
  chondrogenesis. BMP2, bone morphogenetic protein 2; RUNX2, runt-related transcription
  factor 2; TRPV3/6, transient receptor potential vanilloid sub-type 3/6; SIRT3, sirtuin
  3; OPA1, optic atrophy 1; SAM, S-adenosyl methionine; SAH, S-adenosyl homocysteine;
  HCY, homocysteine; SP1, specificity protein 1; HDAC6, histone deacetylase 6
