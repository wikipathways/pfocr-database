caption: Cellular and molecular components involved in blood–brain barrier (BBB) integrity.
  The BBB is a dynamic structure composed of endothelial cells (red), pericytes (orange),
  and astrocytic endfeet (green) that modulate brain homeostasis. Endothelial cells
  are connected by tight junctions (Claudins 3/5, Occludin, ZO‐1/2/3) and adherens
  junctions (VE‐cadherin), while also expressing PECAM1 and connexin‐43 (Cx43), which
  contribute to barrier selectivity and leukocyte trafficking. Astrocytes modulate
  barrier function through their perivascular endfeet, which are enriched in aquaporin‐4
  (Aqp4), a water channel involved in fluid regulation; Kir4.1, a potassium channel
  important for ion homeostasis; and Cx30/Cx43, which facilitates intercellular communication.
  They also secrete trophic factors such as glial cell‐derived neurotrophic factor
  (GDNF) and fibroblast growth factor 2 (FGF2), which enhance endothelial stability.
  Pericytes regulate endothelial signaling, vascular permeability, and capillary diameter
  via PDGFB/PDGFRB signaling, and are characterized by markers including NG2, CD13,
  and Desmin. Disruption of these molecular pathways can compromise BBB integrity,
  contributing to the development of psychiatric disorders, such as depression, bipolar
  disorder, and generalized anxiety disorder
