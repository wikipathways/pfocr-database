caption: Using crosstalk manipulation of tryptophan metabolites to enhance resistance
  to abiotic stress.(A) The exogenous application of tryptophan improves plant cold
  resistance. Cold stress negatively impacts plant processes, including germination,
  seedling growth, pollen viability, and fruit quality. The effects of exogenous tryptophan
  on strawberry cold resistance were assessed by comparing drench-only (T1) and combined
  drench plus foliar spray (T2) treatments. Tryptophan-treated strawberries exhibited
  enhanced development of reproductive organs, and T2 significantly increased flower
  and fruit numbers at harvest compared with T1, indicating improved resilience. Mechanistic
  studies revealed that exogenous tryptophan stabilized competition among various
  metabolic pathways, increasing levels of key metabolites such as N-acetyl-serotonin
  (NAS), 2-OHMEL, and AFMK, which have strong antioxidant properties. It also triggered
  reproductive processes in parallel with genetic responses in the tryptophan metabolic
  pathway. The mechanism model was redrawn with permission from Ayyanath et al. (2024).
  Copyright © 2024, John Wiley and Sons.(B) Engineered tryptophan metabolism enhances
  plant resistance to drought and salinity. Drought and salinity exert strong environmental
  pressures, impairing physiological functions such as photosynthesis, respiration,
  and nutrient uptake. The role of TSB1 in improving plant development and stress
  resistance was examined by constructing the amiR-TSB1 line with reduced TSB1 levels,
  as well as TSB1 mutants trp2-1 and trp2-8 through gene silencing. These plants displayed
  increased drought and salt resistance with higher survival rates. Mechanistic analysis
  showed that, under normal conditions, TSB1 promotes tryptophan and auxin synthesis
  to support growth and suppresses β-glucosidase 1 (BG1) activity to avoid ABA accumulation.
  In contrast, under salt and drought stress, TSB1 expression is downregulated, reducing
  tryptophan and auxin synthesis, inhibiting BG1, and promoting ABA accumulation to
  enhance stress tolerance. Sulfenylation of TSB1 at cysteine-308 (TSB1 Cys308) under
  stress disrupts the TSB1–BG1 interaction, further increasing ABA levels and abiotic
  stress resilience. The crosstalk between tryptophan metabolism and ABA synthesis
  pathways can be modulated via gene regulation, supporting precise control of the
  plant growth–defense balance. The mechanism model was redrawn with permission from
  Liu et al. (2022). Copyright © 2022, Elsevier. All figures were created with BioRender
  (BioRender.com)
