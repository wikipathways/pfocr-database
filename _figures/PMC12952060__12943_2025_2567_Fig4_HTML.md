caption: Lipid metabolism and alternative pathways in ferroptosis regulation. Lipid
  peroxides in ferroptosis predominantly originate from phospholipid-polyunsaturated
  fatty acids (PL-PUFAs), especially phosphatidylethanolamines containing arachidonic
  acid (PE-AA) or adrenic acid (PE-AdA). Free PUFAs are first activated by acyl-CoA
  synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) and subsequently esterified into membrane
  phospholipids by lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 3 (LPCAT3), forming substrates
  prone to lipid peroxidation (LPO). These substrates are then oxidized by lipoxygenases
  (ALOXs) or cytochrome P450 oxidoreductase (POR), generating phospholipid hydroperoxides
  (PLOOHs) that damage membranes and trigger ferroptosis. In contrast, saturated fatty
  acids (SFAs) synthesized by fatty acid synthase (FASN) are converted by stearoyl-CoA
  desaturase 1 (SCD1) into monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs), which are subsequently
  activated by ACSL3. MUFAs compete with PUFAs for phospholipid incorpotation, thereby
  reducing PUFAs content and limiting LPO. To counteract ferroptosis, cells deploy
  multiple antioxidant and membrane repair systems, including the FSP1-CoQ10 axis,
  the mitochondrial DHODH-CoQ10 pathway, the GCH1-BH4 system, ESCRT-III-mediated membrane
  remodeling, and enzymatic degradation of oxidized phospholipids by calcium-independent
  phospholipase A2β (iPLA2β)
