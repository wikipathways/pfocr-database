caption: In the early stage of tumors, CXCL10 + macrophages recruit CXCR3 + effector
  T cells; however, as the disease progresses, the proportion of CXCR3 + Tregs gradually
  increases, while the number of effector T cells decreases. Tumor-infiltrating macrophages
  (TAM) will polarize into M2-TAM, and simultaneously upregulate the expression of
  CCL20, which binds to the CCR6 receptor on the surface of Tregs. Nuclear factor
  κB2 (NF-κB2) is overexpressed in EBV-positive NPC. It activates the infiltration
  of Tregs through the interaction signal of tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily
  member 7 and its receptor (CD70-CD27). Regulatory B cells (Bregs) actively secrete
  pro-inflammatory substances such as TGF-β1 and IL10, and promote the expansion of
  Tregs by increasing TNF-TNFR2 of tumor necrosis factor and interleukin 16-CD4 receptor
  (IL16-CD4). LMP1 can upregulate the expression of chemokines CCL5 and CXCL10 through
  activating the NF-κB pathway, promoting the recruitment of T cells and myeloid cells;
  LMP1 may also promote the production of CCL17 and CCL22 in the tumor microenvironment
  by recruiting dendritic cells, attracting CCR4 + Tregs to migrate towards the tumor
  tissue. EBNA1 upregulates the expression of TGF-β1, inducing the differentiation
  of initial CD4 + T cells towards Tregs, and by activating the TGF-β1-SMAD3/c-JUN
  signaling axis, downregulating the expression of miR-200a, thereby inducing the
  secretion of chemokine ligand CXCL12. CXCL12 binds to its receptor CXCR4, not only
  directly attracting CCR4 + Tregs to migrate towards the tumor, but also promoting
  the secretion of chemokine ligand CXCL12
