caption: Locus coeruleus norepinephrine (LC-NE) pathway degeneration in Alzheimer’s
  disease and atomoxetine inhibition of the NE transporter to restore NE regulation
  of memory. (a) Degeneration of the LC-NE pathway and NE loss in AD. The LC-NE pathway
  innervates the cortex and hippocampus regions that regulate cognition and memory
  functions.,  In AD, degeneration of LC-NE neurons results in (1) loss of NE neurotransmitter
  in LC neurons due to neurodegeneration and (2) diminished NE in cortical and hippocampus
  brain regions that regulate cognition and memory functions. This (a) is adapted
  from Bari et al. (ref ); the article of ref  is under the terms of the CC-BY-NC-SA
  license, which permits use in any medium with proper citation that is provided here.
  (b) Atomoxetine inhibits the NE transporter (NET) to elevate extracellular NE transmitter
  signaling to facilitate cognition and memory. Synaptic nerve terminals of LC-NE
  neurons produce and store NE, which is released upon neuronal activation. Extracellular
  NE at the synaptic cleft regulates adrenergic receptors that control cognition,
  memory, and related behaviors. NE actions are terminated by reuptake by the NE transporter
  (NET). In AD, the NE in nerve terminals of LC-NE neurons is diminished, which can
  be restored by atomoxetine inhibition of NET, to elevate extracellular NE to promote
  cognitive and memory functions
