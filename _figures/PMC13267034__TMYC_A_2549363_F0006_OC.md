caption: 'Summary of the regulatory mechanisms of G protein signalling pathways in
  Saccharomyces cerevisiae and typical filamentous fungi (such as Neurospora crassa,
  Aspergillus nidulans, and Botrytis cinerea). In these fungi, G proteins predominantly
  orchestrate multiple biological processes including growth, development, and environmental
  adaptation through two canonical pathways: cAMP/PKA (yellow part) and MAPK signalling
  (blue part) cascades. In filamentous fungi, three major MAPK signalling pathways
  are commonly recognised: the cell wall integrity (CWI) pathway, the high osmolarity
  glycerol (HOG) pathway, and the development/pathogenicity-associated pathway. Notably,
  only the developmental/pathogenic filamentation-related pathway is directly regulated
  by G proteins, while the other two pathways are indirectly influenced by G protein
  signalling. Specifically, G proteins can activate Rho GTPase in the CWI pathway;
  and PKA generated through the cAMP signalling cascade exerts specific regulatory
  effects on MAPK Hog1 in the HOG pathways. These signals regulate the various phenotypic
  traits of fungi by activating downstream transcription factors, including AtfA,
  Cdtf1, Mbp1, Mcm1, Msn2/4, RlmA, Som1, Ste12, StuA, Swi6, and Tec1. AC, adenylate
  cyclase; PKC, protein kinase C; WCS, cell wall integrity and stress response component;
  Sho1/Sln1, receptor proteins. The dotted line represents indirect regulation, and
  the solid line represents direct regulation. This figure was created with BioGDP.com'
