caption: Dietary fiber regulates bone health through modulating osteoblast function.
  In the gut, dietary fiber undergoes fermentation, yielding short-chain fatty acids
  (SCFAs). These SCFAs suppress the NF-κB signaling pathway by upregulating NF-κBIA,
  an inhibitor of NF-κB. This upregulation prevents the activation of NF-κB, subsequently
  inhibiting the transcription of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-18, and
  NLRP3, which in turn reduces systemic inflammation. Moreover, SCFAs activate the
  TGF-β, NFAT, and SMAD signaling pathways. The activation of the TGF-β pathway leads
  to the phosphorylation and activation of SMAD proteins, which then translocate to
  the nucleus and bind to specific DNA sequences to promote gene expression. Meanwhile,
  the activation of NFAT allows it to enter the nucleus and cooperate with other transcription
  factors. Through the coordinated action of these pathways, the expression of WNT10b,
  a key factor in osteogenesis, is promoted. WNT10b then initiates a cascade of events
  that enhance osteoblast differentiation and function, thereby limiting inflammation-related
  bone loss and tipping the balance in favor of bone formation over resorption. Ultimately,
  this regulatory process reduces osteoclast-mediated apoptosis, contributing to enhanced
  bone strength and density. NF-κB = nuclear factor-kappa B; NF-κBIA = nuclear factor-kappa
  B inhibitor alpha; IL-1β = interleukin-1β; IL-18 = interleukin-18; NLRP3 = nucleotide-binding
  domain; leucine-rich-containg pyrin domain-containing-3; WNT10b = wingless-related
  integration site 10b; TGF-β = transforming growth factor-β; NFAT = nuclear factor
