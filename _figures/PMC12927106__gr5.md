caption: 'The regulatory role of CBD in intestinal function. CBD exerts antioxidant
  and anti-inflammatory effects in the gut by activating the Nrf2 pathway and suppressing
  NF-κB activity. Its ability to maintain intestinal redox homeostasis prevents oxidative
  stress-induced shifts from strict anaerobes to aerotolerant or facultative bacterial
  species. The SCFAs produced by strict anaerobes enhance the intestinal barrier and
  provide benefits in controlling ROS generation and mitigating inflammation. Furthermore,
  through stimulation of the CB1 receptor, CBD promotes the expression of tight junction
  proteins, thereby reducing intestinal permeability. When facultative anaerobes release
  LPS, it translocates across the compromised barrier, triggering the production of
  ROS and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8) – a key driver of IBD. These
  inflammatory mediators may subsequently enter the portal vein and systemic circulation
  during intestinal nutrient absorption, potentially initiating inflammation in distant
  tissues or organs. CBD, cannabidiol; CB1, cannabinoid receptor 1; NOX1, NADPH oxidases
  1; ROS, reactive oxygen species; O2−•, superoxide; H2O2, hydrogen peroxide; PUFAs,
  polyunsaturated fatty acids; LPS, lipopolysaccharide; SOD, superoxide dismutase;
  HO-1, heme oxygenase-1; NQO1, NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1; SCFAs, short-chain
  fatty acids; IBD, inflammatory bowel disease; TNF-α: tumor necrosis factor-alpha;
  IL-6, interleukin-6; IL-8, interleukin-8. Closed arrows indicate inhibition; open
  arrows indicate activation'
