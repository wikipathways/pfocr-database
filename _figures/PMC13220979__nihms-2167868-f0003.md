caption: 'This schematic summarizes four proposed cellular pathways through which
  environmental exposures may influence plasma cell disorders; the strength of evidence
  varies by exposure and pathway and includes epidemiologic associations and experimental
  model data. (Top left) Oxidative stress: agents such as ionizing radiation and pesticides
  increase reactive oxygen species (ROS) through mitochondrial, peroxisomal, and NADPH-oxidase
  activity, leading to oxidative DNA lesions and impaired redox homeostasis. (Bottom
  left) DNA-damage response (DDR): heavy metals, tobacco smoke, and industrial emissions
  interfere with various DNA repair mechanisms, promoting genomic instability and
  defective double-strand-break resolution. (Top right) Aryl hydrocarbon receptor
  (AhR) activation: aromatic hydrocarbons, including TCDD and related dioxins, bind
  AhR, inducing its nuclear translocation and transcriptional reprogramming that enhances
  angiogenesis, suppresses cytotoxic T-cell activity, and favors immune evasion. (Bottom
  right) Activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) dysregulation: exposure to certain
  herbicides and xenobiotics increases off-target AID activity, generating cytosine
  deamination events, chromosomal translocations, and aberrant class-switch recombination'
