caption: Dose-dependent regulation of immune cell function by dopamine receptors (DRD).
  This figure illustrates the dual effects of dopamine on immune cells via DRD subtypes
  in a dose-dependent manner. At low dopamine concentrations (10⁻⁸ ~ 10⁻⁷M, left,
  green background), dopamine is secreted by sympathetic nerves or immune cells and
  binds to D1-like receptors (D1/D5) on NK cells and T cells. This activates the Gas/adenylate
  cyclase/cAMP/PKA pathway, enhancing NK cell cytotoxicity (with increased IFN-γ/TNF-α
  secretion) and reducing T cell PD-1 expression to promote proliferation and antitumor
  activity. At high dopamine concentrations (μM level, right, red background), excessive
  dopamine (from systemic secretion or stress) binds to D2-like receptors (D2/D3/D4),
  inhibiting adenylate cyclase via Gi to decrease cAMP and CREB phosphorylation. This
  suppresses NK cell cytotoxicity and T cell activation (reduced ERK1/2 phosphorylation,
  increased KLF2 expression, and decreased CD69/CD25/IL-2), inducing immune suppression.
  The narrow therapeutic window between the two concentration ranges poses a major
  challenge for clinical application of dopamine receptor modulators
