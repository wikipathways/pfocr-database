caption: 'Red blood cell metabolic pathways affected by caffeine exposure. Schematic
  diagram indicating that caffeine is both a competitive inhibitor of adenosine binding
  to the ADORA2b adenosine receptor, which downregulates subsequent downstream signaling
  through AMP-activated protein kinase, and a direct inhibitor of glucose-6-phosphate
  dehydrogenase, the rate-limiting enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway. Taken
  together, this demonstrates that caffeine negatively affects the three major red
  blood cell pathways that utilize glucose (i.e., glycolysis, the Luebering-Rapoport
  pathway, and the pentose phosphate pathway), thereby inhibiting their synthetic
  capacities (downward facing red arrows) to generate energy (i.e., ATP), enhance
  oxygen offloading by hemoglobin (i.e., 2,3-BPG); and resist storage-induced oxidative
  stress (i.e., NADPH); respectively. ADORA2b: an adenosine receptor; AMPK: AMP-activated
  protein kinase; ATP: adenosine triphosphate; BPGM: biphosphoglycerate mutase; 1,7DMU:
  1,7-dimethyluric acid; 1,3,7TMU: 1,3,7-trimethyluric acid; CYP1A2 and CYP2A6: two
  cytochrome p450 isoforms; LRP: Luebering-Rapoport pathway; GLUT1: glucose transporter
  1; Glc-6-P: glucose-6-phosphate; G6PD: glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase; PPP: pentose
  phosphate pathway; RBC: red blood cell'
