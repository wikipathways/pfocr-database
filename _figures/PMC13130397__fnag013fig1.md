caption: Structural features and signaling pathways of key CLRs in immune responses.
  Dectin-1, recognizes β-glucans from fungi (e.g. Candida albicans, Aspergillus fumigatus)
  and certain bacteria. Upon ligand binding, Dectin-1 clusters and activates Syk kinase
  via its ITAM-like motif. Syk phosphorylates CARD9, leading to the recruitment of
  BCL10 and MALT1, forming the complex, which activates the canonical NF-κB pathway.
  Additionally, Dectin-1 can trigger the non-canonical NF-κB pathway through Raf-1-MAPK
  signaling, promoting pro-inflammatory cytokine production (e.g. TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6).
  Mincle, recognizes glycolipids such as trehalose-6,6’-dimycolate (TDM, cord factor)
  from Mycobacterium tuberculosis and SAP130 from damaged cells. Ligand engagement
  induces Mincle dimerization and recruitment of the FcRγ adaptor, which contains
  ITAM motifs. FcRγ-associated Syk becomes activated, leading to CARD9-BCL10-MALT1
  complex formation and canonical NF-κB activation, resulting in pro-inflammatory
  cytokine secretion and enhanced antimicrobial responses. Dectin-3 recognizes α-mannans
  from fungi (e.g. Malassezia, Candida tropicalis). Similar to Mincle, Dectin-3 pairs
  with FcRγ, and ligand binding activates Syk, initiating the CARD9-BCL10-MALT1 cascade
  and NF-κB-dependent inflammatory responses, including cytokine production. CD206
  contains eight CTLDs and an FNII domain. It recognizes mannose-, fucose-, and N-acetylglucosamine-containing
  glycans on pathogens (e.g. fungi, mycobacteria) and apoptotic cells. Unlike Syk-coupled
  CLRs, MR primarily mediates endocytosis and antigen presentation rather than direct
  Syk/NF-κB activation
