caption: Physiological roles of mitochondria in neural development. This schematic
  summarizes the major physiological roles of mitochondria during neural development,
  including energy metabolism, Ca2 + homeostasis, redox regulation, control of apoptosis,
  mitochondrial dynamics and quality control, and intercellular signaling. In developing
  neurons, oxidative phosphorylation couples the tricarboxylic acid cycle to the electron
  transport chain to generate ATP and support processes such as neuronal differentiation,
  axonal and dendritic growth, and synaptogenesis. Mitochondria also buffer intracellular
  Ca2 + via the MCU complex, NCLX, and ER–mitochondria contact sites, thereby shaping
  Ca2 + signals that govern neurotransmission and synaptic plasticity. ROS produced
  by the respiratory chain act as signaling molecules at physiological levels, while
  antioxidant systems including SOD2, GPx, catalase, Trx2, and Prx3 maintain redox
  homeostasis. The intrinsic apoptotic pathway, controlled by B-cell lymphoma 2（Bcl‑2）
  family proteins, cytochrome c release, and caspase activation, refines neural circuits
  by eliminating excess or damaged cells. Dynamic fission–fusion cycles and PINK1/Parkin‑dependent
  mitophagy preserve mitochondrial morphology and remove dysfunctional organelles,
  whereas extracellular vesicles, tunneling nanotubes, and gap junctions mediate mitochondrial
  and metabolic communication between cells. Together, these integrated mitochondrial
  functions are essential for normal neuronal maturation and network formation (created
