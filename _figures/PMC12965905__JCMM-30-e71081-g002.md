caption: Schematic illustration of HBx‐mediated ADAM10 to cut MICA/B shedding from
  the membrane of HCC cell leads to escape natural killer cell attack. MICA/B binds
  to the NKG2D receptor on NK‐92 cells, and NK‐92 cells recognise and kill liver cancer
  cells. Under HBV infection, HBx regulates the transcription initiation of HIF‐1α
  by activating PI3K/AKT signalling pathway and promotes the expression of ADAM10.
  ADAM10 is regulated by HIF‐1α signalling pathway to cut MICA/B shedding from the
  membrane of HCC cell. After MICA/B is shed from the cellular membrane of liver cancer,
  on the one hand, the content of MICA/B on the cellular membrane of liver cancer
  is reduced; on the other hand, the shed MICA/B enters the tumour microenvironment
  and forms sMICA/B, which binds with NKG2D of NK‐92 cells. By blocking the recognition
  of MICA/B on the cellular membrane of liver cancer, NKG2D reduces the binding of
  NK‐92 cells to liver cancer cells, interferes with the attack of NK‐92 cells on
  liver cancer cells, and causes liver cancer cells to escape immune surveillance
