caption: 'Process of glycoRNA biosynthesis. GlycoRNA biogenesis depends outilizes
  the canonical N-glycosylation pathways, the same pathways used by glycoproteins
  and glycolipids. The biosynthetic pathway of tRNA glycosylation is presented here
  as an example. 1) RNAs are synthesized in the nucleus and the glycosylation process
  commences with the modification of specific RNA molecules through the addition of
  acp3U, a reaction driven by DTW domain-containing 2 (DTWD2) enzyme. This modification
  enables their subsequent translocation into the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) lumen.
  Within the ER lumen, GlcNAc2Man5 is transferred to dolichol phosphate, and then
  flipped to the luminal side by a flippase. After sequential adding of four mannose
  and three glucose residues to form Glc3Man9GlcNAc2-PP-dolichol, the glycan moiety
  is transferred by oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) to an N-X-S-T sequon on a nascent
  peptide chain. 2) The glycoRNAs then traffics through the secretory pathway, undergoing
  further glycan maturation in Glogi. 3) Finally, the mature glycoRNAs are packaged
  into vesicles and transported to cell surface to execute their biological functions.
  Graphical representations with different colors and shapes in the upper left denote
  different monosaccharides: Hexose (Hex, green or yellow circle), Fucose (Fuc, red
  triangle), N-Acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc, blue square), and N-Acetylneuraminic Acid
  (NeuAc, purple diamond) or N-Hydroxyneuraminic Acid (NeuGc, white diamond) residues.
