caption: 'Dietary choline is metabolized by gut microbial TMA lyase activity to produce
  trimethylamine (TMA), which is subsequently oxidized in the liver by flavin-containing
  monooxygenase 3 (FMO3) to generate trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO). TMAO acts through
  multiple molecular pathways with diverse physiological and pathological effects:
  chaperone and osmolyte function: TMAO stabilizes proteins by promoting hydration
  shell formation, preserving native structure, and protecting cellular function.
  Type I interferon signaling: TMAO enhances type I interferon (IFN) pathway activation,
  thereby boosting antitumor immunity in pancreatic cancer. PERK and ER stress: TMAO
  activates PERK in hepatocytes, triggering eIF2α phosphorylation and ATF4 induction,
  leading to increased gluconeogenesis and hepatic insulin resistance. In triple-negative
  breast cancer (TNBC), TMAO induces tumor cell pyroptosis through PERK activation,
  enhancing CD8+ T cell-mediated antitumor immunity. Scavenger receptor, cholesterol,
  and bile acid homeostasis: TMAO upregulates macrophage scavenger receptors (CD36
  and SR-A1) to enhance uptake of modified lipoproteins and impairs reverse cholesterol
  transport (RCT) by repressing FMO3–CYP7A1 and suppressing efflux transporters (ABCG5/8),
  leading to atherosclerosis (AS) and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). Inflammasomes
  and reactive oxygen species (ROS): TMAO promotes AS by increasing mitochondrial
  ROS via SIRT3–SOD2 inhibition and activating the TXNIP–NLRP3 inflammasome pathway.
  TMAO-induced ROS and inflammasome activation also contribute to GVHD pathogenesis.
  ER: endoplasmic reticulum; GVHD: graft-versus-host disease; PERK: protein kinase
  RNA-like ER kinase; SOD2: superoxide dismutase 2.; ATF4: activating transcription
  factor 4; SIRT3: sirtuin3; TXNIP: thioredoxin interacting protein'
