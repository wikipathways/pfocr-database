caption: a) Role of RT within the LADME Profile [58]. After drug administration, the
  active compound can undergo a series of processes, including release from its pharmaceutical
  formulation, absorption into the bloodstream, and conversion into its pharmacologically
  active form. Once in circulation, the free drug fraction can be subject to plasma
  protein binding, tissue distribution, metabolism, and excretion. A portion of the
  active drug reaches its target site, where it binds—in this case, a G protein-coupled
  receptor—initiating the pharmacological effect b) Key mechanisms underlying sustained
  signaling and prolonged pharmacological response, as evidenced by in vivo studies
  in which rapid ligand washout following receptor saturation revealed persistent
  target engagement and downstream signaling. These include RT, where extended target
  engagement leads to prolonged pathway activation; rebinding, observed in ligands
  with rapid binding kinetics; and signal propagation through internalized receptors,
  allowing intracellular signaling to persist beyond initial receptor-ligand interaction;
