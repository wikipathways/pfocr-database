caption: Renal epithelial-immune cell interactions in sterile inflammation. Injury
  to PTs triggers the release of DAMPs such as UDP-Glc, which accumulates in the CD
  and activates apical P2Y14 receptors on A-ICs. This activation stimulates downstream
  signaling pathways and induces the production of proinflammatory chemokines (IL-1β,
  CXCL1, CXCL2, and CCL2), promoting leukocyte recruitment and renal inflammation.
  In parallel, ATP released from injured tubules or immune cells activates P2X7 receptors
  on both epithelial and immune cells, amplifying cytokine release. Damaged PTs also
  upregulate KIM-1 and become semiprofessional phagocytes capable of engulfing apoptotic
  or necrotic debris. By inhibiting NF-κB signaling, KIM-1 helps limit DAMP release
  and restrain excessive inflammation. Conversely, ATP-induced activation of P2Y2
  receptors in renal tubular cells exerts protective effects, reducing tubular apoptosis
  and inflammation. In addition, HMGB1 released by necrotic tubules binds to TLR4
  on neutrophils, inducing neutrophil NET formation and further epithelial injury.
