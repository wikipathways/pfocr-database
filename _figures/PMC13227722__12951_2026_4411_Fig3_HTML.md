caption: TF mechanobiological regulation pathway. (a) Mechanical cues within the tumor
  microenvironment, including ECM stiffening, interstitial flow, and compression,
  activate mechanoreceptors such as integrins and PIEZO1 channels [36, 37]. Notably,
  the engagement of tissue factor (TF) with factor VIIa (FVIIa) facilitates the formation
  of a high-affinity complex that associates with activated integrins, anchoring the
  procoagulant machinery at the membrane and priming cells for thrombotic signaling
  [38]. (b) Mechanotransduction activates multiple intracellular cascades. Integrin-FAK-SRC
  signaling initiates Hippo-independent YAP/TAZ nuclear translocation, while FAK also
  stimulates PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK/ERK pathways [39–45]. Concurrently, PIEZO1-mediated
  Ca²⁺ influx activates PKC and calcineurin, promoting NFAT dephosphorylation and
  nuclear translocation [46, 47]. Together, these pathways converge on the TF promoter,
  amplifying its transcription. (c) Newly synthesized TF is transported from the Golgi
  through vesicular trafficking pathways to the plasma membrane. A subset of TF is
  packaged into EVs, while others are inserted into the membrane surface for coagulation
  engagement [35, 38, 48]. (d) TF is surface-presented in an encrypted (non-coagulant)
  state. Mechanical and biochemical cues induce the exposure of phosphatidylserine
  (PS) and disruption of sphingomyelin, decrypting TF and enabling complex formation
  with FVIIa and FXa, thereby initiating extrinsic coagulation [49, 50]. Created with
