caption: 'A model for PDH-dependent coordination of host immunometabolism and DT.
  Schematic summary of ΔPdh versus WT S. pyogenes infection outcomes in murine skin
  tissue: (1) Lesion morphology and tissue damage: WT infection produces large, necrotic
  ulcers, whereas ΔPdh infection generates smaller, contained lesions with preserved
  tissue architecture. (2) Immune composition: ΔPdh infection expand a macrophage-
  and neutrophil-dominated innate program. (3) Neutrophil and macrophage polarization:
  ΔPdh infection reprograms myeloid cells toward stress-adaptive, pro-repair transcriptional
  states, with upregulation of ferritin genes (Ftl1, Fth1), Srgn, Fau, and Gadd45b,
  and downregulation of inflammatory and matrix-degrading effectors (Retnlg, Lcn2,
  Plac8, Mmp8, S100a6). (4) Host metabolism: Loss of bacterial PDH reduces acetate/formate
  production, suppressing acetyl-CoA synthesis and mitochondrial metabolism in immune
  cells, and limiting histone acetylation (H3K9ac1). (5) Cytokine and chemokine regulation:
  ΔPdh infection induces IL-10 while maintaining controlled neutrophil and monocyte
  recruitment, promoting coordinated, non-cytotoxic immune responses. (6) DT pathways:
  ΔPdh-infected tissue exhibits upregulation of hypoxia, iron homeostasis, NRF2 oxidative
  stress, AMPK–mTOR signaling, and regulated cell death via apoptosis/autophagy, while
  lytic pathways appear to be suppressed. Figure created with Biorender.com'
