caption: Alteration of DAMPs after TBI leads to multi-organ pathological and systemic
  changes. The primary injury in TBI leads to the overproduction of DAMPs. This excessive
  release of DAMPs triggers an inflammatory response that is detrimental to brain
  cells. The activation of microglia and astrocytes, alongside neuronal injury, further
  exacerbates secondary brain injury, manifesting as oxidative stress, cell death,
  excitotoxicity, BBB dysfunction, brain edema, and neuroinflammation. During the
  early stages of secondary brain injury, the immune response in the brain predominates;
  however, the peripheral immune response subsequently influences the entire body.
  The HPA axis and ANS may affect the immune response following TBI. On one hand,
  neurohormones weaken innate immunity and increase the risk of bacterial or aseptic
  infections in patients with TBI. On the other hand, extracellular vesicles (EVs)
  carrying DAMPs released by nerve cells can be distributed through various bodily
  fluids, including blood, lymph, and urine, leading to systemic pathological changes.
  Pulmonary complications are often among the most serious and frequent consequences
  of TBI. Respiratory failure associated with ALI has a notably high mortality rate.
  NPE exacerbates dyspnea in patients with TBI, potentially linked to the MAPK–NFκB
  inflammatory pathway triggered by S100B and HMGB1 in the lungs; changes in liver
  function following TBI are characterized by increased SAA1. As hepatocytes are the
  primary source of SAA1, its elevation serves as a damage marker in patients with
  TBI. Furthermore, within 12–24 h post-TBI, increased liver enzyme activity (ALT
  and AST) reflects hepatic injury. Numerous experimental and clinical studies indicate
  that, during acute and chronic hepatitis, the expression of HMGB1 and IL-33, along
  with that of their receptors RAGE and ST2, respectively, is significantly upregulated.
  It can be speculated that, in the long-term pathological phase of secondary brain
  injury, the onset and progression of hepatitis are related to the transport of DAMPs
  from the CNS to the entire body via EVs after TBI; TBI also impacts kidney function,
  where elevated renal load following systemic vasoconstriction may heighten the risk
  of AKI. Impaired kidney function could disrupt fluid balance within the body, exacerbating
  cerebral edema and increasing the risk of systemic edema. Kidney diseases that are
  closely associated with the overexpression of multiple HSP subtypes, including HSP70,
  HSP90, and HSP27, include chronic glomerulonephritis, ischemic and toxic AKI, and
  obstructive nephropathy. EVs laden with DAMPs can circulate throughout the body
  via various fluids, underscoring the close relationship between post-TBI renal functional
  changes and DAMP transport; the heart is subject to numerous cardiovascular abnormalities,
  including ECG changes, arrhythmias, and the release of cardiac injury biomarkers.
  Severely affected patients with TBI may exhibit LVD, reduced left ventricular ejection
  fraction, NSM, and abnormal heart rates, indicating persistent autonomic dysfunction.
  The binding of HMGB1 to TLR4 on cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells is a significant
  contributor to hypertension following TBI; gastrointestinal function after TBI is
  primarily characterized by mucosal damage and intestinal flora imbalance. The inflammatory
  factors and chemokines released into the gastrointestinal tract due to heightened
  neuroinflammation are critical causes of mucosal damage. The imbalance of intestinal
  flora is also influenced by the ANS, hindering the digestion and absorption processes
  in patients with TBI, prolonging disease duration, and negatively impacting prognosis.
  Consequently, TBI involves multi-organ damage throughout the body via the HPA axis,
  ANS, and immune system. Importantly, DAMPs within the brain can be transported to
  various organs via EVs, which play a crucial role in this process. HPA hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal
  axis, ANS autonomic nervous system, EVs extracellular vesicles, ALI acute lung injury,

  glycation, BBB blood–brain barrier, DAMPs damage-associated molecular patterns,

  system, EVs extracellular vesicles, NPE neurogenic pulmonary edema, AKI acute kidney
  injury, ECG electrocardiogram, LVD left ventricular dysfunction, NSM neurogenic
  stunned myocardium
