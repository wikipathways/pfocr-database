caption: ZmPEPCK2 enhances TCA cycle flux to redirect amino acid carbon skeletons
  into starch and nitrogen into protein synthesis.Nitrogenous assimilates, such as
  Asp and Glu, are imported into kernels via phloem unloading. Unloaded Asp is converted
  to OAA, which is then catalyzed by PEPCK to form PEP. As indicated by the two green
  arrows (labeled 1 and 2), PEP can enter two distinct metabolic pathways. The first
  pathway involves conversion of PEP to sugars through gluconeogenesis. In the second
  pathway, PEP is converted to pyruvate, which subsequently enters the TCA cycle or
  contributes to amino acid synthesis. Glu substantially contributes through its conversion
  to key metabolites; it enters the TCA cycle as 2-OG or is utilized for synthesis
  of gamma-aminobutyric acid and Arg. Metabolism of 2-OG within the TCA cycle results
  in OAA production, replenishing substrate for PEPCK-mediated PEP production. PEP
  is then used as a precursor for synthesis of carbon and nitrogen storage compounds,
  such as starch and amino acids. Coordinated unloading of Asp and Glu promotes gluconeogenesis
  and amino acid synthesis, ultimately leading to increased starch and protein accumulation
  during grain filling. In the bar graph, black, red, and blue bars represent WT,
  OE, and knockdown materials (R), respectively. The molecular formula of each amino
  acid is presented; nitrogen atoms derived from Asp are indicated in red, whereas
  those originating from Glu are circled in blue. Values are presented as means ±
  SD (n = 3; ∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01, ∗∗∗P < 0.001; Student’s t-test). Abbreviations
  are defined in Supplemental Dataset 11
