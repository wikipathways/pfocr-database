caption: Application of endogenous microorganisms in cancer therapy. Treatment strategies
  involving endogenous microorganisms in cancer therapy. (a) Under the synergistic
  effect of Bifidobacterium and anti-CD47 antibodies, dendritic cells activate the
  STING signaling pathway and type I interferon signaling, thereby enhancing their
  capacity for cross-presentation of tumor antigens and subsequently activating CD8⁺
  T cells to induce an antitumor immune response. (b) The microbe-derived metabolite
  indole-3-acetic acid (3-IAA) enhances the efficacy of the chemotherapeutic agents
  5-fluorouracil, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin in pancreatic cancer; specific intratumoral
  microbes (Escherichia coli, Bacteroides fragilis, Bifidobacterium breve, and Parvimonas
  micra) diminish the therapeutic bioavailability of the first-line colorectal cancer
  drug 5-fluorouracil by metabolizing it into inactive forms. (c) Lactobacillus reuteri
  colonizes melanoma lesions and produces the tryptophan metabolite indole-3-aldehyde,
  which enhances interferon-γ-producing CD8 + T cells and synergizes with immune checkpoint
  inhibitor therapy. (d) Direct microbial therapeutic approaches include the administration
  of antibiotics and intratumoral injection of bacterial spores
