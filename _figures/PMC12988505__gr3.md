caption: Mechanism Link Between Depression and Osteoporosis. Depression contributes
  to bone metabolic disorders through interconnected disturbances in immunity, oxidative
  stress, neuroendocrine signaling, sex hormones, and gut microbiota. Elevated pro-inflammatory
  cytokines and immune cell imbalances disrupt the osteoimmune microenvironment, promoting
  osteoclast activation and impairing osteoblast function. Oxidative stress and RANKL-NF-κB
  activation exacerbate bone loss, while Nrf2-mediated protection is weakened. Neuroendocrine
  and hormonal dysregulation, including altered serotonin, cortisol, sex hormones,
  and thyroid hormones, further impair bone formation directly and indirectly via
  immune-inflammatory pathways. Gut dysbiosis reduces short-chain fatty acid production
  and disrupts bile acid metabolism, compromising intestinal barrier integrity and
  amplifying systemic inflammation. Together, these mechanisms form a multidimensional
  “neuro-immune-endocrine-gut-bone” network underlying depression-associated osteoporosis
  and highlight potential targets for integrated interventions
