caption: Metabolic and epigenetic changes in the development of innate immune tolerance.
  a Repeated exposure to an initial inflammatory stimulus, such as LPS, leads to the
  functional reprogramming of monocytes and macrophages, resulting in a state of tolerance.
  This process is characterized by the suppression of NF-κB pathway activation and
  a decreased expression of iNOS. Simultaneously, metabolic reprogramming occurs,
  featuring reduced glycolysis and an increased reliance on OXPHOS. This metabolic
  shift also involves intermediates from the TCA cycle, such as itaconate, as well
  as α-ketoglutarate. At the epigenetic level, there is a decline in markers associated
  with transcriptional activation in pro-inflammatory genes mediated by histone modifications
  (H3K27ac, H3K4ac, and H3K4me3), which leads to reduced gene transcription. These
  metabolic and epigenetic changes result in decreased production of pro-inflammatory
  cytokines and a relative increase in anti-inflammatory mediators. b Following a
  second stimulus, tolerant cells exhibit an attenuated yet functional inflammatory
  response, which is a hallmark of innate immune tolerance. Created using BioRender.
  iNOS, inducible nitric oxide synthase
