caption: '(A) XX/XY: Male heterogametic sex determination (e.g., Drosophila melanogaster).
  The presence of two X chromosomes leads to functional, female Sex-lethal (SxlF),
  which represses DC and leads to female-specific splicing of tra and dsx. The wMel
  Wolbachia strain can restore fertility by indirectly impacting sxl [7,8]. In males,
  the absence of functional Sxl leads to DC, a nonfunctional male-specific Tra (TraM),
  and then male-specific Dsx (DsxM). Male-killing Spiroplasma poulsonii produce the
  effector protein SpAID which interferes with DC. (B) ZW/ZZ: Female heterogametic
  sex determination (e.g., Bombyx mori). The Z-linked masc is transcribed in both
  sexes but in females, quickly degraded via a W-linked piRNA. In males functional
  Masc leads to DC and functional IMPM, which together with PSI, leads to male-specific
  DsxM. Many lepidopteran-infecting male-killing Wolbachia encode for the effector
  protein Oscar, which binds to and represses Masc, ultimately interfering with DC
  and sex determination. (C) Haplodiploidy: Males develop from haploid embryos, and
  females develop from diploid embryos. The paternally-derived genome copy is imprinted
  to drive female differentiation via transcription of a “Zygotic Paternal” (ZP*)
  factor which facilitates downstream female-specific splicing of tra, giving rise
  to functional TraF, leading to DsxF. In Nasonia and relatives the ZP* gene is wom
  [25]. The ZP* gene is not known in other non-CSD haplodiploids, but it is likely
  that a different gene under similar paternal imprinting takes on this role in other
  lineages. Some PI-Wolbachia encode for a protein (either named PifA or Piff; these
  are homologs) that are proposed to act as a replacement for TraF in the absence
  of host-derived TraF [26,27]. TraF autocatalytic activity is essential for driving
  maintenance of female programming in Nasonia [25], but it is unknown how broadly
  that is the case across other haplodiploids, and how PifA/Piff might integrate into
  such a pathway. (D) Haplodiploidy (sl-CSD): This model is based on knowledge from
  Apis mellifera. Under single-locus Complementary Sex Determination (sl-CSD), femF
  (fem is homologous to tra) splicing is facilitated by active CSD proteins. CSD proteins
  are activated by conformational shape changes that are made possible by heteroallelic
  binding (eg., a CSDA and a CSDB allele). In animals that are hemi- or homozygous
  at the CSD locus, the proteins are not activated and fem splicing, and downstream
  dsx splicing, are male. Homozygous diploidy is typically lethal or male-sterile.
  (A-D) In all cases above, TraF interacts with its binding partner, Tra2, to recruit
  splicing factors to dsx pre-mRNA, facilitating dsxF splicing. Tra2 is assumed and
  not shown for simplicity. Abbreviations: DC: Dosage Compensation; 2N-het: diploid,
  heterozygous; 2N-hom: diploid, homozygous; 1N: haploid; sl-CSD: single-locus Complementary
  Sex Determination. Please refer to the main text for more detailed explanations'
