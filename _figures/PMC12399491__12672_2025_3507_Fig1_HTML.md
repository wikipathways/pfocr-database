caption: Schematic Representation of the Ferroptosis Pathway in Cancer Cells, Highlighting
  Key Regulatory Components and Their Interactions with Non-Coding RNAs. This figure
  presents a comprehensive schematic of the ferroptosis pathway, a critical iron-dependent
  regulated cell death mechanism in BC, delineating its molecular architecture across
  mitochondrial, cytoplasmic, and nuclear compartments. The mitochondria illustrate
  iron metabolism and lipid peroxidation, with the electron transport chain (ETC)
  generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) via the Fenton reaction, modulated by transferrin
  receptor (TRFC), ferritin (FT), and lipoxygenases (ALOX), the latter repressed by
  miR-7-5p and miR-522. In the cytoplasm, key regulators such as the cystine/glutamate
  antiporter system xc- (SLC7A11/SLC3A2), GPX4, ACSL4, and LPCAT3 are depicted, with
  ncRNAs like miR-23a-3p and miR-424-5p inhibiting ACSL4, and the AMP-activated protein
  kinase (AMPK) pathway influencing sensitivity, activated by acetyl-CoA (Ac-CoA)
  and malonyl-CoA (Mal-CoA). Non-coding RNA regulation is highlighted by miR-5096
  and miR-375 targeting SLC7A11, miR-335-5p targeting GPX4, and circRNAs (e.g., circCDK14,
  circDTL) and lncRNAs (e.g., lncPVT1, circ0001742) interacting with miRNAs (e.g.,
  miR-324-3p, miR-431-5p) to modulate ferroptosis, with nuclear epigenetic effects
  via miR-153 and circ_0000643 through the FOXQ1 pathway. Cellular interactions include
  endosomal iron transport via mitoferrin (Mfrn1/2) and dimethyltransferase (DMT1),
  regulated by miR-137, and exosomal ncRNA release (e.g., miR-18b), with arrows and
  dashed lines denoting activation, inhibition, and indirect effects, respectively,
  underscoring the intricate ncRNA-ferroptosis interplay
