caption: m⁶A RNA methylation in Tenon’s fibroblasts and RGCs in glaucoma. A schematic
  representation of m⁶A RNA methylation in the context of glaucoma is presented. Central
  to this schematic is the m⁶A machinery, where the "writers" METTL3, METTL14, and
  WTAP are responsible for the addition of m⁶A marks on specific mRNAs. Conversely,
  the "erasers" FTO and ALKBH5 facilitate the removal of these modifications, while
  the "reader" YTHDF2 identifies m⁶A-modified transcripts. In Tenon’s fibroblasts
  (illustrated in the left panel), TGF-β1 induces upregulation of METTL3, resulting
  in an increased presence of m⁶A marks on profibrotic transcripts. This process promotes
  bleb scarring and filtration failure, ultimately leading to elevated IOP. In retinal
  ganglion cells (RGCs, depicted in the right panel), various stressors influence
  a METTL3–YTHDF2 axis, which modifies m⁶A marks on transcripts that regulate ferroptosis
  and autophagy. This modulation leads to dysregulation of ferroptosis and autophagy,
  contributing to RGC loss. Collectively, these instances underscore the role of m⁶A-dependent
  post-transcriptional regulation as an additional epigenetic mechanism that links
  subconjunctival fibrosis in the outflow pathway with neurodegeneration in glaucoma
