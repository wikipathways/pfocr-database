caption: Scheme illustrating the functional role of TARDBP in late stages of the HIV-1
  life cycle. A The increase in TARDBP activity enhances the action of the autophagic
  HDAC6 enzyme (i.e., by first stabilizing the HDAC6 mRNA levels that lead to an increase
  of the antiviral HDAC6 enzyme), which targets HIV-1 Pr55Gag for autophagic degradation
  together with the viral Vif protein. These HDAC6-mediated degradation could occur
  either in an antiviral HDAC6/A3G complex manner (i.e., protecting A3G from Vif proteasomal
  clearance) or by free HDAC6 [57, 86]. The scheme shows that the transcription and
  translation of the integrated HIV-1 genome generate viral RNA + and proteins, with
  the structural Pr55Gag polyprotein being relevant for recognizing viral RNA + and
  recruiting a complex to stable MTs to travel to the plasma membrane; however, under
  these conditions, Pr55Gag is degraded, limiting virus assembly, budding and egress.
  Similarly, the MT-associated HDAC6 enzyme deacetylates stable MTs, impeding Pr55Gag
  cell-surface localization. The absence of Nef or the loss of Nef function allows
  these events by stabilizing HDAC6 and enabling its antiviral activities [57, 93].
  B In contrast, TARDBP silencing (low levels of the cellular protein) that results
  in a decrease of the HDAC6 protein, or the chemical inhibition of HDAC6 or its degradation
  by the HIV-1 Nef protein (i.e., by acidic/endosomal-lysosomal pathway) favors the
  stabilization of the Pr55Gag and Vif proteins, together with the acetylation of
  MTs, thereby promoting the trafficking of the HIV-1 Pr55Gag MT-dependent cellular
  machinery to the cell surface, where it assembles and buds to form viral particles
  that incorporate Vif (red dots; which are not shown in virions on the (A) panel-virus
  scheme)) to ensure the infectivity of nascent HIV-1. HIV-1 Nef could therefore hijack
  the TARDBP/HDAC6 axis. Designs and templates were created with BioRender and licensed
  were obtained for publication
