caption: The OsRALF4-THE3-RbohB signaling pathway controls root developmental progression.
  In the meristem zone, the presence of unknown signals, such as RGF1 homologs, activates
  the interaction between THE3 and RbohB, which phosphorylates RbohB and promotes
  O2– production. This signal pathway is competed by OsRALF4. The low level of OsRALF4
  in the MZ mildly inhibits THE3-RBohB signaling, balancing the distribution of O2–
  in the RAM. The accumulation of O2– disables PMEs through degrading PMEs, sustaining
  pectin in methylesterified state and maintaining meristem homeostasis. As root develops,
  OsRALF4 level increases in the transition region, which enhances the inhibitory
  effect on the THE3-RbohB signaling pathway and decreases O2– production, thereby
  stabilizing PMEs and promoting pectin demethylesterification. The meristematic cells
  thus convert to expansion. In the elongation zone, the further enhanced OsRALF4
  inhibits O2– generation, which activates pectin demethylesterification through promoting
  PME stability. The resulting dmPectin binds to OsRALF4 to form the condensed pectin
  pattern in the EZ cell walls, facilitating cell wall integrity during cell elongation.
  In this figure, the thickness of arrows and lines indicates the degree of promotion
  and inhibition. The question marker indicates the signals need to be confirmed.
  CW, cell wall; mPectin, methylesterified pectin; dmPectin, demethylesterified pectin;
  PM, plasma membrane
