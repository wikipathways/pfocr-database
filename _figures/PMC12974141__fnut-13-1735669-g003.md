caption: 'This figure illustrates the dual roles of key GM-derived metabolites, SCFAs,
  BAs, and tryptophan derivatives in MASLD. SCFAs: Their effects are context-dependent.
  They primarily exert protective effects by activating AMPK and G protein-coupled
  receptors (e.g., GPR41/43), leading to enhanced lipid oxidation, improved insulin
  sensitivity, strengthened intestinal barrier, and attenuated inflammation. Conversely,
  they can also serve as substrates for de novo lipogenesis in the liver, potentially
  promoting hepatic steatosis. BAs: The GM converts primary BAs to secondary BAs,
  thereby altering signaling through receptors such as the FXR and TGR5. This process
  regulates BA homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and inflammation. MASLD is associated
  with an altered BA pool, which disrupts these pathways and promotes steatosis and
  inflammation. Tryptophan derivatives (e.g., IAA and IPA): These metabolites exhibit
  protective effects by improving insulin sensitivity, reducing lipogenesis, strengthening
  the intestinal barrier, and inhibiting inflammatory pathways such as NF-κB'
