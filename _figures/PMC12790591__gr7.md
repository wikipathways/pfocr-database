caption: The hypothetical role of functional genes in peripheral blood monocytes in
  regulating the generation of core exhaled VOC biomarkers. Tumour cells shape an
  immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment by releasing inflammatory molecules (such
  as cytokines and chemokines) and neurotransmitters (such as GABA). These molecules
  also form the blood-tumour barrier (BTB) by altering the permeability of the blood–brain
  barrier (BBB), allowing them to enter the circulatory system and suppress the systemic
  immune response in PBT patients. The immune-suppressive functions of NCF1 and NCF2
  promote the expression of the kynurenine pathway in tryptophan metabolism, reducing
  the production of the volatile metabolite indole, which ultimately leads to a decrease
  in the exhaled indole concentration. Fig. 7 was created with BioRender.com
