caption: NLRP3 inflammasome overview. (A) The NLRP3 protein consists of three main
  domains, the pyrin domain, also known as the PYD domain, the NACHT domain and the
  LRR domain. The polybasic linker is located between the PYD domain and the NACHT
  domain. (B) The priming step of the NLRP3 activation. When TLR4 is activated, the
  downstream NF‐κB pathway, phosphorylated IRF3 and STING‐IRF3 pathway are activated,
  which together contribute to NLRP3 transcription. (C) Recent studies in posttranslational
  modification of the NLRP3 protein. Acetylation in the PYD domain and ubiquitination
  in the NACHT domain both facilitate NLRP3 activation. Phosphorylation in the NACHT
  domain leads to opposite outcomes. PKD phosphorylates S295 in the NACHT domain could
  facilitate NLRP3 activation while PKA phosphorylates S295 in NACHT domain inhibits
  NLRP3 activation. (D) Structure of NLRP3 cage. NLRP3 protein can form six‐, seven‐,
  and eight‐fold NLRP3 cage. The diagram shows the top view of a sixfold NLRP3 cage.
  The NLRP3 cage prevents the PYD domains from nucleating ASC, thus preventing the
  formation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. (E) The diagram shows the top view of a 10‐fold
  NLRP3 disc. Centrosome‐localized NEK7 probably interacts with NLRP3 cages at dTGN
  to open the double‐ring NLRP3 cages, enabling the NLRP3 disc formation in the MTOC.
  ASC, apoptosis‐associated speck‐like protein containing a CARD; dTGN, dispersed
  trans‐Golgi network; IRF3, IFN‐Regulatory Factor 3; MTOC, microtubule organizing
  center; NEK7, NIMA‐related kinase 7; NLRP3, NLR family pyrin domain containing 3;
  PKA, protein kinase A; PKD, protein kinase D; STING, stimulator of interferon genes;
  TLR4, Toll‐like receptor 4
