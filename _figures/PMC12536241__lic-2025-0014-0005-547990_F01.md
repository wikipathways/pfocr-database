caption: Mechanistic link between glycolysis-driven metabolic reprogramming and immune
  suppression in PET-positive HCC. Enhanced glycolytic activity in HCC leads to overexpression
  of GLUTs and increased glucose uptake, which is detected as high 18F-FDG uptake
  on PET/CT. Inside cancer cells, glucose is rapidly metabolized through glycolysis
  (the Warburg effect), resulting in the accumulation of lactate and decreased G6Pase
  activity. The buildup of lactate and acidification of the tumor microenvironment
  suppresses effector immune cell function (such as CD8+ T cells) and promotes the
  recruitment and activation of immunosuppressive cells, including Tregs, MDSCs, and
  TAMs. Tumor cells undergoing aerobic glycolysis produce excess lactate, which accumulates
  in the tumor microenvironment. Lactate is taken up by TAMs and Tregs via monocarboxylate
  transporters (MCT1 and MCT4), leading to HIF-1α stabilization and the subsequent
  induction of Arg1 and VEGF expression in TAMs. This promotes M2 polarization, angiogenesis,
  and suppression of antitumor immunity. In contrast, MDSCs sense extracellular lactate
  through GPR81 signaling, which activates the mTOR/HIF-1α/STAT3 pathway, enhancing
  their immunosuppressive function. These lactate-driven mechanisms contribute to
  an immune-exhausted tumor microenvironment, characterized by abundant suppressive
  immune cells and impaired cytotoxic T-cell activity. Potential therapeutic strategies
  include MCT inhibition (targeting TAMs and Tregs) and HIF-1α blockade (targeting
  TAMs and MDSCs). This metabolic-immune interplay creates an immunosuppressive TIME,
  contributing to immune evasion and resistance to PD-1/L1 monotherapy in PET-positive,
  glycolysis-driven HCC. Nevertheless, combination strategies involving PD-1 blockade
  with agents targeting immunosuppressive components of the tumor microenvironment,
  such as anti-VEGF therapies (e.g., bevacizumab, LEN) or anti-CTLA-4 antibodies,
  may demonstrate efficacy in a subset of PET-positive, glycolysis-driven HCCs. 18F-FDG,
