caption: Pathways for generating cellular energy. The process of glycolysis converts
  2 mol of glucose into 4 mol of ATP. This is the pathway primarily used by fast replicating
  cells as they require rapid but low levels of ATP. The cell types would include
  embryonic cells, embryonic stem cells, tumour-initiating cells, and blood cells.
  Glycolysis also produces substrates, such as pyruvate and NADH, which enter the
  mitochondrion. Pyruvate is broken down by β-oxidation and the citric acid cycle
  to produce NADH and FADH2. They enter the ETC as electron donors to promote the
  generation of higher levels of ATP through the biochemical process of OXPHOS. This
  results in 34–38 molecules of ATP being generated from the original investment of
  2 mol of glucose. Heart, muscle and brain cells are primary users of OXPHOS as they
  prefer greater levels ATP production over prolonged periods of time
