caption: Proposed mechanism of action of Ole-4. In tumor cells, soluble molecules
  like growth factors, cytokines and integrins, constitutively signal for growth through
  their receptors (GPCR, IR, CR, RTK) (1). Membrane anchorage of Ole-4 (likely via
  its 16- carbon aliphatic chain; 2) disrupts the PI3K/Akt/mTOR axis either by affecting
  the availability of PIP3 (3), or by preventing PIP3 interaction with Akt at the
  cell membrane (4). Downstream decreased activity (↓) of key kinases, including Akt1/2,
  PKAα and Pim2/3, likely suppresses mTORC1, which consequently reduces the activity
  of P70S6KB (5). These effects result in impaired cell cycle progression and reduced
  proliferation (6), reduced protein synthesis (7), and increased pro-apoptotic signaling
  (8) via blockade of BAD inhibition (9). In addition, suppression of IKKα (↓) dampens
  NF-κB-driven survival pathways, while reduced Src activity attenuates cell migration
  and metastatic potential (10). These effects culminate in tumor cell apoptosis (8)
  and the subsequent release of tumor antigens (Ag) (11). Tumor antigens are processed
  and presented by professional antigen-presenting cells (APC), leading to the activation
  of cytotoxic T cells (Tc), helper T cells (Th) and natural killer (NK) cells (12).
  Activated tumor antigen-reactive immune effectors infiltrate the tumor mass (13),
  secrete cytokines [e.g., tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interleukin (IL)-2, interferon
  (IFN)-γ] and release the content of their cytotoxic granules (perforin, granzyme
  B), leading to tumor cell clearance (14). Pamgene-identified kinases are shown in
  red. Molecules implicated in the affected pathways are shown in gray
