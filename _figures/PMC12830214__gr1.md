caption: 'Immune evasion strategies employed by various ASFV proteins. ASFV employs
  multiple proteins to disrupt type I IFN signaling at various stages. These include
  competitive binding to IFNAR1/IFNAR2 receptors (e.g., B125R, B318L, and P22), inhibition
  of JAK1 and TYK2 activation associated with the receptors, and blockade of STAT1/STAT2
  phosphorylation and nuclear translocation (e.g., MGF360-9L, B475L, and F778L). Furthermore,
  ASFV proteins suppress IFN-stimulated response element (ISRE)-driven transcription
  (e.g., D129L), resulting in a broad downregulation of ISG expression. ASFV also
  targets pattern-recognition receptor (PRR)-dependent pathways, including TLR and
  tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR) signaling. Viral proteins such as A137R, I177L,
  F317L, MGF300-2R, and MGF300-4L interfere with adaptor proteins and kinase complexes
  (e.g., MyD88, TRAF6, TAK1, and IKK), leading to impaired IκB degradation, reduced
  nuclear translocation of NF-κB subunits (p50/RelA), and decreased expression of
  inflammatory and immunomodulatory genes. The cGAS-STING pathway is negatively regulated
  by several ASFV proteins (e.g., C129L, B175L, DP96L, EP364R, B318L, MGF360-11L,
  L83L, P17, MGF505-2R, PE301R). These act at multiple checkpoints to antagonize cytoplasmic
  DNA-induced signaling: inhibiting cGAS binding to viral DNA (QP383R), promoting
  cGAS degradation (MGF505-4R), reducing STING trafficking from the endoplasmic reticulum
  (ER) to the Golgi (PB318L), and blocking TBK1 (M1249L, A151R) and IRF3 (MGF360-4L)
  phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, ultimately suppressing type I IFN gene
  transcription. Additionally, ASFV encodes proteins (e.g., MGF360-16R and A179L)
  that modulate mitochondrial integrity and apoptotic signaling. These factors delay
  or prevent apoptosis early in infection to sustain viral replication, while selectively
  promoting cell death at later stages. Red-labeled viral gene products indicate ASFV
  proteins that interact with, inhibit, or promote specific host signaling components
  involved in innate immune response. (Created with the BioRender online software
  (www.biorender.com))'
