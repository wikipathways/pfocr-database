caption: Mechanism of the unfolded protein response. EnR stress initiates an adaptive
  signaling cascade to counteract the regulatory effects induced by unfolded or misfolded
  proteins within the endoplasmic reticulum, a process termed UPR. Under normal conditions,
  the molecular chaperone GRP78 binds to three UPR sensors (IRE1α, ATF6, and PERK),
  impeding their activation, while GRP78 is sequestered by unfolded or misfolded proteins,
  leading to the activation of UPR. IRE1α degrades a specific subset of EnR stress‐responsive
  mRNAs through a process known as regulated IRE1‐dependent decay (RIDD) and induces
  unconventional splicing of XBP1 mRNA (XBP1u) to generate spliced XBP1 (XBP1s), a
  transcription factor that crucially upregulates folding capacity and endoplasmic
  reticulum‐associated degradation. Furthermore, under overloaded EnR stress, IRE1α
  activates the JNK pathway via binding TRAF2, XBP1s triggers the NF‐κB pathway, and
  both pathways could induce apoptosis. ATF6 (ATF6p90) translocated to the Golgi apparatus,
  where it is cleaved by S1P and S2P to form an ATF6 fragment (ATF6p50) trafficked
  to the nucleus to upregulate folding capacity and XBP1's transcription, while upregulating
  CHOP's transcription when EnR stress persists. PERK phosphorylates eIF2α to attenuate
  protein translation and concomitantly upregulates ATF4, which facilitates the expression
  of GADD34 and CHOP, coordinating the release of EnR stress to maintain endoplasmic
  reticulum homeostasis. ATF4, activating transcription factor 4; EnR, endoplasmic
  reticulum; GADD34, growth arrest and DNA damage‐inducible 34; JNK, c‐Jun N‐terminal
  kinase; PERK, protein kinase RNA‐like endoplasmic reticulum kinase; TRAF2, tumor
  necrosis factor receptor‐associated factor 2; UPR, unfolded protein response
