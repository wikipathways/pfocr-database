caption: Schematic Model Human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUC-MSCs) secrete
  exosomes containing lncRNA ENST00000629969, which functions as a “sponge” for mmu-miR-6361,
  leading to the release of target mRNAs. These exosome-delivered RNAs promote neuroprotection
  by enhancing membrane lipid remodeling and modulating glial cell functions. Specifically,
  they facilitate M2 polarization of glial cells, improve microglial energy metabolism,
  and enhance Aβ clearance via PLD1-mediated pathways. Phosphatidic acid (PA) derived
  from these pathways promotes recruitment and activation of mTORC1, which in turn
  regulates the ULK1/Beclin1-Vps34 complex to initiate autophagy, promoting the clearance
  of damaged mitochondria and maintaining healthy mitochondrial function (ATP production).
  Additionally, the pathway modulates arachidonic acid (AA) metabolism, reducing pro-inflammatory
  cytokine production and thereby alleviating neuroinflammation. Overall, this integrated
  mechanism highlights how hUC-MSC-derived exosomal lncRNA orchestrates autophagy,
  lipid metabolism, glial cell polarization, and inflammatory responses to confer
  enhanced neuroprotection
