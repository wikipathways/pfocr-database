caption: Immune cells cause liver injury or exert an anti-HBV infection effect in
  chronic hepatitis B through the CXCL10–CXCR3 axis. In CHB, IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IRF-1
  promote CXCL10 expression at the transcriptional level in hepatocytes, HSCs, and
  biliary epithelial cells by activating the JAK/STAT and NF-κB signaling pathways.
  CXCL10-positive cells recruit CXCR3-positive immune cells, including CTLs, NK cells,
  and monocytes, which express effector molecules such as granzyme, perforin, and
  components of the Fas/FasL pathway. This leads to liver injury, infection persistence,
  or HBV clearance, depending on immune homeostasis, and may involve additional signaling
  pathways. Additionally, IFN-γ and TNF-α secreted by activated T cells in hepatitis
  B patients stimulate liver cells to secrete CXCL10
