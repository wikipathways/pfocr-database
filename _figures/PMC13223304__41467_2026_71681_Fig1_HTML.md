caption: a Dual-substrate visualization facilitates the elucidation and development
  of ME biosynthetic pathways using alcohol acyltransferases (AATs) in Escherichia
  coli. The three distinct biosynthetic routes of ME biosynthesis include (I) esterification
  of monoterpenol (MT, outer right circle) with short-chain fatty acid- or aromatic-derived
  acyl-CoAs (inner right circle) into monoterpenol ester (MTE), (II) esterification
  of monoterpenoic acid (MTa)-derived acyl-CoAs (MT, outer left circle) with short-chain
  fatty alcohol (inner left circle) into monoterpenoate ester (MTaE), and (III) esterification
  of MTa-derived acyl-CoAs with MT into monoterpenyl monoterpenoate ester (MtMtaE),
  a type of dimeric MEs that are composed of four isoprene units same to the diterpenes,
  while not biosynthesized from geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate. MT1-11 includes chrysanthemol,
  lavandulol, geraniol, nerol, perillyl alcohol, L-carveol, fenchol, borneol, linalool,
  α-terpineol, and terpinen-4-ol. Short-chain fatty acids a–h, enzymatically activated
  with HS-CoA, include acetyl-CoA, propionyl-CoA, butyryl-CoA, isobutyryl-CoA, and
  valeryl-CoA. Aromatic acids 6-8, enzymatically activated with HS-CoA, include benzoyl-CoA,
  phenylacetyl-CoA, and phenylpropionyl-CoA. MTa 1-5, enzymatically activated with
  HS-CoA, include chrysanthemoyl-CoA, lavanduloyl-CoA, geranoyl-CoA, neroyl-CoA, and
  S-(-)-perillyl-CoA. Short-chain fatty alcohols a’-c’ include ethanol, propanol,
  and butanol. b Structure-guided protein engineering, dual-substrate molar ratio
  tuning, and modular coculture engineering enable ME biosynthesis from diversity
  to specificity
