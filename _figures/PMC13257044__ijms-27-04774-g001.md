caption: Mitochondrial dysfunction as a central hub in polycystic kidney disease (PKD)
  pathogenesis. This schematic illustrates the complex network of mitochondrial abnormalities
  that drive cyst formation and renal fibrosis in PKD. Loss of polycystin-1 (PC1),
  polycystin-2 (PC2), or fibrocystin (FPC) disrupts primary cilia signaling, leading
  to reduced intracellular calcium (Ca2+). AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) inactivation
  results in activation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1)
  and hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α), which upregulate glucose transporter
  1 (GLUT1) and promote glycolysis. Simultaneously, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS),
  fatty acid oxidation (FAO), and their key regulators peroxisome proliferator-activated
  receptor alpha (PPARα) and PGC1α are suppressed. MicroRNA-17 (miR-17) further impairs
  mitochondrial metabolism. Damaged mitochondria produce excessive reactive oxygen
  species (ROS) and release mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), activating the NLRP3 inflammasome
  and the cGAS-STING pathway, which drives inflammatory cytokine production. These
  events promote ferroptosis, impair mitophagy, and ultimately lead to renal fibrosis
  and cyst formation. Disrupted mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membranes
  (MAMs) contribute to Ca2+ dysregulation and mitochondrial dysfunction
