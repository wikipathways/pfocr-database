caption: 'System Xc−/GPX4 axis mechanism. a.Synthesis of Glutathione: In the system
  Xc−, the imported cystine is reduced to cysteine by thioredoxin reductase (TrxR1)
  inside the cell. Subsequently, cysteine binds with glutamate (Glu) under the catalysis
  of glutamate cysteine ligase (GCL) to form gamma-glutamylcysteine (γ-GC). Gamma
  GC then binds to glycine (Gly) under the action of glutathione synthase (GSS), ultimately
  synthesizing glutathione (GSH). In addition to the System Xc− pathway, the transsulfuration
  pathway is also one of the sources of intracellular GSH. b.GSH regeneration and
  GPX4 mediated antioxidant defense: In this reaction, GSH is oxidized to glutathione
  disulfide (GSSG). Glutathione reductase (GSR) relies on NAD(P)H to reduce GSSG back
  to GSH, thereby maintaining cellular redox balance and ensuring the sustained function
  of GPX4. c. Occurrence and inhibition of ferroptosis: Ferrous ions (Fe2⁺) catalyze
  the production of large amounts of phospholipid hydroperoxides (PL-OOH) via the
  Fenton reaction, triggering uncontrolled lipid peroxidation and ultimately leading
  to ferroptosis. In contrast, the GPX4-GSH axis plays a critical role in inhibiting
  ferroptosis by neutralizing PL-OOH. Created with BioRender.com'
