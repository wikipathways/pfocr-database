caption: Schematic of the proposed AR-FOXO4 feedback loop in PCOS pathophysiologyGut
  microbiota dysbiosis, obesity, and insulin resistance contribute to the pathogenesis
  of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). PCOS is characterized by hyperandrogenism,
  ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology. Elevated androgens activate
  the androgen receptor (AR) signaling pathway, which disrupts granulosa cell (GC)
  proliferation and impairs ovulation. Concurrently, hyperandrogenism suppresses FOXO4,
  a key upstream regulator of luteinizing hormone (LH) surge, leading to decreased
  CDKN1A and increased CCND1 expression, further inhibiting GC proliferation and exacerbating
  anovulation. Additionally, FOXO1, another member of the FOXO family, plays a critical
  role in obesity and insulin resistance, suggesting its potential involvement in
  PCOS progression. Notably, gut microbiota-derived bile acids can also activate AR
  signaling, reinforcing the hyperandrogenic milieu. These findings highlight a central
  AR-FOXO regulatory network, integrating endocrine, metabolic, and microbial factors,
  and provide novel insights into the mechanistic underpinnings of PCOS
