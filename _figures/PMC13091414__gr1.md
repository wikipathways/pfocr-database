caption: Pathway-centered schematic of cyclophosphamide-related bladder injury, highlighting
  the mechanistic cascade and phenotype continuumCyclophosphamide (CYP) undergoes
  hepatic metabolism by cytochrome P450 enzymes, producing acrolein, which accumulates
  in the bladder. Acrolein interacts with proteins, nucleic acids, and glutathione
  (GSH) in epithelial cells, inducing apoptosis and downregulating tight junction
  proteins, thereby disrupting the urothelial barrier. This disruption results in
  GSH depletion and accumulation of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (ROS/RNS),
  triggering oxidative stress and disrupting the antioxidant system. Under these conditions,
  TRIM21-mediated inhibition of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)
  further activates inflammatory pathways, including NF-κB, NOD-like receptor pyrin
  domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3), and the FcRγ-CARD9-γδT-IL-17 axis, thereby
  promoting immune cell infiltration and increased production of pro-inflammatory
  mediators. The resulting inflammatory response, along with the upregulation of receptors
  such as protease-activated receptors (PARs) and transient receptor potential vanilloid
  1 (TRPV1), promotes the release of neuropeptides such as substance P (SP) and calcitonin
  gene-related peptide (CGRP), as well as nerve sprouting, which contribute to peripheral
  and central sensitization. Simultaneously, dysregulation of sphingosine-1-phosphate
  (S1P)-Rho kinase/protein kinase C (PKC), purinergic/cholinergic signaling, and nitric
  oxide (NO) pathways leads to the remodeling of detrusor muscle excitability and
  abnormal contraction. These structural alterations, including upregulation of connexin
  43 (Cx43) junctions, fibrosis, and epithelial proliferation, ultimately result in
  functional disorders such as hemorrhagic cystitis, interstitial cystitis/bladder
  pain syndrome (IC/BPS), overactive bladder (OAB), and low-compliance bladder
