caption: Tryptophan metabolic pathways and their sources in the retina. Most of tryptophan
  can be metabolized to KYN or its derivatives by IDO or TDO enzymes, a small part
  is metabolized to 5-HTP in enterochromaffin cells by TPH1, and another small part
  is metabolized to indole or its derivatives by gut microbiota. These metabolites,
  along with unmetabolized tryptophan, can reach the eye through the circulation.
  It crosses the BRB and further catabolizes in the eye and affects the physiological
  function of the eye. In microglia under pathological conditions, the promotion of
  IDO and KMO enzymes produce neurotoxic substances QA, 3-HK; in contrast, astrocytes
  rely on KAT and TPH1 to produce protective substances such as KYNA, XA, and 5-HT.
  In addition to this, photoreceptor cells and amacrine cells also have the function
  to produce 5-HT or MT. KYN, kynurenine
