caption: Schematic illustration of the promiscuous, non-linear interactions of families
  of homologous BMP ligands with different Type I and Type II receptor subtypes in
  a single cell. Subtypes of BMP ligands (represented by different shapes) are categorized
  into the same “equivalence group” (represented by red, green, and blue colors) if
  the pairwise ligand-receptor interactions activate redundant cellular responses
  (Klumpe et al., 2022). Type I and Type II receptors are represented by lighter and
  darker shades of the same color. Equivalence groups may bind preferentially to one
  BMP receptor subtype and activate a strong cellular response, but ligands may also
  bind promiscuously to other receptor subtypes in a cross-wired manner, initiating
  a different cellular response. The cellular response to promiscuous ligand-receptor
  binding is illustrated by different shades of ovals, representing differential activation
  of transcription factors as a result of specific ligand-receptor interactions. These
  promiscuous interactions result in different “addressing” of cells based on unique
  receptor expression profiles (Su et al., 2022). Strong and weak ligand-receptor
  interactions are represented by solid and dashed arrows, respectively. The combinatorial
  ligand-receptor interactions result in activation of different transcription factors,
  symbolized by the different shades of each color of oval. The diverse cellular responses
  that arise from the promiscuous, cross-wired interactions of BMP ligands and receptors
  give rise to an array of developmental processes
