caption: 'A schematic representation of the purine nucleotide cycle in relation to
  the tricarboxylic acid cycleA schematic representation of the pathways with major
  metabolite structures addressed in the present study. The diagram represents the
  anaplerotic role of purine nucleotide cycle (PNC) in the conversion of aspartate
  to fumarate, increasing tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA) capacity and leading to succinate
  accumulation through canonical or reversed TCA. In PNC, aspartate condensates with
  IMP to produce adenylosuccinate (AdSucc). The reaction requires energy in the form
  of GTP and might be catalyzed by adenylosuccinate synthase (ADSS) represented by
  two isoenzymes: ADSS like enzyme 1 (ADSSL1, also called ADSS1, mainly expressed
  in muscle) and ADSS (also called ADSS2). In the next step, AdSucc is cleaved by
  adenylosuccinate lyase (ADSL) to produce fumarate and AMP, which regenerates IMP
  through AMP deaminase (AMPD). Upon entering TCA, fumarate might increase its capacity
  by generating oxaloacetate in the canonical pathway, generating succinate through
  substrate phosphorylation in order to maintain energy production under hypoxia/ischemia.
  Alternatively, it might undergo the reverse TCA reaction (orange arrow) to directly
  produce succinate and restore the reduced cofactor FADH2 to maintain reduction potential
  under tissue hypoxia or ischemia'
