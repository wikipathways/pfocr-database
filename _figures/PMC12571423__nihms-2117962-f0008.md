caption: 'Multiple modes by which opioid peptides, and in turn receptor signaling,
  can be modulated by antidepressants. Conventional antidepressants such as fluoxetine
  have been shown to upregulate the level of proenkephalin mRNA in some rodent brain
  regions following chronic treatment. In secretory granules co-expressing catecholamines
  and opioid peptides, the catecholamines inhibit enzymes involved in enkephalin biosynthesis
  such as prohormone convertase 1, prohormone convertase 2, and carboxypeptidase E.
  Reserpine reduces levels of catecholamines within secretory granules, thereby leading
  to elevated levels of enkephalin in these secretory granules. Chronic treatment
  with fluoxetine has been found to decrease expression of Mme mRNA in some rodent
  brain regions, although these studies did not provide a sufficient ‘n’ for statistical
  testing; Mme encodes a peptidase named ‘enkephalinase’ which degrades extracellular
  enkephalin. Opioid peptides bind receptors such as the mu opioid receptor (MOR)
  and activate signaling by the G protein pathway or β-arrestin pathway leading to
  biological effects. MOR is directly activated by the rapid-acting antidepressant
  tianeptine, which is a full agonist. The MOR partial agonist buprenorphine, commonly
  used for maintenance therapy in patients with opioid use disorder, also has been
  found to be an effective rapid-acting antidepressant. Another rapid-acting antidepressant,
  ketamine, activates MOR as an allosteric modulator, augmenting the effectiveness
  of low concentrations of opioid peptides. Key: Green, upregulation or activation;
  Red, downregulation or inhibition'
