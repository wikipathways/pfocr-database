caption: 'Misfolded or unfolded client proteins, resulting from continuous mechanical
  stress at the Z-disk or other insults, are managed by a complex network of molecular
  chaperones. Small heat shock proteins (sHSPs), such as αB-crystallin (CRYAB, HSPB5)
  and HSPB8, function as ATP-independent “holdases,” initially recognizing and transiently
  stabilizing unfolded client proteins to prevent their irreversible aggregation.
  Subsequently, J-domain proteins (JDPs, also known as DNAJ proteins or HSP40 family
  members), including DNAJB6 and DNAJB4, recognize misfolded client proteins and transfer
  them to the ATP-bound form of HSP70. HSP70, acting as an ATP-dependent “foldase,”
  utilizes the energy from ATP hydrolysis to facilitate client protein refolding or
  triage towards degradation pathways. JDP-stimulated ATP hydrolysis stabilizes the
  HSP70–client complex. Nucleotide exchange factors (NEFs), such as BAG3, catalyze
  the exchange of ADP for ATP, enabling client release and returning HSP70 to its
  ATP-bound state for subsequent cycles. Mutations in these chaperones can impair
  their protein quality control functions, resulting in the accumulation of misfolded
  proteins, pathological aggregates, and consequent myofibrillar myopathy (MFM) pathology.Potential
  therapeutic strategies (indicated by red interventions in the diagram) aim to modulate
  this pathway, including: (i) reducing the load of aggregation-prone client proteins
  (e.g., via siRNA), (ii) modulating the interaction between client proteins and JDPs,
  (iii) inhibiting the JDP-HSP70 interaction to potentially reduce toxic gain-of-function
  effects [22], or (iv) inhibiting the NEF-HSP70 interaction to potentially counteract
  toxic gain-of-function effects [23]. Created in BioRender. Inoue, M. (2025) https://BioRender.com/cvh004p'
