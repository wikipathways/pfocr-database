caption: 'A: Clustered enzymes characterized in this work produce the lactone 15,
  a central intermediate in briarane biosynthesis by subsequent terpene cyclization,
  dual hydroxylation and two dehydrogenations. A missing central transformation is
  a 1,10-cyclization, the last fundamental step to obtain a compound containing the
  complete briarane scaffold (16). These steps must be conserved in all briarane producers
  (blue background). Other modifications of the briarane core are most likely introduced
  later and are lineage and species specific (purple background). These biosynthetic
  steps are evolutionarily younger and might not be present in gene clusters. B: A
  putative cytochrome P450 mediated 1,10-ring closing reaction, initiated by epoxidation
  of 15 to 19, which could be opened to produce alcohol 20 bearing the briarane scaffold.
  The resulting oxidized 14 position occurs as either oxygenated or olefinic position
  in most briaranes. C: Simplified octocoral phylogeny, rooted at Scleralcyonacea.
  Estimated timescale of BGC evolution. The circular mark in purple denotes the latest
  acquisition time of the briarane BGC, given that Ellisellidae, Pennatuloidea, as
  well as Briareidae, and Erythropodiidae (members of the top 9 families) are shown
  to contain the briarane BGC and are known briarane producers. The purple-colored
  X-mark denotes a likely BGC deletion event, in which the whole gene cluster or parts
  of it were lost. None of the 8 families in this clade are reported to produce briaranes
  and the only genome-sequenced species, H. coerulea, does not harbor a cembrene B
  synthase'
