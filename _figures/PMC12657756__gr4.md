caption: Methionine-mediated regulation of autophagy. DNA methylation and protein
  methylation affect the occurrence of autophagy. Specifically, DNA methylation is
  involved in autophagy through epigenetic modification of autophagy signaling pathways
  such as PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway. Protein methylation
  participates in autophagy through epigenetic modification of autophagy related proteins.
  Stress signals are transmitted to the interior of cells through multiple pathways,
  activating autophagy-related proteins (such as ULK1/2, Beclin-1, etc.). The activation
  of these proteins promotes the formation and maturation of autophagosomes, which
  in turn initiates the autophagy process. Polyamines are first acetylated through
  the action of acetyltransferase to produce acetylated polyamines. Subsequently,
  interacting with autophagy-related proteins, this interaction promotes the maturation
  of autophagosomes and the progression of the autophagy process. AC = acetylation;
  AKT = protein kinase B; AMBRA1 = autophagy and beclin 1 regulator 1; AMPK = AMP-activated
  protein kinase; ATG = autophagy-related genes; DNMT = DNA methyltransferase; EP300
  = E1A binding protein p300; G9A = euchromatic histone lysine N-methyltransferase
  2; LC3-Ⅲ = microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3-Ⅲ; MDM2 = murine double
  minute 2; mTOR = mammalian target of rapamycin; NRBF2 = nuclear receptor binding
  factor 2; PI3K = phosphoinositide 3-kinase; PtdIns3K = phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase;
