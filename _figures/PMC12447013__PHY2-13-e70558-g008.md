caption: Simplified MK2 signaling pathway leading to inflammation and cytoskeletal
  remodeling. MK2 is part of a classical MAPK pathway, which is a cascade structured
  in a three‐step phosphorylation of a MAP3K, a MAP2K, and a MAPK following an extracellular
  stimuli or stress (Arthur & Ley, 2013; Coulombe & Meloche, 2007; Dingar, Benoit,
  et al., 2010; Kim & Choi, 2010; Moens et al., 2013). MK2 is activated by its upstream
  MAPKs p38α or p38β through the phosphorylation of Thr222 and Ser272 (Cross et al., 2009;
  Fiore et al., 2016; Menon et al., 2017; Risco & Cuenda, 2012). MK2 then phosphorylates
  its multiple downstream targets, resulting in various cell responses. Phosphorylation
  of TTP, AUF1, and HuR leads to the stabilization of the transcripts coding for pro‐inflammatory
  cytokines such as IL‐6, IL‐1β, and TNF‐α (Khabar, 2017; Soni, Anand, & Padwad, 2019).
  Phosphorylation of HSP27/25 inhibits its ability to bind and destabilize actin filaments,
  allowing cytoskeletal remodeling involved in cell division and migration (Fiore
  et al., 2016; Gurgis et al., 2014; Soni, Anand, & Padwad, 2019; Tanaka et al., 2018).
  Other MK2 targets are known, but are not shown in this figure
