caption: Production and elimination of ROS. (A) ROS are produced through several cellular
  processes, including mitochondrial electron transport chains (ETC), NADPH oxidases
  (NOX), endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, Fenton reactions, and enzymatic reactions
  in peroxisomes. The generated ROS mainly include superoxide anions (O2-), hydrogen
  peroxide (H2O2), and hydroxyl radicals (OH·). (B) ROS are eliminated via endogenous
  antioxidant systems, including enzymatic antioxidants such as catalase (CAT), superoxide
  dismutases (SOD1 and SOD2), thioredoxin reductase (TrxR), and glutathione peroxidase
  (GPx), as well as low-molecular-weight scavengers like vitamins, flavonoids, and
  coenzyme Q (CoQ). The Nrf2/Keap1 pathway is activated in response to oxidative stress,
  resulting in the expression of antioxidant proteins that contribute to cellular
  defense against ROS-induced damage. These systems work in concert to maintain cellular
  homeostasis and prevent oxidative damage. AQP, aquaporin; CIC-3, cytochrome c oxidase
  subunit III; RAC, ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate; Phox, phagocytic oxidase;
  TNFα, tumor necrosis factor α; ETC, electron transport chain; XOs, xanthine oxidases;
  CYPs, cytochrome P450s; LOXs, lipoxygenases; Prx, peroxiredoxin; GSH, glutathione;
  GSSG, oxidized glutathione; GR, glutathione reductase; NF-κB, nuclear factor κB;
  Nrf2, nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2; ARE, antioxidant response element;
  Keap1, Kelch-Like ECH-Associated Protein 1; Ub, ubiquitin; NQO1, NAD(P)H quinone
  oxidoreductase 1; HO-1, heme oxygenase-1; GSTs, glutathione S-transferases
