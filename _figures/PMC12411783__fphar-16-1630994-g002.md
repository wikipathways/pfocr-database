caption: 'The major pathways of DNA DSBs (Double-Strand Breaks). (A) Unprocessed DSBs
  can be repaired through classic nonhomologous end joining (cNHEJ) allowing the two
  ends of the DSB to be re-ligated. (B) DSB ends can also be processed by the MRE11-RAD50-NBS1
  (MRN) complex and its interacting factors to yield short 3′ single-strand (ss)DNA
  overhangs. (C) The short 3′ ssDNA overhangs can then be channeled into the microhomology-mediated
  end joining (MMEJ) pathway. (D) Alternatively, the DSB ends can undergo further
  long-range resection by either EXO1 or Bloom helicase (BLM)/DNA2. These longer ssDNA
  overhangs are first bound by replication protein A (RPA) and can then be channeled
  into the (E) single-strand annealing (SSA) pathway, which is mediated by the protein
  RAD52. (F) Alternatively, the RPA-ssDNA can serve as a substrate for RAD51 filament
  assembly, allowing the resulting DNA intermediates to be directed towards repair
  by (G) homology repair (HR). For HR, both ssDNA and dsDNA templated homology-directed
  repair (HDR) pathways are shown. Abbreviations: CtIP, C-terminal binding protein
  interacting protein; DNA-PK, DNA-dependent protein kinase; XLF, XRCC4-like factor;
  XRCC4, X-ray cross complementing group 4. Taken from Xue and Greene (2021) with
  permission'
