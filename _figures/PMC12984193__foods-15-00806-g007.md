caption: 'Schematic diagram of the integration of the Ala anti-obesity signaling pathway.
  The diagram integrates findings from gut microbiota analysis and tissue-specific
  molecular assays. Alanine supplementation acts through two converging pathways:
  the indirect gut–immune axis (left, dashed lines) and alanine remodels the gut microbiota
  by enriching beneficial Acinetobacter and reducing Lachnospiraceae_NK4A136_group.
  This microbial shift reduces systemic proinflammatory cytokines, thereby creating
  a permissive environment for metabolic signaling. Absorbed Ala directly acts on
  host cells (hepatocytes/adipocytes). Center: these two pathways converge to activate
  a hierarchical signaling cascade. p-AMPK acts as the primary initiator, which subsequently
  activates SIRT1. The combined action of the AMPK/SIRT1 axis, coupled with reduced
  inflammatory inhibition, restores insulin signaling via p-AKT. In the liver, this
  cascade suppresses gluconeogenesis (Pepck, G6pc) and lipogenesis (Fas, Acc) while
  promoting fatty acid oxidation (Cpt1, Ppara). In adipose tissue, improved p-AKT
  signaling enhances GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake and reduces adiposity. Green boxes
  = downregulation; red boxes = upregulation. Arrows = strong direct stimulation;
  dashed arrows = indirect or multi-step association'
