caption: Lipopolysaccharide induces Ca2+ flow to mitochondria. Gram-negative bacterial
  lipopolysaccharide (LPS) binds to TLR receptors. Under LPS stimulation, tumor necrosis
  factor receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) is transported to mitochondria and ubiquitinated
  by the essential signaling intermediate (ECSIT) in the Toll pathway, thereby increasing
  the production of mitochondrial ROS (mtROS). In addition, TRAF6-mediated Rac ubiquitination
  and mitochondrial fusion protein 2 (Mfn2) expression, as well as inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate
  (IP3) mediated Ca2+ influx, can also generate mtROS. The phosphorylation of Mfn2
  protein further narrows the distance between mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum,
  promoting the influx of Ca2+ from the endoplasmic reticulum into mitochondria. The
  Ca2+ influx mediated by IP3 receptor (IP3R) interacts with mitochondrial calcium
  transporter (MCU) on the inner membrane of mitochondria through VDAC protein, promoting
  an increase in mitochondrial Ca2+ levels
