caption: Schematic diagram of various host signaling pathways. (a) Activation of TLR
  and NOD receptors occurs upon binding to MAMPs/PAMPs, initiating downstream signaling
  cascades. TLR activation leads to the phosphorylation of Raf (MAPKKK), subsequently
  activating MAPK. The activated MAPK translocates to the nucleus, promoting transcription
  of pro-inflammatory genes. NOD receptor activation similarly triggers phosphorylation
  of the NF-κB, IκBα, IκBβ, and IκBε complex, resulting in IκBα, IκBβ, and IκBε degradation
  and release of active NF-κB, which then enters the nucleus to initiate pro-inflammatory
  gene expression. (b) The Wnt ligand binds to the Frizzled receptor and LRP5 in the
  Wnt signaling pathway, activating Disheveled and inhibiting the destruction complex
  (APC, Axin, GSK3β, CK1). This prevents β-catenin degradation, allowing its accumulation
  and nuclear translocation, where it binds to TCF/LEF transcription factors to activate
  Wnt target genes. (c) Microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)
  activate G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), leading to adenylate cyclase activation,
  increased cAMP production, and subsequent activation of PKA, which translocates
  to the nucleus to regulate gene transcription. (d) Ligands like fatty acids activate
  nuclear receptors such as LXR and PPAR. They translocate to the nucleus upon activation
  and modulate gene expression by binding to specific DNA response elements. (e) In
  the Akt signaling pathway, ligand binding activates receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs),
  leading to Ras activation and phosphorylation of IRS. This recruits PI3 K, which
  converts PIP2 to PIP3. The PIP3 facilitates PDK1 and Akt activation, resulting in
  mTORC1 and mTORC2 signaling pathways
