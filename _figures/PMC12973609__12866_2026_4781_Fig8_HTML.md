caption: Proposed tryptophan and phenylalanine degradation pathways in B. cenocepacia.
  A Chorismate is converted to anthranilate by anthranilate synthase (TrpE), then
  processed via TrpD/TrpF/TrpC to form indole-3-glycerol phosphate. The tryptophan
  synthase α-subunit (TrpA) cleaves indole-3-glycerol phosphate to indole, which is
  channelled to the β-subunit (TrpB) to produce tryptophan. Tryptophan is initially
  oxidized by tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase (KynA) to form N-formyl-kynurenine. This
  intermediate is then hydrolysed by formamidase (KynB) to yield kynurenine, which
  serves as a central metabolite for downstream reactions leading to the formation
  of 3-hydroxykynurenine, anthranilic acid, or quinolinic acid, precursors in NAD⁺
  biosynthesis. Adapted from [54, 55]. B The phenylacetic acid (PAA) degradation pathway
  in B. cenocepacia proposed in [32]. Phenylalanine undergoes conversion to phenylacetic
  acid (PAA), which is subsequently ligated to phenylacetyl-CoA (PAA-CoA) by the enzyme
  PaaK. The resulting PAA-CoA is then oxidized by the multicomponent monooxygenase
  complex PaaABCDE, allowing the metabolites to enter the tricarboxylic acid (TCA)
  cycle for further breakdown, facilitated by PaaGZJFH
