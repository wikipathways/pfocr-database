caption: Mitochondrial–lactylation–calcium axis in sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy.
  Pathogen infection activates the NF-κB signaling pathway, triggering the release
  of inflammatory cytokines that induce oxidative stress and disrupt the tricarboxylic
  acid (TCA) cycle. This cascade depletes ATP and increases reactive oxygen species
  (ROS) levels. Concurrently, lactate increases p300-mediated histone lactylation,
  repressing mitochondrial biogenesis genes. Intracellular acidosis activates the
  Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger, causing Na⁺ influx; subsequent Na⁺ overload drives the Na⁺/Ca²⁺
  exchanger (NCX) to the reverse mode, resulting in cytosolic Ca²⁺ accumulation. ROS-oxidized
  ryanodine receptor 2 (RyR2) further exacerbates sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ leakage.
  The downregulation of β-adrenergic signaling inhibits L-type Ca²⁺ channel (DHPR)
  activity and SERCA2a activity. Collectively, Ca²⁺ mishandling and bioenergetic failure
  impair actin–myosin cross-bridging, leading to systolic and diastolic dysfunction. DHPR,
  dihydropyridine receptor; MCT, monocarboxylate transporter; NCX, Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger;
  NHE, Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger; RyR2, ryanodine receptor 2; SERCA2a, SR Ca²⁺-ATPase; TCA,
  tricarboxylic acid cycle
