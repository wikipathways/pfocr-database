caption: 'Resolution of inflammation and promotion of liver regeneration during H-IRI.
  During the resolution phase of H-IRI, MoMFs are reprogrammed by factors such as
  GM-CSF, TGF-β, IL-10, and IL-4 toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype. These MoMFs
  promote efferocytosis of necrotic cells and secrete MMPs to remodel the extracellular
  matrix. Eosinophils contribute to repair by releasing IL-4, which drives macrophages
  to produce regenerative factors such as HB-EGF and thereby stimulates hepatocyte
  proliferation. In addition, eosinophils release IL-13, which limits neutrophil transmigration
  and early neutrophil-mediated injury. Neutrophils can also release pro-regenerative
  mediators, including FGF2, HGF, and microRNAs, further supporting hepatocyte proliferation.
  However, the origin and regulatory pathways that give rise to these reparative neutrophils
  remain poorly understood. Abbreviations: FGF2, fibroblast growth factor 2; GM-CSF,
  granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor; HB-EGF, heparin-binding EGF-like
  growth factor; HGF, hepatocyte growth factor; H-IRI, hepatic ischemia–reperfusion
  injury; IL-4, interleukin-4; IL-10, interleukin-10; IL-13, interleukin-13; MMPs,
  matrix metalloproteinases; MoMFs, monocyte-derived macrophages; TGF-β, transforming
  growth factor beta. Created in BioRender. Zhao, J. (2025), https://BioRender.com/dd6sqej'
