caption: 'This figure shows the interconnection between the intestinal and renal axis
  through both metabolic and immune pathways: first the metabolic pathway. When hypoxia
  leads to a change in the ecological niche of the microorganisms in the gut, this
  leads to an increase in the production of IL-12, IL-18, and ligands from MAIT cells,
  this will activate the MAIT that reside in a resting state in the kidneys, and after
  the activation of the MAIT cells they will secrete granzyme. When MAIT cells are
  activated, they will secrete granzyme and perforin, which act on the epithelial
  cells of renal tubules, causing them to be damaged and ultimately leading to fibrosis.
  Second, the immune pathway, when the abnormal environment of the intestine causes
  MAIT cells to be activated abnormally in large quantities, and then enter into the
  kidneys along with the bloodstream, these MAIT cells, on the one hand, can work
  together with the monocytes and dendritic cells in the kidneys and amplify the inflammation,
  and on the other hand, they can inhibit the medullary inflammatory cells in the
  kidneys, thus inhibit inflammation, and their specific functions need to be determined
  by their corresponding immune microenvironment'
