caption: 'Schematic representation of oil biosynthesis in canola (Brassica napus);
  detailing the main metabolic steps from de novo fatty acid synthesis in plastids
  to triacylglycerol (TAG) assembly in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Plastidial
  Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex (PDC) converts pyruvate into Acetyl‐CoA, followed
  by the action of Acetyl‐CoA Carboxylase (ACCase) to convert Acetyl‐CoA to Malonyl‐CoA.
  Malonyl‐CoA: ACP malonyltransferase (MCMT) further converts Malonyl CoA to Malonyl
  ACP, followed by elongation and reduction reactions catalyzed by Fatty Acid Synthase
  (FAS) to produce C16:0 and C18:0 ACP chains, which are then desaturated by Stearoyl‐ACP
  Desaturase (SAD) into C18:1ACP. These acyl‐ACP chains are hydrolyzed by thioesterases
  to release free fatty acids, which are later activated to acyl‐CoA by Long‐chain
  Acyl‐CoA Synthetase (LACS) and transported to ER. The acyl‐CoA pool (in step 7)
  acts as the central substrate source for TAG assembly via the Kennedy pathway. Other
  than direct incorporation (7a), It receives elongated products such as very long‐chain
  fatty acids (VLCFAs) including erucic acid by the Fatty Acid Elongase complex 1
  (FAE1) action (7c), and FAD2/FAD3‐desaturated acyl chains (7b) transferred from
  PC through Phosphatidylcholine Diacylglycerol Cholinephosphotransferase (PDCT)‐mediated
  exchange (8). Arrows specify the direction of metabolic flow, with enzymes (represented
  by triangles) indicated at relevant steps'
