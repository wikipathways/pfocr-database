caption: 'Phytochemical modulation of oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and apoptosis
  in neurodegeneration. This figure illustrates the molecular pathways activated under
  oxidative stress and how they contribute to inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction,
  and programmed neuronal cell death. Elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  and pro-inflammatory cytokines—such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-1
  beta (IL-1β), and interleukin-6 (IL-6)—bind to tumor necrosis factor receptor 1
  (TNFR1) and tumor necrosis factor receptor 2 (TNFR2), initiating downstream signaling
  cascades. Binding of TNF-α to TNFR1 recruits adaptor proteins including TNF receptor–associated
  death domain (TRADD), receptor-interacting protein (RIP or RIP1), and TNF receptor–associated
  factor (TRAF), which activate multiple parallel pathways. These include the apoptotic
  cascade mediated through Fas-associated death domain (FADD) and caspase activation,
  and the inflammatory/survival pathways mediated by nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer
  of activated B cells (NF-κB). NF-κB activation occurs through phosphorylation of
  its inhibitor, inhibitor of κB (IκB), by the IκB kinase (IKK) complex. Simultaneously,
  oxidative stress and TNF signaling activate mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)
  pathways, including c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), p38 MAPK, and the mitogen-activated
  protein kinase/extracellular signal-regulated kinase kinase (MEK)–extracellular
  signal-regulated kinase (ERK) axis. These kinases phosphorylate transcription factors
  such as activator protein-1 (AP-1), promoting the expression of genes involved in
  inflammation, stress responses, and neuronal apoptosis. Oxidative stress also initiates
  mitochondrial injury by activating B-cell lymphoma 2–associated X protein (Bax),
  leading to mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and release of cytochrome-c
  (Cyt-c). The released Cyt-c forms the apoptosome, activating caspase-9 and caspase-3,
  which execute apoptosis. Collectively, these interconnected pathways illustrate
  how ROS, cytokine signaling, and mitochondrial dysfunction converge to promote inflammation,
  apoptosis, and neurodegeneration. ASK1: apoptosis signal–regulating kinase 1; iNOS:
  inducible nitric oxide synthase. Reprinted from Ref. [44] with permission'
