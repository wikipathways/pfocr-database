caption: A schematic summary of aberrant T-cell immunity in glaucoma. Elevated IOP
  induces stress in retinal ganglion cells (RGCs), leading to the upregulation and
  release of heat shock proteins (HSPs). Disruption of the blood–retina barrier allows
  infiltrating T cells to encounter HSP antigens presented by antigen-presenting cells
  (e.g., microglia*, astrocytes) via MHC-II to CD4⁺ T cells, or directly by stressed
  RGCs via MHC-I to CD8⁺ T cells. The persistent release of HSPs and the cyclical
  migration of activated T cells allow peripheral immune organs to maintain and amplify
  this response. Upon antigen recognition, activation of the mTORC1 pathway in T cells
  further enhances clonal expansion and exacerbates immune dysregulation. This dual
  antigen-presentation pathway triggers T-cell activation, clonal amplification, and
  the secretion of pro-inflammatory mediators, initiating a neuroinflammatory cascade
  that drives sustained RGC injury
