caption: ERf receptors integrate canonical and non‐canonical signaling pathways. Schematic
  representation of ERf signaling pathways.(a) Stomatal development. The ERf receptors
  (ERECTA, ERL1, ERL2) form complexes with the receptor‐like protein (RLP) TOO MANY
  MOUTHS (TMM) and SERK co‐receptors, which are essential for the specific recognition
  of both the inhibitory EPF1/2 peptides and the stimulatory EPFL9/Stomagen peptide.
  Ligand perception triggers heterodimerization with BAK1, displacing BKI1, then recruiting
  U‐box E3 ligases PUB30/31, which ubiquitinate the receptor to promote internalization
  and degradation. The activated receptor complex also recruits receptor‐like cytoplasmic
  kinases (RLCKs) such as BSK1, which bridge the signal to the conserved mitogen‐activated
  protein kinase (MAPK) cascade MAPKKK (YODA)‐MAPKK (MKK4/5)‐MAPK (MPK3/6), leading
  to phosphorylation‐dependent degradation of the bHLH transcription factor pairs
  (SPCH and SCRM) to enforce stomatal patterning.(b) Stem elongation and vascular
  development. Here, ERf receptors from the vascular region (phloem) perceive EPFL4/6
  peptides secreted from the adjacent endodermis, which provide a paracrine signaling
  for stem elongation and vascular organization. A critical difference from the stomatal
  development context is that TMM is absent here.(c) Non‐canonical nuclear signaling.
  In root meristem cells, an ERECTA fragment containing the kinase domain is detected
  and translocates to the nucleus, where it interacts with SWI3B, a core subunit of
  the SWI/SNF chromatin‐remodeling complex. This ERECTA fragment thereby directly
  or indirectly modulates chromatin accessibility (e.g., H2A.Z deposition) and promotes
  the transcription of growth‐related genes (e.g., PRE1). BAK1, BRI1‐ASSOCIATED KINASE1;
  EPF/EPFL, EPIDERMAL PATTERNING FACTOR; ERf, ERECTA‐family; ERL1, ERECTA‐LIKE1; SERK,
  SOMATIC EMBRYOGENESIS RECEPTOR KINASE
