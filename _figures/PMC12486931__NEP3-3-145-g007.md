caption: Etiology of brain insulin resistance. As brain insulin is primarily derived
  from the periphery, changes in insulin blood‒brain barrier transport will impact
  the availability of insulin within the brain. Additionally, degradation by enzymes,
  including the insulin‐degrading enzyme (IDE), will also contribute to the availability
  of insulin. Central nervous system cell type expression of the insulin receptor
  and changes in this expression between the cell types can also impact brain insulin
  signaling. Cellular distribution of the insulin receptor (i.e., cell surface or
  internalization) will also impact the ability of a cell to respond to insulin. Finally,
  any changes in the downstream mediators following activation of the insulin receptor
  can lead to brain insulin resistance (detailed on right). Figure modified from Rhea
  et al.85 Created with BioRender.com. Modification permission granted. AKT, Akt serine/threonine
  kinase; BVR‐A, Biliverdin reductase‐A; FOXO, class O of forkhead box transcription
