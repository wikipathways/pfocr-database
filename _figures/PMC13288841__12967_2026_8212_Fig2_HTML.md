caption: 'Schematic illustrating experimentally supported mechanisms by which human
  birth tissue products may modulate pathological pain. (A) Post-surgical inflammation
  and acute neuronal sensitization. Left: Incisional injury triggers the recruitment
  of immune cells, including mast cells, monocytes, and neutrophils, leading to local
  inflammation and the activation and sensitization of nociceptive nerve terminals
  that express sodium and calcium ion channels. Right: Local application of human
  birth tissue products FLO (CLARIX FLO®) and purified heavy chain-hyaluronic acid/pentraxin
  3 (HC-HA/PTX3) has been shown in preclinical models to reduce neuronal activity
  and attenuate established post-surgical pain acutely. FLO has also been observed
  to reduce immune cell recruitment, support wound healing, and may help limit the
  transition from acute to chronic pain. (B) Cellular mechanisms underlying HC-HA/PTX3–induced
  neuronal modulation. Acute HC-HA/PTX3 treatment induces broad electrophysiological
  changes in DRG neurons, including membrane hyperpolarization, altered intrinsic
  membrane properties, and decreased sodium (INav) and calcium channel currents (Ica.),
  collectively decreasing neuronal excitability. These effects depend on membrane-bound
  CD44 and may involve cytoskeletal rearrangements and interactions with membrane
  ion channels and receptors. With prolonged exposure, HC-HA/PTX3 is associated with
  transcriptional changes in genes involved in receptor-ligand activity, extracellular
  matrix function, and cytoskeletal regulation. Among these, pro-opiomelanocortin
  (POMC) signaling may be a key pathway contributing to the long-term modulation of
  pain. (C) Integrated mechanisms influencing post-surgical pain. Human birth tissue-derived
  products may exert both direct effects on sensory neurons by reducing DRG excitability
  and indirect effects by limiting immune cell recruitment, thereby attenuating inflammation
  and supporting wound healing. Together, these experimentally supported mechanisms
  may reduce the likelihood of persistent post-surgical pain'
