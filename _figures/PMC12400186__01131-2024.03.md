caption: Antiviral interferon (IFN) response. Induction of IFN production starts with
  the recognition of invading viruses through various pattern recognition receptors
  (PRRs). These PRRs then signal through various adaptor molecules and signalling
  pathways. MYD88 is a common adaptor molecule used by all Toll-like receptors (TLRs)
  except TLR3, which signals through TRIF. TLR4, not pictured, can signal via both
  pathways mentioned. The receptors RIG-I, MDA5 and NOD2 rely on the mitochondrial
  antiviral signalling protein MAVS [110]. The end result of these signalling pathways
  is the activation of transcription factors IRF3/IRF7 and nuclear factor (NF)-κB
  into the nucleus [95]. Thereafter, these translocate into the nucleus and induce
  transcription and production of IFN proteins and other inflammatory molecules. The
  produced IFNs will then bind to their respective receptors on the surfaces of surrounding
  cells. All receptors for the three classes of IFNs activate and signal via the Janus
  kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK–STAT) pathway [57].
  This results in translocation of transcription factors into the nucleus and the
  transcription of IFN-stimulated genes
