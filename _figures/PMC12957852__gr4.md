caption: Effects of S-sulfhydration in kidney diseases. Within the kidney, cystine
  is initially converted to cysteine, which is then further transformed into H2S with
  the assistance of CBS and CSE enzymes. The presence of H2S promotes the S-sulfhydration
  modification of SIRT1. On one hand, it exerts regulatory control over autophagy
  through the promotion of LC3B. Conversely, it effectively inhibits renal fibrosis
  and NFκB activation by suppressing STAT3 phosphorylation. This dual action serves
  to attenuate the heightened levels of inflammatory factors in renal tissue, ameliorate
  damage to renal podocytes and mesangial cells, and ultimately impede the progression
  of chronic renal disease. The S-sulfhydration of MEK1 facilitates DNA damage repair
  through ERK1/2 signalling and inhibits RAS/RAF-mediated MEK1 phosphorylation. The
  S-sulfhydration of Keap1 accelerates the dissociation of Keap1 and Nrf2, enabling
  Nrf2 to translocate into the nucleus and regulate the glomerular filtration rate.
  Additionally, S-sulfhydration of AMPK suppresses fibroblast activation through the
  AKT/AMPK/mTOR signalling pathway. H2S donors GYY4137 and NaHS inhibit IL-4/STAT6
  signalling and diminish the infiltration of M1 and M2 macrophages, thereby eliciting
  anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects. H2S donors also inhibit TGFβ1-mediated
  SMAD3 phosphorylation and the formation of SMAD3/SMAD4 complex, thereby ameliorating
  renal fibrosis. NFκB, nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells;
  STAT3, signal transducer and activator of transcription 3; MEK1, MAP kinase 1; ERK1/2,
  extracellular regulated protein kinases; Keap1, kelch-like ECH-associated protein
  1; Nrf2, nuclear factor-erythroid-2-related factor 2; AMPK, adenosine 5‘-monophosphate
  (AMP)-activated protein kinase; TGFβ1, transforming growth factor-β1; SMAD3, SMAD
  family member 3
