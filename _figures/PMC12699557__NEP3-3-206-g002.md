caption: Mechanistic crosstalk between microglia and astrocytes in major depressive
  disorder (MDD). The figure illustrates the mechanistic interactions between microglia
  and astrocytes under pathological conditions associated with MDD. Quiescent microglia
  become activated in response to pathogen‐associated molecular patterns (PAMPs),
  damage‐associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), and psychosocial stress via pattern
  recognition receptors (PRRs), Toll‐like receptors (TLRs), and the NOD‐like receptor
  thermal protein domain‐associated protein (NLRP3) inflammasome pathway. Upon activation,
  microglia adopt a pro‐inflammatory phenotype and release cytokines such as tumor
  necrosis factor‐alpha (TNF‐α), interleukin‐1 beta (IL‐1β), IL‐6, and IL‐18, as well
  as adenosine triphosphate (ATP) and complement proteins (C1q, C3). Astrocytes, initially
  exhibiting a neuroprotective A2 phenotype, respond to these inflammatory signals
  and convert to a neurotoxic A1 phenotype. A1 astrocytes are characterized by increased
  expression of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), excessive secretion of glutamate
  and d‐serine, exacerbating excitotoxic stress in microglia, and the release of ATP
  and lactate. Lactate induces metabolic activation of quiescent microglia and contributes
  to histone lactylation, an epigenetic modification that sustains the A1 reactive
  state. Furthermore, A1 astrocytes secrete pro‐inflammatory cytokines (TNF‐α, IL‐1β,
  IL‐6, interferon‐gamma [IFN‐γ]), amplifying immune responses and facilitating recruitment
  of peripheral immune cells, such as monocytes and T cells. Astrocyte‐derived ATP
  also drive calcium signaling cascades that activates purinergic P2X7 receptors on
  microglia, further enhancing microglial inflammatory activation. This reciprocal
  signaling establishes a positive feedback loop between microglia and astrocytes,
  perpetuating neuroinflammation and neuronal stress. In contrast, A2 astrocytes,
  which are associated with regenerative processes such as neurogenesis and tissue
  repair are functionally suppressed under MDD‐associated conditions. The cumulative
  outcome of this glial interplay includes chronic neuroinflammation, impaired neuroplasticity,
  synaptic loss, and the emergence of mood and cognitive symptoms characteristic of
  MDD
