caption: 'Hijacking the communication axis: translating exosome-based mechanisms into
  targeted cancer therapy. This schematic illustrates the therapeutic potential of
  exosomes as natural and engineered nanocarriers for targeted delivery to cancer
  stem cells (CSCs). A Naturally occurring exosomes derived from source cells such
  as mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) or dendritic cells (DCs) exhibit high biocompatibility,
  low immunogenicity, and intrinsic stability, enabling protection of therapeutic
  cargo and efficient tissue penetration and cellular uptake. These vesicles can transport
  diverse payloads, including chemotherapeutic drugs, nucleic acids (miRNAs, siRNAs),
  and proteins, across biological barriers to achieve effective intracellular delivery.
  B Engineered exosomes are bioengineered and surface-modified to enhance specificity
  toward CSCs by incorporating CSC-associated markers (e.g., CD44, CD133, EpCAM) and
  ligand or antibody conjugates. Cargo engineering further improves therapeutic efficacy
  through delivery of pathway-specific inhibitors (Wnt, Notch, Hedgehog), regulatory
  RNAs, or pro-apoptotic proteins, while also enabling immunomodulatory strategies
  such as DC-derived exosomes or anti-CSC vaccines. C Scalable production of engineered
  exosomes is achieved through advanced 3D culture systems, bioreactors, and mechanical
  stimulation, combined with genetic and biological strategies to modulate gene expression
  and lipid metabolism (e.g., STEAP3, syndecan-4), resulting in high-yield, functionally
  optimized exosomes suitable for translational and clinical applications'
