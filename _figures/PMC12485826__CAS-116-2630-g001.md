caption: Mechanisms related to gastric cancer hyperprogression. In patients with gastric
  cancer, specific genetic backgrounds (such as MDM2/4 amplification, positive EGFR
  mutation, or CDKN2A/B deletion) may trigger an increase in local IFN‐γ levels in
  the tumor through ICIs, thereby abnormally activating theJAK–STAT‐MDM2‐p53 and FGF2‐Wnt/β‐catenin
  signaling axes. This cascade reaction can inhibit the activity of the PI3K‐AKT pathway,
  ultimately inducing an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (“cold tumor”) and
  promoting the occurrence of HPD. Furthermore, clinical high‐risk factors (age > 65 years,
  previous radiotherapy history, EBV infection) can also lead to the immunosuppressive
  microenvironment after ICIs treatment. Its characteristics include the expansion
  of regulatory T cells (Treg) and M2‐type tumor‐associated macrophages (TAMs), accompanied
  by increased secretion of pro‐inflammatory factors such as IL‐6, IL‐10, and TGF‐β.
  This chronic inflammatory state drives the acceleration of tumor cell proliferation
  through the STAT3/NF‐κB pathway, significantly increasing the risk of HPD
