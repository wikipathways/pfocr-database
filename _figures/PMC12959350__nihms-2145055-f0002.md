caption: This schematic illustrates the HPA axis response to acute stress and its
  modulation by the neurosteroid allopregnanolone. In response to a stressor, the
  hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), which stimulates the
  anterior pituitary to secrete adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH). ACTH then acts
  on the adrenal glands to promote cortisol synthesis. Cortisol facilitates key metabolic
  responses (e.g., gluconeogenesis, fat/protein mobilization) and modulates immune
  and insulin sensitivity pathways. Simultaneously, acute stress also stimulates the
  production of neurosteroids such as allopregnanolone, which normally exert negative
  feedback on CRH and ACTH release to temper the HPA axis response. Chronic stress,
  however, disrupts this regulatory loop, leading to reduced neurosteroid levels and
  impaired inhibitory control over the HPA axis. This dysregulation contributes to
  the pathophysiology of stress-related disorders including major depressive disorder
  (MDD), post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and postpartum depression (PPD), where
  deficient allopregnanolone feedback fails to counteract the effects of sustained
  cortisol exposure. Created with BioRender.com
