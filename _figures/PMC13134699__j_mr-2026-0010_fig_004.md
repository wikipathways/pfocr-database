caption: 'Mechanisms of gut microbiota-derived metabolites in aortic diseases. This
  illustration summarizes the roles of four major classes of gut-derived metabolites
  in vascular homeostasis and pathology, within the gut-liver-vascular axis. (a) Trimethylamine
  N-oxide (TMAO), derived from choline, L-carnitine, and phosphatidylcholine via trimethylamine
  (TMA), induces reactive oxygen species (ROS), endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress
  (via PERK-ATF4/5-CHOP), and vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) apoptosis. (b) Short-chain
  fatty acids (SCFAs), like butyrate and propionate, acting through receptors such
  as GPR41/43 and histone deacetylases (HDACs), reduce ROS, promote Treg differentiation
  from naive T cells, inhibit NLRP3/NFκB in M1 macrophages, and support a synthetic
  VSMC phenotype. (c) Secondary bile acids, including ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)
  and tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA), modulate FXR-CRE2 signaling to decrease ceramide,
  MMP2/9, and VSMC apoptosis, while inhibiting VSMC apoptosis and ROS via NRF2 and
  palmitate-serine pathways. (d) Tryptophan metabolism: Under pathological conditions,
  tryptophan metabolism shifts from the protective gut microbiota-mediated indole
  pathway to the host-driven kynurenine pathway, contributing to extracellular matrix
  degradation and disease progression in aneurysms, dissections, and atherosclerosis.
  This figure was created with Biorender.com'
