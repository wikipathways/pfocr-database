caption: Proposed model linking Capns1 to cholesterol metabolism in ethanol‐treated
  liver. Schematic illustration summarizing the proposed role of Capns1‐dependent
  calpain activity in regulating hepatic cholesterol metabolism during ethanol exposure.
  Ethanol feeding is associated with dysregulated calpain signaling, altered LXRα
  subcellular localization, and activation of cholesterol biosynthetic pathways. Capns1
  knockdown suppresses ethanol‐induced cholesterol biosynthesis, promotes LXRα nuclear
  translocation, and increases the cleaved/pro‐HMGCR ratio, thereby limiting functional
  HMGCR availability. Green arrows indicate regulatory relationships between Calpain
  and downstream targets. Question marks (?) denote connections that are proposed
  based on the collective data but for which the direct molecular mechanisms remain
  to be established. Collectively, these changes contribute to improved cholesterol
  homeostasis and are associated with reduced hepatic cholesterol accumulation in
  ethanol‐treated mice
