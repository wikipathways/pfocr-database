caption: Biosynthesis of melatonin in microorganismsIn Escherichia coli (left), tryptophan
  is converted to serotonin through the intermediate HTP via the action of tryptophan
  hydroxylase (TPH) and aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (DDC). Serotonin is acetylated
  by aralkylamine N-acetyltransferase (AANAT) to form N-acetylserotonin, which is
  then methylated by acetylserotonin O-methyltransferase (ASMT) using S-adenosylmethionine
  (SAM) from the SAM cycle, ultimately producing melatonin. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae
  (right), melatonin biosynthesis involves a similar pathway with an additional tetrahydrobiopterin
  (BH4)-dependent system. The BH4 biosynthesis pathway originates from GTP and involves
  key enzymes such as GTP cyclohydrolase (OCH1), 6-pyruvoyl-tetrahydropterin synthase
  (PTS), and sepiapterin reductase (SPR). BH4 is regenerated through the activities
  of dihydropteridine reductase (DHPR) and pterin-4α-carbinolamine dehydratase (PCBD).
  The SAM cycle (inset) provides the methyl group donor SAM for the final methylation
  step catalyzed by ASMT.Created in BioRender. Kwon, E. (2025) https://BioRender.com/7ensxfr
