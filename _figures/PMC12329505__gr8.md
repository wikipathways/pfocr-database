caption: Graphical summary of our findings regarding the thiamine uptake pathway in
  B. hordeiThe scheme shows a germinated powdery mildew conidiospore on the surface
  of a leaf epidermal plant cell. The fungal sporeling has a germ tube and an appressorium
  and has formed successfully a haustorium inside the plant host cell. Our data suggest
  that powdery mildews lack enzymes for the biosynthesis of thiamine (T) (Figure 2)
  but still have a functional uptake and conversion pathway, comprised of the DUR31
  thiamine transporter (Figure 6) and the thiamine pyrophosphate kinase THI80 (Figure
  4), which converts thiamine to its bioactive form thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP).
  We further provide B. hordei candidates for the extracellular acid phosphatase PHO3,
  which dephosphorylates thiamine phosphates (TPP and/or TP) to thiamine, and the
  mitochondrial TPP transporters THI74 and TPC1 (Figure 7). Most of the enzymes that
  use TPP as a cofactor are located in mitochondria. Our in silico analysis depicts
  the presence of the TPP binding fold in these enzymes (Table S3), and we experimentally
  validated the TPP dependency for transketolase (TKL; Figure 3). The most likely
  source of thiamine is the host plant
