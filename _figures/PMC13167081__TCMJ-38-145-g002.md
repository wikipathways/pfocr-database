caption: 'The Complement–T-cell axis in age-related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis.
  Drusen accumulation activates both classical and alternative complement pathways,
  generating the anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a. C5a recruits γδ T cells and CD4+ T cells
  to the choroid and retina, promoting secretion of interleukin (IL)-17 and IL-22.
  IL-17 consistently disrupts Retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) tight-junction proteins,
  increases outer blood-retinal barrier permeability, and contributes to choroidal
  neovascularization (CNV). IL-22, classically associated with epithelial protection
  and tissue repair, demonstrates context-dependent behavior: In a cytokine-rich or
  complement-activated microenvironment – particularly when co-expressed with IL-17
  or driven by C5a – it can shift toward a proinflammatory and pro-angiogenic phenotype,
  enhancing RPE stress and vascular endothelial growth factor production. Dysregulated
  complement activity, impaired CFH function, and the IL-17/IL-22 axis converge to
  promote chronic para-inflammation, microglial overactivation, and CNV formation
  in AMD (Created with BioRender.). IL: Interleukin, CNV: Choroidal neovascularization,
  RPE: Retinal pigment epithelial'
