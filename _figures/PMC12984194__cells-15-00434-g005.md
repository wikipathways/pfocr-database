caption: Schematic summary of NF1–RAS–MAPK signaling and adaptive responses observed
  during prolonged MEK inhibition in NF1 tumor models. Neurofibromin, encoded by NF1,
  functions as a Ras GTPase–activating protein (RAS-GAP) that promotes conversion
  of Ras-GTP to Ras-GDP under normal conditions. Loss of NF1 in pNF1 and MPNST cells
  results in constitutive Ras-GTP and activation of downstream RAF–MEK–ERK and PI3K–AKT
  signaling pathways. MEK inhibitors (CI-1040, U0126, selumetinib, and binimetinib)
  suppress ERK phosphorylation downstream of MEK. During prolonged MEK inhibition,
  sustained ERK suppression is associated with feedback release, leading to increased
  Ras signaling and adaptive phosphorylation of MEK and AKT. These signaling changes
  occur in parallel with increased tumor cell–mediated ECM proteolysis in 3D tumor
  structures. Black solid lines indicate established NF1–RAS–MAPK signaling relationships.
  Red solid line with blunt bar indicates the site of MEK inhibitor action. Red solid
  lines with arrows indicate concurrent adaptive phosphorylation during prolonged
  MEK inhibition
