caption: Ecological, metabolic, taxonomic, and biotechnological framework of autochthonous
  D-xylose-fermenting yeasts. A Natural reservoirs of pentose-fermenting yeasts include
  lignocellulose-rich environments such as decaying wood, agricultural soils with
  plant residues, insect-associated substrates, and forest litter, where selective
  pressures favor yeasts adapted to D-xylose metabolism and inhibitor tolerance. B
  Metabolic diversity among these yeasts involves specialized D-xylose transport systems
  and intracellular pentose-phosphate pathway fluxes enabling ethanol and other metabolite
  production, with some species also secreting lignocellulose-degrading enzymes and
  co-fermenting pentoses. C Molecular taxonomy based on D1/D2 and ITS regions clarifies
  relationships among key genera such as Spathaspora, Scheffersomyces, Candida, and
  Meyerozyma, revealing cryptic diversity and refined phylogenetic boundaries. D Biotechnological
  exploitation follows a workflow integrating ecological prospecting, molecular/phylogenomic
  classification, strain improvement, and industrial bioprocess development for lignocellulosic
  biorefineries
