caption: 'Important inflammatory cytokines regulating Th17 differentiation and IL‐17
  production via JAK/STAT and non‐JAK pathways. JAK‐dependent cytokines: IL‐6 and
  IL‐23 promote the differentiation and function of Th17 cells through the JAK/STAT3
  signaling pathway. When these cytokines bind to their cellular receptors, they activate
  associated JAK proteins (JAK1, JAK2, and TYK2), which then phosphorylate the receptors.
  This creates docking sites for STAT3. Once bound, STAT3 is also phosphorylated by
  JAKs, allowing it to form dimers and translocate to the nucleus. In the nucleus,
  STAT3 switches on genes for Th17 development such as RORc, IL‐17, and the IL‐23
  receptor (IL‐23R). IL‐23 then binds to his receptor to ensure the Th17 lineage is
  sustained. Conversely, IFNγ acts as an inhibitor, activating STAT1 to competitively
  block Th17 differentiation. Non‐JAK‐dependent cytokines: IL‐17, TNF, and IL‐1β do
  not directly utilize JAKs for signaling. Instead, they activate adaptor proteins
  such as MyD88, TRAF6/Act1, and RIPK1/TRADD, which lead to the activation of the
  NF‐κB, MAPKs, and C/EBPs signaling pathways. IL‐1β‐activated NF‐κB and AP‐1 (from
  MAPKs), work together with STAT3 to drive the full Th17 differentiation program.
  They enhance the expression of genes like ROR C. IL‐17 and TNF often promote Th17
  differentiation indirectly by stimulating other cells (such as monocytes or dendritic
  cells) to release the primary Th17 polarizing cytokines, IL‐6 and IL‐1β. These pathways
  are not directly inhibited by JAKi and therefore remain active even in the presence
  of these drugs. IFN, interferon; IL, interleukin; JAKi, JAK inhibitor'
