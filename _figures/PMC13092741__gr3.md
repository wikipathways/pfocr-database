caption: Model of the role of the interplay of PTI and ETI in rhizobial symbiotic
  signaling during early legume–rhizobium interactions. During these interactions,
  cell surface LysM-type receptors recognize Nod factors and dampen PTI (including
  ROS and SA accumulation) while activating the symbiotic signaling pathway. Rhizobial
  effectors are delivered into the host cell where they are recognized by intracellular
  R proteins, activating ETI and localized ROS bursts. Importantly, rhizobial effectors
  can target legume Nod factor receptors, thereby facilitating symbiotic signaling
  and promoting nodulation. Host immunity-related proteins such as MtNAD1 and MtDNF2
  are essential for nodulation, symbiosome development, and nitrogen fixation in Medicago.
  Key transcription factors in symbiotic pathways (e.g., ERN1, NIN, and others) concurrently
  inhibit defense genes and fine-tune phytohormone-related responses. In summary,
  effector–host protein interactions balance immune suppression with the activation
  of symbiotic programs, ensuring successful colonization while preventing inappropriate
  defense activation
