caption: The key role of histone deacetylation mediated by histone deacetylases in
  maintaining retinal homeostasis and intervention strategies. (A) SIRT1-mediated
  protection. SIRT1 deacetylates H3K9ac at the p53-associated p66Shc promoter to repress
  p66Shc transcription, thereby limiting downstream Rac1/NOX2-dependent ROS generation.
  In parallel, SIRT1 engages the LKB1/AMPK pathway to restrain PARP activity and oxidative
  stress, attenuating NF-κB/Bax-linked inflammatory and apoptotic signaling and the
  persistence of injury consistent with metabolic memory. SIRT1 also counterbalances
  the histone acetyltransferase p300; disruption of the SIRT1-p300 equilibrium favors
  pro-permeability and pro-fibrotic outputs (e.g., ET-1, TGF-β, and Collagen I), contributing
  to vascular leakage. (B) SIRT6 and neurovascular stability. SIRT6 deacetylates H3K9ac
  and H3K56ac to sustain neurotrophic signaling (BDNF) while repressing pathological
  angiogenic programs (VEGF). Dysregulation of this axis contributes to neurovascular
  unit dysfunction and promotes DR progression. (C) Therapeutic strategies. Pharmacological
  restoration of the NAD⁺-SIRT axis (sirtuin activators and metabolic modulators)
  is proposed to re-establish deacetylation-dependent protective programs and improve
  retinal outcomes under diabetic stress. SIRT Sirtuin, p66shc p66 isoform of Src
  homology 2 domain-containing transforming protein 1, Rac1 ras-related C3 botulinum
  toxin substrate 1, NOX2 NADPH oxidase 2, ROS reactive oxygen species, LKB1 liver
  kinase B1, AMPK AMP-activated protein kinase, PARP Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase,

  x protein, ET-1 endothelin-1, TGF-β transforming growth factor-beta, BDNF brain-derived
  neurotrophic factor, VEGF vascular endothelial growth factor, HDAC histone deacetylase
