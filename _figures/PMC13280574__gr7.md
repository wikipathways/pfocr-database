caption: 'Conceptual model of antagonistic pleiotropy linking RV susceptibility and
  gastrointestinal cancer protection. Our model proposes an example of antagonistic
  pleiotropy, in which genetic factors that predispose infants to more severe RV infection
  simultaneously provide long-term protection against gastrointestinal cancers. This
  dual effect illustrates how evolutionary pressures may sustain certain genetic variants
  in the population, despite their early-life disadvantages. Our findings indicate
  that susceptibility to RV infection in our cohort is influenced by specific genetic
  factors, with risk associated with genes such as BCAT1, A4GNT, CLDN4, CKAP2, PTCHD4,
  and PRKCH, and a protective effect linked to TANC1. Interestingly, these patients
  also exhibit a genetic profile, as reflected in PRS, that suggests protection against
  various gastrointestinal cancers, particularly gastric cancer [point 1]. Based on
  these observations, we propose that RV infection may induce epigenetic modifications,
  such as changes in DNA methylation, which could alter gene expression profiles in
  infected children [point 2]. These molecular changes may persist throughout childhood
  development and into adulthood, potentially establishing biological conditions that
  influence susceptibility to gastrointestinal system cancers [point 3]. RV infection
  and potentially exposure to live attenuated vaccines likely target tight junction
  proteins and other biological pathways. This interaction may disrupt epithelial
  barriers, offering a mechanistic explanation for the paradoxical effects: facilitating
  viral entry during infection while conferring long-term protection against gastrointestinal
  cancers [point 4]. Furthermore, the systemic immune response to RV, particularly
  through CD8+ T cells and IFN-γ, may play a dual role in both viral control and the
  mechanisms underlying cancer prevention [point 5]'
