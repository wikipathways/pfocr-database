caption: Overview of the steps involved in the metaorganismal formation of trimethylamine-N-oxide
  (TMAO). The dietary precursors betaine, L-carnitine and choline are transformed
  by gut microbiota to trimethylamine (TMA) via different biochemical pathways (grd,
  bbu/cnt and cut). A few gut resident archaea are able to demethylate TMA to methane
  (CH4) and ammonium (NH3). The absorbed TMA is oxidized to TMAO by hepatic flavin-containing
  monooxygenase 3 (FMO3) and circulatory TMAO is subsequently renally cleared from
  the body. However, long-term high concentrations can promote cardiovascular disease
  (CVD) and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Various pathomechanisms are involved including
  foam cell formation, reduction of the reverse cholesterol transport (RCT) and endothelial
  inflammation via activation of nuclear factor kappa B and the inflammasome that
  all promote atherosclerosis; TMAO is also enhancing platelet hyperreactivity. Furthermore,
  it was shown to directly induce kidney fibrosis initiating a positive feedback loop
  resulting in TMAO accumulation in the body. For details see text
