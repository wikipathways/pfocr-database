caption: MSCs mitochondrial transfer–mediated immunoregulatory network. (a) MSCs transfer
  mitochondria to neutrophils to restore mitochondrial function, reduce NETs release,
  and weaken NETs-driven Th17 differentiation, which helps lower Th17 cell numbers.
  (b) Healthy exogenous mitochondria transfer can reduce ROS levels and activate the
  mTOR pathway to boost NK cell cytotoxicity, and MSCs mitochondrial transfer may
  have a similar effect. (c) Macrophages that take up mitochondria from MSCs show
  reduced M1 polarization, increased M2 polarization, and higher IL-10 production
  to suppress inflammation. Mitochondrial transfer boosts OXPHOS, which supports M2
  polarization, so MSCs likely regulate macrophages by this mechanism. (d) Higher
  OXPHOS and lower ROS help B cells become Bregs that secrete IL-10. Mitochondrial
  transfer can create the same conditions, so MSCs may regulate B cells this way.
  (e) Mitochondria transferred from MSCs to T cells have been found to reduce Th17
  cells and increase Treg cells by stabilizing FoxP3 and reducing ROS. As mitochondrial
  transfer boosts the TCA cycle and reduces glycolysis, we suggest that it also regulates
  the Th17/Treg ratio through this metabolic shift, leading to reduced levels of IL-17,
  TNF-α, and IFN-γ. Bisedese, MSCs can transfer mitochondria into Th1 cells, down-regulate
  the transcription factor T-bet, and reduce secretion of IFN-γ, helping to regulate
  inflammation
