caption: 'Schematic illustration of the mechanisms underlying cccDNA persistence in
  infected cells and sustained HBVZ10 expression in transduced cells(A) The cccDNA
  pool in infected cells is maintained through two major replenishment pathways: intracellular
  recycling (black) and extracellular de novo infection (green). (B) The intracellular
  recycling pathway can be blocked by nucleos(t)ide analogs such as entecavir (ETV).
  (C) The anti-HBs antibody expressed by HBVZ10 blocks the extracellular de novo infection
  pathway. Combination therapy with HBVZ10 and ETV thus inhibits both cccDNA replenishment
  routes. (D1) Schematic representation of the HBVZ10 construct. (D2–D4) Following
  intramuscular injection, HBVZ10 enters and transduces skeletal muscle cells. Multiple
  copies of the HBVZ10 genome within the same nucleus form concatemers, enabling long-term
  and stable expression of anti-HBs antibody'
