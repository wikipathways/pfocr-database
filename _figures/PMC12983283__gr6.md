caption: 'Roles and signaling cascades of NO and H2S in ethylene biosynthesis and
  fruit responses(A) Model illustrating the action of NO and H2S in the ethylene biosynthesis
  pathway. Ethylene is synthesized from methionine via a pathway involving several
  key enzymes: S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) synthetase (also known as methionine adenosyltransferase,
  MAT), ACC synthase, and ACC oxidase. SAM is regenerated through the methionine cycle
  (Yang cycle), which ensures continuous ethylene production. SAM synthetase activity
  can be inhibited by S-nitrosation (Lindermayr et al., 2006). In addition, NO has
  been reported to inhibit both ACC synthase and ACC oxidase activities, although
  the underlying mechanism—either S-nitrosation or nitration—has not been determined
  (Cheng et al., 2009; Freschi, 2013). H2S can also inhibit ACC oxidase through persulfidation
  (Jia et al., 2018). Moreover, melatonin has been shown to stimulate NO biosynthesis
  (Liu et al., 2019), adding another regulatory layer to ethylene production. Although
  melatonin can also promote H2S production via LCD activity in higher plants (Mukherjee
  and Bhatla, 2021), evidence supporting its role in fruit is lacking. Similar mechanisms
  may operate during fruit ripening and postharvest physiology.(B) Cascade of events
  triggered by exogenous application of NO and H2S to fruits. NO or H2S act directly
  on the activity of specific protein targets through distinct PTMs or indirectly
  by regulating the expression of genes coding target proteins, either via transcription
  factors (TFs) or epigenetic mechanisms. Within this signaling cascade, reactive
  oxygen species (ROS) metabolism is markedly affected, typically resulting in enhanced
  activity of both enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems. This enhancement
  helps mitigate potential oxidative damage to macromolecules. The final beneficial
  outcomes vary depending on the horticultural product and may include delayed fruit
  senescence, extended shelf life, and improved nutritional quality. bZIP, basic region/leucine
  zipper motif; HD-ZIP, homeodomain leucine zipper; KMB, 2-keto-4-ethylthiobutyrate;
  MTA, 5-methylthioadenosine; MTR, 5-methylthioribose; SOD, superoxide dismutase;
  and WRKY, TF characterized by a highly conserved WRKY amino acid sequence at the
  N terminus'
