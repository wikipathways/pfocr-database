caption: Schematic of the molecular mechanism of Cu‐induced NADH‐reductive stress
  leading to cuproptosis in neural cells. Mitochondrial Cu accumulation activates
  DLD through disulfate bond formation and promotes NADH generation. The excess NADH
  subsequently translocates to the cytosol following Cu‐induced mPTP opening, which
  drives NADH‐reductive stress. The cytosol NADH accumulation provokes the purine
  biosynthesis pathway and triggers massive ATP consumption, ultimately leading to
  neural cell death. This Cu‐NADH‐reductive stress‐mediated cell death can be suppressed
  by inhibition of DLD or mPTP, deletion of cytosolic NADH, disruption of purine biosynthesis,
  and activation of AMPK
