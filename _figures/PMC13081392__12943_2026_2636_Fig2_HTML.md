caption: 'Mechanistic overview of the RT-cGAS-STING axis and its multi-level regulation
  in the tumor immune microenvironment. Layer 1 (Sources & Regulators): RT induces
  double-strand breaks (DSBs), leading to micronuclei formation and mtDNA release
  into the cytosol, engaging cGAS. Simultaneously, RT-induced apoptosis activates
  Caspase-3/7/9, proteolytically cleaving cGAS/STING and suppressing immune signaling.
  Negative regulators include TREX1, ENPP1, and chromatin tethering. Layer 2 (Signaling):
  Activated cGAS synthesizes 2′3′-cGAMP, which spreads to bystander cells via SLC19A1
  and LRRC8/VRAC channels. STING translocates from the ER to the Golgi via COP-II
  vesicles, facilitated by ACBD3, recruiting TBK1/IRF3 to initiate type I IFN and
  chemokine transcription. Layer 3 (Immune consequences): Secreted cytokines promote
  DC maturation and antigen cross-priming. Neutrophil NETosis forms a barrier limiting
  circulating CD8⁺ T cell infiltration, making tumor clearance dependent on tissue-resident
  CD8⁺ T cells. The pathway also supports vascular normalization via VCAM-1/ICAM-1
  upregulation. Layer 4 (Modulators): Pathway output is dose-dependent; moderate doses
  favor activation, whereas ablative doses induce shutdown. Chronic activation triggers
  negative feedback, including adaptive PD-L1 upregulation, myeloid skewing (M2 polarization,
  IDO pathway), fibrosis, and ER stress–induced T-cell exhaustion. Created with Adobe
  Illustrator 2024'
