caption: Bone marrow MSCs (top left) engage in mitochondria and metabolite exchange
  with AML cells and influence metabolic signalling pathways through the CXCL12/CXCR4
  axis. Acetate secreted by MSCs is used to fuel the TCA cycle in AML cells, while
  GSH provided by MSCs neutralizes ROS, by-products of a hyper-active AML cell mitochondrial
  metabolism. Mito-transfer refers to the transfer of mitochondria from MSCs to leukaemic
  cells, increasing their OXPHOS activity. Aspartate and taurine secreted by MSCs
  support nucleotide synthesis and glycolytic activity in AML cells, respectively.
  Adipocytes (top right) contribute to the niche by releasing growth factors and FFAs
  in response to leukaemic stimuli; these FFAs are taken up via CD36 transporters
  to fuel the TCA cycle and mitochondrial OXPHOS in AML cells. Overexpression of FABP4
  in AML cells governs fatty acid uptake from adipocytes. Osteoblasts express JAGGED-1
  and adhere to AML cells via N-cadherin (bottom left), and AML progression is further
  supported by osteoblastic HTR1B, activated by AML-secreted kynurenine. Kynurenine
  induces a proinflammatory state in osteoblasts leading to serum amyloid A (SAA)
  production, which enhances indolamine-2,3-dioxygenase (IDO1) expression in AML cells,
  establishing a positive feedback loop that promotes leukaemia. Leukaemic cells close
  to osteoblasts exhibit high NADH levels and glycolytic activity. In addition, AML
  cells in the endosteal niche show high NADPH levels regulated by the folate cycle
  enzyme methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase 1 (MTHFD1). Metabolic interactions
  between AML cells and ECs (bottom right) remain largely unknown. AML engraftment
  induces hypoxia and affects bone marrow ECs by activating pro-angiogenic pathways
  and upregulating hypoxia responses and NADPH oxidase (NOX4), increasing NO levels
  in the bone marrow microenvironment
