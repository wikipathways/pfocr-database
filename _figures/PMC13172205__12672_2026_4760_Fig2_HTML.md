caption: 'Metformin activity in cancer: tumor microenvironment and tumor cell-intrinsic
  mechanisms. A Effects of metformin on the tumor microenvironment. Metformin reduces
  insulin- and IGF-1-driven oncogenic signaling and modulates the immune landscape
  by decreasing the abundance and suppressive activity of myeloid-derived suppressor
  cells (MDSCs) and regulatory T cells (Tregs). These changes contribute to the attenuation
  of tumor-promoting inflammation, angiogenesis, and immune evasion. B Tumor cell-intrinsic
  mechanisms of metformin action. Following cellular uptake, metformin induces energetic
  stress by increasing the AMP/ATP ratio, leading to the activation OCT1 is essential
  for metformin uptake and may influence the therapeutic response of the LKB1-AMPKα
  axis. Activated AMPK inhibits mTOR signaling through TSC1/2-dependent and-independent
  mechanisms, resulting in reduced protein synthesis, lipogenesis, and gluconeogenesis.
  In parallel, metformin attenuates insulin- and IGF-1-mediated activation of the
  PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Together, these effects suppress cell growth and proliferation
  and promote metabolic reprogramming, which is unfavorable for tumor progression'
