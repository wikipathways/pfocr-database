caption: 'Bioproduct diversification, strain development, and industrial deployment
  of D-xylose-fermenting yeasts. A Diversification of bioproducts derived from D-xylose
  metabolism. After uptake by pentose transporters, D-xylose is converted through
  oxidoreductive or isomerase pathways and enters central metabolism, linking the
  pentose phosphate pathway, glycolysis, and the TCA cycle. Carbon flux can be directed
  toward ethanol, xylitol, organic acids (e.g., lactic or succinic acid), or aromatic
  precursors, while hydrolytic enzymes such as cellulases and xylanases contribute
  to lignocellulose breakdown. B Integration of lignocellulosic biomass processing
  into fermentation pipelines, where pretreatment and enzymatic hydrolysis generate
  D-xylose-rich hydrolysates that feed distinct production lines (ethanol, xylitol,
  or organic acids) under integrated biorefinery schemes. C Yeast discovery and improvement
  workflow, including ecological prospecting, selective cultivation in D-xylose media,
  adaptive evolution under inhibitor stress, and phenotypic screening to identify
  robust strains. D Genetic and omics-based engineering strategies involving targeted
  gene deletions, insertions, and pathway rewiring, supported by genomics, transcriptomics,
  proteomics, and metabolomics, together with synthetic biology tools such as RFOSC,
  Gibson Assembly, and SLIC. E Industrial validation through fermentation of lignocellulosic
  hydrolysates in bioreactors, enabling iterative optimization of strain performance
  and scalable bioprocess deployment. Abbreviations: XR, xylose reductase; XDH, xylitol
  dehydrogenase; XI, xylose isomerase; ADH, alcohol dehydrogenase; LDH, lactate dehydrogenase'
