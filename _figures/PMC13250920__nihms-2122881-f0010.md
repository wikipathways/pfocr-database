caption: Glucocorticoid receptor (GR) signaling shifts from stress-induced analgesia
  to chronic stress-induced pain via NF-κB and inflammasome activation in neurons
  and immune cells. This schematic illustrates the temporal and cellular progression
  of GR signaling in response to restraint stress (RS), highlighting its functional
  transition from anti-inflammatory/antinociceptive to pro-inflammatory/pronociceptive
  roles. During acute stress, GR activation suppresses NF-κB and other inflammatory
  mediators, promoting stress-induced analgesia. With sustained stress, GR undergoes
  phosphorylation (p-GR), which enhances its transcriptional activity. In the chronic
  phase, p-GR facilitates NF-κB activation via phosphorylation of P65 (p-P65), leading
  to upregulation of NLRP3, caspase-1, and IL-1β-key mediators of the inflammasome
  pathway and neuroinflammation. These molecular events underlie the transition to
  mechanical hypersensitivity and pain. The timeline indicates that these changes
  initiate in the spinal cord and later involve the dorsal root ganglia (DRG). These
  processes were observed in neurons and immune cells (microglia in the spinal cord
  and macrophages in the DRGs). Pharmacological inhibition of NF-κB activity reversed
  RS-induced pain behaviors, supporting a causal role for this pathway in the maintenance
  of chronic stress-induced allodynia
