caption: 'Mechanism of ferroptosis in radiotherapy. This figure illustrates strategies
  to combine ferroptosis inducers with immunotherapy and highlights context-dependent
  outcomes. (Top left panel) Combination of Immunotherapy with Other Treatments: The
  natural compound Icariin (ICA) combined with anti-PD-1 promotes mitochondrial damage-induced
  ferroptosis and enhances T cell receptor (TCR) signaling (e.g., CD69). Radiation
  therapy (RT) induces reactive oxygen species (ROS) and upregulates ACSL4, promoting
  lipid peroxidation. (Top right panel) Ferroptosis reduces therapeutic efficacy:
  In specific contexts, DAMPs released from ferroptotic cells can be phagocytosed
  by macrophages, driving their polarization towards a pro-tumor M2-like phenotype,
  an effect potentiated by oncogenic KRASG12D. Metabolic reprogramming in the tumor
  microenvironment (TME), including iron overload and lipid peroxidation, can increase
  the sensitivity of CD8⁺ T cells to ferroptosis, potentially undermining antitumor
  immunity.(Bottom left panel) Targeting tumor cells: Inducing ferroptosis in tumor
  cells leads to the release of DAMPs. This process can stimulate antitumor immunity,
  contributing to tumor control.(Bottom right panel) Targeting immune cells: Inducing
  ferroptosis in specific immune cell populations (e.g., immunosuppressive macrophages)
  within the TME. This can help transform immunologically “cold” tumors (low immune
  infiltration) into “hot” tumors (high immune infiltration), thereby promoting antitumor
  immunity.(Central section) Immune therapy and Ferroptosis: Key molecular interactions
  include p53-mediated ferroptosis induction, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) enhancing
  therapeutic efficacy, and in glioblastoma, ferritin light chain (FTL) promoting
  macrophage polarization towards an M2 phenotype, which can increase sensitivity
  to PD-1 blockade. Ionizing radiation (IR) generates reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  and free radicals, leading to DNA damage and disruption of various biomolecules,
  including lipids. IR promotes ferroptosis through multiple mechanisms. On one hand,
  IR upregulates ACSL4 expression, enhancing lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell
  death. On the other hand, IR downregulates the expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4, two
  key regulators that suppress ferroptosis. Additionally, X-ray-induced hydrated electrons
  can directly reduce ferric ions (Fe³⁺) and protein-bound iron (such as ferric succinylprotein
  complexes) without the need for auxiliary ligands. Both in vitro and in vivo studies
  demonstrate that ferric succinate effectively promotes ferroptosis and exhibits
  potent tumor growth inhibition. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), METTL3 enhances
  the stability of SLC7A11 mRNA by targeting the adenosine residue at position + 1795
  and reduces SLC7A11 protein ubiquitination. In combination with immunotherapy, SLC7A11
  plays a central role in mediating synergistic anti-tumor effects. Immune checkpoint
  blockade activates CD8⁺ T cells, while radiotherapy activates ATM signaling and
  promotes interferon-γ (IFNγ) release. Together, these pathways converge to suppress
  SLC7A11 expression, thereby enhancing lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis in tumor
  cells'
