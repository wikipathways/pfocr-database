caption: 'Life cycle and immune evasion mechanisms of DBV(A) DBV possesses a tri-segmented
  genome (L, M, and S), encoding the RdRp, Gn/Gc, NP, and NSs, respectively. Viral
  entry is mediated by Gn/Gc binding to host receptors (e.g., DC-SIGN and CCR2) and
  lipid-dependent conformational changes (e.g., GlcCer) that enable clathrin-mediated
  endocytosis. After endosomal trafficking, viral RNPs are released into the cytoplasm
  and undergo replication and assembly in the Golgi body. Subsequently, the virus
  particles are transported and released through vesicle transport. During this process,
  NP and NSs are expressed after the release and translation of the viral genome.
  The NP-mediated Beclin-1 release and NSs-induced vimentin degradation disrupt host
  autophagy, which is part of the immune evasion.(B) DBV employs multiple immune evasion
  mechanisms: NSs employs distinct strategies to antagonize the host immune response:
  (i) Sequestration: NSs forms inclusion bodies that sequester STAT1/STAT2, blocking
  their phosphorylation and nuclear translocation to inhibit type I/III IFN signaling.
  (ii) Capture: NSs captures RIG-I pathway signaling molecules (including TBK1), preventing
  the activation of downstream antiviral responses. (iii) Direct Interaction: NSs
  binds directly to LSM14A to suppress IFN-β production. Furthermore, the NP protein
  binds mitochondrial TUFM to induce LC3-dependent mitophagy, leading to the degradation
  of MAVS. Additionally, DBV suppresses mDC differentiation and CD86 expression, resulting
  in impaired antigen presentation and T cell activation'
