caption: Neurons, microglia, and astrocytes use damage-associated molecular patterns
  (DAMPs) as mediators for intercellular communication and functional crosstalk following
  TBI. Neurons, microglia, and astrocytes communicate and engage in crosstalk through
  various mechanisms, including direct contact and paracrine signaling, which requires
  the release of signaling molecules and the presence of receptors on the nerve cells
  to facilitate signal transmission. After TBI, the signaling molecules involved in
  intercellular communication and functional crosstalk include most DAMPs, such as
  ATP, HMGB1, S100B, hyaluronan, Aβ, IL-33, miRNA, DNA, and Ca2+. The receptor types
  involved include TLRs, RAGEs, P2XR, P2YR, and TRP channels. Following TBI, neurons
  and astrocytes in the affected area release ATP, creating an ATP concentration gradient
  in the tissue. As the earliest responding immune cells, microglia utilize this ATP
  gradient as a chemotactic signal, primarily through P2Y12R and P2X4R located on
  microglia, to migrate toward the damaged area. During this process, receptors such
  as RAGE, TLR2, and TLR4 on microglia bind DAMPs, including HMGB1, S100B, and APOE
  released by neurons or astrocytes, or inflammatory mediators, such as TNF-α, IL-18,
  and IL-1β, inducing microglia to polarize toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype.
  Microglia also respond to fibrinogen and cytochrome c released by neurons through
  TLR4, activating NOX in microglia. This promotes the generation of ROS, exacerbating
  oxidative stress after TBI, and affecting astrocytes and neurons. Similarly, astrocytes
  respond early to injury after TBI. ATP and Ca2+ released from microglia and neurons
  activate the JAK2–STAT3 pathway through P2Y1R in astrocytes, leading to the polarization
  of astrocytes to the pro-inflammatory A1 phenotype. A1 astrocytes release inflammatory
  factors, such as IL-6 and INF-γ, further aggravating inflammation, or release Aβ
  to induce microglia to adopt the M1 phenotype. The transmission of hyaluronan between
  microglia and astrocytes via receptors such as TLR and CD44 affects the morphology
  and chemotaxis of both cell types during neuroinflammation. IL-33 and HMGB1 exhibit
  self-feedback loops in astrocytes and microglia, respectively, stimulating these
  cells to produce inflammatory factors that amplify the inflammatory response and
  lead to the release of additional IL-33 and HMGB1. Throughout this crosstalk process,
  neurons continuously exchange DAMPs, such as ATP, HMGB1, S100B, Aβ, and HSP, with
  glial cells through receptors, including TLR, RAGE, and TREM, resulting in various
  pathological changes, e.g. synaptic inhibition, inflammatory damage, oxidative stress,
  and programmed cell death. Therefore, DAMPs facilitate intercellular communication
  and functional crosstalk between neurons and glial cells during the secondary brain
  injury stage. While they may exacerbate pathological reactions, they can also provide
  neuroprotective effects in certain contexts. Taken together, the influence of DAMPs
  at the cellular level after TBI remains critically important. P2XR P2X receptors,
  P2YR P2Y receptors, TRP transient receptor potential, APOE apolipoprotein E, NOX
  NADPH oxidase, ROS reactive oxygen species, TBI traumatic brain injury, DAMP damage-associated
  molecular pattern, TNF tumor necrosis factor, HMGB1 high-mobility group box 1, IL
  interleukin, INF interferon
