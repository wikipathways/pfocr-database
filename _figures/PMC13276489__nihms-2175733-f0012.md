caption: Dysregulation of astrocyte signaling in the SCDH drives neuropathic pain.
  Nerve injury induces robust activation of astrocytes, resulting in astrogliosis
  characterized by morphological changes, including reduced complexity of astrocytic
  processes and thickening of the main processes, as well as upregulation of GFAP.
  This reactivity is associated with loss of protective astrocyte functions, as indicated
  by down-regulation of GLT-1, Kir4.1, and GPR37L1, leading to increased excitability
  of neurons and enhanced excitatory synaptic transmission. Astrogliosis is also accompanied
  by phosphorylation of ERK and JNK (P-ERK, P-JNK). The collective activation of these
  signaling pathways triggers the release of astroglial mediators that modulate excitatory
  and inhibitory synaptic transmission, increase neuronal excitability, and drive
  central sensitization, thereby promoting the transition from acute to chronic pain
  (403). In neuropathic pain conditions, astrocytes exhibit increased Cx43 expression
  and undergo a functional switch from gap-junction communication to Cx43 hemichannel-mediated
  paracrine signaling, leading to elevated release of proinflammatory chemokines (CXCL1
  and CCL2), glutamate, and ATP. Spinal microglia express several ATP receptors, including
  P2X4R, P2X7R, and P2Y12R (100, 118). Activation of these receptors induces the release
  of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF and IL-1β, which sensitize dorsal horn
  neurons. These cytokines also stimulate JNK and ERK phosphorylation in astrocytes,
  further amplifying the production of proinflammatory mediators. Astrocyte-derived
  mediators, including CXCL1, CCL2, and IL-1β, promote central sensitization through
  neuronal CXCR2, CCR2, and IL-1R signaling, which in turn enhances ERK activation
  in primary afferent central terminals and SCDH neurons (489, 517). Nerve injury–induced
  release of CXCL13 from SCDH neurons further activates astrocytes via CXCR5 to facilitate
  neuropathic pain (494). Additionally, activation of cytokine receptors (TNFR and
  IL-1R) and Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) on astrocytes by endogenous ligands and cytokines
  released from microglia and other astrocytes induces TRAF6 upregulation and downstream
  ERK and JNK activation, further promoting the release of astroglial mediators (518).
  Astrocytes may also contribute to neuropathic pain through the release of growth
  factors, including WNT3A and FGF2 (519, 520). Finally, astrocyte-mediated metabolic
  dysregulation also contributes to the development and maintenance of neuropathic
  pain. PTG (protein targeting to glycogen) promotes glycogen accumulation in astrocytes,
  thereby facilitating neuronal plasticity and pain sensitization. The astrocyte–neuron
  lactate shuttle plays an active role in sustaining neuropathic pain by providing
  metabolic support for hyperactive neurons. Lipid signaling through sphingosine-1-phosphate
  receptor subtype 1 (S1PR1) in astrocytes further contributes to neuropathic pain
  by amplifying neuroinflammatory responses
