caption: 'Microbiota-regulated circadian immunometabolic mechanisms in the gutThis
  schematic illustrates the key pathways through which the gut microbiota influences
  host immunometabolic processes in a circadian manner. The intestinal epithelial
  layer serves as a central interface, integrating microbial signals into rhythmic
  host responses. Pathway A: microbial LPS signals through the TLRs-MYD88 pathway
  to activate the circadian regulator REV-ERBα in the nucleus, REV-ERBα then recruits
  HDAC3-NCoR complex to periodically silence genes involved in lipid metabolism. Pathway
  B: microbial stimulation through the TLRs-MYD88 pathway to induces IL-22 production
  via the DC-ILC3 axis, activating epithelial STAT3, which suppresses REV-ERBα and
  promotes NFIL3 expression, further coordinating the rhythm of lipid metabolism and
  immune cell. Immune barrier: bacteria and their products rhythmically bind to epithelial
  TLRs, driving the rhythmic expression of antimicrobial peptides(AMPs). The microbiota
  can induce circadian rhythmic expression of MHC class II molecules in small intestinal
  epithelial cells (SIECs), driving periodic proliferation of intestinal epithelial
  lymphocytes (IELs). Immune cell regulation: NFIL3 modulates Th17/Treg differentiation.
  Disruption of these rhythms contributes to inflammation, obesity, and insulin resistance.
  Inflammasome activity: microbial metabolites activate the NLRP6 inflammasome, promoting
  IL-18 secretion and AMPs expression under circadian control of REV-ERBα'
