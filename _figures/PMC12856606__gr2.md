caption: 'The ferroptosis mechanism of retinal ganglion cell (RGC) death in glaucoma.
  The accumulation of Fe2+ occurs because extracellular ferric ions are taken up through
  the transferrin (TF) receptor (TFR), reduced to Fe2+ by six-transmembrane epithelial
  antigen of the prostate 3 (STEAP3), and then transported into the cytoplasm via
  divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1). Iron autophagy protein nuclear receptor co-activator
  4 (NCOA4) releases Fe2+ by degrading ferritin. N-methyl-d-aspartic acid (NMDA) receptors
  mediate the entry of Fe2+ into the cytoplasm through a signal cascade that activates
  Dexras1, while FPN is involved in the excretion of Fe2+. Excess intracellular Fe2+
  generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) through the Fenton reaction, ultimately leading
  to the accumulation of lipid peroxides. Glutathione (GSH) peroxidase 4 (GPX4), with
  the involvement of GSH, reduces toxic phospholipid hydroperoxides (pL-OOH) to non-toxic
  phospholipid alcohols (PL-OH). Interestingly, both Fe2+ and GPX4 can localize to
  the mitochondria. Additionally, solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) mediates
  the transport of glutamate and cystine. Pathologically, the dysfunction of the glutamate
  aspartate transporter (GLAST) receptor on Müller cells and astrocytes leads to the
  accumulation of extracellular glutamate, inhibiting system Xc− and ultimately triggering
  ferroptosis. Notably, intracellular ROS promotes the release of inflammatory factors
  through the nuclear factor kappaB (NF-κB) pathway, thereby activating microglia
  and Müller cells. FPN: ferroportin; PAP7: prostate-specific acid phosphatase 7;
  DFO: desferrioxamine; GSSG: GSH disulfide; NADPH: nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide
  phosphate; PUFAs: polyunsaturated fatty acids; OPA1: optic atrophy 1; MFN1/2: mitofusin
  1/2; Drp1: dynamin-related protein 1; GSR: GSH reductase; RSL3: RAS-selective lethal
  3; Fer-1: ferrostatin-1; GLT-1: glutamate transporter 1; IL-1β: interleukin-1 beta;
  TNF: tumor necrosis factor'
