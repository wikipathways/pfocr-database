caption: Force-triggered signal transduction pathways driving biofilm formation and
  maturation. (A) In Bacillus subtilis, restricted flagellar movement from surface
  activates the DegS-DegU two-component system, inducing biofilm genes such as bsIA.
  (B) P. aeruginosa explores surfaces using type IV pili (T4P). Successful T4P retraction
  generates a mechanical signal that increases intracellular cAMP, enhancing twitching
  motility. When the pilus encounters PsI trails deposited by preceding cells, the
  adhesin CdrA binds to PsI and resists retraction. This frustration in retraction
  increases c-di-GMP levels, driving transition toward stable adhesion and biofilm
  formation. (C) In P. aeruginosa , surface contact sensed by WspA activates the Wsp
  pathway, leading to WspR activation, which synthesizes c-di-GMP. Elevated c-di-GMP
  induces genes for extracellular polysaccharides (Pel, PsI, Alginate), producing
  a structurally robust matrix that supports stable microcolony formation and biofilm
  maturation. Created in BioRender. Cabo, M. (2025) https://BioRender.com/8vokrxt
