caption: C. trachomatis alters key host cell signaling pathways to regulate cell death
  pathways and facilitate intracellular reproduction via the m6A methyltransferase
  METTL14. Chlamydia infection alters the expression levels of m6A methyltransferase
  METTL14, the distribution of m6A methylation motifs, and the m6A modifications on
  specific cellular mRNAs. METTL14, a key m6A writer enzyme, regulates C. trachomatis
  intracellular reproduction. In C. trachomatis-infected cells, the m6A methyltransferase
  METTL14 plays a crucial role in inhibiting apoptosis and promoting the intracellular
  reproduction of C. trachomatis by activating the MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways.
