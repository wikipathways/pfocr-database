caption: 'CMA contributes to maintaining calcium and mitochondrial homeostasis in
  skeletal muscle. (A) Schematic diagram of the structure of the sarcoendoplasmic
  reticulum (SR) calcium ATPase proteins ATP2A1 and ATP2A2. The 3D crystal structure
  shows the ATP2A1 (PDB: 2ZBD) and ATP2A2 (PDB: 7E7S) Ca2+ pumps as cartoons. The
  KFERQ-like motifs of ATP2A1 are depicted in the magnified panel on the right as
  orange (173LRVDQ177) and yellow (234KIRDQ238) sticks. The KFERQ-like motif of ATP2A2
  is depicted in the magnified panel on the right as orange (173LRVDQ177) sticks.
  (B) The CMA pathway contributes to cellular homeostasis in skeletal muscles. HSPA8
  surveils the protein quality and directly binds ATP2A/SERCA isoforms, targeting
  them to the lysosome for degradation. During aging, the CMA pathway declines due
  to the lysosomal instability of LAMP2A, leading to a reduced ATP2A turnover. The
  ATP2A aggregates, resulting from protein accumulation, disturb Ca2+ trafficking
  and storage in the SR and the stability of SR and mitochondrial calcium-transport
  proteins. The function of calcium-regulated proteins involved in mitochondrial pathways,
  such as the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and electron transport chain (ETC), is
  compromised, leading to elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, mitochondrial
  dysfunction, abnormal organelle morphology, and metabolic alterations. ITPR3, inositol
  1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 3; MCU, mitochondrial calcium uniporter; VDAC,
  voltage dependent ion channel. (C) Schematic diagram depicting the age-related decline
  of the CMA pathway, which is associated with the deregulation of Ca2+ homeostasis,
  mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic shifts, and the onset of myopathy in skeletal
  muscle'
