caption: Intrinsic and extrinsic factors regulating AT2 cell fate in aging lungs.
  This figure highlights the intrinsic and extrinsic factors that influence AT2 cell
  fate and repair capacity in aging lungs. Intrinsic factors include telomere dysfunction
  and metabolic dysregulation. Telomere dysfunction, along with DNA damage, occurs
  when telomeres uncap, driven by FBW7-mediated degradation of TPP1, leading to cellular
  senescence. Metabolic dysregulation, particularly inactivation of the AMPKPFKFB2
  pathway, impairs glycolysis and promotes the formation of transitional AT2 cells,
  limiting their ability to differentiate and self-renew. Extrinsic factors include
  mechanical stress and changes in the alveolar microenvironment. Mechanical stress,
  caused by ECM remodeling and increased tissue stiffness, disrupts the Cdc42–YAP
  signaling axis, worsening alveolar remodeling and fibrosis in aging lungs. Additionally,
  the secretion of growth factors such as Wnt and Pdgfr is reduced in senescent fibroblasts
  (expressing p16INK4a), and aged immune cells (regulatory T cells and macrophages)
  further impair the regenerative capacity of AT2 cells. Collectively, these factors
  contribute to diminished lung repair and increased fibrosis in the aging lung. AT2
  alveolar type 2 cells, FBW7 F-box and WD repeat domain containing 7, TPP1 telomere
  protection protein 1, AMPK AMP-activated protein kinase, PFKFB2 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase
  2, Krt8 keratin 8, Cdc42 cell division control protein 42, YAP yes-associated protein,
  PDGFA platelet-derived growth factor A, p16INK4a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor
  2A, ECM extracellular matrix
