caption: Working model for proposed mechanism by which pyrimidines promote SaeS kinase
  activity. During cellular growth, pyrimidine sufficiency dictates whether S. aureus
  needs to turn on the de novo pyrimidine biosynthetic pathway. Pyrimidines promote
  SaeS kinase activity, in part, by promoting and maintaining cell envelope and membrane
  homeostasis. Under pyrimidine-limited conditions where S. aureus cannot synthesize
  pyrimidines de novo, cell envelope biosynthesis goes unchecked, resulting in increased
  LTA production and a thickening of the cell wall. Substrate limitation for CdsA
  results in a bottleneck in phospholipid biosynthesis. This leads to an unstable
  membrane due to the accumulation of phospholipid precursors, including free fatty
  acids, that act to reduce SaeS kinase activity. This figure was generated using
