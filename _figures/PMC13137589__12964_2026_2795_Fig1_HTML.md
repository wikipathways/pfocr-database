caption: 'Molecular mechanisms driving DNPC pathogenesis and therapeutic resistance.
  a Epigenetic and transcriptional regulation drives lineage plasticity. On the one
  hand, ADT induces or occurs concurrently with KMT2C mutation/loss which activates
  TET enzymes, leading to PROX1 promoter demethylation and consequent PROX1 overexpression.
  PROX1 interacts with HDAC complexes, suppressing apoptosis and enhancing cell survival.
  Concurrently, ADT combined with KMT2C mutation/loss decreases ASPP2 levels, resulting
  in increased SREBP1c and Delta Np63 expression. Delta Np63 drives FASN activity
  and facilitates HRAS palmitoylation, leading to constitutive activation of MAPK
  signaling, thereby enabling DNPC transdifferentiation and ADT resistance. On the
  other hand, Pathologically activated PRC1 complex induces CCL2 expression. CCL2
  binding to CCR2 and CCR4 receptors promotes infiltration of TAMs and MDSCs, establishing
  an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that enhances cancer stemness and self-renewal,
  facilitating metastasis. Concurrent DKK1 promoter demethylation contributes to NK
  cell functional inactivation. b Aberrant activation of compensatory signaling pathways
  in DNPC. Following ADT pressure, FGF ligands activate FGFR receptors, triggering
  a phosphorylation cascade involving RAF, MEK, and ERK. This signaling axis converges
  with parallel pathways: JNK-mediated phosphorylation and activation of STAT transcription
  factors, alongside UCH-L1-dependent stabilization of mTORC1 signaling. Upregulated
  regulators amplify oncogenic signaling while suppressing androgen-responsive genes.
  This signaling landscape culminates in increased activity of MYC oncogenic targets,
  CDK4/6-RB-E2F mediated cell cycle progression, and Bcl-2 dependent suppression of
  apoptosis, collectively driving DNPC cell survival and proliferation independently
  of androgen receptor signaling. DNPC double-negative prostate cancer, ADT androgen
  deprivation therapy, TAMs Tumor-Associated Macrophages, MDSCs Myeloid-Derived Suppressor
  Cells'
