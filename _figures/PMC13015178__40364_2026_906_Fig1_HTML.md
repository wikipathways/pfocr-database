caption: 'Subcellular localization of cGAS. A. cytosolic cGAS primarily originates
  from two sources: de novo synthesis through ribosomal translation and nuclear export
  via the NES of cGAS. The amino acids 1–212 in the N-terminal domain of cGAS are
  essential for its retention in the cytosol, while the phosphorylation of tyrosine
  215 enhances cytosolic retention by blocking nuclear import. B. cGAS enters the
  nucleus through two pathways: passive diffusion during NE breakdown and active transport
  via the NLS located in both the C- and N-terminal domains of cGAS. Within the nucleus,
  cGAS binds to the AP of the H2A-H2B dimer in nucleosomes, forming a cGAS-nucleosome
  2:2 complex. C. cGAS is anchored to the plasma membrane through electrostatic interactions
  between the negatively charged phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PI(4,5)P₂)
  and the highly positively charged N-terminal domain of cGAS (PDB ID: 4LEV). D. proteolytic
  processing or structural changes in cGAS can remove the N-terminal domain (amino
  acids 1–160) that masks the mitochondrial targeting signal (MTS). This removal leads
  to mitochondrial localization and activation of cGAS. aa: amino acid; AP: acidic
  patch; ER: endoplasmic reticulum; MTS: mitochondrial targeting signal; NES: nuclear
  export signal; NLS: nuclear localization signal; PI(4,5)P2: phosphatidylinositol
  4,5-bisphosphate. The figure was created with BioRender.com'
