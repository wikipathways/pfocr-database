caption: 'Mitochondrial dysfunction in aortic disease pathogenesis. This schematic
  highlights mitochondrial metabolic alterations implicated in the development of
  aortic aneurysm and dissection (AAD). (a) TCA cycle metabolic reprogramming. This
  panel illustrates disruptions in the TCA cycle contributing to AAD progression,
  integrating mechanisms from three key studies. Pathways are color-coded for clarity:
  Blue represents the NR1D1-ACO2 axis, in which the nuclear receptor NR1D1 targets
  mitochondrial ACO2 to regulate TCA cycle flux and abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA)
  development. Red highlights succinate accumulation as a biomarker and therapeutic
  target that drives AAD’s inflammation, oxidative stress, and extracellular matrix
  (ECM) degradation. Orange depicts ANK deficiency-mediated cytosolic citrate buildup,
  which promotes histone acetylation, CCL2/IL-6 expression, and MMP activation, exacerbating
  aortic aneurysm formation. (b) Electron transport chain (ETC) dysfunction. This
  panel depicts mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) impairments in aortic
  cells, drawing from three seminal studies on aortic disease. Downregulation of nicotinamide
  phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT) leads to NAD+ depletion, impairing OXPHOS in vascular
  smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), promoting cell senescence, DNA damage, and synthetic
  phenotype switching. NAMPT-enriched extracellular vesicles from mesenchymal stem
  cells restore NAMPT levels and mitigate AAA. CD36 signaling in macrophages, triggered
  by oxidized LDL, shifts mitochondrial metabolism from OXPHOS to superoxide production,
  upregulating fatty acid uptake, downregulating fatty acid oxidation (FAO), and activating
  NFκB for inflammatory cytokine release. TNF signaling dampens OXPHOS by enhancing
  AP-1 transcriptional activity, which drives VSMC degenerative programs. CoQ10 or
  AP-1 inhibitors rescue OXPHOS and improve survival. (c) Dysregulated lipid metabolism
  and mitochondrial injury. This panel summarizes dysregulated lipid pathways in vascular
  cells during AAA and atherosclerosis. PKM2 in T lymphocytes drives EV release enriched
  in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and phospholipids, inducing macrophage iron
  accumulation, lipid peroxidation, migration, and AAA. Triglycerides inhibit lysyl
  oxidase (LOX) maturation and reduce LOX activity, and hypertriglyceridemia is a
  key contributor to AAA pathogenesis. Platelet ACER1 deficiency elevates ceramides,
  activating p38 MAPK to promote pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion; ACER1-mediated
  ceramide catabolism mitigates inflammation and AAA. Long-chain ceramides act as
  endogenous ligands for the G protein-coupled receptors CYSLTR2 and P2RY6 on endothelial
  cells and macrophages. This direct receptor activation triggers Gq signaling and
  NLRP3 inflammasome activation, aggravating plaque inflammation independently of
  cholesterol levels. Endothelial soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) upregulation metabolizes
  PUFAs to diols, disrupting mitochondrial protein-cholesterol associations, inhibiting
  complex I, elevating ROS, reducing membrane potential, activating TGFβ, and promoting
  atherosclerosis progression. This figure was created with Biorender.com'
