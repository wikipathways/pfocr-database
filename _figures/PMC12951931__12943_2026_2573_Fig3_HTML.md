caption: The mechanisms of lactylation modification. Lactylation arises through both
  enzymatic and non-enzymatic mechanisms. The enzymatic pathway utilizes lactyl-CoA
  as a substrate and is catalyzed by transferases such as P300, AARS1, and AARS2,
  occurring across multiple cellular compartments including the nucleus, cytoplasm,
  and mitochondrial matrix. In contrast, the non-enzymatic process employs LGSH as
  the lactyl donor and predominantly takes place in the cytoplasm. These modifications
  are tightly linked to glycolytic and metabolic fluxes involving key enzymes such
  as HK (hexokinase), GPI (glucose-6-phosphate isomerase), PFK-1 (phosphofructokinase-1),
  TPI (triose phosphate isomerase), GAPDH (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase),
  PGK (phosphoglycerate kinase), PGM (phosphoglycerate mutase), PK (pyruvate kinase),
  LDH (lactate dehydrogenase), and ACSS2 (acetyl-CoA synthetase 2). Associated metabolic
  mediators include GLO1 (glyoxalase 1), ATP, GSH, and MGO (methylglyoxal), as well
  as additional acyltransferases such as CBP and KAT8 that may participate in lysine
  lactylation under specific metabolic contexts
