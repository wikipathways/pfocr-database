caption: 'Overall strategy of the heterotrophic CO2 fixation and its application in
  itaconate production. (A) Schematic diagram of heterotrophic CO2 fixation. In the
  wild-type strain, CO2 was inevitably released from the conversion of pyruvate to
  acetyl-CoA and other decarboxylation reactions. In engineered strains, CO2 is actively
  incorporated and utilized to produce various biochemicals, including itaconate.
  (B) Net addition of 1 mol CO2 to 1 mol citrate by heterotrophic CO2 fixation increases
  the theoretical maximum carbon yield (TMCY) of TCA-derived chemicals, including
  itaconate. (C) Rewired pathway for heterotrophic CO2 fixation. The overexpression
  of target genes for active CO2 fixation is shown in red. Genes that were knocked
  out or downregulated for higher performance are colored in green and blue, respectively.
  Abbreviation: OAA, oxaloacetate; PEP, phosphoenolpyruvate; PYR, pyruvate; AcCoA,
  acetyl-CoA; CIT, citrate; ACO, cis-aconitate; ISO, isocitrate; αKG, α-ketoglutarate;

  dehydratase; icd, isocitrate dehydrogenase; aceA, isocitrate lyase'
