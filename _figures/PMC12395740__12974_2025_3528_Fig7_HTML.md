caption: Schematic model of circulatory EVs transport complement C1q for promoting
  neuronal Aβ42 production in AD. During the progression of AD (in the APP/PS1 mouse
  model), circulatory extracellular vesicles (APPEVs) can cross the blood-brain barrier
  (BBB) without disrupting its tight junctions, allowing them to be delivered into
  the brain. APPEVs transport complement protein C1q to neurons, activating the neuronal
  JAK2-STAT1 signaling pathway, which leads to the upregulation of BACE1 expression.
  Subsequently, BACE1 cleaves APP within lipid rafts, increasing Aβ42 production and
  promoting the aggregation of Aβ plaques in the brain, thereby accelerating the progression
  of AD
