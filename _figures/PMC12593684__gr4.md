caption: HIV Replication cycle and viral assembly pathway associated with exosomes
  release. HIV binding (1) and fusion (2) with the host cell membrane, (3) Post reverse
  transcription, the HIV-1 genome is integrated into the genomic DNA (proviral DNA)
  (4), where it is subsequently transcribed into viral RNA (5); (6) The viral mRNA
  is then translated using the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) or cytoplasmic ribosomes
  to synthesize the viral proteins. (7) The newly produced viral proteins undergo
  post-translational modifications (PTMs) in the trans-Golgi network (TGN) before
  being packaged into multivesicular bodies MVBs. (8) The endosomes taken up by the
  cells during endocytosis can be converted into multivesicular bodies (MVBs) via
  endosomal sorting complex required for transport (ESCRT)-independent or ESCRT-dependent
  mechanisms. (9) In the final stage, MVBs are merged with the plasma membrane, releasing
  exosomes into the extracellular milieu. This fusion is facilitated by Rab GTPases,
  which drive vesicle budding and docking, and by SNARE proteins, which are involved
  in Ca2+-membrane-regulated fusion. (10) Exosomes secreted from HIV-infected cells
  had been found to contain chemokine receptors, CCR5 and CXCR4, that were delivered
  to recipient cells to facilitate HIV establishment and spreading. Created in BioRender.
