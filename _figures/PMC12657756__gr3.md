caption: 'Methionine (Met) metabolism. Methionine is involved in complex and significant
  metabolism in vivo. Upon ingestion by the organism, Met enters the cell and initiates
  the methionine cycle. Within this cycle, Met binds to SAM via the action of MAT
  and ATP. Methyltransferase subsequently transfers the methyl group to the methyl
  substrate, thereby converting SAM to SAH. Subsequently, SAH can be hydrolyzed by
  SAHH to yield homocysteine (Hcy). Homocysteine has two potential pathways: it can
  either enter the methionine regeneration pathway, utilizing 5-MTHF or betaine as
  methyl donors, and be reconverted to Met by MS or BHMT, thus completing the methionine
  cycle. Alternatively, Hcy can enter the transsulfuration pathway, where it is irreversibly
  converted to cysteine and other sulfur-containing compounds. Moreover, SAM, acting
  as a propylamine donor, can exit the methionine cycle and enter the polyamine synthesis
  pathway, where it is decarboxylated by SAMDC to generate spermidine and other polyamine
  substrates, as well as MTA, which is subsequently utilized to regenerate Met through
  the methionine salvage pathway. 3-MST = 3-mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase; 5-MTHF
  = 5-methyltetrahydrofolate; 5′-dA = 5′-deoxyadenosine radical; γ-GCS = γ-glutamylcysteine
  synthetase; ATP = adenosine triphosphate; BHMT = betaine homocysteine methyltransferase;
  CBS = cystathionine β-synthase; CGL = cystathionine γ-lyase; dcSAM = decarboxylated
  -adenosylmethionine; DMG = dimethylglycine; GSH = glutathione; GSS = glutathione
  synthetase; H2S = hydrogen sulfide; Hcy = homocysteine; MAT = methionine adenosyltransferase;
  MTAP = methylthioadenosine phosphorylase; MTs = methyltransferases; ODC = ornithine
  decarboxylase; SAH = s-adenosylhomocysteine; SAHH = s-adenosyl-l-homocysteine hydrolase;
  SAM = s-adenosylmethionine; SAMDC = s-adenosyl-l-methionine decarboxylase; SPD =
  spermidine; SPDS = spermidine synthase; SPMS = spermine synthase; MS = methionine
  synthase; MTA = 5′-methylthioadenosine; MTOB = 4-methylthio-2-oxobutapoate'
