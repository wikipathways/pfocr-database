caption: Schematic representation of mitochondrial and caspase cascade‐mediated tumor
  cell death in cancer photodynamic therapy. After the administration of the photosensitizer,
  the photosensitizer will preferentially co‐localize within various organelles of
  the cell. When irradiated at a specified wavelength, the administered photosensitizer
  will move from its ground state to the excited state. The interactions of the excited
  state of the photosensitizer with molecular oxygen via the Type II pathway will
  lead to the generation of cytotoxic singlet oxygen (1O2) molecules. Increased levels
  of 1O2 during PDT may lead to the activation of caspase‐mediated cancer cell death.
  Additionally, increased levels of 1O2 may alternatively lead to the expression of
  transformation‐related protein 53 (p53), which in turn prevents the formation of
  tumor cells by blocking the activities of anti‐apoptotic proteins (Bcl‐w, Bcl‐2,
  Bfl‐1, and Mcl‐1), thus promoting the initiation of apoptotic activities
