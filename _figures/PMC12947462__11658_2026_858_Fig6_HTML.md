caption: 'The role of LONP1 in neurological, hepatic, and renal diseases. A Neurological
  diseases: Under hypoxic or I/R conditions, compensatory upregulation of LONP1 expression
  inhibits cell death by reducing oxidative stress. In the early stage of I/R, baicalin
  exerts neuroprotective effects by synergistically regulating the LONP1-HIF-1α signaling
  axis: on the one hand, it inhibits the abnormal activation of PDK2 mediated by HIF-1α,
  and on the other hand, it promotes the degradation of PDK2 by LONP1, thereby restoring
  PDH activity and enhancing ATP generation. Additionally, tDCS releases PTEN’s transcriptional
  inhibition of LONP1 through the MCT1/PPA/PTEN axis, ultimately enhancing the neuroprotective
  effect mediated by LONP1. TRPC6-mediated activation of ERK1/2 enhances the resistance
  of dentate granule cells to status epilepticus by promoting LONP1 expression. Furthermore,
  LONP1 exerts neuroprotective effects by clearing caspase-3, HMGB1, and TDP-43. Metformin
  and SRT1720 exert neuroprotective effects under high glucose stress by upregulating
  LONP1 expression. Aβ42 interferes with the function of LONP1 through a dual pathway,
  leading to neuronal death in AD: it negatively regulates LONP1 transcriptional expression
  by activating m6A RNA methylation modification mediated by METTL3; concurrently,
  its direct binding to LONP1 inhibits its protease activity. ATF5 activates the UPRmt
  by regulating LONP1 to prevent apoptosis induced by Pb. B Hepatic diseases: APAP
  significantly downregulates LONP1 expression, leading to leakage of HSP60 from mitochondria
  and inducing inflammatory damage to liver cells. CGA reduces the hepatotoxicity
  of APAP by rescuing LONP1 expression. Additionally, LONP1 participates in the degradation
  of TDP-43 during APAP and CCL4-induced liver injury, reducing the cytotoxicity caused
  by abnormal accumulation of TDP-43. Overexpression of LONP1 alleviates hepatic gluconeogenesis
  by restoring the expression and activity of G6Pase and PCK1. The downregulation
  of LONP1 expression leads to an increase in ROS, subsequently inducing phosphorylation
  of JNK and p38 MAPK, ultimately resulting in increased gluconeogenesis and insulin
  resistance. C Renal diseases: Aging reduces the levels of METTL3 and IGF2BP2, leading
  to decreased stability and accelerated degradation of LONP1, resulting in proximal
  tubular cell death. TRPC6 inhibits the expression of LONP1 by activating ERK1/2,
  thereby promoting podocyte death in MCD. Additionally, OTA impairs Aco2 and HSP75
  function by suppressing LONP1 expression, ultimately culminating in ROS-dependent
  cell. Exogenous H2S exerts therapeutic effects on DKD by inhibiting the degradation
  of SOD2 mediated by LONP1, alleviating oxidative stress-induced cell apoptosis.
  Furthermore, 84-B10 promotes LONP1 expression and degradation of HMGCS2, thereby
  improving mitochondrial function and staying CKD progression. LONP1, Lon protease
  1; I/R, ischemia/reperfusion; HIF, hypoxia-inducible factor; PDK, pyruvate dehydrogenase
  kinase; PDH, pyruvate dehydrogenase; tDCS, transcranial direct current stimulation;
  MCT1, monocarboxylate transporter 1; PPA, propionate; PTEN, phosphatase and tensin
  homolog; TRPC6, transient receptor potential cation channel 6; ERK, extracellular
  signal-regulated protein kinase; HMGB1, high-mobility group box 1; TDP-43, TAR DNA-binding

  dismutase; HMGCS2, 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA synthase 2; CKD, chronic kidney
  disease'
