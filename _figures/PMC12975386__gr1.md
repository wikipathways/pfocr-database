caption: 'Canonical and noncanonical STING signaling. A: Schematic structure of human
  STING. B: Canonical STING signaling pathway in immune response. Cytosolic dsDNA
  from various sources, including foreign DNA from virus, retrovirus, bacteria, and
  self-DNA, can be recognized directly by cGAS that generates cGAMP. CDNs secreted
  by bacteria can directly and strongly activate STING. After activation, STING undergoes
  a conformational change and oligomerization before translocating to the Golgi via
  the ERGIC. In the TGN, STING recruits and activates TBK1, which in turn autophosphorylates
  itself and phosphorylates IRF3, leading to IRF3 dimerization and nuclear translocation
  to initiate transcription of IFNs and ISGs. Meanwhile, activated STING recruits
  IKK, which simultaneously phosphorylates IκB, inducing IκB degradation and NF-κB
  nuclear translocation-induced gene expression of proinflammatory cytokines. C: STING
  in autophagy induction. After activation, STING undergoes translocation to the ERGIC
  and triggers autophagy, a process reliant on ATG5 and WIPI2. The ERGIC functions
  as a membrane reservoir for the lipidation of LC3. STING can also induce V-ATPase-ATG16L1–mediated
  noncanonical autophagy. In the presence of Listeria innocua infection, ER-phagy
  is stimulated in a manner dependent on STING. D: STING in organelle calcium regulation.
  STIM1 binds to STING, anchoring it within the ER membrane. Upon activation, STING
  modulates activity of calcium channels, including IP3R and TRPV2. Additionally,
  STING binds with VDAC2 to hinder VDAC2-mediated mitochondrial calcium transfer.
  E: STING in the ER and Golgi. ER-localized STING interacts with and activates PERK
  in the ER. PERK then phosphorylates eIF2α, initiating a selective mRNA translation
  program to modulate fibrotic response. STING also functions as a proton pump in
  Golgi and post-Golgi STING vesicles. ATG, autophagy-related protein; ATG16L1, ATG16-like
  protein 1; CDNs, cyclic dinucleotides; cGAMP, 2′,3′-cyclic GMP-AMP; cGAS, cyclic
  GMP-AMP synthase; eIF, eukaryotic initiation factor; ER, endoplasmic reticulum;
  ERGIC, ER-Golgi intermediate compartment; IFN, interferon; IκB, inhibitor of NF-κB;
  IKK, IκB kinase; IP3R, inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor; IRF, interferon regulatory
  factor; ISGs, IFN-stimulated genes; LC3, microtubule-associated protein 1 light
  chain 3; mTOR, mechanistic target of rapamycin; ORAI, calcium release–activated
  calcium channel protein; PERK, protein kinase R–like ER kinase; STIM, stromal interaction
  molecule; STING, stimulator of interferon genes; TBK, tumor necrosis factor receptor–associated
  factor family member–associated NF-κB activator binding kinase; TGN, trans-Golgi
  network; TM, transmembrane; TRPV, transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily
  V member; V-ATPase, vacuolar-type ATPase; VDAC, voltage-dependent anion-selective
  channel protein; WIPI, WD repeat domain phosphoinositide-interacting protein'
