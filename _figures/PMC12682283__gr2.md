caption: Regulatory mechanisms of intratumoral microbiota in gynecologic malignanciesIntratumoral
  microorganisms can promote the secretion of specific transcription factors such
  as NANOG, OCT4, and SOX2 and activate the WNT/β-catenin and IGF-1 receptor pathways,
  thereby inducing cervical cancer cell stemness and promoting cervical cancer invasion
  and metastasis. Microorganisms in the tumor cause the release of inflammatory factors
  such as IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6, triggering an inflammatory response, activating
  the Hedgehog signaling pathway, and promoting the occurrence of ovarian cancer.
  Intratumoral microorganisms stimulate the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines
  and chemokines and reduce the secretion of H2O2 and lactic acid, leading to the
  destruction of the epithelial barrier and promoting microbial invasion. Intratumoral
  microorganisms activate the NF-κB signaling pathway and promote the occurrence and
  development of gynecological tumors. Intratumoral microorganisms promote immune
  escape by upregulating PD-L1 and inhibiting STING pathway
