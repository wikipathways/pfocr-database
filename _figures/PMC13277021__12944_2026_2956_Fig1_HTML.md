caption: Lipid metabolic reprogramming and its interaction with oncogenic signaling
  in cancer. This schematic diagram illustrates the reprogramming of lipid metabolism
  in cancer cells and its interactions with oncogenic signaling pathways. Extracellular
  fatty acids (FFAs) and oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL) are taken up by
  transporters such as FATP, CD36, FABP, and LDLR and converted into acyl-CoA for
  β-oxidation or storage in lipid droplets. De novo lipid synthesis is driven by ACLY,
  ACC, and FASN, whereas cholesterol synthesis occurs via the HMGCR-mediated mevalonate
  pathway. These processes are regulated primarily by SREBP-1 and SREBP-2. Oncogenic
  pathways, including the KRAS-PI3K-AKT-mTOR and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathways,
  promote lipid synthesis and metabolic adaptation, whereas AMPK inhibits lipidogenesis
  by targeting ACC. Furthermore, NF-κB synergistically enhances the expression of
  lipid metabolism-related genes with SREBP. These pathways collectively constitute
  a metabolic signaling network that supports tumor growth and progression
