caption: 'Molecular mechanism of pyroptosis and its inhibitors. In the canonical pathway,
  stress signals such as PAMPs and DAMPs trigger the assembly of the NLRP3 inflammasome,
  which recruits and activates caspase-1. Activated caspase-1 proteolytically cleaves
  GSDMD to release GSDMD-NT and simultaneously processes pro-inflammatory cytokines
  (pro-IL-1β/IL-18) into their mature forms. In the non-canonical pathway, intracellular
  LPS from Gram-negative bacteria directly binds to and activates human caspase-4/5
  (Caspase-11 in mice). These caspases also cleave GSDMD to liberate the GSDMD-NT.
  In both pathways, the liberated GSDMD-NT oligomerizes and inserts into the plasma
  membrane, forming transmembrane pores. These pores trigger a rapid K⁺ efflux, leading
  to cell swelling and eventual lysis (pyroptosis), and facilitate the extracellular
  release of mature cytokines and other DAMPs. Pyroptosis inhibitors include DMF,
  disulfiram, NSA, and LDC7559. Abbreviation: DAMPs: Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns,
  GSDMB: Gasdermin B, GSDMD: Gasdermin D, GSDMD-CT: Gasdermin D C-Terminal domain,
  GSDMD-NT: Gasdermin D N-Terminal domain, IL-1β: Interleukin 1 beta, IL-18: Interleukin
  18, NLRP3: NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3, NSA: Necrosulfonamide,
  PAMPS: Pathogen-associated molecular patterns'
