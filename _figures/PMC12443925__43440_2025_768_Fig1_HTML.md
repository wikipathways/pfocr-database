caption: 'A) Selected Metabolic Targets in Cancer: Glycolytic Enzymes, Lactate Handling,
  and Mitochondrial Shuttles. Summary of selected metabolic targets in cancer, including
  glycolytic enzymes (HK2, PKM2, PFKFB3/4), reducing equivalents shuttles (MAS, G3PS),
  lactate metabolism, NAD⁺ regeneration, and NADPH-producing enzymes (ME1, G6PD, IDH).
  The figure highlights the metabolic reprogramming supporting tumor growth and survival.
  HK2 – hexokinase 2, PKM – muscle pyruvate kinase M (PKM2), MAS – malate–aspartate
  shuttle, G3PS – glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle, NAD⁺ – nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide
  (oxidized form), NADH – nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (reduced form), NADP⁺
  – nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (oxidized form), NADPH – nicotinamide
  adenine dinucleotide phosphate (reduced form), PFKFB3–6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase
  3, PFKFB4–6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase 4, LDHA – lactate
  dehydrogenase A, LDHB – lactate dehydrogenase B, MCT1 – monocarboxylate transporter
  1, MCT4 – monocarboxylate transporter 4, PPP – pentose phosphate pathway. B) De
  novo, Preiss–Handler, and salvage pathways of NAD⁺ biosynthesis. Schematic representation
  of key enzymatic reactions involved in NAD⁺ production from tryptophan, nicotinic
  acid, and nicotinamide, highlighting the roles of NAPRT, NAMPT, and NMNAT in maintaining
  NAD⁺ levels essential for tumor growth. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD),
  Nicotinic acid adenine dinucleotide (NAAD) Quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase
  (QPRT), Nicotinic acid phosphoribosyltransferase (NAPRT), Nicotinic acid mononucleotide
  (NAMN), Nicotinamide mononucleotide adenylyltransferase (NMNAT), Nicotinamide phoribosyltransferase
  (NAMPT), Nicotinamide riboside kinase (NRK), NAD synthetase (NADSYN). C) Overloading
  of shuttles triggers lactate production in highly proliferative cells. Saturation
  of the malate–aspartate shuttle (MAS) and glycerol–3–phosphate shuttle (G3PS) limits
  NAD⁺ regeneration in proliferating cancer cells. When these pathways are overloaded
  due to high glycolytic and biosynthetic activity, excess NADH is diverted to lactate
  production via LDH, reinforcing the Warburg effect. Lactate dehydogenase (LDH),
  Glyceraldehyde 3–phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), Malate–aspartate shuttle (MAS),
  Glycerol–3–phosphate shuttle (G3PS)'
