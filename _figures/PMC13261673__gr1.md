caption: 'An integrated signaling network for hypoxia perception and hormonal activation
  under waterlogging or hypoxic stress.(1) Oxygen sensing and transcriptional reprogramming:
  declining oxygen levels cause Ca2+ influx and inhibit the N-end rule pathway mediated
  by PCOs and PRT6, leading to the stabilization of ERF-VIIs (e.g., RAP2.12), which
  directly initiates the transcription of HRGs (e.g., ADH and PDC) and metabolic reprogramming.(2)
  Hormonal hub formation: hypoxia impedes gas diffusion, leading to the accumulation
  of ethylene. Ethylene signaling via the EIN2–EIN3/EIL module further amplifies the
  expression of selected ERF-VIIs and HRGs.(3) Signal amplification and execution:
  ethylene enhances tissue sensitivity to auxin (IAA) and modulates its redistribution,
  which is crucial for morphological adaptation. It also activates the RBOH/NOX complex
  to amplify ROS production and upregulates PHGB1 to scavenge NO, thereby limiting
  NO-mediated ERF-VII destabilization through protein S-nitrosylation. This integrated
  network ensures rapid signal transduction and coordinates downstream physiological
  and anatomical responses.ADH, alcohol dehydrogenase; Ca2+, calcium; EIN2–EIN3/EIL,
  ethylene-insensitive 2–ethylene-insensitive 3/EIN3-like; ERF-VII, group VII ethylene
  response factor; HRG, hypoxia-responsive gene; PCO, plant cysteine oxidase; PDC,
  pyruvate decarboxylase; PHGB1, phytoglobin 1; PRT6, proteolysis 6; RBOH, respiratory
  burst oxidase homolog; and ROS, reactive oxygen species'
