caption: The level of stabilization and/or activation of p53 and the impact of phosphorylation
  are drug-dependent. (A) Doxorubicin induces DNA double strand breaks, resulting
  in activation of DNA damage response pathways, which includes ATM-dependent phosphorylation
  of p53, MDM2, and MDM4 and reduction in the levels of the latter protein due to
  degradation. The entire process results in robust p53 upregulation that transactivates
  genes inducing cell cycle arrest, feedback inhibition, programmed cell death (e.g.,
  apoptosis), and adaptive immune response, which may possibly trigger immunogenic
  cell death (ICD). The robust increases in p21 also contribute to cell death by facilitating
  p53-dependent transrepression of anti-apoptotic genes. (B) MDM2 inhibitors, such
  as nutlin-3a, stabilize and partially activate p53, which modestly induces p21 for
  cell cycle arrest and MDM2 to increase the pool for feedback inhibition; there is
  general absence of phosphorylation of p53 and endogenous inhibitors, p53-dependent
  apoptosis, and adaptive immune response at the physiological drug concentrations
