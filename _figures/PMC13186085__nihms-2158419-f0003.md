caption: (A) Ketone body production and utilisation decrease the brain’s reliance
  on glucose as an energy source. During low carbohydrate intake, hepatic β-oxidation
  of fatty acids generates excess acetyl coenzyme A, which is converted into ketones.
  Pharmacological targeting of the glycolytic pathway (glycolysis inhibitors) with
  2-deoxy-D-glucose shows similar antiseizure and antiepileptogenic effects. Likewise,
  the glycolytic intermediate fructose 1, 6-bisphosphate suppresses glycolysis and
  diverts glucose metabolism towards alternative pathways. Anaplerotic substrates
  replenish TCA cycle intermediates, counteracting seizure-induced energy deficits,
  helping preserve the metabolic conditions that favour GABA synthesis under energy-challenged
  states. (B) Schematic representation of the mitochondrial electron transport chain
  depicting respiratory complexes I-IV and ATP synthase. Ketogenic diet therapies
  improve mitochondrial electron transport chain efficiency, increasing ATP production
  and lowering oxidative stress by decreasing mitochondrial electron leak and the
  generation of ROS (eg, superoxide). They further enhance redox balance through Nrf2-dependent
  glutathione synthesis. Nrf2 activation induces antioxidant response genes, including
  glutamate cysteine ligase, the rate-limiting enzyme in glutathione biosynthesis.
  Elevated glutathione inhibits mTOR signalling, a central regulator of energy metabolism,
  cellular proliferation, and inflammation, stabilising the blood–brain barrier, and
  contributing to antiepileptogenic effects. Dashed lines represent hypothesised effects,
  while solid arrows depict more established effects. Together, these mechanisms enhance
  inhibitory neurotransmission, support tricarboxylic acid cycle activity, promote
  mitochondrial ATP generation, decrease neuroinflammation, and minimise oxidative
  damage to lipids, nuclear and mitochondrial DNA, and proteins. CoQ=coenzyme Q. Cyt
  c=cytochrome c. ETC=electron transport chain. FAD=flavin adenine dinucleotide. GCL=glutamate
  cysteine ligase. GPX=glutathione peroxidase. GR=glutathione reductase. mTOR=mammalian
  target of rapamycin. NAD=nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. ROS=reactive oxygen
  species. SOD=superoxide dismutase. TCA=tricarboxylic acid. Figure created with BioRender.com
