caption: Paradoxical effects of ferroptosis in chemotherapy resistance. Ferroptosis
  plays a dual role in tumor chemotherapy, acting as both a contributor to drug resistance
  and a potential strategy to enhance therapeutic efficacy. On one hand, dysregulation
  of ferroptosis pathways can lead to chemoresistance. In lung cancer, disturbances
  in ferroptosis signaling within TME contribute to cisplatin resistance. Similarly,
  in ovarian cancer, the mitochondrial chaperone protein DNAJC15 regulates sensitivity
  to cisplatin. In osteosarcoma, the linear deubiquitinase OTULIN prevents the proteasomal
  degradation of GPX4, thereby facilitating cisplatin resistance. In CRC, inhibiting
  GPX4 has been shown to help overcome OXA resistance. The transcription factor Nrf2,
  when upregulated, promotes the expression of antioxidant genes and increases GPX4
  levels, while its inhibition reduces GPX4 expression and may restore drug sensitivity.
  Moreover, gut microbiota components such as Fusobacterium nucleatum and Clostridium
  have been implicated in mediating OXA resistance in CRC. (Right panel) On the other
  hand, ferroptosis can be leveraged to enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy. The
  ferroptosis inducer erastin, by inhibiting xCT, increases the sensitivity of cancer
  cells to anti–PD-L1 treatment and enhances the cytotoxicity of chemotherapeutic
  agents such as doxorubicin and topotecan, particularly in ovarian cancer cells that
  express P-gp and BCRP. Sorafenib induces ferroptosis by downregulating SLC7A11,
  thereby depleting intracellular GSH and promoting oxidative stress. Ferroptosis
  can also be triggered by mitochondrial dysfunction. In TNBC, co-treatment with CNSI-Fe
  reduces GPX4 levels, leading to ferroptotic cell death. Furthermore, the natural
  triterpenoid compound AA induces ferroptosis by accumulating GPX4 and FTH1 in tumor
  cells
