caption: In the skeletal system, bitter receptor agonists activate TAS2Rs expressed
  in osteoblasts, triggering calcium signaling that causes CaM to bind with Ca²⁺,
  which then activates calcineurin (Cn). Cn binds to and dephosphorylates the nuclear
  factor of activated T cells (NFAT) transcription factor. NFAT then translocates
  to the nucleus to regulate gene expression. Additionally, bitter receptors, through
  extracellular calcium stimulation, inhibit the activity of pro-inflammatory factors
  and regulate the activity of immune cells (such as leukocytes, mast cells, and macrophages).
  In bone marrow (BM)-derived macrophages, AHL-12 may utilize TAS2R38 to attract phagocytes
  to biofilm formation sites, clearing planktonic bacteria, preventing the spread
  of infection, and participating in early immune responses. Aebutin activates bitter
  receptors expressed in bone marrow-derived neutrophils, regulating neutrophil migration
  through TAS2R126/143-ROCK-MLC2 dependent signaling pathways
