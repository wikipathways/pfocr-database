caption: Schematic representation of the leptin receptor-mediated signal transduction.
  Leptin binding to the long isoform of the leptin receptor induces receptor-associated
  phosphorylation and activation of Janus kinase 2 (JAK2). Activated JAK2 subsequently
  phosphorylates signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), leading
  to STAT3 dimerization, nuclear translocation, and transcriptional regulation of
  genes involved in tumor progression, cell survival, proliferation, migration, and
  invasion. In addition to the canonical JAK2/STAT3 pathway, leptin signaling activates
  multiple downstream oncogenic and metabolic signaling cascades, including Ras/Raf/ERK1/2,
  NF-κB, activator protein-1 (AP-1), MAPK, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, GSK3, and AMPK-associated
  pathways. These pathways contribute to metabolic reprogramming, enhanced cell growth,
  inflammatory signaling, and cancer progression through the induction of transcription
  factors such as FOS, JUN, and JUNB. Leptin-mediated signaling also modulates metabolic
  regulators, including AMPK and acetyl-CoA carboxylase 1 (ACC1), thereby influencing
  lipid metabolism and cellular energy homeostasis. Suppressor of cytokine signaling
  3 (SOCS3) and protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B) serve as negative regulators
  of leptin signaling by inhibiting JAK2 phosphorylation and attenuating downstream
  pathway activation
