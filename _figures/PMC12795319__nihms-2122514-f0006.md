caption: Mechanisms of Asbestos Carcinogenesis. Asbestos exposure induces reactive
  oxygen species (ROS) production, leading to DNA damage and necrosis. Necrotic mesothelial
  cells release high mobility group box 1 protein (HMGB1) into the extracellular space,
  where it acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP). HMGB1 binds to its
  receptors on macrophages and other immune cells, activating the NLRP3 inflammasome
  and inducing the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6,
  and TGF-β, thereby establishing a chronic inflammatory microenvironment. In parallel,
  HMGB1 engages the receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) on surviving
  mesothelial cells, activating the mTOR–ULK1–Beclin1 signaling cascade to promote
  autophagy. HMGB1 also directly interacts with Beclin1 in the cytoplasm to induce
  autophagy, supporting mesothelial cell survival under stress conditions. TNF-α,
  acting through TNF-R1, stimulates the NF-κB pathway, further sustaining cell survival.
  The combination of persistent ROS production, chronic inflammation, and enhanced
  pro-survival signaling promotes mesothelial cell hyperplasia and malignant transformation
