caption: Copper ions transport and function in cells. Copper ions are transported
  into the cell via the membrane channel protein CTR1 after reduction by steap4 at
  the cell membrane. Within the cell, they are transported out of the membrane via
  vesicles or ATP7B. Some intracellular copper ions are transported toward the Golgi
  for storage via ATP7A/B. Most of the copper ions then function in the cytoplasm
  in combination with metabolic enzymes. Copper ions in mitochondria are involved
  in the synthesis of DLAT and the structural stability of iron–sulfur proteins, which
  in turn affects energy metabolism. ATOX1 carries copper ions to the nucleus and
  is involved in the activation of various signalling pathways
