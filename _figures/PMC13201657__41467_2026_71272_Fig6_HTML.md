caption: Left, Structural basis of the 4:4 assembly. The active 2:2 Lac1-Lip1 complex
  (top) can further dimerize via a specific interface involving the TM8 helices of
  the Lac1-B/B’ subunits (bottom). In this 4:4 structure, the twisted conformation
  of TM8 physically occludes the acyl-CoA binding pocket (red blockade), suggesting
  a potential autoinhibitory mechanism. Right, Complex in vivo functional outcomes.
  Under conditions of complex sphingolipid depletion (AbA treatment), Lac1WT cells
  (top) maintain an equilibrium between 2:2 and 4:4 assemblies and produce ceramides
  at regulated levels, yet exhibit growth defects. In contrast, cells harboring the
  Lac1L379A/L383A/I387A interface mutation (bottom) fail to form the 4:4 complex,
  accumulate lower levels of ceramides under the same stress, yet show a partial growth
  rescue. This indicates that the oligomerization interface is not a simple off-switch
  but is essential for the proper regulation of ceramide accumulation and cellular
  adaptation to sphingolipid pathway stress
