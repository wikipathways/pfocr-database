caption: Activated platelets synthesize and release TXA2, which binds to its receptor
  (TP) on T cells. This binding activates ARHGEF1, a guanine exchange factor that
  converts inactive GDP-bound Ras homolog family member A (RhoA) into its active GTP-bound
  form. The activation of RhoA inhibits T-cell receptor-driven kinase pathways, T-cell
  proliferation, and effector functions, ultimately suppressing antimetastatic immunity.
  The production of TXA2 by platelets depends on COX-1, which aspirin can inhibit.
  As a result, aspirin effectively releases T cells from TXA2-mediated suppression.
