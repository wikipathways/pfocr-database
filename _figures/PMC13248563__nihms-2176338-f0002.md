caption: 'Morphology of bipolar cell types and rod signaling pathways. (A) The primate
  retina exhibits 12 types of bipolar cells. Six types of diffuse bipolar cells (DB1-6),
  flat and invaginating midget bipolar cells (FMB & IMB), a blue bipolar cell (BB),
  and a giant bipolar cell (GB) are shown. The figure is adapted with permission from
  Tsukamoto and Omi (2016). (B) Representative bipolar cells in mice: type 3 (left)
  and type 5 (right). (C) Mouse retina contains 15 types of bipolar cells (BC): BC1
  through BC4 are OFF BCs (orange), BC5 through BC9 are ON BCs (purple), and a rod
  bipolar cell (RBC, blue). Adapted with permission from Shekhar et al (2016). (D)
  A schematic showing rod signaling pathways: (D-1) Primary rod-signaling pathway.
  Rod signals travel through rod bipolar cells (RBC), their axon terminals, and reach
  AII amacrine cells. AII cells transmit the signal through Gap junctions (yellow
  star) to the ON cone bipolar cells (ON CBC) (indicated by red arrows). Simultaneously,
  AIIs provide chemical synaptic input to OFF cone bipolar cells (OFF CBC) (indicated
  by black arrows), which then relay the signal to ganglion cells (GC). (D-2) Secondary
  rod-signaling pathway. Inputs from rods are transmitted directly to cones through
  Gap junctions (rod-cone coupling, yellow stars). Cones mediate rod signals to ON
  (red arrows) and OFF (cyan arrows) CBC, which subsequently transmit the signal to
  GC. (D-3) Tertiary rod-signaling pathway. Rod signals are transmitted directly to
  OFF CBC, which in turn relays the signal to GC (cyan arrows)'
