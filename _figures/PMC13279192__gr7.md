caption: Schematic representation of the proposed mechanism for the mast cell stabilizing
  effects of IFN-α. IFN-α induces transcriptional suppression of the CYP51A1 gene
  in mast cells. This attenuates the conversion of lanosterol to downstream intermediates
  in the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway. Consequently, lanosterol accumulates, triggering
  both SREBP-mediated transcriptional suppression and ERAD-mediated degradation of
  HMGCR. This HMGCR downregulation leads to the blockade of the MVA pathway, which
  is responsible for the synthesis of FPP and GGPP required for prenylation of various
  proteins and small biomolecules. The shortage of these nonsterol isoprenoids results
  in the loss of cortical actin dynamics via a mechanism that warrant further investigation.
  This lack of dynamic actin rearrangement obstructs recruitment of PLC-γ1 to LAT,
  thereby preventing the assembly of multiprotein signaling complex essential for
  PLC-γ1 activation and the subsequent extracellular influx of Ca2+. ERAD, the endoplasmic
