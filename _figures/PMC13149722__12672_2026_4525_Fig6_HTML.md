caption: Polyphenols are extensively metabolized in the liver, while gut microbial
  communities further convert them into bioactive metabolites that reach the liver
  via the enterohepatic circulation. These compounds and their metabolites exert protective
  effects by modulating key pathological processes, including oxidative stress, lipid
  peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, inflammation,
  and immune responses. Polyphenols also influence gut barrier integrity, microbiome
  composition, and regulatory T cell (Treg) activity, thereby intervening in critical
  pathways such as adipocyte dysfunction, insulin resistance, hepatic steatosis, fibrosis,
  and hepatocellular injury. Collectively, these mechanisms underline the potential
  of polyphenols to disrupt the progression from metabolic imbalance to NASH and advanced
  liver disease
