caption: After ischemic stroke, the microbiota-gut-brain axis (MGBA) establishes bidirectional
  communication through neural, immune, and metabolic pathways. In the neural pathway,
  cerebral ischemia activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the
  vagus nerve, leading to intestinal barrier disruption and dysbiosis. Conversely,
  the gut microbiota influences brain function recovery through enteric nerve and
  vagus nerve signaling. Regarding the immune pathway, brain injury triggers systemic
  inflammation, with gut immune cells, such as T cells, migrating to the brain and
  exacerbating neuroinflammation. Meanwhile, metabolites produced by the gut microbiota
  aggravate brain damage by activating microglia. Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)
  produced by the gut microbiota exert neuroprotective effects by crossing the blood-brain
  barrier; however, harmful metabolites promote stroke progression. Figure generated
