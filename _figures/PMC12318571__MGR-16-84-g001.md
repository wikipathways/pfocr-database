caption: 'Pathophysiological mechanisms of long-COVID neurological manifestations
  and neuroprotective effects of medical gases.Schematic representation of SARS-CoV-2
  neuroinvasion pathways and the neuroprotective mechanisms of medical gases in long-COVID
  brain fog. (A) Pathophysiology: SARS-CoV-2 enters the CNS via three main routes:
  the olfactory pathway (through the nose), trigeminal ganglia, and retrograde axonal
  transport. This neuroinvasion leads to neuroinflammation (characterized by activated
  microglia and astrocytes releasing inflammatory cytokines) and oxidative stress
  (with increased reactive oxygen species and cell apoptosis). (B) Medical gases intervention:
  Different medical gases exert neuroprotective effects through specific molecular
  mechanisms. Hyperbaric oxygen (O2) upregulates oxygen- and pressure-sensitive genes
  and promotes stem cell proliferation and neurogenesis, leading to enhanced neuroplasticity
  and improved cognitive function. Hydrogen (H) upregulates regulatory T cells (T-reg)
  and inhibits IL-1β and ROS production, reducing neuroinflammation. Xenon (Xe) inhibits
  NMDA receptors at the glycine site, reducing excitotoxicity and preserving neuronal
  function. Argon (Ar) modulates TLR2/4, activates the ERK1/2 pathway, and upregulates
  Nrf2, resulting in reduced oxidative stress and inhibition of apoptosis. Volatile
  anesthetics (isoflurane/sevoflurane/desflurane) inhibit the TLR-4/NF-κB pathway
  and activate HIF-1α, attenuating inflammatory responses and reducing pro-inflammatory
  cytokines. Collectively, these interventions contribute to improved neurological
  outcomes in long-COVID patients experiencing brain fog. Created with BioRender.com.
  CNS: Central nervous system; COVID-19: coronavirus disease 19; ERK1/2: extracellular
  signal-regulated kinase 1/2; HIF-1α: hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha; IL: interleukin;
  NF: nuclear factor; NMDA: N-methyl-D-aspartate; Nrf2: nuclear factor erythroid 2-related
  factor 2; ROS: reactive oxygen species; SARS-CoV-2: severe acute respiratory syndrome
  coronavirus 2; TLR: Toll-like receptor'
