caption: 'Genetic and molecular adaptations to hypoxia in native high‐altitude populations.
  Under normoxic conditions (left), HIF‐2α (encoded by EPAS1) is hydroxylated by PHD‐2
  (encoded by EGLN1) at the ODD domain in the presence of oxygen, marking it for proteasomal
  degradation and suppressing hypoxia‐associated genes such as EPO, VEGF, ACE, and
  eNOS. In this state, EPO levels, red blood cell counts, and Hb remain within normal
  ranges in sea levels. In short‐term hypoxia (middle), individuals, especially those
  carrying the wild‐type alleles in genetic variants of EPAS1 (rs13419896:G, rs4953354:A)
  and EGLN1 (rs1453166:G, rs2153364:A), show increased gene mRNA expressions, impaired
  HIF‐2α degradation, and overactivation of hypoxia‐associated genes, potentially
  leading to polycythemia and CMS. With hypoxia exposure over generations (right),
  high‐altitude adaptation favors genetic variants in EPAS1 (rs13419896:A, rs4953354:G)
  and EGLN1 (rs1453166:A, rs2153364:G), resulting in downregulation of the mRNA expression
  levels of EPAS1 and EGLN1, lower HIF‐2α levels, and moderated activations of hypoxia‐associated
  genes. This HIF pathway eventually regulates EPO and Hb production, preventing excessive
  erythropoiesis and reducing CMS risk, contributing to a hypoxia‐tolerant phenotype
  in Sherpa highlanders. Abbreviations: ACE: Angiotensin‐converting enzyme; CMS: Chronic
  mountain sickness; EGLN1: egl‐9 family hypoxia inducible factor 1; eNOS: Endothelial
  nitric oxide synthase; EPO: Erythropoietin; EPAS1: Endothelial PAS domain protein
  1; Hb: Hemoglobin; HIF: Hypoxia‐inducible factor; ODD: Oxygen‐dependent degradation;
  PHD‐2: Prolyl hydroxylase domain‐containing protein 2; VEGF: Vascular endothelial
  growth factor'
