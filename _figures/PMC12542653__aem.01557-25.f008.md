caption: Five-tier model summarizing the adaptive response of D. hansenii to BaP exposure.
  After internalization (1), BaP is activated via cytochrome P450 (CYP) monooxygenases,
  NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductases (CPR), and epoxide hydrolases (EHs); (2) antioxidant
  defense through glutathione-related enzymes (glutathione S-transferases [GSTs],
  glutathione reductase [GR], glutathione peroxidase [GP], and glutathione synthetase
  [GS]) and ROS-scavenging enzymes (superoxide dismutase [SOD] and catalase [CAT]);
  (3) metabolite conjugation and export by ATP-binding cassette (ABC) and major facilitator
  superfamily (MFS) or vacuolar internalization; (4) catabolism (supported by the
  overexpression of short-chain dehydrogenase/reductases [SDR], alcohol dehydrogenases
  [ADH], aldehyde dehydrogenases [ALDH], and other enzymes) and metabolic reprogramming
  involving the pentose phosphate pathway, glyoxylate cycle, β-oxidation, and the
  tricarboxylic acid cycle to maintain redox balance; and (5) membrane remodeling.
  Under cometabolic conditions (presence of both glucose and BaP), glucose functions
  as the primary energy source. This figure was constructed using data from KEGG pathways
  that are significantly overrepresented in the presence of BaP (see Fig. S3 and S4;
  Table S2). Dotted lines indicate pathways that were not verified by RT-qPCR
