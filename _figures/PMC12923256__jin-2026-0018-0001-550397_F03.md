caption: Core intracellular signaling pathways in macrophages in UC. Receptors on
  the cell membrane (e.g., TLR4, TNFR) activate the NF-κB pathway upon receiving signals
  like TNF-α and LPS, prompting the IKK complex to phosphorylate and degrade IκB,
  releasing NF-κB dimers into the nucleus to initiate transcription of pro-inflammatory
  genes (e.g., TNF, IL-6). Simultaneously, cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, IL-4/13) activate
  JAK kinases and STAT proteins (e.g., STAT1 driving M1 gene iNOS expression, STAT6
  regulating M2 gene Arg1) via the JAK-STAT pathway, controlling macrophage polarization
  direction. Intracellular danger signals (e.g., ATP, ROS) activate the NLRP3 inflammasome,
  which, upon assembly, activates caspase-1, subsequently cleaving pro-IL-1β/pro-IL-18
  into their active forms and inducing pyroptosis. These pathways cross talk with
  each other, collectively driving macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, inducing metabolic
  reprogramming such as aerobic glycolysis, and promoting the massive synthesis and
  secretion of pro-inflammatory factors and ROS/RNS, ultimately mediating the inflammatory
  progression and tissue damage in UC
