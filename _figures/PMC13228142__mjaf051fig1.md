caption: Schematic of Notch signaling pathway activation. The Notch receptor precursor
  undergoes O-glycosylation and O-fucosylation in the endoplasmic reticulum. Within
  the Golgi apparatus, furin-like convertase cleaves the glycosylated precursor at
  the S1 site, forming a heterodimer that translocates to the cell surface. Upon binding
  to Notch ligands (DLL and JAG families), the receptor undergoes ADAM-mediated cleavage
  at the S2 site, releasing NECD. The ligand–receptor complex is endocytosed into
  the signal-sending cell and degraded via ubiquitination, though ligands may recycle
  to the membrane. The residual receptor (NEXT) is cleaved at the S3 site by γ-secretase,
  releasing NICD into the signal-receiving cell. NICD translocates to the nucleus,
  binds to CSL, and recruits MAML and RBPJ to form a transcriptional activation complex.
  This displaces corepressors, derepressing downstream target genes (e.g. HES and
  HEY)
