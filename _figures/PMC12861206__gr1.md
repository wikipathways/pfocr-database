caption: 'Exosomes loaded with noncoding RNAs mediate intercellular communication.
  In the donor cell, RNA polymerase II (RNA Pol II) transcribes precursor noncoding
  RNAs (pre-miRNA from pri-miRNA, lncRNA, and circRNA from pre-mRNA via back-splicing)
  in the nucleus. Following nuclear export and maturation in the cytoplasm, these
  ncRNAs are selectively packaged into exosomes through interactions with RNA-binding
  proteins (RBPs). This packaging occurs during the formation of intraluminal vesicles
  within multivesicular endosomes (MVEs). Upon fusion of the MVE with the plasma membrane,
  exosomes are released into the extracellular space. Recipient cells (top) internalize
  these exosomes through 4 distinct, numbered mechanisms: direct fusion with the plasma
  membrane; endocytosis of extracellular constituents; receptor-ligand binding, which
  triggers an intracellular signaling pathway; and phagocytosis or macropinocytosis.
  The delivered ncRNAs subsequently modulate gene expression and cellular functions
  in the recipient cytoplasm'
