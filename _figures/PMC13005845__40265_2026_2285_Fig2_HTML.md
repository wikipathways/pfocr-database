caption: The preclinical CRISPR-based gene editing strategies under investigation
  for EB. A Gene editing facilitated by Cas9 nuclease or Cas9n can be used for gene
  reframing, gene inactivation, or HDR. Gene reframing aims to introduce INDELs or
  excise faulty exons mediated by EJ repair to restore the open reading frame and
  consequent protein expression. Gene inactivation employs an sgRNA designed to overlap
  with a dominant mutation to facilitate INDEL formation specifically on the mutant
  allele to disrupt the open reading frame and protein expression. For precise gene
  repair, an HDR template containing a desired edit (shown in green) flanked by homology
  arms promotes genomic integration into the target locus. B Gene editing facilitated
  by BE aims to convert A•T→G•C or C•G→T•A base pairs. To achieve this, a deaminase
  enzyme fused to a Cas9n deaminates a target nucleotide to form an intermediate nucleotide
  (uracil if using a cytosine base editor, inosine if using an adenine base editor),
  which is then converted to a different base via the MMR pathway. The Cas9n generates
  a nick on the opposite strand to bias repair towards the strand containing the deaminated
  nucleotide. Cytosine base editors contain a UGI enzyme fusion to prevent reversion
  of the uracil intermediate back to the original nucleotide. C PE employs an RT fused
  to a Cas9n complexed with a specialized ‘pegRNA.’ Following the generation of a
  nick on the non-target strand, a 3ʹ flap is liberated and hybridizes with a PBS
  within the pegRNA, initiating reverse transcription and installation of the desired
  edit using the RT template. The newer PE3 system includes an additional nicking
  gRNA targeting the unedited strand (target strand) to enhance gene editing efficiency
  by biasing retention of the edited strand. Created in BioRender (www.biorender.com).
