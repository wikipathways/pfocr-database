caption: 'General mechanisms of ferroptosis − Ferroptosis is regulated through multiple
  metabolic pathways. The transferrin receptor (TFR1) mediates the import of ferric
  iron (Fe3+), which is converted to its ferrous form (Fe2+). This conversion catalyzes
  and contributes to the formation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydroxyl
  radicals, via the Fenton reaction. Additionally, ROS, including lipid peroxides,
  are generated through lipid peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs)
  in the cell membrane. The accumulation of ROS propagates oxidative damage, which
  is typically detoxified by two key ferroptosis regulators involved in defense mechanisms:
  GPX4 and FSP1. Concurrently, depletion of glutathione (GSH), inactivation of GPX4,
  and compromise of the FSP1-CoQ10-NADH pathway lead to cell death. (Green arrow –
  promotion/activation/upregulation of lipid peroxidation; black arrows indicate different
  progressing molecular pathways; red T ending lines – inhibition). Created with BioRender.com.
