caption: Molecular and cellular effects of oxygen-ozone mixture mediated by redox
  signalling pathways. Schematic representation of the molecular mechanisms and cell-specific
  effects induced by medical ozone exposure. Ozone-generated reactive oxygen species
  (ROS) activate the Nrf2/Keap1 signalling pathway, promoting Nrf2 nuclear translocation
  and binding to antioxidant response elements (ARE), which leads to the transcription
  of cytoprotective and antioxidant genes. Concurrently, Nrf2 activation counteracts
  NF-κB signalling by preventing IκBα degradation, thereby reducing the expression
  of pro-inflammatory genes. These molecular events result in pleiotropic effects
  across different cell types, including enhanced glutathione recycling and oxygen
  release in erythrocytes; increased NO synthesis in endothelial cells; platelet activation
  and aggregation; immune modulation; stimulation of bone formation and collagen II
  production in skeletal cells; reduced muscle fatigue in skeletal muscle cells; increased
  activation, migration, proliferation, and extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition
  in fibroblasts; enhanced migration, proliferation, and differentiation in keratinocytes;
  and reduced parthanatos and apoptosis in neuronal cells. Created with BioRender.com
