caption: Potassium signal transduction in plants under K+ starvation. K+ deficiency
  in plants is sensed by KSN, leading to hyperpolarisation of the membrane. It further
  leads to activation of the Ca2+ channel, resulting in increased Ca2+ concentration
  in the cytoplasm. Low K+ also leads to ROS accumulation via RIC, activating the
  Ca2+ channels. These Ca2+ bursts activate SGN1/3 via phosphorylation and CIF peptides.
  This induces RBOHs peroxidases responsible for ROS generation. RBOHD/F‐induced ROS
  is involved in Casparian strip formation and suberin deposition, while ROS produced
  via RBOHC/D is responsible for HAK5 upregulation. In addition, ROS facilitates RICR,
  leading to an increase in cytosolic Ca2+. This spike in ([Ca2+]cyt) generates Ca2+
  signatures, further activating CBL‐CIPKs complexes. At the plasma membrane, AKT1,
  AKT2 and HAK5 are activated via (CBL1/9‐CIPK23), (CBL4‐CIPK6) and (CBL1/9‐CIPK1/9/23)
  complexes, respectively. Activation of AKTs and HAKs is responsible for increasing
  the K+ uptake. AKT1 also interacts with KC1 to limit the K+ leakage from the roots.
  This expression of AKT1 and HAK5 is also enhanced by the binding of WRKY6 and MYB77
  to their respective promoter. Simultaneously, TPCs and TPKs, the transporters present
  in the tonoplast, are also activated via CIPK3/9/23/26, thus participating in K+
  efflux. These CBLs‐CIPKs complexes inhibit the TORC pathway via phosphorylation.
  Under higher K+ conditions, TORC reciprocally inhibits the CBL‐CIPKs‐dependent K+
  deficiency response. Furthermore, phosphatases and PA are involved in maintaining
  the K+ homoeostasis in the cell. Under normal conditions, AIP1 and PP2C1 negatively
  regulate AKT1 and CIPK9, respectively. In addition, another transcription factor,
  ARF2, negatively regulates the HAK5 expression. Apart from these, other transporters,
  including KUP7, SKOR and NRT, are involved in loading K+ into the xylem tissue,
  which is then unloaded into leaf tissue for further root‐shoot‐translocation of
  K+ in plants. (Potassium, K+; K+ SENSING NICHE, KSN; Calcium, Ca2+; Reactive Oxygen
