caption: 'MicroRNAs involved in the regulation of TLR2 signaling pathway in intestinal
  epithelium. Blue arrows indicate stimulation, red arrows indicate inhibition, and
  dashed red arrows represent the removal of inhibition. LPS: Lipopolysaccharide,
  TLR2: Toll-like receptor 2, TRAP: TNF Receptor-associated protein, MyD88: Myeloid
  differentiation primary response gene 88, SHIP1: Src Homology 2 Domain-containing
  Inositol Phosphatase 1. IRAK: Interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase, TRAF: TNF
  receptor-associated factor, TAK1: Transforming growth factor beta-activated kinase-1,
  TAB: TAK1 binding protein, MAPK: Mitogen-activated protein kinase, NEMO: NF-κB essential
  modulator, IKKα: IκB kinase alpha, IKKβ: IκB kinase beta, JNK: c-Jun N-terminal
  kinase, P38: p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase, AP-1: activator protein-1, IRF3:
  Interferon regulatory factor-3, IKKβ: IκB kinase beta, NF-κB: Nuclear factor kappa-light-chain
  enhancer of activated B cells, TNF-α: Tumor necrosis factor alpha, IL-1β: Interleukin-1
  beta, IL-6: Interleukin-6. One of the key effects of miR-155 on the TLR2 signaling
  pathway is the inhibition of the SHIP-1 enzyme. SHIP-1 normally inhibits MyD88,
  helping to limit inflammation. However, miR-155 suppresses SHIP-1, which leads to
  increased inflammation in the intestinal epithelium and disrupts barrier function.
  Additionally, miR-155 activates MAPK/JNK and p38/MAPK pathways, promoting excessive
  intestinal inflammation and disrupting epithelial integrity. The inhibition of TAB2
  and IKK-β by miR-155 further amplifies these inflammatory responses. In contrast,
  miR-146a suppresses TLR2 signaling by targeting molecules such as MyD88, IRAK1.
  TRAF6, and IKKα'
