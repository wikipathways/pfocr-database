caption: 'Lactate and lactylation regulate macrophage polarization and function. Left
  panel (light blue): Lactate exerts anti-inflammatory effects by binding to G protein-coupled
  receptor 81 (GPR81) on macrophages, inhibiting the secretion of proinflammatory
  cytokines. Additionally, lactate promotes M2 polarization through the hypoxia-inducible
  factor-1α (HIF-1α) and HIF-2α pathways. In the hindlimb ischemia (HLI) model, lactate
  enhances M2 polarization, which in turn promotes angiogenesis and muscle regeneration.
  Right panel (pink): In inflammation and irritable bowel disease (IBD) models, histone
  lactylation drives M2 polarization, exerting anti-inflammatory and prorepair effects.
  The HLI model demonstrates the histone H3 lysine 18 (H3K18) lactylation-mediated
  regulation of postischemic repair genes. Microglial histone lactylation promotes
  Alzheimer''s disease (AD) progression via a glycolysis-lactylation-pyruvate kinase
  M2 (PKM2) loop, whereas exercise-induced methyl-CpG binding proteins 2 (MeCP2) lactylation
  attenuates atherosclerosis (AS) through M2 polarization. AMPK: adenosinemonophosphate
  (AMP)-activated protein kinase; YAP: Yes-associated protein; NF-κB: nuclear factor-κB;
  TNF-α: tumor necrosis factor-α; IL-6: interleukin-6; mTORC1: mechanistic target
  of rapamycin complex 1; ATP6V0d2: ATPase H+ transporting V0 subunit d2; VEGF: vascular
  endothelial growth factor; Arg1: arginase-1; MCT1: monocarboxylate transporter 1;
  la: lactylation; RUNX1: Runt-related transcription factor 1; TLR: Toll-like receptor;
  BCAP: B cell adapter for phosphoinositide 3-kinase; PI3K: phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase;
  Akt: protein kinase B; Klf4: Kruppel-like factor 4; Aβ: amyloid-beta'
