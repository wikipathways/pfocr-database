caption: Opposing effects of gut- and brain-derived serotonin on bone remodeling.
  Tryptophan metabolism produces serotonin in two distinct compartments with opposing
  effects on bone. Gut-derived serotonin (95% of total) acts on osteoblasts via the
  5-HT1B receptor, inhibiting bone formation by suppressing cAMP signaling. In contrast,
  brain-derived serotonin signals through hypothalamic 5-HT2C receptors to promote
  bone formation and reduce resorption. This duality explains the bone loss associated
  with drugs that increase peripheral serotonin (e.g., SSRIs), highlighting the gut-derived
  pathway as a therapeutic target
