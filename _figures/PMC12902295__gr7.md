caption: Proposed model of the phospholipase C4 pathway and its central role in the
  tight crosstalk between Ca2+ and ROS in Ca2+-mediated ferroptotic cell death downstream
  of resistosome activation.In plants, apoplastic Ca2+ ions are released and imported
  into the cytoplasm through calcium channels and resistosomes to drive iron- and
  ROS-dependent ferroptotic cell death. Elevated intracellular Ca2+ stimulates PLC
  localization and activity. At the plasma membrane, PLC4 hydrolyzes PIP2 to generate
  DAG and IP3. IP3 is recognized by its receptor in the ER and promotes Ca2+ release
  from ER stores. Increased intracellular Ca2+ further enhances PLC activity, producing
  additional DAG and IP3. ROS sensors detect accumulated ROS and subsequently activate
  calcium channels, resulting in sustained cytosolic Ca2+ elevation. Cytoplasmic Ca2+
  regulates Rboh during ROS production. In the cytoplasm, ROS reacts with ferrous
  (Fe2+) ions to initiate the Fenton reaction, leading to lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis.
  EGTA chelates apoplastic Ca²⁺. Neomycin inhibits the PLC substrate PIP2. NDP blocks
  transmembrane L-type calcium channel activity. TFP stimulates Ca²⁺ release via IP3-gated
  calcium channels in the ER. ASM, a salicylic acid analog, may regulate defense-gene
  expression and systemic acquired resistance (SAR). Arrows and T-shaped lines indicate
  activation or inhibition, respectively. Bold lines indicate the major pathway in
  which PLC, acting downstream of the resistosome, enhances Ca2+ and ROS accumulation
  to induce ferroptosis. The dotted line represents a hypothetical event. ER, endoplasmic
  reticulum; PIP2, phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate; DAG, diacylglycerol; IP3,
  inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate; Rboh, respiratory burst oxidase homologs; NDP, nifedipine;
  TFP, trifluoperazine dihydrochloride; ASM, acibenzolar-S-methyl; EGTA, ethylene
  glycol-bis(2-aminoethylether)-N,N,N′,N′-tetra-acetic acid; NDP, nifedipine
