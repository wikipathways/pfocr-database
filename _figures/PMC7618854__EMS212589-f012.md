caption: The brain interstitial space and the cerebrospinal fluid are poor in glucose.
  Thus, metastatic cells upregulate the enzymes to catabolize glucose efficiently
  and make use of alternative metabolites including branched-chain amino acids (BCAA),
  acetate, glutamine (also contributing to antioxidant defense through the antiport
  with cysteine), γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) and speculatively ketone bodies. Furthermore,
  metastatic cells upregulate de novo synthesis pathways to feed membrane and nucleotide
  synthesis necessary for proliferation. However, resident cells metabolically support
  cancer cells by supplying nutrients such as glucose, fatty acids, speculatively
  through gap junctions, and enhance protumorous signaling via glutamate-activated
  N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR). FBP2, Fructose bisphosphatase 2; PCK1/2,
  Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase 1/2; LDH, Lactate dehydrogenase, NK, Natural killer;
  ACSS2, Acyl-coenzyme A synthetase short-chain family member 2; PHGDH, Phosphoglycerate
  dehydrogenase; SREBP1, Sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1; FASN, Fatty
  acid synthase; ACC1, Acetyl-CoA carboxylase 1; TCA, Tricarboxylic acid; ETC, Electron
  transport chain; ATP, Adenosine triphosphate; α-KG, α-Ketoglutarate; SLC7A11, Solute
  carrier family 7 member 11; ROS, Reactive oxygen species; PPARγ, Peroxisome proliferator
  activated receptor gamma; miR-122, microRNA 122
