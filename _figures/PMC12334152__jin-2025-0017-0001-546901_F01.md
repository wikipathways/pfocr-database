caption: Role of oxidative stress and chronic inflammation in the development and
  progression of IC/BPS. IC/BPS is considered a multifactorial disease with a complex
  pathobiology ultimately leading to unresolved inflammation and bladder dysfunction.
  Reactive oxygen species (ROS), derived from poor blood supply to the bladder, can
  directly injure urothelial cells, increasing their permeability and allowing toxic
  substances from urine to permeate into the underlying lamina propria leading to
  inflammation. ROS can also cause damage to the mitochondrial membrane, which results
  in release of fragmented mtDNA into the cytosol and extracellular space. Mitochondrial
  DNA (mtDNA) can bind to innate immunity receptors (NOD- and toll-like receptors;
  NOD and TLR) on the surface of urothelial cells and activate NF-kB signaling cascade
  leading to inflammation. ROS can directly activate NF-kB pathway leading to increased
  production of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α and dysregulation between regulatory and effector
  T cells. Damaged and activated urothelial cells trigger the local immune response
  of the bladder, increase production and secretion of various pro-inflammatory cytokines
  and chemokines, and orchestrate immune cell infiltration. In turn, these events
  also cause increased production of ROS, ultimately leading to a self-reinforcing
  cycle of inflammation. The figure was created using Biorender.com
