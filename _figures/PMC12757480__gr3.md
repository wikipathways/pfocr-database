caption: Photoelectrical and redox-driven mitochondrial apoptosis mediated by voltage-gated
  ion channels (VGICs). This schematic illustrates the mechanism by which photoelectrical
  stimulation, through both photocapacitive and photofaradaic pathways, induces apoptosis.
  Photostimulation depolarizes the plasma membrane, activating voltage-gated calcium
  and potassium channels (VGICs), which derive intracellular Ca2+ influx and ionic
  imbalances. Concurrently, photofaradaic processes and external ROS species (e.g.,
  hydrogen peroxide) further increase intracellular ROS levels. These factors disrupt
  the mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), leading to excessive ROS production
  within mitochondria. Mitochondrial dysfunction facilitates cytochrome c release
  through voltage-regulated channels and permeability transition pores (PTPs), initiating
  caspase activation and the apoptotic cascade. VGICs are key regulatory nodes in
  this electrobioenergetic interface, linking light-triggered bioelectric changes
  to mitochondrial-driven cell fate
