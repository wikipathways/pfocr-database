caption: 'Nociceptive pathway, neuropathic pain models, and major glial cell types.
  A: Illustration of the neuronal pathway underlying nociceptive pain. Nociceptor
  afferents transmit signals from the periphery through the dorsal root ganglia (DRG)
  to the spinal cord (SC), where local interneurons and descending pathways modulate
  the signals before relaying them to the brain. B: Peripheral nerve injury induces
  neuropathic pain. Several widely used animal models include: spared nerve injury
  (SNI), which involves ligating and transecting the tibial and common peroneal nerves
  while leaving the sural nerve intact; chronic constriction injury (CCI), which involves
  placing ligatures around the sciatic nerve; partial sciatic nerve ligation (PSNL),
  which creates a partial ligation of the sciatic nerve; and spinal nerve ligation
  (SNL), which involves ligating and transecting the L5 spinal nerve. C and D: Glial
  cells interact with neurons in both peripheral and central nervous systems. In the
  peripheral nervous system, satellite glial cells (SGCs) surround the cell bodies
  of all sensory neurons in the DRG, while Schwann cells myelinate the axons of Aβ
  and Aδ afferents. In the central nervous system, microglia patrol the tissue environment,
  astrocytes provide metabolic and synaptic support to neurons, and oligodendrocytes
  myelinate neuronal axons. Figure created using a licensed version of Biorender.com'
