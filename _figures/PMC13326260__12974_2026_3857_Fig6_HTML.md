caption: 'Therapeutic strategies targeting mechanical pathologies in tumors. Therapeutic
  strategies targeting tumor mechanical pathologies can be broadly categorized into
  three classes: external mechanical interventions, normalization of mechanical microenvironment
  and modulation of intracellular signaling pathways. External mechanical interventions
  include focused ultrasound, MNPs and implantable hydrogels, exerting biomechanical
  modulation through distinct mechanisms. Focused ultrasound enhances drug penetration
  via sonoporation [135] or induces tissue ablation [136]. Magnetic fields apply periodic
  traction force to trigger tumor cell death with the use of MNPs [137]. Biohybrid
  chiral hydrogels inhibits GSC stemness and enhances postoperative GBM therapy [138].
  Mechanical microenvironment normalization focuses on two important components of
  ECM, collagen and HA, through collagen degradation via bacteria expressing collagenase
  (ST-SOT) [139], inhibition of collagen crosslinking using LOX inhibitors [140] or
  LOXL2 inhibitors [141], and HA degradation using PEGylated hyaluronidase [142] or
  engineered hyaluronidase systems based on bacteria [143], viruses [144] or CAR T
  cells [145]. Alternatively, normalization can be achieved by inhibiting ECM synthesis
  instead of promoting degradation, through TGF-β blockade [146] or CTGF blockade
  [147]. Finally, modulation of intracellular mechanotransduction targets key signaling
  molecules. Inhibition of mechanosensors includes Piezo1 blockade [148–150], TRPV4
  blockade [151], TRPC1 blockade [152], integrin blockade [153–155]. Inhibition strategies
  targeting signaling pathways include calcineurin inhibitors [156, 157], FAK inhibitors
  [158–160], ROCK inhibitors [161, 162] and non-muscle myosin II inhibitors [163,
  164]. At the transcriptional level, YAP/TAZ activity can be suppressed by simvastatin,
  verteporfin, ION537 or IK-930 [165–168]'
